# Playbook: Clone-and-Rebrand

A working product — the user's own, or one already rebuilt under the Clone / Rebuild playbook — keeps its functionality, data, and users, and gets a completely new identity: name, brand, design language, voice and copy, domains, and search presence. Nothing about what the product *does* changes. Everything about what it is *called, looks like, sounds like, and is found as* does — in one coordinated pass, because a half-finished rebrand is worse than none: two names in the wild split search equity, confuse users, and leave the old brand legally and operationally alive in places nobody remembered to look.

> **The legal line, which governs every step below: function may be cloned; protected expression may not be copied.** This playbook keeps the product's function and replaces its identity — so the new name, design language, voice, and copy must be original expression authored for this product, never copied from a third party's product, and never a continuation of protected expression the product had no right to carry. Where the product being rebranded is itself a clone, clone-ethics' authorization record and LG-186/LG-187 decide what may carry over at all.

## Goal

Ship the product under its new identity with zero unapproved old-brand residue anywhere a user, a search engine, a mail server, or a lawyer can find it: rendered pages, assets, metadata, structured data, emails, notifications, legal documents, app-store and directory listings, social profiles, and the codebase's user-visible strings. Carry the old identity's search equity across deliberately — a complete one-to-one redirect map from old URLs to new, the old domain kept alive and redirecting, sitemaps and canonicals pointing only at the new identity — and prove both halves (no residue, no lost routes) with scans and crawls, not by inspection. Functionality, user accounts, and data are untouched: if a change would alter behavior, it is not part of this playbook.

## Departments + order

Step 0 (always-on core) → **D14 pre-flight (rights and clearance only)** → D1 → D9 (baseline + surface inventory) → **rebrand-execution (anchor, D16 — loaded at step 3, executed at step 4)** → D3+D13 → D4+D11+D18 (touchpoints) → D14 (full) → D9 (seo-migration) → D7+D8 → D6 → D9+D12+D18 (cutover verification, monitoring, announcement)

**Order deviations, and why.** Three, all forced by the mission:

1. **D14's clearance pre-flight runs before D1**, not in its usual post-build position, because a new name that fails trademark/domain clearance after design work has started burns the entire design step. Clearance is cheap at step 1 and expensive at step 6.
2. **D9 runs twice, at both ends.** Up front it captures the SEO baseline and the brand-surface inventory while the old identity is still fully live — after cutover, the old state can no longer be measured. At the end it executes the migration (redirects, sitemaps, canonicals) and verifies continuity. A rebrand that inventories after it redesigns will miss surfaces, and one that measures rankings after it switches cannot prove what the switch cost.
3. **D6 (domains/DNS) runs before the final D9 verification, not as the last build step**, because the redirect map can only be proven against the real new domain with real certificates. The announcement (D18) is the actual last step, and it waits for that proof.

There is deliberately **no D2/D5 build step**: no new architecture and no schema change. D4 appears only as a touchpoint step (user-visible strings, email and notification templates, metadata the backend emits) — handlers, contracts, and data stay exactly as they were. If the commission also asks for new features or a rebuild, that work runs under its own playbook first; this playbook starts from a working product and ends with the same product, renamed.

## Docs to write first

Before any identity work, scaffold from `project-templates` and write:

The full mandated set for this type is `templates/mandated-manifest-clone-and-rebrand.txt`, which governs (G-PM-001); the list below is the hand-written starting subset, not the whole set.

- `requirements.md` — the rebrand's scope in both directions: what changes (name, marks, palette, type, voice, domain, handles) and what explicitly does not (functionality, data, accounts, pricing, contracts). The not-list is the scope-control instrument for the whole run.
- `fact-sheet.md` — the old identity's verified facts (current name, domains, handles, listings, legal entity name as it appears in existing documents) and the new identity's, each with its source. The clearance checks in step 1 read from here, not from memory.
- `brand-surface-inventory.md` — every place the old identity exists, produced in step 3 but with its file and classification scheme agreed before the audit starts: routes and templates; static assets and filenames (logos, favicons, og images, screenshots, PDFs); metadata and structured data; email and notification templates; legal documents; app-store, directory, and social listings; third-party integrations that display the name (payment descriptors, OAuth consent screens, embedded widgets); documentation and help content; user-visible strings in code. Each row gets an owner step (5, 6, 7, or 8) and a disposition: replace, redirect, retire, or retain-with-reason.
- `old-new-mapping.md` — the single mapping table every later step works from: old name → new name, old terms → new terms (product-specific vocabulary, plan names, feature names if they change), old domain/paths → new domain/paths, old handles → new handles, old asset files → new asset files. Frozen at step 4; a change after the freeze is a `DECISIONS.md` entry and re-triggers the affected scans.
- `redirect-map.md` — drafted in step 3 from the route inventory, finalized in step 8: one row per old URL, its new target, and its type. Written before cutover, never improvised during it.
- `progress.md`, `DECISIONS.md` — session log and decision record.
- `cutover-runbook.md` — the ordered cutover sequence for steps 10–11 with the rollback trigger and the rollback steps (per `rollback`), written before step 9 so verification tests what cutover will actually do.
- `.env.example` — new-domain and mail-domain values by name + purpose (§5.9); sending-domain credentials are secrets and follow the secrets baseline.

## Step-by-step sequence

**Step 0 — Core and scaffold.** Load `bundles/core.md`; generate the project (G-PM-001, G-PM-002); write the docs above; load `secrets-management` before any registrar, DNS, or mail-provider credential is touched.

**Step 1 — D14 pre-flight: rights and clearance before design.** Establish the basis for rebranding at all: the product is the user's own, or the engagement covers identity work (for a cloned product, the Clone / Rebuild playbook's authorization record is the input — read it, do not re-create it). Then clear the *new* identity: trademark and trade-name search in the markets the product operates in, domain availability for the exact name, and handle availability on the platforms in the inventory. Clearance results are recorded in `fact-sheet.md` with sources and dates.
◆ **DECISION — is the new name clear?** A name with a conflicting mark in the product's own category, or whose exact-match domain and primary handles are all taken, does not proceed to design on hope. The options are exactly: pick a different name, or proceed with the conflict recorded and counsel's view noted (G-LG-035's counsel-review pattern). (Unattended run: BOOTSTRAP's approval-points rule applies at this point — a decision recorded in the commissioning brief or `requirements.md` counts as the answer. If none is recorded and no user is reachable, clearance is never log-and-continued and never safe-defaulted: whether a name may be used is a legal position (§6.3 Step 5 never list), and the proceed-with-conflict option requires counsel's view, which an unattended run cannot have. Stop before design, log the blocker in `progress.md` (§6.3 step 3), and ask the user with concrete options (§6.3 step 4). Clearance is the one step whose failure gets more expensive every day it is deferred.)

**Step 2 — Requirements and scope freeze (D1).** requirements-writing turns the commission into the two lists in `requirements.md` (changes / does-not-change); scope-control records the freeze. Name the audiences who must not be surprised: existing users (they will log in to a product that looks and is called something else), search visitors arriving on old URLs, and recipients of already-queued email. Those three audiences set the step 6 and step 11 work.
◆ **DECISION — big-bang or phased identity switch?** Big-bang (one cutover: name, design, domain together) is the default and the only mode this playbook's gates fully prove. A phased switch (name first, domain later, or the reverse) leaves a deliberate mixed-identity period: it requires a `DECISIONS.md` entry naming the interim state, which surfaces carry which identity during it, and the end date — and the residue gates in step 9 are then run against the *interim* mapping, not the final one. (Unattended run: BOOTSTRAP's approval-points rule applies at this point — a decision recorded in the commissioning brief or `requirements.md` counts as the answer; if none is recorded and no user is reachable, follow the §6.3 stuck protocol (log-and-continue, then the safe defaults) and record the substitution in `progress.md`.)

**Step 3 — Baseline and surface inventory (D9, first pass).** While the old identity is still fully live: technical-seo runs the route inventory crawl (G-CO-002) and the redirect and status audit (G-CO-003) on the old domain; web-analytics records the baseline — which routes carry organic landing traffic, which carry none — in `brand-surface-inventory.md`, citing Search Console / analytics evidence per route class, so step 8 can rank redirect-map rows by what is actually at stake. Then the surface audit itself, per the inventory scheme in the docs above: crawl the rendered site, dump the templates and string catalogs, list the asset folders, export the email/notification template set, and enumerate the off-site listings from `fact-sheet.md`. Every old-brand occurrence found becomes an inventory row. **Handoff:** this inventory is the input contract for steps 5–8; a surface discovered after step 8 is a defect in this step, and is logged as one.

**Step 4 — Identity spec and mapping freeze (anchor: rebrand-execution).** The new identity is specified as a buildable artifact, not a mood board: name and its exact spellings/capitalization, logo and mark files, palette, type, voice and terminology (the words the product uses for itself and its actions), and the naming rules for files, handles, and metadata. `old-new-mapping.md` is completed from the spec and the inventory and **frozen**; the rebrand-execution skill's inventory gate (G-CL-701) must pass on the inventory and the mapping before step 5 starts — the skill's remaining gates run at their own phases (G-CL-702, G-CL-703, and G-CL-704 at step 9; G-CL-705 at step 11). **Handoff:** D3 receives tokens-and-type decisions, D9-copy and D13 receive voice-and-terminology, D14 receives the legal name forms, D6 receives the domain list — all from this one document, so the departments cannot drift into slightly different versions of the new name.
◆ **DECISION — what happens to the old identity's assets?** Keep the old domain redirecting (default: indefinitely — it is the redirect map's home and the backlink equity's entry point), keep or release the old handles, keep or retire old-brand content pages. Releasing the old domain while external links still point at it hands those links to whoever registers it next. (Unattended run: BOOTSTRAP's approval-points rule applies at this point — a decision recorded in the commissioning brief or `requirements.md` counts as the answer; if none is recorded and no user is reachable, follow the §6.3 stuck protocol (log-and-continue, then the safe defaults) and record the substitution in `progress.md`.)

**Step 5 — Design language and copy (D3+D13, D9 copy).** design-tokens replaces the token source (palette, type scale, logo-derived values) and design-system re-expresses the components in the new language — tokens first, components second, pages last, so no page carries hand-set values the system does not own (G-FE-021 token build, G-FE-014 token and contrast enforcement, G-FE-011/G-FE-012 design-system acceptance). New marks are checked at favicon and app-icon sizes, not only at hero size. In the same step, copywriting + inclusive-copy rewrite the user-visible copy in the new voice: headlines, onboarding, empty states, errors, and transactional strings, worked from `old-new-mapping.md`'s terminology column (G-CO-053 naming lint — the old name and retired terms are the lint's pattern list; G-AC-091 banned-pattern lint, G-AC-093 glossary conformance). accessibility-audit runs on the re-skinned templates in this step, not after: a new palette is a new contrast surface (G-AC-001, G-AC-005; inherited G-EXP-05, G-EXP-06). **What differs from building new:** the layout, components, and content structure already exist and are not redesigned — a rebrand that quietly becomes a redesign breaks the step 2 scope freeze and invalidates the step 3 baseline.

**Step 6 — Touchpoints (D4+D11+D18).** Every non-page surface in the inventory is rebranded from the mapping table: transactional-email templates (sender name, From domain, headers, footers, legal lines) with mail authentication for the new sending domain proven before cutover (G-IN-061 authentication check, G-IN-064 end-to-end arrival test; G-CO-071/G-CO-072 for lifecycle-email); notifications (sender/app name, icons, copy — G-BE-131 trigger test per template class); help-center and user-onboarding content (names, screenshots, URLs — G-CX-022 UI-label and link audit, G-CX-025 stale-article sweep, where "stale" here means *old-brand*); app-store and directory listings, social profiles, OAuth consent screens, and payment descriptors, each updated or scheduled in the cutover runbook with its owner named. Screenshots are re-shot from the rebranded build — an old-UI screenshot under a new name is residue. **Handoff:** step 6 returns a per-row disposition to the inventory; a row it cannot complete (a listing controlled by a third party, a descriptor with a provider lead time) is escalated into the cutover runbook as a dated follow-up, not left as a silent gap.

**Step 7 — Legal identity (D14, full pass).** legal-documents regenerates terms, privacy, and any DPA/cookie text under the new name and entity details, diffed against the privacy register (G-LG-031 document-vs-register diff) and linted for template residue (G-LG-032 — run with the *old* brand's names and the template's placeholder names both on the pattern list). Check the places legal identity hides outside legal documents: copyright footers, invoice and receipt templates, email legal footers (step 6 owns the templates; this step owns what they must say), cookie-banner text, and the imprint/contact pages. If the legal entity itself is renamed or the product was assigned to a different entity, that fact and its effective date are stated in the documents and the announcement — a product rename and a company rename are different events and must not be blurred. licensing confirms the new marks and any new licensed assets (fonts, imagery) are covered for this use (G-LG-051 license scan).

**Step 8 — SEO migration (anchor: seo-migration, with D9).** The migration is built and tested on staging before any DNS change. seo-migration's gates (G-CO-141–G-CO-145) must pass on the redirect map and the migration plan; the map itself is executed and verified with technical-seo's instruments: every row of `redirect-map.md` is a one-hop 301 (or 308) from the old URL to its named new URL — no chains, no blanket redirect of everything to the homepage, no row without a target (a retired page redirects to its closest live successor or is deliberately answered 410, with the reason on the row). Canonicals, sitemaps (G-CO-091 sitemap audit, G-CO-092 count reconciliation against the route inventory), structured data (G-CO-081 CI schema validation, G-CO-082 markup-vs-visible diff — the Organization entity carries the new name and URL with one stable identity site-wide, inherited G-SCH-04), social/Open Graph tags and images (G-CO-111 tag dump diff, G-CO-112 og:image resolution), and metadata (G-CO-011 metadata lint) are all regenerated for the new domain and name — never find-and-replaced blindly, because titles, og URLs, and JSON-LD `@id`s fail in different ways. Internal links point at new URLs directly; a site that links to its own redirects taxes every crawl. **Handoff:** the tested redirect map and the new sitemap set are frozen artifacts handed to D6 — step 10 deploys exactly these files, and any change after this step re-runs this step's verification.

**Step 9 — Verification (D7+D8).** The residue hunt, run against the rendered build and the shipped string set, not source alone: the brand-residue scan (G-LG-094, applied to this mission — the "source brand" is the product's own old brand, and the approved-reuse list is the inventory's retain-with-reason rows: historical changelog entries, the redirect map itself, and legal version archives are legitimate old-name occurrences; everything else blocks) plus placeholder-scan (G-QA-141 — old name, old domain, old handles, and retired terms added to the sweep patterns for this run) and the naming lint re-run (G-CO-053). Asset-level residue gets its own pass: hash- and filename-level check for old logo files, favicons, og images, and screenshots in the shipped build and the asset manifest. Security pass (G-SE-001 ZAP baseline, 0 critical findings — the surface did not change, but the domain and mail configuration did, and those are security-relevant), and qa-gates (G-QA-111). The anchor skill's own gates run in this step as well: G-CL-702 (residue scan over code, catalogs, rendered HTML, sitemap, email templates, and downloadable assets, with the declared Organization logo URL and favicon fetched and confirmed as the new mark), G-CL-703 (URL and search integrity — the production URL set diffed against the step 3 baseline, every pre-launch URL answering directly or by its approved one-hop migration redirect), and G-CL-704 (functional regression — the critical-flow suite returns identical results before and after the swap, because the change set is identity-only under CL-711). **No cutover until green.**

**Step 10 — Domains and cutover (D6).** In runbook order: domains-dns records for the new domain proven against the record sheet (G-DO-101), registrar posture checked (G-DO-102), mail authentication present (G-DO-103); ssl-certificates live and probed (G-DO-111 external TLS scan, G-DO-112 redirect and HSTS probe); the old domain's redirect configuration deployed from step 8's frozen map; ci-cd (G-DO-001) and hosting (G-DO-071 external production check on the *new* domain, and the same check confirming the *old* domain answers with the expected redirect, not a parked page or an error). Performance budgets re-asserted on the rebranded templates (G-AN-101) — new fonts and hero assets are the usual regression.
◆ **STOP AND ASK — registrar, DNS, and sending-domain changes (§5.9/§6.3).** Transferring a domain, changing nameservers, or switching the production sending domain is hard to reverse inside the propagation window and breaks mail and traffic if wrong; it requires the user's explicit go-ahead, with the rollback steps from `cutover-runbook.md` rehearsed on staging first (G-DO-151). (Unattended run: a go-ahead recorded in the commissioning brief or `requirements.md` for this specific change counts as the answer. If none is recorded and no user is reachable, this point is never log-and-continued and never safe-defaulted — registrar, nameserver, and sending-domain changes are destructive-class actions on the §6.3 Step 5 never list. Log the blocker in `progress.md` (§6.3 step 3), ask the user with concrete options (§6.3 step 4), and make no registrar, DNS, or sending-domain change until the explicit go-ahead exists.)

**Step 11 — Continuity verification, monitoring, announcement (D9+D12+D18, last).** Only after step 10 is green: technical-seo's promotion indexability diff on the new domain (G-CO-001 — no staging directive, no old-domain canonical survives), the redirect and status audit re-run against the *live* old domain (G-CO-003 — every redirect-map row fetched and its status and final target compared to the map), the new sitemaps submitted and the change-of-address / property steps for the new domain completed in the search consoles the product uses, and the coverage review log started (G-CO-005). web-analytics confirms the tracking property reports the new domain under the new name (G-CO-121 tracking-plan diff) and monitoring watches the old domain's redirect traffic, 404s on the new domain, and mail bounce rates through the cutover window. Then, and only then, D18 announces: in-product notice and changelog entry (G-PM-061 changelog format), help-center announcement article, email to existing users from the *new* sending domain explaining the rename, what did not change (accounts, data, billing), and that the old address redirects. support-ticketing is briefed with the mapping table — "is this the same product?" is the predictable ticket, and the answer is scripted from `old-new-mapping.md`, not improvised. The run then closes under the anchor skill's G-CL-705: every third-party surface on the inventory opened in its own console, the step 3 baseline re-checked, and the residue report filed from `brand-surface-inventory.md` — every row `swapped`, `kept by exception` with its reason, or `missed` with a fix date. A rebrand with no residue report is not closed, however clean the cutover looked.

## Failure handling

- **Clearance fails at step 1 (name conflicted or unavailable):** stop before design. The cost of a rename is one step of legal checking; the cost of discovering the conflict at step 7 is steps 4–6 redone under a different name. No "proceed and rename the risky parts" partial pass exists.
- **Residue found at step 9 (G-LG-094 / G-QA-141 hit):** the hit's inventory row reopens and returns to its owning step (5, 6, or 7) as the work order; the scan re-runs over the whole surface class, not just the fixed file — one leftover string in a template family usually means the family was edited by hand instead of from the mapping table. A second hit in the same surface class triggers a full re-audit of that class against `brand-surface-inventory.md` before proceeding. Legitimate historical occurrences are resolved by adding the row to the approved-reuse list with its reason, never by allow-listing the old name globally — a global allow-list silently disables the scan for real residue.
- **Redirect verification fails at step 11 (G-CO-003: chains, loops, wrong targets, rows answering 200 or 404):** fix the map row and its server configuration, re-fetch the full map, not just the failed rows — chain and loop faults are configuration-level and rarely unique. If a class of old URLs has no honest target, the disposition is decided per row (closest successor, or deliberate 410) and recorded; bulk-redirecting the class to the homepage is recorded as a failure of this gate, not a fix for it.
- **Search continuity degrades after cutover (coverage review, G-CO-005):** first check the mechanical causes in order — redirect rows still live, sitemaps submitted and clean, canonicals on the new domain, no robots or noindex leftover from staging (G-CO-001 re-run). Do not "fix" a migration by reverting to the old domain on a traffic dip alone; reversal is a rollback decision under the runbook, taken on evidence of a broken migration (failed redirects, wrong canonicals), not on ranking movement, which the baseline in step 3 exists to interpret.
- **Mixed identity discovered live (old name in an email, a store listing, a payment descriptor):** treat as a step 3 inventory defect: add the surface to the inventory, fix it under its owning step, and re-run step 9's scan for that class. Then record in `progress.md` why the audit missed it, so the next rebrand's inventory scheme inherits the lesson.
- **Cutover breaks production (new domain unreachable, mail rejected, redirect loop on the old domain):** execute `cutover-runbook.md`'s rollback — restore the prior DNS/redirect configuration and the prior build — and record the incident. The old identity stays fully live until a second cutover attempt passes step 9 again; a rebrand is never urgent enough to leave users on a broken domain.
- **Any other gate fails mid-playbook:** fix in the owning department, re-run that gate, record both results in `progress.md`. No gate is waived silently; a waiver needs a `DECISIONS.md` entry, and a waiver on G-LG-094 or the seo-migration skill's gates (G-CO-141–G-CO-145) needs the user's explicit agreement, because those two are the mission.

## Definition of done

- New identity live end-to-end: name, marks, design language, voice, and domain consistent across pages, assets, metadata, emails, notifications, legal documents, listings, and profiles — proven by G-LG-094 and G-QA-141 at zero unapproved hits, with the approved-reuse list naming every legitimate old-name occurrence and its reason.
- Search continuity proven, not assumed: every row of `redirect-map.md` fetched live and matching (status, one hop, target); sitemaps reconciled to the route inventory and submitted; canonicals, structured data, and Open Graph tags naming only the new identity (G-SCH-02/03/04); baseline-vs-after coverage review log open and current (G-CO-005).
- Existing users unharmed and informed: accounts, data, and billing untouched (step 2's does-not-change list verified, not just asserted); announcement sent from the new sending domain with mail authentication passing (G-IN-061, G-DO-103); support briefed with the mapping table.
- Old identity disposed of as decided in step 4: old domain redirecting under the user's ownership, old handles kept or released per the `DECISIONS.md` entry, no old-brand asset still served from the new build.
- All gates above green; `progress.md` shows the full gate trail including the step 3 baseline; `cutover-runbook.md`, `handover.md`, and the inventory are current — the inventory is the next rebrand's starting document.
- **Pre-provisioning complete:** where the new domain or sending domain is not yet provisioned, the project may be recorded as *pre-provisioning complete* when every gate except the provisioning-waived ones (G-DO-071, G-DO-111, G-CO-001, and step 11's live redirect verification) is green, each waived gate is recorded as a provisioning waiver per §6.3, and the unblocking conditions are listed in `progress.md` — the cutover, announcement, and continuity requirements above remain open until the domains exist. A rebrand is not announced on a pre-provisioning build.

## Worked mini-example

Sample project: **Listloop**, a small shared-bookmarking web app (web only, no native apps), renamed to **Shelfmark** with a new domain, because the owner found an unrelated product already trading under a similar name.

1. Step 0: core loaded; project scaffolded; registrar and mail-provider credentials handled under the secrets baseline.
2. Step 1: pre-flight confirms the product is the owner's own; clearance search finds no conflicting mark for "Shelfmark" in its category, the exact-match domain is available, and the social handles are free. Recorded in `fact-sheet.md`. The name proceeds.
3. Step 2: requirements freeze the two lists — changes: name, logo, palette, type, voice, domain, handles; does-not-change: accounts, saved bookmarks, the importer, pricing (there is none — the app is free, and the fact-sheet says so rather than implying a pricing page exists).
4. Step 3: route inventory crawl finds 12 public routes and 3 authenticated routes; the baseline marks the pricing-free landing page, the blog index, and 4 help articles as the organic landing routes. The surface audit finds the old name in: 15 templates, 41 string-catalog entries, 6 email templates, the privacy policy and terms, 1 app-directory listing, the OAuth consent screen, and — the catch of the run — the favicon file and the og image, both still carrying the old wordmark.
5. Step 4: identity spec written (name forms, new wordmark, palette, type, voice notes); `old-new-mapping.md` frozen with 12 route rows and the terminology column (the product's "loops" become "shelves" — a feature rename, recorded as in-scope in `DECISIONS.md` because it changes user-visible vocabulary, not behavior). The rebrand-execution skill's gates pass on both documents.
6. Step 5: tokens replaced, components re-skinned, no layout changes; copy rewritten from the mapping table; naming lint (G-CO-053) catches "loop" surviving in two empty-state strings — fixed at source. Accessibility scan passes on the new palette after one accent color is darkened to meet contrast (G-AC-005, G-EXP-06).
7. Step 6: 6 email templates rebranded and re-authenticated on the new sending domain (G-IN-061, G-IN-064 pass on seed sends); help articles updated and re-screenshotted (G-CX-025); the directory listing and OAuth consent screen are updated, with the listing's provider review noted in the runbook as a dated follow-up.
8. Step 7: terms and privacy regenerated under the new name; G-LG-032 catches the old name in the privacy policy's version-archive header — classified as a legitimate historical occurrence and added to the approved-reuse list with that reason, not edited out of the archive.
9. Step 8: redirect map built — 12 public rows one-to-one, the 3 authenticated routes mapped to their new-domain equivalents, the retired `/pricing` URL (a leftover page with no successor content) answered 410 with the reason on its row. Sitemaps reconciled (G-CO-092); Organization structured data updated to the new name and URL (G-CO-082). The seo-migration skill's gates pass.
10. Step 9: residue scan (G-LG-094) finds one hit — the old name inside a PDF user guide linked from the help center. The guide is regenerated from the rebranded source and the scan re-runs clean over the whole document class. G-SE-001: 0 criticals. G-QA-141 clean.
11. Step 10: user approves the DNS change; new domain live with certificates probed (G-DO-111/112); old domain deployed with the frozen redirect map; G-DO-071 passes on the new domain, and the old domain answers with redirects, not a parked page. Rollback had been rehearsed on staging (G-DO-151).
12. Step 11: live re-fetch of all 15 redirect rows matches the map (G-CO-003); new sitemaps submitted; coverage review log opened against the step 3 baseline (G-CO-005). Announcement email sent from the new domain and the in-product notice published: same account, same bookmarks, new name — and the predictable "is this the same product?" tickets are answered from the mapping table. Done.
