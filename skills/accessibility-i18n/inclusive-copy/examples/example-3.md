# Example 3 — Idiom and ability-assuming copy rewritten plainly

## Before

```
Ready to hit the ground running? Simply click below to see your
report — it's a no-brainer, and you'll be up and running in a flash.
Don't turn a blind eye to your deadlines!
```

Almost every phrase assumes something: the user clicks (not taps, types, or uses voice), sees the screen, and shares the source culture's idioms. Translated literally, "hit the ground running" and "no-brainer" are meaningless or insulting; read by a screen-reader user, "simply click below to see" describes an interaction they may not be performing at all.

## After

```
Your first report is ready. Open it to check this month's totals
and your upcoming deadlines.
[ Open the March report ]
```

The rewrite says the fact, names the action on the button itself, and assumes nothing about how the user perceives or operates the product. It translates word for word into any locale without losing its meaning.

Note: corrects AC-187 and AC-188 — no idiom carries meaning, and copy assumes neither an ability nor an input method.
