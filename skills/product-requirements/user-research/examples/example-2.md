# Example 2 — Separating a finding from its interpretation

## Before

Finding F-11: "Dispatchers hate the reporting screen and need a guided wizard
that walks them through month-end."

Observation and prescription are fused. The "finding" already contains a
feature, so discovery never gets to compare solutions.

## After

Finding F-11 (from S-03): In 7 of 9 sessions, the dispatcher exported the raw
CSV and finished the month-end report in a spreadsheet. Two said the on-screen
totals "never match what finance expects."

Interpretation I-04 (team conclusion, from F-11): Month-end reporting is an
unmet job. Candidate responses (wizard, fixed totals, spreadsheet template)
belong on the opportunity tree in discovery — none is part of the finding.

Note: corrects PR-021 — findings state what was observed; interpretations are written separately and labeled as conclusions.
