# Example 1 — The $45 CAC

## Before

Quarterly growth report:

> "CAC: $45. We spent $9,000 on ads and acquired 200 customers ($9,000 ÷ 200). With LTV at $3,000, our ratio is a world-class 66:1 — scale spend aggressively."

The numerator counts only media. The same quarter's sales and marketing cost also included salaries, commissions, tools, and an agency — none of which appear.

## After

Fully loaded CAC for the same quarter, recomputed under PR-221:

| Cost line (Q3) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Ad spend | $9,000 |
| S&M salaries | $18,000 |
| Commissions | $3,600 |
| Tools (CRM, analytics, automation) | $900 |
| Agency + content production | $4,500 |
| **Total sales + marketing** | **$36,000** |

New customers in the same period: 200.

**CAC = $36,000 ÷ 200 = $180** — four times the reported figure.

Against the margin-based LTV of $3,000 (Example 2), the ratio is $3,000 ÷ $180 ≈ **16.7** — still healthy, but the "scale aggressively" decision now rests on a real number, and payback (PR-223) can be computed honestly: monthly gross profit per customer is $120 × 75% = $90, so payback = $180 ÷ $90 = **2 months**.

## Note

Corrected under PR-221: CAC is fully loaded — salaries, commissions, tools, agencies, and content — never media spend alone.
