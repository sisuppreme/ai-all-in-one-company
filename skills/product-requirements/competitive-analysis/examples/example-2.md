# Example 2 — "Best" with no set

## Before

From the positioning document, later copied to the homepage hero:

> "FlowLedger is the best invoicing tool for freelancers — faster and easier than the competition."

No competitor was trialed for this claim, no criteria were named, and no date exists. The sentence is an unsubstantiated superlative: it cannot be defended, and it teaches every downstream page to repeat it.

## After

From the revised positioning document:

> "Of the three invoicing tools trialed on 2026-11-10 (FlowLedger, InvoiceFox, SoloBooks), FlowLedger was the only one where a first-time user sent a complete invoice — including tax line and payment link — in under five minutes without opening the help docs (task test, 1 user per tool, notes in research/competitive-2026-11/)."

Homepage copy derived from it:

> "Send a complete invoice — tax and payment link included — in under five minutes, no help docs needed."

The claim now names its comparison set, criteria, and date, and the hero line states the verifiable fact instead of the superlative.

## Note

Corrected under PR-168: differentiation claims name the comparison set they were measured against; setless "#1/best" language is cut in the analysis before it reaches copy.
