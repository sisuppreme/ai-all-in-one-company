# market-expansion

## Metadata

- **Owner department:** D1 Product & Requirements (PR)
- **Skill version:** 1.0.0
- **Last verified:** 2026-11-21
- **Sources:** World Bank Global Findex Database (payment-method and account-ownership data by economy) — https://globalfindex.worldbank.org/ ; Unicode CLDR (locale data: currencies, date/number formats, names) — https://cldr.unicode.org/ ; W3C Internationalization — https://www.w3.org/International/
- **Depends on:** competitive-analysis (local competitive set and prices), localization-workflow (locale support tiers and release sign-off), privacy-laws (obligations register for the new market), data-residency (transfer/residency verdicts), pricing-monetization, unit-economics, fact-sheet
- **Feeds into:** roadmapping, requirements-writing, scope-control

## When to use / Do NOT use

- **Use when:** an existing, already-shipping product is being considered for launch into a new country or distinct market, and someone must decide go / conditional-go / no-go. This skill owns that decision: one readiness record per market, six readiness dimensions scored on evidence, pre-registered criteria, a pilot with kill thresholds, and a recorded decision a reviewer can re-run.
- **Do NOT use when:** running the translation process itself (use localization-workflow; this skill consumes its per-locale tier and sign-off, it does not redo them), determining which privacy or residency obligations apply (use privacy-laws and data-residency; this skill consumes their registers as inputs and never re-derives the law), analysing competitors as an end in itself (use competitive-analysis; this skill consumes its matrix), or launching a brand-new product with no existing market (use discovery and roadmapping — there is no home-market baseline to expand from).

## Rules

1. **PR-261 — One readiness record per market.** Each candidate market gets exactly one readiness record (file or tracker entry) that is the single artifact for the decision. It carries: market, target launch window, named decision owner, named decider, the six dimension sections of PR-264, the criteria of PR-263, the pilot plan of PR-274, and the decision of PR-262. A judgement that lives only in a meeting, a chat thread, or a slide deck does not exist as far as this skill is concerned.
2. **PR-262 — The decision is written, dated, and one of three values.** The record closes with exactly one decision — GO, CONDITIONAL GO (conditions listed, each with an owner and a date), or NO-GO (reasons listed, each citing the dimension evidence behind it) — signed by the named decider and dated. Work that spends money, signs contracts, or announces dates before this decision exists is unauthorised expansion work, whatever its intent.
3. **PR-263 — Criteria are fixed before scoring.** The pass thresholds for every dimension are written into the record before any dimension is scored. A threshold changed after its dimension was scored voids that dimension's score; the dimension is re-scored against the new threshold and both versions stay in the record. Criteria invented to fit a score already reached are not criteria.
4. **PR-264 — Six dimensions, scored separately, never averaged.** The record scores each of: (1) localization readiness, (2) payments readiness, (3) legal readiness, (4) pricing readiness, (5) support readiness, (6) cultural fit. Each dimension gets its own verdict — READY, READY WITH CONDITIONS, or BLOCKED — with its evidence. There is no overall percentage and no average: a strong dimension never compensates for a weak one in the arithmetic, only in the decider's written reasoning.
5. **PR-265 — A BLOCKED dimension blocks GO.** If any dimension is BLOCKED at decision time, the only available decisions are CONDITIONAL GO (with the unblocking work as dated conditions, and no public launch until they close) or NO-GO. A GO recorded over a BLOCKED dimension is invalid and is treated as no decision.
6. **PR-266 — Every dimension verdict cites dated evidence.** Each verdict names its evidence: a document, register row, test record, or named person's dated assessment, with the date it was produced. Evidence older than the record's review date (PR-279) is expired and cannot support a verdict. "The team is confident" is not evidence and scores nothing.
7. **PR-267 — Localization verdict is imported, not re-judged.** The localization dimension copies the locale's support tier and release sign-off from localization-workflow (full / UI-only / best-effort, reviewer sign-off per locale) for the market's primary language(s). The dimension is READY only when the tier the launch will claim publicly has actually been signed off there. This skill never upgrades a tier; a gap is recorded as a condition or a blocker, and the fix is routed back to localization-workflow.
8. **PR-268 — Payments are proven by live transactions, not by a provider logo.** The payments dimension is READY only when: the payment methods the record claims are the ones in-market users actually use (sourced — e.g. provider documentation for the market, Global Findex data, or in-market user research — never assumed from the home market); at least one real end-to-end transaction per claimed method has been executed in production or the provider's live environment, including a successful charge, a failure path, and a refund; and prices, tax, and receipts display in the market's currency and format. A method integrated in test mode only is recorded as not ready.
9. **PR-269 — Legal verdict is imported from the registers.** The legal dimension copies its inputs from privacy-laws (the obligations register rows for this market) and data-residency (transfer/residency verdicts for the infrastructure that will serve this market), plus three market-entry facts recorded with sources and dates: whether a local entity, registration, or licence is required to sell here and its status; the tax-registration position (e.g. VAT/GST or local equivalent) and its status; and any sector-specific licence or content rule that applies to this product. This skill records the status of each; it does not interpret the law. Any item whose status is "required, not obtained" makes the dimension BLOCKED. Where privacy-laws marks an escalation stop, qualified local counsel's written input is attached before the dimension can be scored at all.
10. **PR-270 — The local price is set deliberately, never converted.** The price charged in the new market is a decision recorded in the record and handed to the fact sheet: it starts from in-market evidence (competitor prices from competitive-analysis captured from live local pricing pages, willingness-to-pay evidence from in-market research) and is checked against the market-level unit economics of PR-275. A price produced by converting the home-market price at a spot exchange rate, with no local evidence step, does not satisfy this rule — see BAN-PR-132.
11. **PR-271 — Support exists before the first local user does.** The support dimension is READY only when the record names: who answers users in this market (person, team, or vendor), in which language(s), during which hours stated in the market's timezone, with what first-response target, and through which channels. Help content for the primary flows exists at the locale tier claimed under PR-267. "The home team will pick it up" with no named coverage for the market's waking hours is BLOCKED, not a condition.
12. **PR-272 — Cultural fit is reviewed by a named in-market reviewer.** A named person who lives in or is from the target market — not a home-market team member who visited — walks the primary flows and the launch copy and records every adaptation the product needs: names and imagery, forms of address, date/time/calendar conventions, units, address and phone formats, locally expected workflows (e.g. invoicing, ID, or delivery norms the product touches), and anything that would read as offensive, implausible, or foreign in a way that breaks trust. Each recorded adaptation is marked done, scheduled as a dated condition, or waived in writing by the decider with a reason. No reviewer, no dimension score.
13. **PR-273 — Demand evidence comes from the market, not from extrapolation.** The record's demand section cites evidence gathered from people in the target market — interviews, a waitlist or landing test run in-market, a pilot cohort, distributor/partner commitments in writing — each dated and counted. Home-market growth rates, home-market conversion, and total-addressable-market figures for the country are context, never demand evidence, and are labeled as context wherever they appear.
14. **PR-274 — Every GO is a pilot first.** A GO decision launches to a limited cohort or region within the market, for a stated period, with success thresholds and kill thresholds written into the record before the pilot starts (drawn from product-metrics: e.g. activation, first-transaction completion, support contacts per user, refund rate — the specific metrics are the project's, the requirement to pre-register them is this rule's). Full launch requires a post-pilot review (PR-278) against those thresholds. A pilot with no kill threshold is a launch with extra steps and does not satisfy this rule.
15. **PR-275 — Unit economics are re-derived for the market.** The record carries a market-level unit-economics check (per unit-economics) built from local inputs: local price (PR-270), the actual payment-processing cost of the methods claimed under PR-268, local support cost from the PR-271 model, local acquisition channel and cost evidence, and applicable taxes/fees. Home-market margins pasted into the new market's row are a scoring error: any input not sourced locally is flagged as an assumption, and a dimension resting on flagged assumptions cannot be READY.
16. **PR-276 — Operational prerequisites are listed and closed.** The record lists the hard prerequisites for operating in the market — app-store or distribution accounts, business/banking/tax registrations from PR-269, domain and trademark position where the product relies on them, data infrastructure region per data-residency — each with status and owner. An open prerequisite that would prevent selling, charging, or operating is a BLOCKED verdict on its dimension, not a footnote.
17. **PR-277 — The exit plan is written before entry.** The record states how the launch is paused or withdrawn: how users are told, in their language; what happens to subscriptions, balances, and prepaid amounts; what data obligations survive exit (from the privacy-laws register); and who owns the decision to exit. A market the team cannot describe leaving is not ready to enter.
18. **PR-278 — The post-pilot review is a second decision.** At the pilot's stated end date the decider records a second decision — EXPAND to full launch, HOLD (extend pilot with new dated thresholds), or EXIT (per PR-277) — citing the pilot's actual metric values against the PR-274 thresholds. Silence at the review date is a HOLD by default, never an automatic expand.
19. **PR-279 — The record expires.** The record carries a review date, set when the decision is made. After that date the record is stale: its verdicts cannot support a new launch, a new claim, or a new market's decision until the affected dimensions are re-evidenced. One market's record is never reused as another market's evidence; a shared input (e.g. the same language) is re-cited per market, not inherited.
20. **PR-280 — Public claims match the record.** Any public statement about the launch — "now available in X", "fully localized", "supports <payment method>", local pricing pages, launch dates — is checked against the record before publication: availability matches the pilot/full status, the localization claim matches the PR-267 tier, payment claims match PR-268's live-tested methods, and prices match the fact sheet. A claim the record cannot support is cut or corrected before it ships, not after users find it.

## BAN list

- **BAN-PR-131 — Invented market numbers.** Banned: stating a market size, demand figure, user count, or growth rate for the target market in the record, a pitch, or launch copy without a checkable source and date. Defeats: DET-CON-011. Bad: the readiness record opens with "a market of 40 million users growing at 25% a year" — a number the author generated because the section needed one; the GO decision and the hiring plan both rest on it. Instead: cite the source and its nature ("per <source>, <date>") and label the figure context under PR-273, or leave the field empty and record demand as unknown. Severity: BLOCK.
- **BAN-PR-132 — FX-converted pricing.** Banned: setting the new market's price by converting the home-market price at a spot exchange rate and presenting the result as the local price. Defeats: DET-CON-016. Bad: the home price of $20 becomes "€18.40" on the local pricing page because that was the conversion on the day the page was built; local competitors charge €12, the page drifts further from any real rate within weeks, and the pricing page, the checkout, and the fact sheet end up showing three different numbers. Instead: set the price from in-market evidence and market unit economics (PR-270, PR-275) and hand the single resulting price to the fact sheet. Severity: BLOCK.
- **BAN-PR-133 — Announcement before backing process.** Banned: publicly announcing availability, a launch date, or "coming soon" sign-ups for a market before the decision exists (PR-262) and the support and payments dimensions are at least READY WITH CONDITIONS. Defeats: DET-CON-028. Bad: a "Now live in Brazil!" post ships on the marketing calendar while support has no Portuguese coverage and the one claimed payment method has never completed a live transaction; the first users meet a promise with no process behind it. Instead: announce only what the record supports, when it supports it (PR-280); a waitlist is announced as a waitlist. Severity: BLOCK.
- **BAN-PR-134 — The verbal go.** Banned: treating a meeting outcome, a chat message, or an executive's remark as the expansion decision, with no written record, criteria, or named decider. Defeats: DET-NONE — no DET parameter names this signal; governed by PR-261, PR-262, PR-263. Bad: "leadership agreed in the sync to enter Poland" — three months later nobody can say who decided, against which criteria, or whether payments were ever checked, but contracts are signed. Instead: no decision exists until the record carries the dated, signed GO / CONDITIONAL GO / NO-GO of PR-262 against the pre-registered criteria of PR-263. Severity: BLOCK.
- **BAN-PR-135 — Test-mode payments presented as live.** Banned: recording or claiming a payment method as supported in the new market when it has only ever run in a sandbox/test environment, or when its refund path has never been executed. Defeats: DET-FAKE-006. Bad: the payments dimension is marked READY because the checkout "works" in the provider's test mode with test credentials; at launch, real charges fail on a market-specific verification step nobody had ever triggered, and the dimension's evidence was a demo. Instead: readiness requires a real charge, a real failure path, and a real refund in the live environment, recorded with dates (PR-268). Severity: BLOCK.
- **BAN-PR-136 — Readiness copy that contradicts the record.** Banned: launch copy, help pages, or sales answers that describe the market's availability, localization, or payment support at a level above what the readiness record and fact sheet state. Defeats: DET-CON-014. Bad: the record tiers the locale as UI-only with English support hours, but the launch page says "fully localized with local support" — the page contradicts the project's own record, and the first support tickets arrive in a language nobody staffed for. Instead: every public claim is checked against the record before publication (PR-280); where copy and record disagree, the record wins and the copy is corrected. Severity: BLOCK.

## Gates

- **G-PR-131 — Record completeness scan (automated).** Verifies: PR-261, PR-264, PR-274, PR-277, PR-279. Check: count each term separately — `for t in localization payments legal pricing support "cultural fit"; do grep -ci "$t" record.md; done` must return at least one hit for every one of the six dimensions, and the same per-term loop over `pilot "kill threshold" exit "review date" decision` must return at least one hit each for the pilot plan, kill threshold, exit plan, review date, and decision fields. A single combined `grep -c` count cannot prove per-term presence and does not satisfy this gate. Pass: all six dimension sections and all five fields present. Fail: block shipment — an incomplete record cannot be decided. Recorded in: progress.md.
- **G-PR-132 — Blocker scan (automated).** Verifies: PR-265. Check: `grep -in "BLOCKED" record.md` — cross-check every hit against the recorded decision. Pass: either zero dimensions are BLOCKED, or the decision is CONDITIONAL GO / NO-GO and each BLOCKED dimension appears as a dated condition or a cited no-go reason. Fail: block shipment — a GO recorded over a BLOCKED dimension is invalid (PR-265) and the decision is voided back to undecided. Recorded in: progress.md.
- **G-PR-133 — Criteria-before-scores audit (manual).** Verifies: PR-263. Check: the reviewer compares the record's version history (or dated drafts) and confirms the criteria section's date precedes the earliest dimension score's date; any threshold edited after scoring must show the dimension re-scored with both versions retained. Pass: criteria demonstrably predate scores, or every post-hoc change has its re-score on file. Fail: block shipment; affected dimensions return to unscored and are re-scored against the standing criteria. Recorded in: progress.md.
- **G-PR-134 — Payments live-fire evidence check (manual).** Verifies: PR-268. Check: for each payment method the record claims, inspect the attached live-environment evidence: one successful real charge, one failure-path record, and one completed refund, each dated and attributable to the production/live environment (provider dashboard export, transaction IDs), plus a screenshot or test record of the price/tax/receipt display in local currency and format. Pass: every claimed method has all three transaction records and the display check. Fail: the method is struck from the claimed set and the payments dimension is re-scored before any GO. Recorded in: progress.md.
- **G-PR-135 — Claims-versus-record check (automated + manual).** Verifies: PR-280, PR-267. Check: `grep -rinE "now available|fully localized|now live|supports .*payment" launch-copy/` lists every public availability/localization/payment claim; the reviewer checks each against the record's decision status, the PR-267 tier, and the PR-268 live-tested method set. Pass: every claim matches the record exactly; the claimed localization level equals the signed-off tier. Fail: block publication of the offending copy until it is corrected to the record. Recorded in: progress.md.

## Anti-patterns

- **The averaged scorecard.** Six dimension verdicts are collapsed into "82% ready" and the launch proceeds on the average while legal sits BLOCKED underneath it. Catch it in your own draft: if the document contains a readiness percentage, it has already violated PR-264 — delete the number and restore the per-dimension verdicts.
- **Home-market mirror.** The record's demand, payment, and support sections describe the home market with the country's name swapped: same payment methods assumed, same support hours, same price converted. Catch it by checking every input for a local source and date (PR-266, PR-275); an input whose source is the home market is an assumption, and assumptions cannot make a dimension READY.
- **Launch-date gravity.** A date was announced or a conference booked, and dimension verdicts quietly soften as the date approaches — conditions become "we'll fix it in week one" without owners or dates. Catch it by diffing the record against its earlier version: any verdict that improved without new dated evidence attached is gravity, not progress.
- **Pilot that cannot die.** The pilot launches with success metrics but no kill threshold, no end date, and no scheduled review, so it drifts into being the full launch by inertia. Catch it at record review: if PR-274's kill threshold and PR-278's review date are missing, the pilot section is unfinished regardless of how detailed the rest is.
- **Flag-planting sequencing.** Several markets are "launched" in one quarter by reusing the first market's record with names changed, so each new flag inherits evidence that was never gathered for it. Catch it per PR-279: open each market's record and confirm its evidence was produced for that market; inherited verdicts are unscored dimensions.

## Recall anchors

- One market, one record, one written decision — a verbal go is no go.
- Six dimensions, six verdicts, no average: a BLOCKED dimension blocks.
- Criteria before scores, or the scores are fiction.
- Payments are ready when a real charge and a real refund have happened, not when the logo is integrated.
- The local price is a decision with local evidence, never an exchange-rate conversion.

## Checklist

- [ ] Readiness record exists for this market with named owner and decider (PR-261).
- [ ] Criteria for all six dimensions were written before any scoring (PR-263, G-PR-133).
- [ ] All six dimensions scored separately with dated evidence; no averaged score anywhere (PR-264, PR-266, G-PR-131).
- [ ] Localization verdict copied from localization-workflow's tier and sign-off, not re-judged (PR-267).
- [ ] Every claimed payment method has a live charge, failure path, and refund on record (PR-268, G-PR-134).
- [ ] Legal dimension imported from privacy-laws / data-residency registers; entity, tax, and licence statuses recorded; no "required, not obtained" item scored above BLOCKED (PR-269).
- [ ] Local price set from in-market evidence and market unit economics, and handed to the fact sheet (PR-270, PR-275).
- [ ] Support named by person/team, language, hours in the market's timezone, and response target (PR-271).
- [ ] Named in-market reviewer walked the flows; every adaptation is done, dated, or waived in writing (PR-272).
- [ ] Demand section cites dated in-market evidence; market-size figures labeled context only (PR-273).
- [ ] Pilot defined with pre-registered success and kill thresholds and a review date (PR-274, PR-278).
- [ ] Exit plan written: user comms, money, surviving data obligations, exit owner (PR-277).
- [ ] Decision recorded as GO / CONDITIONAL GO / NO-GO, dated and signed; G-PR-132 blocker scan passes (PR-262, PR-265).
- [ ] Record carries a review/expiry date and is not reused as another market's evidence (PR-279).
- [ ] All public launch claims checked against the record before publication (PR-280, G-PR-135).
