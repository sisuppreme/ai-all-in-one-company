# Example 3 — Closing the loop for real

## Before

```text
Release email: "You asked, we shipped! Dark mode is here —
thanks to your feedback. Our users rate us 4.8 out of 5!"
  Reality: the shipped change renames a settings toggle;
  no dark mode exists. The "4.8" is a bare average with no
  count, window, or segment, from a survey retired in March.
  Users who actually requested dark mode: not notified,
  because their requests were never linked to any backlog item.
```

The announcement claims a loop that never closed: the change did not ship, the score is unsourced, and the real requesters heard nothing.

## After

```text
Backlog item #312 "Add dark mode" links 214 originating
responses from the feature-request path and day-30 NPS
comments. Dark mode ships in the release.
  Notification sent to the 214 requesters: "You asked for
  dark mode — it shipped today. Here's how to turn it on."
  Release notes cite the request count and link the item.
  Score in the same email: none — the retired survey's
  figures are not quoted (CX-131).

G-CX-063 audit: sampled announcement traced to backlog
item #312, its linked responses, the shipped feature, and
the notification sent to the requesting users.
```

## Note

Corrected under CX-128, CX-129, and CX-130 (BAN-CX-065): shipped changes trace to their originating responses, the users who asked are the ones told, and no score is published without its count, window, and segment.
