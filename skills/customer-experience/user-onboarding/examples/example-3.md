# Example 3 — Removing a forced invite gate

## Before

Step three of the four-step setup wizard is "Invite your team": two teammate
email addresses are required, the Continue button stays disabled until both
fields validate, and there is no skip link.

Solo users — the majority of signups — abandon here or type invented
addresses to get past it. The product fills with undeliverable invitations,
and per-step metrics show the wizard's biggest drop-off at a step that has
nothing to do with the product's value.

## After

The gate is removed and the invite is repositioned:

1. The wizard drops to three steps, all aimed at the core action; the user
   reaches their first real result with no other person's involvement.
2. Immediately after that first result exists, an optional card offers
   "Invite a teammate to see this" — dismissible, and never shown again once
   dismissed.
3. Per-step drop-off is re-measured after the change; the wizard's worst
   step is now the core-action step itself, which is where onboarding effort
   belongs.

Invites still happen — sent by users who have something worth sharing,
instead of hostages paying a toll.

Note: corrects CX-071 — reaching the core action never requires inviting teammates or completing unrelated requirements.
