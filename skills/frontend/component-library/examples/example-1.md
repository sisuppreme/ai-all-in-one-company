# Example 1 — Interchangeable feature cards become weighted, specific cards

## Before

Three cards, identical height, identical icon chips, two-line descriptions:

| Card | Heading | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Powerful routing | Powerful tools that help modern teams plan better and work smarter every day. |
| 2 | Built for teams | Everything you need to collaborate seamlessly and get more done together. |
| 3 | Total visibility | Gain complete visibility into your operations with insights that drive results. |

Swap any description onto any card — or onto a competitor's product — and
nothing breaks. That interchangeability is the tell (DET-VIS-003), and the
tinted-circle icons repeat the formula (DET-VIS-026).

## After

The same features, weighted by importance and written against the
product's facts:

| Card | Span | Heading | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (featured) | 2 columns, with a live route-map visual | Routes that replan themselves | When a driver calls in sick, the day's 214 stops are re-sequenced across the remaining vans in under a minute — no dispatcher touches a spreadsheet. |
| 2 | 1 column | Proof of delivery | Photo, signature, and GPS stamp on every drop, searchable by address. |
| 3 | 1 column | Customer ETAs | Customers get a live 30-minute arrival window by text, not a day-long guess. |

The featured card carries the product's core claim with a real number
from its own operation; the smaller cards state one concrete capability
each. No description survives being moved to another card.

Note: corrects FE-069 — cards are weighted by importance and their copy is specific to this product, never interchangeable.
