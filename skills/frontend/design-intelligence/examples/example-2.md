# Example 2 — Kiln & Co: gallery default replaced by a theme applied whole

Corrects rules **FE-327** (signature elements must carry content) and **FE-331** (adapt the theme, never abandon it); the full reasoning record is §7, Example B of the skill.

## Before

A pottery studio with a repair service and a shop is built as a minimal white gallery: the same template every competitor uses, product photographs in an even grid, no story, no motion system. The work photographs beautifully — and so does every other studio's. The one thing that makes this business different, the maker's hands and the mending service, appears nowhere in the experience. The page is competent and completely interchangeable (§4, symptom 10: new colours, same template smell).

## After

Theme #41 Kintsugi is taken from the theme-library as-is — its material language (warm palette, serif display + humanist body, vessel vocabulary) stays intact on every page (FE-331). Narrative chapters carry the studio story at dial 4; the shop bends to a catalogue grid at dial 2 and checkout to dial 1 (§6, commerce row). The signature element is the story itself: the hero vessel cracks and mends with gold as the visitor scrolls the studio's own history, so the animation *demonstrates the paid repair service* instead of decorating beside it; shop cards reuse the same vessel language at hover scale, and story and shop read as one world. Rejected, with reasons recorded: the minimal gallery (erases the craft), and a WebGL clay world at dial 5 (unproven performance on shoppers' phones, and it would fake the product instead of photographing it — FE-030 in design-system).
