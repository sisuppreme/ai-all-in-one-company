# Theme 88 — Subglacial Dispatch

*A dispatch from beneath two kilometres of Antarctic ice. The page is a field report coming up a long wire, a paragraph at a time — terse, cold, and gripping, because the place is.*

---

## 1. Concept & world

Somewhere under the Aurora Basin ice sheet, under 2,147 metres of ice that fell as snow before there were cities, there is a lake that has not seen sunlight in at least fifteen million years. A team of nine people is drilling toward it. This theme is their dispatch home: not a brochure about the expedition, but the expedition's own log, typeset. The reader is not a visitor. The reader is the other end of the wire — the person at the surface station, or at home, who receives each entry hours after it was written, reads it, and stamps it RECEIVED.

Everything in the theme is governed by that fiction's physics. Text arrives slowly because the wire is long, and the deeper the drill goes, the longer the wire gets — so the page's signal delay literally grows from 2.0 s at the surface to 6.0 s at the lake. Progress is not a percentage; it is cable paid out down a margin, stamped every 100 m. Cold is not a colour palette; it is a falling instrument reading (−58.0 °C at the surface, −62.1 °C at the lake roof) that the prose keeps agreeing with. Warmth exists in exactly one colour, tent orange `#E86A17`, and it is rationed by law: **orange appears only where a human hand has been** — a tent, a flag a person planted, a glove in a photograph, the RECEIVED stamp a reader presses. Anywhere orange appears without a human cause, the theme has failed.

**The theme's one law: *the log does not embellish, and nothing arrives faster than the wire.*** Every figure on the page — depth, temperature, day count, delay, lake dimensions — traces to one declared drill record (§6.17), printed on the page itself, and any element that needs a number reads it from that record. A dispatch that invents a reading is a broken dispatch. And nothing on the page may appear instantly that the fiction says travelled: dispatches fade up the wire over seconds, stamps land with weight, the lake is drawn by its own sonar at walking pace. Slowness here is not a loading problem. It is the subject.

**The world in five objects:** the drill string (a cable, a margin, a debt of depth), the tent (the only warm thing), the wire (every word arrives by it), the stamp (proof a human received this), and the lake (the thing the whole page is falling toward, drawn once, at the end, by sonar).

**Best fits:** polar and extreme-environment research programmes publishing real field logs; expedition and documentary series releasing episodes as written dispatches; deep-sea, space-analogue, cave and volcano field science with the same structure (a remote team, a long wire, a log); museums mounting a "messages from the field" exhibition site; a book or film site structured as the log itself.

**When NOT to use it:** anything that must convert quickly — the theme's whole grammar is delay, and a pricing page that fades in over 6 s is not atmospheric, it is broken (checkout is routed out of the theme entirely, §7). Anything cheerful, tropical, culinary, or fashion-led: the cold is total and the palette refuses decoration. Anything whose content is not genuinely a log — if there is no dated sequence of entries written by people in a place, the stamps and cable become costume. And never for a team that will not write the mundane entries: this theme's credibility is a broken generator and a bowl of soup (§9); a log of pure triumph reads as advertising within two entries.

**The mundane clause (from the original entry, promoted to law):** dispatches include the mundane alongside the profound — a broken generator, a birthday, soup — because real field logs do, and the mundane entries are what make the breakthrough entry land. A build of this theme must schedule at least one mundane dispatch per three profound ones, and the worked examples in §8 obey it.

**Usage ratios (per viewport, audited in §10):** ice white ≈ 78% of pixels; generator black (as ink) ≈ 12%; crevasse blue ≈ 7%; pencil grey ≈ 2.5%; **tent orange ≤ 0.5%** — the orange census is a gate, not a guideline. If a screenshot's orange exceeds half a percent outside a photograph's own content, warmth has leaked.

**Pairing rules:** body text is generator black on ice, always (14.1:1). Links are crevasse blue, underlined by the sastrugi rule (§4), 4.7:1 — licensed for text at ≥1 rem. Tent orange never carries a sentence on ice (2.8:1); on the black finale chapter it may set the mono depth readout (5.0:1). Pencil grey never carries unique information — anything written in pencil also exists in ink somewhere on the page (the log's rule: pencil is a copy, ink is the record).

**Dark-mode behaviour:** there is no inverted colour scheme and no `prefers-color-scheme` response — a field log does not change its paper at night. Instead, the theme has one authored dark chapter: the finale (§6.8's lake section and everything after the 2,100 m stamp) prints on `--night`, because by then the reader is under the ice with the drill. The transition is geographic, not chromatic — the page darkens where the story darkens, at a declared scroll position, and lightens never. OS dark mode leaves the theme untouched.

## 3. Typography

Three faces. **Archivo** — display, with the expedition-stencil feel the original specifies: squared apertures, no-nonsense grotesque, set in capitals with wide tracking for station names and dispatch headers. **Newsreader** — the dispatch prose: an old-style serif with a true italic, because the log is written by a person at the end of a twelve-hour shift, not typeset by a brand. **IBM Plex Mono** — the dispatch mono the original entry's stamps and counters call for: depth stamps, instrument readouts, wire delays, receipt marks. Mono is the voice of the instruments; it never writes a sentence of prose.

**Type scale (root 16 px):**

| Role | Face / weight | Size | Line-height | Tracking | Case |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Station name (masthead) | Archivo 800 | clamp(1.75 rem, 4vw, 2.75 rem) | 1.0 | +0.14 em | Caps |
| Dispatch headline | Archivo 700 | clamp(1.5 rem, 3vw, 2.25 rem) | 1.08 | +0.06 em | Caps |
| Section marker (depth band) | Archivo 600 | 0.875 rem | 1.2 | +0.22 em | Caps |
| Dispatch body | Newsreader 400 | 1.1875 rem | 1.78 | 0 | Sentence |
| Dispatch aside / quotation | Newsreader italic 400 | 1.1875 rem | 1.78 | 0 | Sentence |
| Dateline | IBM Plex Mono 500 | 0.8125 rem | 1.4 | +0.08 em | Caps |
| Depth stamp | IBM Plex Mono 700 | 0.6875 rem | 1.0 | +0.1 em | Caps |
| Instrument readout | IBM Plex Mono 500 | 0.9375 rem | 1.3 | +0.04 em | Caps |
| Caption / pencil note | Newsreader italic 400 | 0.9375 rem | 1.55 | 0 | Sentence |
| Button / form label | Archivo 600 | 0.8125 rem | 1.2 | +0.12 em | Caps |

**Five type laws:**

1. **Archivo never writes a paragraph.** Display face is for names, headers and markers only; the moment Archivo sets a second sentence it has become a poster, and this is a log.
2. **The dateline precedes the headline, always.** Every dispatch opens with its mono dateline (`DAY 66 · 1,180 M · −60.1 °C`) before its Archivo headline — the instruments speak first, the human second, in every entry without exception.
3. **Numerals are mono, everywhere.** A number set in Archivo or Newsreader is a defect (§9): depths, temperatures, days, delays, lake dimensions — if it can be measured, it is set in IBM Plex Mono.
4. **Newsreader italic is the team's voice off-duty.** Italics are reserved for quotation, asides and the mundane (the soup review is italic; the depth report is not). Italic never appears in a headline.
5. **Nothing is ever set in tent orange below 1 rem, and no prose is ever orange** (§2). Orange type exists in exactly two places: the RECEIVED stamp and the masthead's live depth readout on the black finale chapter.

**Measure and rhythm:** dispatch prose sets at 36–40 rem measure (§4); the 1.78 line-height is the theme's breathing — cold air, long lines of type, nothing crowded. Vertical rhythm is a 0.5 rem grid; dispatch-to-dispatch separation is 6 rem of open ice with a sastrugi rule, because entries are separated by days, not by cards.

## 4. Layout language

The page is a white, wind-scoured sheet. Its geography is fixed: a **margin rail** on the left carries the drill cable and its stamps; a **single prose column** carries the dispatches; the **right margin** carries pencil annotation — the surface station's marginalia, the flags, the weather. Nothing else exists. There are no cards in a grid, no hero split, no sidebar widgets: a log has a margin and a column, and this page is a log.

**Grid:** at ≥1200 px, the page is `rail 7.5 rem | gutter 3 rem | column minmax(36 rem, 40 rem) | right margin flexible to 16 rem`, the whole measure starting 8 vw from the left edge — deliberately off-centre, because the cable needs its ice. The column never widens past 40 rem at any viewport; extra width goes to the right margin (more weather, more annotation room), never to longer lines.

**Sastrugi texture:** the ground is not flat white. Faint horizontal wind-lines — 1 px `--ice-deep` rules at irregular 7–13 px intervals, generated once as a repeating SVG background at 3% opacity — cross the whole page, denser near the top (the surface, where wind works hardest) and fading to nothing below the 1,500 m stamp, where the ice is older than weather. Section breaks are a single sastrugi rule drawn heavier, with the depth band marker set into it in Archivo.

**Frost at the edges:** the viewport's corners carry a fixed frost vignette (radial `--ice-frost` masks, pointer-events none) — the reader is looking at this place through a lens, and the lens is cold. Frost never overlaps the prose column's text at readable contrast: it lives in the margins and over photographs (§6.9), thinning as images centre.

**Density rules:** one dispatch per screen, roughly — an entry plus its dateline, headline, prose, and any photograph should occupy 90–140 vh, because entries are days and days are long. Whitespace is not emptiness here; it is the ice between events, and compressing it makes the log read as marketing. The right margin stays mostly empty — a flag every few screens, a pencil note per dispatch at most — so that when something does appear at the margin, it reads as an event.

**The descent structure:** the page is organised as a single descent. Masthead (surface, Day 1 of the reader's visit) → dispatch sequence in day order, depth increasing → the pinned depth stamp travelling the rail (§6.11) → the finale chapter on `--night` from the 2,100 m stamp down → the lake (§6.8) → the log closes (§6.17 footer-as-record). There is no "back to top" — the wire only goes down; the footer offers the surface link as a text link in the colophon, styled as a haul-up (§11), because returning is work in this world too.

## 5. Full motion system

Five named easings, used nowhere else and named in code exactly as here:

| Easing | Curve | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `drift` | `cubic-bezier(0.22, 0.61, 0.36, 1)` | Snow settling, frost thinning, photographs clearing — things the wind does |
| `haul` | `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` | The cable and stamp landings — weight paid out and caught |
| `wire` | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.6, 1)` | Signal arrivals — long, even, unhurried fades over seconds |
| `stamp` | `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` | The RECEIVED stamp and stamp-rail ticks — a press with a small overshoot, capped at 1.04 scale |
| `gust` | `cubic-bezier(0.45, 0, 0.55, 1)` | Gusts and flag flutter — wind breathing in and out |

**The shared loop:** every scroll-scrubbed value on the page — cable length, depth readout, temperature, stamp positions, sonar draw — is driven by **one** `requestAnimationFrame` loop reading a single lerped scroll value (`current += (target − current) × 0.10`). The 0.10 factor is deliberately heavy: scrolling this page feels like hauling a sledge across sastrugi, a slight lag and settle behind the reader's hand, specified in the original entry and kept as law. No element runs its own scroll listener or its own lerp; a second loop is a defect (§10).

**Motion terms, each in this theme's own language:**

- **Scroll-triggered reveals** are *arrivals*: a dispatch does not "animate in", it comes up the wire — opacity 0→1 over its computed delay (§6.3) with a wire-static speckle resolving in the first 30% of the fade. Trigger: dispatch's dateline crosses 78% of viewport height.
- **Scroll-driven / scrubbed animation** is the descent itself: cable length, depth, temperature, and the lake's sonar line are all functions of the one lerped scroll value, reversible on scroll-up (the cable hauls back in; the lake, once drawn, stays drawn — §6.8's ratchet, because sonar is a record).
- **Parallax** is rationed to two layers: snow (two depths, §6.4) and frost over photographs (§6.9). Prose never parallaxes — the log does not drift behind its reader.
- **Pinned sections:** the current-depth stamp pins in the rail for the whole descent (§6.11); the lake chapter pins the sonar drawing for its draw duration (§6.8). Nothing else pins — pinning is for instruments.
- **Masked reveals:** the headlamp's frost-clearing is a radial mask (§6.6); the white-out is a density mask (§6.1). Text is never mask-revealed letter by letter — the wire delivers paragraphs, not letters.
- **Kinetic typography:** refused, with one exception — the masthead temperature readout's digits change as instruments do, in mono, without flourish. Headlines do not move. A log's headlines sit still and are believed.
- **Marquee:** refused. Nothing at the pole scrolls sideways forever; the one horizontal motion is wind, and wind gusts (§6.4), it does not loop a message.
- **Counters:** depth, temperature and days-on-ice count in mono from the record (§6.5) — depth scrubs with scroll (it *is* scroll), temperature and days step at dispatch boundaries, never free-running.
- **Progress indicator:** the cable itself (§6.2) plus the pinned stamp (§6.11). No progress bar exists; a bar would be a lie about the shape of the journey, which is vertical.
- **SVG line-draw:** the cable (a line that pays out) and the lake outline (a sonar trace) — the page's only two drawn lines, both instruments drawing themselves.
- **Image scale-in-frame:** photographs clear frost and settle from scale 1.06→1 inside their frames (§6.9) — a lens clearing, not a zoom effect.
- **Micro-interactions:** hover is a headlamp (§6.6); click is a stamp (§6.7) or a switch with a generator's thunk (§11 buttons). Magnetic and tilt effects are refused — nothing at −60 °C is playful, and gloves do not do hover physics.
- **Page transitions:** the white-out (§6.16) — leaving a page is going back out into the weather.
- **Ambient loops:** exactly two are licensed — snowfall in the hero (§6.1/§6.4) and flag flutter (§6.4). Both pause off-screen and both die under reduced motion. A third ambient loop is a gate failure.
- **Reduced motion:** clear skies, full text, no delay (§6.3's variant is the whole page's posture): every dispatch printed in full on arrival, cable drawn complete, lake printed as a finished trace. Specified per element in §6 and as a page posture in §10.

**Master timing table (the numbers every element obeys):** white-out resolve 3000 ms; dispatch fade = `delay(d)` = 2.0 + (d ÷ 2147) × 4.0 s (§6.3); stamp land 420 ms `stamp`; cable stamp tick 260 ms `haul`; headlamp frost clear 600 ms `drift`; photograph frost clear 900 ms `drift`; lake sonar draw 6000 ms scrubbed; white-out page transition 1100 ms; watchdog ceilings: arrivals 8000 ms, loader 3000 ms, transition 1400 ms (§10 drills them).

## 6. Signature elements

Eighteen elements: the nine originals (§6.1–§6.9), deepened, and nine page-family elements (§6.10–§6.18) that carry the theme across a whole site. Every element carries the full v1 field set and every v2 layer. Shared numbers — final depth 2,147 m, delay function, temperature function, day range — are declared once in the drill record (§6.17) and referenced, never re-invented.

### 6.1 The white-out entrance

- **What & why:** The hero opens in blowing snow at near-illegible density; over ~3 s the wind eases, the snow thins, and the headline resolves like a tent emerging from a storm. Arrival at this page is weather, exactly as arrival at the site is weather — the reader's first experience of the theme is the place refusing, then permitting, sight. It is the theme's threshold ritual and its only full-screen effect.
- **Structure:** A `<canvas>` fixed over the hero only (not the page), ~120 snow particles in two depth layers, above a hero whose headline and first dateline are fully rendered in the DOM beneath at full contrast. The white-out is a density layer, never a content gate: text contrast is reduced by the particle field and a `--ice-frost` wash, not by setting text opacity.
- **Motion spec:** On load, particle density starts at 1.0 (storm) and eases to 0.25 (hero ambient) over 3000 ms on `drift`; wind gust factor oscillates ±30% on `gust` throughout. Headline legibility resolves as density crosses 0.55 (~1400 ms in). The first dispatch (§6.3) begins its wire arrival only after the white-out completes — the storm clears, then the wire speaks.
- **Choreography timeline:** 0 ms — storm at full density, masthead outline barely readable. 0–1200 ms — gusts, no thinning (the storm is not a loading bar; it does not reward waiting immediately). 1200–2400 ms — density 1.0→0.5, station name resolves, then the headline. 2400–3000 ms — density →0.25 ambient, dateline sharp, first flag flutter starts. 3000 ms — white-out hands over to ambient snowfall (§6.4); canvas begins its off-screen pause checks.
- **State matrix:** Rest (post-storm): ambient density 0.25, hero fully legible. Loading-slow: if assets delay, the storm persists but text beneath is already readable at density ≤0.7 from 800 ms — the wash caps so content is never hostage. Scrolled-past: canvas pauses (IntersectionObserver), zero cost. Revisit (session): storm shortened to 1200 ms — the reader has been here; the weather is remembered, not repeated. Disabled (JS off): no canvas, no wash — hero prints plain. Touch: identical; no interaction is required or offered.
- **Content-merge:** The storm is over the hero's real content — station name, expedition headline, first dateline — not over a splash screen. There is no separate intro page; the white-out happens *to* the page.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — full 120 particles, two layers. 768–1199 px — 80 particles, two layers, wash reduced 20% (smaller screens, less legibility to spare). <768 px — 44 particles, one layer, storm duration 2200 ms; the masthead type scale (§3) already assumes a phone in a glove.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Canvas unsupported → CSS-only wash that fades on the same 3000 ms `drift` via a JS-armed class, with the no-JS state fully clear. Slow network → storm never exceeds the 0.7 density cap after 800 ms (above). Tab hidden mid-storm → timeline is clock-based, not frame-counted; on return, density is wherever the clock says. User scrolls during the storm → storm continues only in the hero's box; nothing follows the reader down.
- **Failure fallback:** A 4000 ms watchdog forces density to ambient and removes the wash regardless of animation state; with JS disabled the element does not exist and the hero is simply printed. No content is ever *inside* the canvas.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** No storm. The hero prints in clear air with a static, faint frost vignette at the corners (§4) — "clear skies", per the original entry's safety note.
- **Accessibility:** The canvas is `aria-hidden`, purely decorative; the hero's heading order and text are untouched by the effect, so a screen reader receives the page in full from the first byte. No information is conveyed by legibility timing. Focus is never moved or trapped; the first focusable element is reachable during the storm.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Canvas ≤ 120 particles, one rAF shared with the page loop (§5), particle update ≤ 0.5 ms/frame; canvas removed from the render tree after the hero leaves the viewport for good. Mobile variant as per breakpoints: 44 particles, one layer, shorter storm — total added weight 0 KB (procedural, no assets).
- **Tokens:** `--ice`, `--ice-frost`, `--generator` (headline ink), `--tent` (the one tent-orange pixel-source: the hero photograph's tent, §6.9, beneath the storm), `--ease-drift`, `--ease-gust`.

### 6.2 Drill-string margin cable

- **What & why:** A cable line descends the left margin, scrubbed to scroll, paying out as the reader descends; every 100 m a depth stamp lands beside it. Progress on this page is not a percentage consumed but a debt of depth accumulating — the reader watches the drill string lengthen exactly as the team does, and the cable is the page's spine: every dispatch hangs off the depth it was written at.
- **Structure:** An SVG `<path>` in the rail (§4), drawn with `stroke-dashoffset` driven by the shared lerped scroll value; stamp marks are real DOM elements in the rail (mono text, §3), positioned at their depth fractions of the total descent height — 22 stamps (0, 100 … 2,100 m) plus the final LAKE ROOF stamp at 2,147 m (§6.17). The cable is 2 px `--crevasse`, with a 1 px `--ice-deep` shadow line offset 2 px — a cable has a shadow on snow.
- **Motion spec:** Cable draw is a pure function of lerped scroll progress `p`: drawn length = `p × rail height`. Each stamp, when the cable's tip passes its position, lands over 260 ms on `haul`: translateY −6 px→0, rotate −4°→ its resting tilt (each stamp has a fixed, seeded tilt between −3° and +3° — stamps are pressed by a human, not typeset), opacity 0→1 in the first 80 ms. On scroll-up the cable retracts and stamps un-land in reverse (the haul is reversible; only the lake's drawing ratchets, §6.8).
- **Choreography timeline:** Per stamp: tip reaches stamp position −40 px → stamp pre-state armed (invisible, offset). Tip arrival → 0–80 ms fade, 0–260 ms settle with `haul`, a 1-frame 1 px overshoot at 240 ms (the press). Stamp then rests for the remainder of the page. The surface stamp (0 m) is landed before any scroll — the reader starts at the surface, stamped in.
- **State matrix:** Rest (at depth): cable drawn to current depth, all stamps above landed, stamps below absent. Hover (stamp): stamp darkens to `--crevasse-deep`, a pencil note in the right margin shows that depth's dispatch day (e.g. `1,000 M — DAY 58`) — hover reads the log, it does not decorate. Focus (stamp is a link to its dispatch): visible outline in `--crevasse-deep` 2 px offset 2 px; Enter hauls the page to that dispatch. Active: stamp presses 1 px. Scrolled-past: stamps remain landed — passed depth stays stamped, as it should. Disabled: none (stamps are links; an unlinked stamp is plain text). Touch: tap = the link behaviour; no hover note (the note is also the link's accessible name).
- **Content-merge:** The cable is the page's table of contents drawn as an instrument: each stamp is anchored to the dispatch written nearest that depth, and the rail's total height *is* the descent's height — the page is as long as the hole is deep, proportionally, and the footer (§6.17) prints the scale (`1 PX ≈ 0.9 M`).
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — full rail, stamps with day notes. 768–1199 px — rail narrows to 4.5 rem; stamps show depth only, day note moves into the dispatch dateline. <768 px — the rail leaves the margin and becomes a top cable: a horizontal 3 px line under the masthead with the current-depth stamp pinned at its tip (§6.11's mobile form); the 22 stamps become ticks, labelled only at 0 / 1,000 / 2,000 / LAKE ROOF.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Content longer than planned (extra dispatches) → stamps are positioned by depth fraction from the record, not by pixel, so added entries stretch the rail and stamps stay truthful. Content shorter → the descent compresses; the LAKE ROOF stamp is always last, always at the foot of the rail. Scroll-up fast → stamps un-land in reverse without flicker because state derives from the same lerped value, never from events. No SVG → cable renders as a 2 px CSS border on the rail with stamps printed statically (progressive base state, below).
- **Failure fallback:** The DOM ships with the cable fully drawn and all 23 stamps printed and legible — the "hauled to the bottom" state. JS arms the scrub by resetting dashoffset and stamp pre-states; if JS or the loop fails, the page shows a complete, stamped descent. Watchdog: if the shared loop has not ticked within 1500 ms of load, pre-states are disarmed and the full-drawn state restored.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Cable fully drawn, all stamps printed at rest tilt, no landing animation; the current-depth indication is carried by the pinned stamp (§6.11) in its static form.
- **Accessibility:** The rail is a `<nav aria-label="Descent">` containing an ordered list of stamp links — screen-reader users get the descent as a list of depths and days, in order, which is exactly what it is. The SVG is `aria-hidden` (the list carries the meaning). Stamp link names include depth and day: "1,000 metres, day 58 dispatch". Contrast: stamp mono in `--generator` on `--ice` 14.1:1; the cable's blue is decorative beside the text.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** One SVG path + 23 small DOM nodes; dashoffset update is a single style write per frame inside the shared loop (≤0.1 ms). Mobile: horizontal form, same nodes, ticks as CSS gradient — no SVG at all below 768 px.
- **Tokens:** `--crevasse`, `--crevasse-deep`, `--ice-deep`, `--generator`, `--pencil` (day notes), `--ease-haul`, `--rail-w` (7.5 rem / 4.5 rem / 0).

### 6.3 Signal-delay dispatches

- **What & why:** The theme's core element: each dispatch — dateline, headline, prose, photograph — fades in over 2–6 s as if the text travelled up two kilometres of wire, the delay growing with the depth the entry was written at. The reading pace is set by the fiction's physics; remoteness is felt in the scrolling hand. This is the element the whole theme exists to house, and its fallback discipline is the theme's honour: the original entry's rule — *signal delay is enhancement with a received-in-full fallback timer, so no message can be lost to the wire* — is implemented literally.
- **Structure:** Each dispatch is an `<article>` with mono dateline, Archivo headline, Newsreader prose. The arrival effect is a JS-armed pre-state (`opacity: 0` + a wire-static speckle overlay via a pseudo-element with a procedural noise background at `--pencil-faint`) removed on the shared loop's clock. The DOM ships with every dispatch at full opacity — pre-states are applied by JS only, after first paint, and only to dispatches below the fold.
- **Motion spec:** Trigger: dateline crosses 78% viewport height. Fade duration = `delay(d) = 2.0 + (d ÷ 2147) × 4.0` seconds on `wire`, where `d` is the dispatch's depth from the record (§6.17): Day 34 surface entry 2.0 s; Day 58 (1,000 m) 3.9 s; Day 66 (1,180 m) 4.2 s; the final lake dispatch 6.0 s. Wire-static speckle occupies the first 30% of the fade, resolving as the signal locks; the dateline arrives first (its fade leads the prose by 12% of the duration — the instruments check in before the human speaks). No transform movement: signals do not slide.
- **Choreography timeline:** For a 4.2 s arrival (Day 66): 0 ms — trigger, speckle at full, text at 0. 0–500 ms — dateline fades to 0.6 through static. 500–1260 ms (30%) — static resolves, dateline locks at full, prose at 0.35. 1260–3800 ms — prose fades evenly to 0.95. 3800–4200 ms — final lock: full opacity, speckle gone, the RECEIVED affordance (§6.7) arms. Any photograph in the dispatch begins its own frost-clear (§6.9) at the 50% mark.
- **State matrix:** Rest (arrived): full opacity, static gone, indistinguishable from print. Arriving: as timeline; text is selectable and readable from ~40% opacity onward — arrival never blocks copying or finding-in-page (find matches invisible-in-DOM text because the text is in the DOM; opacity does not remove it from find). Hover: headlamp (§6.6) may apply. Focus: if focus enters an arriving dispatch (keyboard reader outrunning the wire), its fade completes instantly — the reader's presence *is* a received signal. Scrolled-past quickly: any dispatch the reader has scrolled beyond completes its fade immediately (the wire catches up with a reader who has moved on; nothing arrives to an empty room). Disabled: n/a. Touch: identical; no gesture required.
- **Content-merge:** The delay is not applied to decoration *around* the content — it is applied to the content, because in this world the content is what travels. The delay value is printed in the dateline's receipt line after acknowledgement (§6.7), so the effect is also data: the reader can verify the wire's length from the log itself.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — full delays as specified. 768–1199 px — delays × 0.85 (slightly shorter wire on smaller screens; the fiction bends to the device, the record's printed delays still show the true values). <768 px — delays × 0.7, static speckle reduced to a single noise layer; a dispatch taller than the viewport triggers on its dateline and its fade is capped at 3.5 s regardless of depth, so a long entry never arrives in pieces the reader has already scrolled past.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Reader scrolls very fast through several dispatches → each completes on being passed (above); no queue of pending fades. Reader returns up the page → arrived dispatches stay arrived (receipts persist for the session; the wire does not un-send). Very long dispatch (>3 viewports) → fade applies to the article as a whole from its dateline trigger; later paragraphs are already at full opacity when reached because the fade is time-based from trigger, and its duration is capped per breakpoint. No-JS → all dispatches printed in full (base state). Print → all full opacity (print stylesheet forces it).
- **Failure fallback:** Hard watchdog: every armed dispatch force-completes at trigger + 8000 ms no matter what — the "received-in-full fallback timer" of the original entry, doubled headroom over the 6.0 s maximum delay. If the shared loop dies, a standalone 8 s `setTimeout` per dispatch still fires (the fallback does not depend on the thing it guards).
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Dispatches print in full on trigger with no fade and no static — "every dispatch printed in full on arrival", the original entry's reduced-motion rule, verbatim in spirit. The dateline's delay figure still prints (as data, after receipt), because the *fact* of the delay is content even when the experience of it is waived.
- **Accessibility:** Opacity-only animation — no motion sickness vector, no focus movement. Screen readers: dispatches are never `visibility:hidden` or removed from the accessibility tree; the pre-state is opacity only, so assistive tech reads the full log in order regardless of visual arrival. The static speckle is a decorative pseudo-element, `aria-hidden` by construction. Arrival is never announced (no live regions — a log does not interrupt). Contrast at full arrival: 14.1:1 body (§2).
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Opacity + one pseudo-element per arriving dispatch; at most two dispatches arriving concurrently (the trigger geometry enforces it), so ≤ 2 compositing layers. The speckle is a 128 px procedural tile, generated once, reused. Mobile: single-layer speckle, capped fades (above) — strictly cheaper.
- **Tokens:** `--generator`, `--ice`, `--pencil-faint` (static), `--ease-wire`, `--delay-min` (2.0 s), `--delay-max` (6.0 s), `--final-depth` (2147, from §6.17).

### 6.4 Gusts and fluttering flags

- **What & why:** Snow gusts cross the hero irregularly, and the flagged-route markers at the margin flutter — small rotation oscillations, wind-timed. The margin of this page is outdoors: the page's furniture weathers with its world, and the flags are not decoration — a flagged route is how you walk home in a white-out, so the flags stand in the right margin where the route home would be, each one planted beside the dispatch it marks safe passage to.
- **Structure:** Gusts reuse §6.1's canvas (same particle system, a gust is a temporary wind-vector event, not a second system). Flags are inline SVG (a pole line and a triangular pennant in `--tent` — human-placed, therefore orange by law, §1) in the right margin, one per major dispatch, rotating around the pole's top anchor. A flag's pennant shape is a path whose tip vertex oscillates — cloth, not a rotating rectangle.
- **Motion spec:** Gusts: every 7–14 s (seeded interval, never metronomic), the canvas wind vector swings +40–70% for 900–1600 ms on `gust`, particles streak (velocity-aligned stretch ×2.2), then ease back. Flags: continuous flutter — pennant tip oscillates ±3.5° equivalent at 1.6–2.3 Hz with amplitude modulated by the same gust events (during a gust, amplitude ×1.8 and rate ×1.3; flags and snow agree about the weather because they read the same wind value from the shared loop). This is one of the page's two licensed ambient loops (§5).
- **Choreography timeline:** Gust event: 0 ms — wind value begins rise (`gust` ease-in). 0–500 ms — particle streaks lengthen, flag amplitude climbs. 500–1100 ms — peak: flags at full flutter, snow near-horizontal. 1100–1600 ms — decay to ambient. Between gusts: flags settle to ±1.2° idle sway; snow falls at ambient density (§6.1).
- **State matrix:** Rest: idle sway as above. Gust: as timeline. Hover (flag): flutter pauses mid-beat and the flag's dispatch title shows in pencil in the margin — a hand holding the flag still to read it. Focus (flags are links to their dispatches): outline on the flag's hit area (44 px square, though the glyph is smaller), Enter navigates. Scrolled-past: flutter pauses off-screen (IntersectionObserver) — wind does not spend itself on an empty margin. Disabled: n/a. Touch: tap navigates; no hover state.
- **Content-merge:** Each flag marks a real dispatch and links to it; the flag row *is* the right-margin index of the log's major entries (§6.12's table is the formal version; the flags are the field version). Removing the flags would remove navigation, not ornament.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — flags in the right margin at full flutter. 768–1199 px — right margin narrows; flags shrink to 60% and sit tighter to the column; flutter amplitude halves. <768 px — no right margin exists; flags move into the dispatch datelines as a small static pennant glyph before the day number (no animation — the route is marked, the weather is implied), and gusts occur only in the hero.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Canvas dead → gusts die but flags still flutter (flags are SVG/CSS, independent system) — the failure of one weather system must not calm the other. Many flags (long log) → flutter phase is seeded per flag so they never synchronise into a chorus line; synchronised flags read as animation, offset flags read as wind. Reader at the finale chapter → no flags under the ice (nothing flutters at 2,147 m; the absence is specified, §13).
- **Failure fallback:** Flags are links with real text alternatives; if all animation fails, the page shows static orange pennants beside the major dispatches — still a working index. Gusts have no content to lose.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Flags printed static, pennants at rest angle; gusts abolished; hero snow static (§6.1 variant). The margin reads as a photographed route, still linked, still orange.
- **Accessibility:** Flags are `<a>` elements with names like "Day 58 dispatch — flagged route" and a 44 px hit area; the pennant SVG is decorative inside the named link. Flutter is small-amplitude rotation of a 20 px glyph — below vestibular concern thresholds, and abolished under reduced motion regardless. Gust streaks are canvas, `aria-hidden`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Flags ≤ 8 per page, each a 2-path SVG animated by one transform on the shared loop (≤0.05 ms each). Gusts cost nothing beyond §6.1's canvas. Mobile: static glyphs, zero animation cost.
- **Tokens:** `--tent`, `--generator` (pole), `--pencil` (hover note), `--ease-gust`, `--wind` (shared loop value), `--ice-frost`.

### 6.5 Falling temperature counter — the instrument cluster

- **What & why:** Depth, temperature and days-on-ice, counted in dispatch mono, agreeing with the prose at all times: it gets colder as the story deepens. The instruments are the theme's promise that the fiction is *measured* — a reader who checks the masthead against a dispatch's dateline finds them identical, because both read the one record (§6.17). Three readouts live in the masthead (§6.10) and travel with the pinned stamp (§6.11): `DEPTH`, `TEMP`, `DAY`.
- **Structure:** Three mono readouts, each a label (Archivo, §3) over a value (IBM Plex Mono). Values are text nodes updated by the shared loop — no canvas, no SVG. Temperature prints to one decimal with a true minus sign (−, U+2212), because −58.0 set with a hyphen is a typo that reads as a range.
- **Motion spec:** Depth is the lerped scroll value mapped through the record: `depth(p) = round(p × 2147)` m, updating per frame but rendering only on integer change. Temperature is a step function of depth, not a continuous slide — instruments here are read at stamps: `temp(d) = −58.0 − 0.19 × (d ÷ 100)`, recomputed at each 100 m stamp landing (§6.2), holding between stamps, printing e.g. −60.1 °C at 1,100 m and −62.1 °C at the lake. Day steps at dispatch boundaries: the day of the latest dispatch whose dateline has crossed 50% viewport. Steps are instant (a 90 ms `wire` crossfade on the changed digits only) — instruments tick; they do not tween.
- **Choreography timeline:** On a stamp landing (e.g. 1,100 m): 0 ms — stamp lands (§6.2). 60 ms — TEMP digits crossfade to −60.1. 120 ms — DEPTH readout settles at 1,100 (it has been counting continuously; the stamp is its punctuation). On crossing a dispatch dateline midpoint: DAY steps to that entry's day within one frame of the crossing, no animation beyond the digit crossfade.
- **State matrix:** Rest: values agree with the nearest dispatch above. Hover: none (readouts are not interactive). Focus: the cluster is not focusable; its values are exposed as text. Scrolled-past / at finale: TEMP holds at −62.1, DEPTH at 2,147, DAY at 79 — the instruments stop at the lake, because the record stops there. Disabled: n/a. Touch: identical.
- **Content-merge:** The cluster is duplicated *inside* every dateline (each dispatch prints its own day, depth and temperature, §3 law 2) — the masthead cluster is the live summary of the same numbers the log prints statically. A reader with JS off loses the live cluster and keeps every dateline: the data survives the instrument.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — full cluster in masthead and pinned stamp. 768–1199 px — cluster in masthead only; pinned stamp shows depth alone. <768 px — masthead shows DEPTH + TEMP (DAY lives in datelines); the top cable's pinned stamp (§6.2 mobile) carries depth.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Scroll-up → depth and temperature retrace exactly (functions of scroll, not accumulators) — the instruments haul back up honestly. Inconsistent content (a dispatch whose printed dateline disagrees with the record) is a build error caught by the §10 figure audit, not something the runtime papers over: the runtime reads the record, so a wrong dateline shows as visibly wrong against the masthead — by design, the log catches its own lies. Tab hidden → loop pauses; on return, values recompute from scroll in one frame.
- **Failure fallback:** With JS off, the masthead prints the surface readings (0 m, −58.0 °C, DAY —) and every dispatch's dateline carries the true local values; the page loses nothing factual. Watchdog: if the loop stalls >1500 ms, readouts freeze at last true values (never blank, never zeroed).
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Identical values, digit crossfade removed — steps are instant text changes. (The values are scroll-driven data, not decoration; reduced motion changes their *manners*, not their readings.)
- **Accessibility:** The cluster uses `aria-live="off"` — it must never announce; it is ambient data, fully duplicated in static datelines for anyone who needs it non-visually. Values are real text, readable by screen readers on demand. Contrast: mono `--generator` on `--ice` 14.1:1; on the finale chapter, `--ice` on `--night` 14.1:1 and the temp readout may use `--tent` at 5.0:1 (§2's one licensed orange text).
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Three text writes per change, change-gated — effectively free. Mobile shows two readouts (above).
- **Tokens:** `--generator`, `--ice`, `--tent` (finale temp only), `--crevasse` (depth value in the pinned stamp), `--ease-wire`, `--final-depth`, `--temp-base` (−58.0), `--temp-rate` (−0.19 / 100 m).

### 6.6 Headlamp hover

- **What & why:** Hovering a dispatch clears the frost in a small warm circle around it — a radial mask lightening, the reader leaning in with the team's headlamp. Inspection in this world is done with carried light, and the warmth is literal: the circle is the one place hover is allowed to be tent-tinted, because a headlamp is a human holding a light. It answers the theme's central sensory problem — a white page about cold can feel merely blank; the headlamp makes the reader's own attention feel like heat against frost.
- **Structure:** Each dispatch carries a frost layer (pseudo-element, `--ice-frost` at low alpha with the procedural noise tile, §6.3) whose mask is a radial-gradient centred on the pointer, radius 180 px, feathered 60 px. Implemented as a CSS mask updated from pointer position inside the shared loop (pointer coordinates are loop inputs, like scroll). The frost layer sits above the text but is a lightening wash only — it never reduces text contrast below its resting state; the "clearing" is the wash lifting plus a 4% warm tint (`--tent` at 0.04 alpha) inside the circle.
- **Motion spec:** Pointer enters a dispatch → circle fades in over 600 ms on `drift`, radius easing 120→180 px. Pointer moves → centre follows with the loop's lerp (the lamp lags the hand slightly, as a head on a neck does). Pointer leaves → circle shrinks to 0 over 400 ms `drift`. The effect is per-dispatch: crossing from one dispatch to the next hands the lamp over with a 200 ms dark beat between (the lamp swings).
- **Choreography timeline:** 0 ms — pointer enters. 0–200 ms — wash begins lifting at entry point, radius 120 px. 200–600 ms — radius to 180 px, warm tint to full 4%. Steady state — centre lerps behind pointer at the shared 0.10 factor. Exit: 0–400 ms — radius and tint to zero.
- **State matrix:** Rest: frost at resting alpha (a barely-there film, 0.10) — the dispatch is cold but fully readable; the lamp is an enhancement of clarity, never its precondition. Hover: as timeline. Focus (keyboard): focus within a dispatch lights the lamp centred on the focused element — keyboard readers get the lamp too, aimed by focus instead of pointer. Active: no change (clicking is §6.7's stamp, not the lamp). Scrolled-past: lamp off. Disabled: n/a. Touch: no hover — a tap-and-hold ≥300 ms lights the lamp at the touch point until release; a normal tap does not (taps are for links and stamps).
- **Content-merge:** The lamp works on the dispatch's own text and photographs — it clears the frost *of that entry*, including §6.9's photograph frost inside it. It is aimed attention, and its object is always content, never chrome: the lamp does not light the navigation or the footer.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — radius 180 px as specified. 768–1199 px — radius 150 px, warm tint 3%. <768 px — hover does not exist and touch-hold lighting is disabled (small screens are read at arm's length, gloves on); dispatches rest at a slightly lower frost alpha (0.07) to compensate — the mobile page is pre-cleared a little, by design.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Pointer moving fast → the lerp lag is the design (lamp swings), capped so the centre never trails more than 260 px. Multiple pointers / pen input → last-active pointer wins; pen hover works identically. Frost layer unsupported (mask-image) → the warm tint circle alone applies (background radial on the pseudo-element), still legible, slightly less "cleared". Reader prefers contrast → frost resting alpha drops to 0.04 via media query (§6 layer note in §10).
- **Failure fallback:** If masks and the loop both fail, dispatches simply rest at their printed state — which is fully readable, because resting frost never drops contrast below 12.6:1 effective. The lamp can only add clarity; its absence removes nothing.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** No lamp. Dispatches rest clear (frost alpha 0.05, static) — under reduced motion the reader is assumed to be *inside* the tent, reading by steady light.
- **Accessibility:** Purely presentational; no information is gated behind the lamp (resting contrast is independently sufficient, §2). Focus-driven lighting (above) keeps the effect parity for keyboard users. The warm tint never carries meaning — it is never the only indication of anything.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** One mask position update per frame per hovered dispatch, inside the shared loop; the frost layer is composited, not repainted (mask position via CSS custom property). Cost ≤0.2 ms/frame while hovering, zero otherwise. Mobile: disabled entirely (above).
- **Tokens:** `--ice-frost`, `--tent` (4% tint), `--pencil-faint` (noise), `--ease-drift`, `--lamp-r` (180 px), `--frost-rest` (0.10).

### 6.7 RECEIVED acknowledgement

- **What & why:** Clicking a dispatch ticks a pencil RECEIVED beside it, with the delay noted — `RECEIVED · rcvd +4.2s`. The reader is the other end of the wire: reading is receipt, and the log shows it. This is the theme's one act of participation, and it is deliberately clerical rather than social — no like count, no share, just a stamp in a logbook, in pencil, because receipts are pencilled by the person on shift. The stamp is also the theme's proof-of-reading made visible: a returning reader sees their own shift's work.
- **Structure:** Each dispatch footer carries a real `<button>` ("Acknowledge receipt") styled as an empty stamp box (1 px `--pencil` border, mono caption). On acknowledgement, an SVG stamp — the word RECEIVED in Archivo 700 inside a double-rule rounded rect, rotated at the dispatch's seeded tilt — presses into the dispatch's right margin beside the dateline, with a mono sub-line `RCVD +{delay}s · {reader's local time}`. State persists in `sessionStorage` (this shift) and is re-applied on load without animation.
- **Motion spec:** Click → 60 ms pause (the hand lifts the stamp) → stamp scales 1.35→1.0 and fades 0→1 over 420 ms on `stamp` (overshoot capped: scale floor 0.98 at 300 ms, settle by 420 ms), rotate from −9° to its seeded rest tilt (−3°…+3°). The delay figure counts up over the same 420 ms from +0.0 to the dispatch's true delay (§6.3's function at that depth — Day 66 prints +4.2 s). A pencil tick (SVG path, 14 px) draws beside the button over 260 ms on `haul`. Sound: none, ever — stamps are silent in this theme's build spec; the press is visual weight only.
- **Choreography timeline:** 0 ms — click; button border darkens to `--generator`. 0–60 ms — anticipation stillness. 60–360 ms — stamp descends (scale/opacity/rotate as above), delay counting. 360–420 ms — overshoot settle; tick draws 300–560 ms overlapping. 560 ms — button state flips to its received form (below); the dispatch's entry in the log table (§6.12) ticks in the same beat.
- **State matrix:** Rest (unreceived): empty stamp box button, caption `ACKNOWLEDGE RECEIPT`. Hover: box border `--generator`, background warms 2% (`--tent` at 0.02 — a hand hovers, warmth gathers, §1's law holds: the warmth is the reader's own hand). Focus: 2 px `--crevasse-deep` outline, offset 2 px. Active: the press (timeline). Received: button becomes a quiet text state — `RECEIVED ✓` in pencil with the tick — and is *disabled as a button* (receipt is not revocable within a shift; the log does not un-stamp), rendered as plain text, not a greyed button, so no disabled-control styling is needed. Scrolled-past: stamps persist. Touch: tap = click; the 44 px button is glove-sized.
- **Content-merge:** The stamp lands *in the dispatch's margin beside its dateline* — on the document, not in a toast or a header counter. The count of received dispatches appears only in the log table (§6.12) and the footer record (§6.17), as log data. The delay printed is the same number the arrival animation took (§6.3): the stamp audits the wire.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — stamp in the right margin at dateline height. 768–1199 px — stamp moves inline, right-aligned on the dateline row. <768 px — stamp inline under the dateline, left-aligned; button full-width (gloves, cold, one thumb).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Reader acknowledges before arrival completes → the fade force-completes first (§6.3's focus rule applies: presence is receipt), then the stamp lands. sessionStorage unavailable (private mode) → stamps work for the page view, simply don't persist; no error surfaces. All dispatches received → the log table's header line gains one pencil sentence — `All received. The wire is quiet.` — written in the §12 voice; nothing celebrates louder than that. Print → received stamps print (they are part of the log the reader made).
- **Failure fallback:** The button is a real button with a real state change in the DOM; if the stamp SVG fails, the text state (`RECEIVED · RCVD +4.2s`) still renders in mono — the receipt survives its ornament. Without JS, the button is absent (it is JS-added) and dispatches print plain — a paper log has no one to acknowledge to, which is honest.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Stamp appears instantly at rest tilt with the delay printed in full; tick printed, not drawn. The clerk's act remains; the theatre of pressing is waived.
- **Accessibility:** Button has an accessible name per dispatch ("Acknowledge receipt of day 66 dispatch"); on activation, an `aria-live="polite"` region (visually hidden) announces "Day 66 dispatch received, delay 4.2 seconds" — once, quietly. The stamp SVG is decorative beside the real text state. Received state is conveyed in text, not by the stamp graphic alone. Button contrast: border and caption `--generator`/`--pencil` on ice — caption at 0.8125 rem in `--generator` (14.1:1) for the actionable form.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** One small SVG + text per dispatch; the press is a single transform/opacity animation, composited. Persistence is one sessionStorage write per receipt. Mobile: same, full-width button — no reduction needed.
- **Tokens:** `--pencil`, `--tent-deep` (stamp ink after drying), `--generator`, `--tent` (hover warmth 2%), `--ease-stamp`, `--ease-haul`, `--delay-*` (from §6.3).

### 6.8 The lake outline

- **What & why:** At the final depth the cable stops, and beneath the last dispatch an SVG sonar outline of the subglacial lake draws itself — the first sight of the thing the whole page drilled toward. It is the page's payoff, and it is drawn by its own instrument at the moment the story arrives: not an illustration revealed, but a measurement taken, live, at the bottom of the descent. Everything before it is approach; this is arrival.
- **Structure:** A full-width finale panel on `--night` (§2's authored dark chapter). Inside: the sonar trace — one SVG path, the lake's outline as a closed, irregular contour (the record's lake: 11.8 km × 3.6 km, §6.17), drawn in `--crevasse-pale` 2 px with a `--crevasse` echo line offset 3 px below at 40% (sonar returns twice off a roof and a floor). Beneath the contour, mono soundings print as the draw passes them (depth-to-roof figures at five stations). The lake's name — the team calls it **the Water** until it is sampled; the panel's caption says so — is set in Archivo caps in `--ice`.
- **Motion spec:** The draw is scrubbed to scroll across a 140 vh pinned range (§6.11's sibling pin, the page's second and last): path length maps to lerped scroll progress through the pin; full draw ≈ 6000 ms at reading pace. Soundings fade in (`wire`, 600 ms) as the trace passes their station. **Ratchet:** once any portion is drawn, scrolling back up does not un-draw it — the trace holds at its maximum reached extent (sonar is a record; §5). The cable (§6.2) terminates at the panel's top edge in a drawn connector: the drill string reaches the roof and stops, visibly.
- **Choreography timeline:** Pin begins (panel top at viewport top): trace at 0%, first sounding armed. 0–25% of pin — the near shore draws left to right; sounding 1 (2,147 m — roof) prints. 25–60% — the long axis draws; the echo line follows 3% behind the main trace; soundings 2–3 print (water column 214 m, 268 m). 60–90% — the far shore and the close of the contour; sounding 4 (301 m — the deepest). 90–100% — contour closes; sounding 5 prints; the caption line arrives by wire (2 s `wire` fade, the last signal of the page); pin releases. The footer record (§6.17) follows on `--night`.
- **State matrix:** Rest (before pin): panel printed with the contour at 12% opacity as a "predicted outline" in pencil dashes — the team's pre-drill survey guess, labelled as predicted — so the panel is never blank and the draw's achievement is measurable against the guess. Drawing: as timeline. Drawn (complete): contour at full, prediction retired (faded to 8% beneath — the guess stays in the log, corrected). Hover: a sounding station under the pointer brightens and its figure enlarges 1.1× — inspecting a measurement. Focus: the panel is a region with an accessible description (below); the trace itself is not interactive. Scrolled-past: drawn state persists (ratchet). Disabled: n/a. Touch: scrub is scroll itself; identical.
- **Content-merge:** The outline is the expedition's data, sized by the record: its aspect on screen is the lake's true 11.8 : 3.6 ratio, and the panel prints the scale. The final dispatch's prose sits directly above the panel and ends with the sentence the trace illustrates — text and instrument are one argument.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — pin range 140 vh, panel full-bleed. 768–1199 px — pin 110 vh, soundings reduce to three stations (roof, deepest, close). <768 px — **no pin**: the draw scrubs across the panel's natural pass through the viewport (200 vh of scroll travel), soundings three, echo line dropped (one trace is enough on a small screen) — the lake still draws, unrehearsed by pinning.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Reader jump-scrolls to the footer → the ratchet draws the lake in full instantly (a reader who arrives at the bottom has, in the fiction, been hauled down; the sonar record is complete in the log regardless). Reader never reaches the finale → the prediction state is a complete, honest panel. SVG unsupported → the contour ships as a static printed path image equivalent: the panel prints the final trace with all five soundings (the DOM's base state is the *completed* drawing; JS arms the scrub by resetting to prediction + 0% draw).
- **Failure fallback:** Base DOM = finished trace + all soundings + caption, printed. JS arms the draw. Watchdog: if the pin's scroll range is exited without the draw completing (any path), the ratchet completes it — the lake cannot be left half-measured by a scroll accident.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** No pin, no scrub: the panel prints the completed trace with soundings, prediction retired, caption present. Arrival at the lake under reduced motion is a printed chart — which is what the team will eventually publish anyway.
- **Accessibility:** The panel is a `<figure>` with a `<figcaption>` carrying the full data in text: the lake's dimensions, the five soundings, the roof depth — a screen reader receives the measurement, not a description of a drawing. The SVG is `aria-hidden`. Contrast on `--night`: `--ice` text 14.8:1, `--crevasse-pale` trace is graphical (its data is in the caption), mono soundings in `--ice` at 0.875 rem.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** One SVG path (~180 points) + one echo path; dashoffset writes inside the shared loop ≤0.1 ms/frame; pin uses `position: sticky`, no JS scroll-jacking. Mobile: no pin, no echo line, three soundings — strictly lighter.
- **Tokens:** `--night`, `--ice`, `--crevasse`, `--crevasse-pale`, `--pencil` (prediction), `--tent` (one licensed pixel: the drill connector's tip marker — the drill is human machinery, §1), `--ease-wire`, `--final-depth`, `--lake-l` (11.8 km), `--lake-w` (3.6 km).

### 6.9 Frost-edged frames

- **What & why:** Photographs sit in frames with frost creeping at their edges; the frost thins as the image centres, like a lens clearing. Even seeing is cold work here — images are earned from the ice. Photographs in a field log are evidence (the tent at Day 34, the drill at 1,000 m, a birthday cake at −60 °C), and the frost makes the reader complicit in the conditions the evidence was gathered under.
- **Structure:** A `<figure>` with the photograph, a frost overlay (pseudo-element: the procedural noise tile at `--ice-frost`, masked by an SVG noise-derived alpha so frost gathers at corners and edges, thinning toward centre), and a mono caption with the day and depth the photograph was taken at — every photograph is stamped with its coordinates in the descent, like every dispatch. The image itself settles from scale 1.06→1.0 inside its frame (overflow hidden) as it centres.
- **Motion spec:** Frost alpha is a function of the image's distance from viewport centre, driven by the shared loop: at entry (image top at 90% viewport) frost 0.85; at centre, frost 0.25 (never 0 — a lens at −60 °C is never fully clear, and the last frost is the photograph's honesty); past centre, frost holds at 0.25 (it does not re-freeze behind the reader — cleared is cleared, a small ratchet). Scale settles 1.06→1.0 across the same travel on `drift`. Caption arrives with its dispatch (§6.3) or standalone by the same wire rule if the figure stands alone.
- **Choreography timeline:** Image enters at 90% viewport height: frost 0.85, scale 1.06. Travel to centre (~one viewport of scroll): frost eases 0.85→0.25 on the loop's lerp, fastest at the corners (mask geometry), scale →1.0 by 70% of the travel. Centre: hold. Exit upward: values hold at cleared state. A photograph inside an arriving dispatch (§6.3) begins clearing only after the dispatch's fade passes 50%.
- **State matrix:** Rest (centred): frost 0.25, scale 1.0. Hover: the headlamp (§6.6) adds its local clearing over the photograph — combined frost can reach 0.1 inside the lamp's circle (leaning close with a light does clear a lens). Focus (figure is not focusable unless it is a link; when linked): outline in `--crevasse-deep`; frost drops to 0.15 while focused — keyboard readers see the evidence clearly. Scrolled-past: cleared state persists. Disabled: n/a. Touch: no hover; the scroll-driven clearing is the whole effect, and it is enough.
- **Content-merge:** The frost is *of* the photograph — it is the condition the picture was taken and viewed in — and the caption ties the image to a day and depth in the record. A photograph without a day/depth caption is not permitted in this theme (§10 checks): an unlocated image is stock, and stock is slop here.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — full frost geometry, photographs up to column width (40 rem) or bleeding into the right margin to 52 rem for the three record photographs (tent, drill, lake camp). 768–1199 px — frost alpha range compresses (0.7→0.25); no margin bleed. <768 px — frost 0.55→0.2, scale settle reduced to 1.03→1.0; photographs full-column-width.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Image fails to load → the frame prints its caption and a pencil note `PLATE NOT RECEIVED — WIRE FAULT` in the frame's ice ground: a missing photograph is logged as a missing plate, in-world, never a broken-image icon. Very tall image → clearing maps to its centre passing viewport centre; frost range shortens so it still fully clears. No mask support → frost becomes a uniform wash at the same alphas (less creep, same legibility contract). Lazy-loading: images load with `loading="lazy"` but the frame and caption are in the DOM from the start — the plate's place in the log never pops in.
- **Failure fallback:** The photograph is a plain `<img>` in the DOM; the frost is a pseudo-element enhancement. No JS → photographs print with a static light frost border (CSS-only, alpha 0.2, purely decorative frame edge) and full legibility. The image can never be hidden by its own effect: frost alpha caps at 0.85 only while the image is entering, and the watchdog (§6.3's loop guard) forces all frost to rest values if the loop stalls.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Photographs print at frost 0.2 static, scale 1.0 — a plate under glass in the logbook. Captions unchanged.
- **Accessibility:** Every photograph carries real alt text written as a log entry ("The drill tent at dusk, day 34, orange tent against blue ice") — alt text in this theme is a caption for the log, not a keyword string. Frost is decorative and never reduces the image's informational content below recognition at rest. Caption text `--generator` on `--ice` 14.1:1.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Frost is one pseudo-element per figure, alpha driven by a custom property from the shared loop (≤6 figures per page, ≤0.15 ms/frame total). Images: responsive `srcset`, the largest variant ≤ 160 KB, total page image weight inside §14's budget. Mobile: compressed frost range and smaller scale settle (above).
- **Tokens:** `--ice-frost`, `--ice-deep` (frame edge), `--generator` (caption), `--pencil` (plate-fault note), `--ease-drift`, `--frost-max` (0.85), `--frost-rest` (0.25).

### 6.10 Masthead & station navigation

- **What & why:** The page opens not with a hero banner but with a station masthead: the expedition's name set like the stencil on a fuel drum, its coordinates, and the live instrument cluster (§6.5). Navigation in this world is a station's notice board — few links, plainly labelled, pinned up in the cold. The masthead's job is orientation in the fiction's terms: where you are (the surface), whose log this is, and what the instruments currently say.
- **Structure:** A header block: station name (Archivo 800, §3), a mono coordinate line (`78°35′S 85°16′W · AURORA BASIN · ELEV 2,847 M`), the instrument cluster (§6.5), and a nav row of text links (Archivo 600 caps): THE LOG · THE TEAM · THE LAKE · SEND WORD (§6.15). A sastrugi rule (§4) closes the masthead. No logo image, no hamburger at any size — the link set is four; it fits everywhere, in gloves.
- **Motion spec:** The masthead is the white-out's stage (§6.1): its elements resolve in the storm's order — station name at density 0.5, coordinates at 0.4, instruments last (they are read once you can see). After the storm, the masthead is still: no sticky shrink, no scroll behaviour of its own; its live values update per §6.5 only. Nav hover: link underline draws as a cable segment — a 2 px `--crevasse` line extending left→right over 220 ms `haul`.
- **Choreography timeline:** Load: governed by §6.1 (0–3000 ms). Steady state: instrument ticks per §6.5. Scroll away: masthead leaves; the pinned stamp (§6.11) assumes the depth-telling duty — there is a designed handover, never two depth displays competing (at ≥1200 px the masthead cluster and pinned stamp overlap for exactly the masthead's exit duration, then the cluster is gone with its header).
- **State matrix:** Rest: as printed. Hover (nav link): cable underline (above); link colour `--crevasse-deep`. Focus: 2 px outline `--crevasse-deep`, offset 3 px, plus the underline — focus is doubly marked, gloves and glare assumed. Active page: its link carries a small tent-orange square marker (■ 8 px) — the current page is where the humans are; §1's law licenses exactly this. Scrolled-past: gone (not sticky). Disabled: n/a. Touch: links are 48 px tall targets.
- **Content-merge:** The masthead's instruments are the page's instruments (§6.5) and its coordinates are the record's (§6.17) — the header is the log's title page, carrying data, not a brand block. The nav names are the site's actual sections in the log's vocabulary.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — full masthead, cluster right-aligned beside the station name. 768–1199 px — cluster drops beneath the coordinates, nav row wraps to two lines max. <768 px — station name at 1.75 rem, cluster reduced per §6.5, nav a single wrapped row of four 48 px targets; coordinates keep only basin + elevation (longitude is a luxury on a small screen, and the record still prints it in the footer).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Long expedition name → Archivo caps at the clamp's floor with tracking tightened to +0.08 em; never wraps past two lines (the name is chosen to fit the drum). Instruments JS-dead → surface readings print (§6.5 fallback). The active-page marker must be set server-side per page — a build that marks THE LOG active on every page fails §10.
- **Failure fallback:** The masthead is static HTML with live values layered on; every failure mode degrades to a printed title page. Nothing in it can block the first dispatch.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** No storm resolution choreography (§6.1 variant): masthead prints clear; nav underline appears instantly on hover/focus.
- **Accessibility:** `<header>` with the station name as the page's `<h1>` on the home page (on sub-pages, the masthead name is a `<p>` and the page's own dispatch headline is the `<h1>` — one h1 per page, ruled here because logs have one subject per page). Nav is a labelled `<nav>`. Skip link styled as a mono dateline line ("SKIP TO THE LOG") — in-voice and functional. Contrast per §2.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Zero assets; the storm interaction is §6.1's canvas. Mobile layout as above — cheaper by subtraction only.
- **Tokens:** `--generator`, `--ice`, `--crevasse`, `--crevasse-deep`, `--tent` (active marker), `--pencil` (coordinate line at ≥1 rem equivalent — set in mono `--generator` at 60% for print parity), `--ease-haul`.

### 6.11 The pinned depth stamp

- **What & why:** The current-depth stamp pins at the margin for the whole descent — a single mono stamp, travelling with the reader, always answering "how deep are we now?" It is the theme's progress indicator in its honest form: not a bar filling, but a reading stamped at the reader's current position, updating as stamps land (§6.2) and carrying the temperature with it (§6.5). When the reader wonders where they are in the hole, the stamp is there, in the rail, at eye level.
- **Structure:** A stamp-styled box (double-rule border, `--generator` mono, slight seeded rotation −2°) positioned `sticky` in the rail at 40% viewport height, containing: `DEPTH 1,180 M` / `−60.1 °C` / `DAY 66`. It lives inside the rail's nav (§6.2) but is not a link. On the finale chapter it inverts: `--ice` mono on `--night`, temperature in `--tent` (§2's licensed orange text).
- **Motion spec:** The stamp does not glide continuously — it *re-stamps* at each 100 m landing: a 420 ms `stamp` press (scale 1.06→1.0, opacity dip to 0.7 at 120 ms) with the new depth, per §6.5's step rule. Between landings it is perfectly still (a stamp does not creep). Its sticky travel is native CSS — the box rides the rail; only its content re-stamps. At the final landing (2,147 m) the stamp changes its third line to `LAKE ROOF` and presses twice — once for the depth, once, 300 ms later, for the roof — the only double-stamp on the page.
- **Choreography timeline:** At a 100 m boundary (lerped progress crosses the stamp's fraction): 0 ms — rail stamp lands (§6.2). 80 ms — pinned stamp begins its press. 80–200 ms — scale to 1.06, old figures fade to 0.7. 200–500 ms — new figures in, scale settles on `stamp`. 500 ms — rest until the next boundary. Day changes (§6.5) re-stamp only the DAY line, a smaller press (scale 1.03, 300 ms).
- **State matrix:** Rest: current readings, still. Hover: none — it is not interactive; a pencil tooltip is refused (instruments do not explain themselves on hover). Focus: not focusable; its values are text, readable by AT in place. Scrolled-past (finale): inverted form (above). At surface (before first landing): reads `DEPTH 0 M · −58.0 °C · DAY —` — the surface is a real reading, not an empty state. Disabled: n/a. Touch: identical; on mobile it becomes the top cable's tip stamp (§6.2).
- **Content-merge:** The stamp's three values are §6.5's cluster, duplicated by design: masthead for the surface, pinned stamp for the descent, datelines for the record. Three printings, one record — the §10 audit greps all three.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — rail stamp as specified. 768–1199 px — stamp shows DEPTH + TEMP (DAY dropped; it is in every dateline). <768 px — the stamp rides the horizontal top cable (§6.2 mobile), DEPTH only, pinned under the masthead as a slim bar; TEMP remains in the masthead cluster.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Fast scroll across multiple boundaries → presses queue-collapse: the stamp shows the latest landing with one press (never a backlog of stamps — a stamp pressed five times in a second is a machine gun, not a clerk). Scroll-up across a boundary → re-stamps to the shallower reading with the same press (the haul back up is also logged). Sticky unsupported → the stamp sits at the rail's top statically and still re-stamps its values (the readings matter, the riding is manners).
- **Failure fallback:** Static print: the stamp box renders with the surface readings and updates only if the loop lives; with no JS it reads 0 M / −58.0 °C permanently beside a fully printed rail (§6.2 fallback) — the descent is still navigable by the landed stamps.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Values step instantly, no press animation, no rotation (stamp prints at 0° — under reduced motion the clerk sets it down straight). Riding and readings unchanged.
- **Accessibility:** The stamp is a `<p>` of live text with `aria-live="off"` (§6.5's rule: ambient, duplicated statically). Its rotation is visual only. Contrast: `--generator` on `--ice` 14.1:1; finale `--ice` on `--night` 14.8:1, temp `--tent` on `--night` 5.2:1 at 0.9375 rem mono — the one place orange text is licensed, and it is an instrument reading, large enough and bold enough (mono 500) to hold it.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** One sticky element, three text nodes, re-stamp animation composited. Mobile: slim bar form, one value — minimal.
- **Tokens:** `--generator`, `--ice`, `--night`, `--tent`, `--crevasse` (border on finale), `--ease-stamp`, `--final-depth`.

### 6.12 The dispatch index — log table

- **What & why:** A formal table of every dispatch — day, depth, headline, wire delay, receipt state — placed after the final dispatch and before the footer: the log's own index, the page turning its records into a finding aid. Field logs end with an index of transmissions; this one lets the reader jump the wire back to any entry and see, at a glance, which entries they have received (§6.7) and which still wait.
- **Structure:** A real `<table>`: columns DAY · DEPTH · TEMP (mono, from the record) · DISPATCH (Archivo headline, linked) · WIRE (delay, mono) · RECEIVED (pencil tick or an empty box glyph). Rows in day order. A header line above the table in mono: `INDEX OF DISPATCHES — 14 ENTRIES · 9 RECEIVED` (counts computed, never hand-typed). Sastrugi rules between row groups at each 500 m band.
- **Motion spec:** Rows arrive by the wire rule when the table first enters: a single 2.0 s `wire` fade for the whole table (it is one transmission — the index itself), not per-row stagger (stagger is a showroom habit; clerks receive documents whole). Received ticks (§6.7) draw in sync when their dispatch is acknowledged while the table is on screen; off-screen, they simply are drawn when next seen. Headline link hover: cable underline per §6.10.
- **Choreography timeline:** Table dateline crosses 78% viewport: 0–2000 ms — table fades in whole, header counts first (200 ms lead). Thereafter: acknowledgement events update the header count and the row's tick within the §6.7 timeline (tick at +300 ms). No other motion exists in this element.
- **State matrix:** Rest: full table, true states. Hover (row): row ground warms to `--ice-deep` at 40% — a thumb resting on the line. Focus (headline link): outline per theme standard; row warms identically (hover and focus share the cue). Active: link press, no transform. Scrolled-past: unchanged. Disabled: n/a. Touch: rows are not targets; the headline links are, at 44 px row height minimum.
- **Content-merge:** The table is generated from the same record as everything else (§6.17) — in a real build it is rendered from the dispatch data itself, so it cannot drift from the page it indexes. Its RECEIVED column is the reader's own session state (§6.7), making the index partly *the reader's* document.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — six columns as specified. 768–1199 px — TEMP column drops (it is derivable from depth by the printed formula, §6.5, and the table's footnote says so). <768 px — table becomes a stacked list: each entry a dateline line (DAY · DEPTH · WIRE · ✓) over its headline link; the table semantics are retained via the list being the same data (a `<ul>` rendering is licensed below 768 px only, with identical content and order).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Zero received → header reads `0 RECEIVED — THE WIRE IS OPEN` (§12 voice). All received → §6.7's quiet line appears here too, once (not twice — the table is its home; §6.7's note defers to it). A dispatch added in a later season → the table gains a row and the count line recomputes; season breaks are a full-width pencil row (`— SEASON 4 —`). Print: the table prints completely, ticks as printed state.
- **Failure fallback:** Static HTML table, fully populated from the record at build time; JS adds only the receipt ticks' animation and live count. No JS → ticks reflect nothing (no receipts exist without JS, §6.7) and the header count line is omitted rather than wrong — the build prints the entries count only.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Table prints in full, no fade; ticks appear instantly.
- **Accessibility:** Real table semantics with `<th scope="col">` and a `<caption>` ("Index of dispatches, in day order, with wire delays and receipt state"). Received state is text (`Received` / `Not received` in a visually-hidden span beside the glyph) — never glyph-only. Header count is plain text above the table.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** One table, ≤ 20 rows in practice; trivial cost. Mobile list form is the same nodes restyled.
- **Tokens:** `--generator`, `--ice-deep`, `--pencil` (ticks), `--crevasse-deep` (links), `--ease-wire`, `--ease-haul`.

### 6.13 The team roster — nine names in the cold

- **What & why:** The team page's core element and the theme's warmest: nine roster entries, one per team member — name, role, and the day-count they have served — each with a photograph in a frost-edged frame (§6.9) and, where the record allows, one line in their own words from the log. This is where tent orange lives most densely on the site, lawfully: these are the humans. The roster exists because the dispatches' "we" must resolve into people, or the log is a machine talking.
- **Structure:** A vertical list (never a grid of cards — a team at a station is a manifest, read top to bottom): each entry a row: photograph (frost-edged, §6.9), name (Archivo 700 caps), role (mono), days-on-ice (mono, from the record), and their line (Newsreader italic) where one exists in the log. The lead glaciologist's entry carries the one permitted orange rule under the name — she is, at the time of the log, the person furthest down the hole on shift rotation; the rule marks the current deepest human, and it moves between entries between seasons (the build sets it from the record's `deepest` field).
- **Motion spec:** Entries arrive by the wire rule (§6.3) at the surface delay band — this page is at the surface, so delays run 2.0–2.6 s, computed from a nominal depth of 0–300 m (the team page's fiction: these files came up from the drill site office, a short wire). Photographs clear per §6.9. The orange rule draws in as a cable segment (`haul`, 300 ms) when its entry arrives.
- **Choreography timeline:** Per entry: dateline-equivalent (role line) triggers at 78% viewport → wire fade per §6.3's timeline at the entry's delay → photograph frost-clear begins at fade 50% (§6.9) → orange rule (lead entry only) draws at fade 80%. Entries never overlap arrivals by more than one (trigger geometry).
- **State matrix:** Rest: printed manifest. Hover (entry): the headlamp (§6.6) applies to the entry's text block — attention warms a person's file. Focus (entries are not links, except a member's quoted line may link to its dispatch): where linked, standard outline; lamp centres on focus. Active: n/a. Scrolled-past: cleared/arrived states persist. Disabled: n/a. Touch: no hover; arrivals and frost-clearing are scroll-driven and complete.
- **Content-merge:** Every fact in an entry is record data (§6.17's roster fields): days-on-ice in a roster entry and the same person's day-count mentioned in a dispatch agree because both print from the roster record. The quoted lines are verbatim from dispatches on the log page — the roster is an index of voices, cross-linked both ways.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — photograph left (14 rem), text right, entries separated by 4 rem of ice. 768–1199 px — photograph 10 rem, quote line drops below the role line. <768 px — stacked: photograph full column width (frost per §6.9 mobile), then name, role, days, quote. The manifest order never changes.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** A member with no photograph (camera failed — it happens, and the log would say so) → the frame prints the §6.9 plate-fault note with their name: `PLATE NOT RECEIVED — CAMERA FROZEN, DAY 51`. A member who rotated home mid-season → their entry stays, with a pencil line `ROTATED HOME DAY 70 — RECEIVED WITH THANKS`; the manifest does not delete people. Ten members (a larger season) → the list simply lengthens; nothing in the layout assumes nine.
- **Failure fallback:** Static list, photographs printed, frost at CSS-only rest value. The roster's facts are HTML text; no failure mode reaches them.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Entries print in full on arrival; photographs at rest frost; the orange rule printed, not drawn.
- **Accessibility:** Photographs' alt text follows §6.9's log-entry standard ("Aki Tanaka in the drill tent door, day 58, orange parka"). The orange rule is decorative; "currently deepest on shift" is stated in the lead entry's text (mono line: `DEEPEST ON SHIFT — 1,180 M ROTA`), never carried by colour alone. List semantics: a real `<ol>` — a manifest is ordered.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Photographs inside §14's image budget (≤ 120 KB each at roster size, `srcset` cut hard on mobile). Frost cost per §6.9.
- **Tokens:** `--tent` (the one rule; parka-orange in photographs is content, not chrome), `--generator`, `--pencil` (rotation note), `--crevasse` (quote link), `--ease-wire`, `--ease-haul`.

### 6.14 Depth band markers — the sastrugi section break

- **What & why:** Between dispatch groups, at each 500 m band, the page breaks with a heavier sastrugi rule and a band marker: `— 1,500 M — THE ICE IS OLDER THAN LANGUAGE HERE —` or whatever the log's band note says. Section breaks in this theme are depth events, not visual rhythm: the reader crosses a band the way the drill does, and the marker tells them what changes at that depth (pressure, age, the drill's behaviour) in one mono line and one Archivo line. It is the theme's divider, and it carries information every time.
- **Structure:** A full-measure break: a 2 px `--ice-deep` rule with the wind-line texture doubled across 3 rem of height, the band depth in Archivo 600 caps centred on the rule in an ice-ground knockout (the rule passes *behind* the text), and below it the band note in Newsreader italic — one sentence, written by the team, from the record's band notes (§6.17). Five bands exist in the descent (500 / 1,000 / 1,500 / 2,000 / LAKE APPROACH at 2,100).
- **Motion spec:** As the break enters, the doubled wind-lines draw outward from the marker — two 1 px lines extending left and right from centre over 700 ms on `haul`, like wind finding a ridge. The marker itself arrives by wire (1.2 s, it is a short signal). Nothing else moves; a band crossing is a quiet event — the drill does not celebrate depth, it notes it.
- **Choreography timeline:** Break top crosses 80% viewport: 0 ms — marker fade begins. 0–700 ms — rules draw from centre outward. 200–1200 ms — marker + band note fade to full. The rail stamp for the band's depth (§6.2) lands inside the same second — margin and column agree that a band was crossed.
- **State matrix:** Rest: printed break. Hover: none (not interactive). Focus: not focusable. Scrolled-past: rules remain drawn. Disabled: n/a. Touch: identical.
- **Content-merge:** The band note is content — a real sentence about that depth from the record — and the marker's depth is the same number the rail stamps. A break without a note is forbidden (§10): an empty divider is decoration, and decoration is what this theme refuses.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — as specified, marker may extend into the right margin. 768–1199 px — rule spans the column + rail. <768 px — rule spans the screen; band note sets at 1 rem italic; the knockout remains (legibility of the marker over its own rule is the point of the device).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Two breaks close together (short dispatches near a band) → minimum separation is one dispatch; if content places two bands within one viewport, the second break's draw is instant (the wind does not perform twice in one glance). Band note missing from the record → build fails (§10), never renders an empty break.
- **Failure fallback:** Static printed break — rule as a border, marker and note as text. The draw is JS-armed over a complete printed rule (the rule ships full-width; JS resets it to centre-collapsed only when armed).
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Break prints complete; no draw, no fade.
- **Accessibility:** The break is a `<hr>` with an `aria-label` ("Depth band: 1,500 metres") plus the visible marker and note as real text in the flow — the note is content and is read as content. The drawn rules are decorative.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Two 1 px pseudo-element lines, one transform each. Negligible. Mobile identical in kind.
- **Tokens:** `--ice-deep`, `--generator`, `--pencil` (note's dateline-style prefix), `--ease-haul`, `--ease-wire`.

### 6.15 Send word down the wire — the message form

- **What & why:** The conversion section, in the theme's own terms: a form for sending a message to the team — a school asking a question, a museum booking the exhibition talk, a reader writing to the log. Submission is a transmission: the form sends the message *down* the wire, and the confirmation is a stamp and a wire receipt, with the honest notice that replies come when the weather and the satellite allow. This is the worked conversion pattern for any site in this theme (§8, example C builds it fully): the ask is real, and the theme's delay grammar turns the usual instant-success fiction into something truer and, handled plainly, more trustworthy.
- **Structure:** A form on ice ground inside a stamped border (the message is a dispatch now): fields NAME, FROM (email), MESSAGE (textarea, "write it like a dispatch — plainly"), and a SEND DOWN THE WIRE button (§11). Above the form, one mono line sets expectations in the §12 voice: `MESSAGES GO DOWN ON THE NEXT WEATHER WINDOW. REPLIES COME UP THE SAME WAY.` On submit, the form does not vanish: it is stamped (§6.7's press, larger) `SENT DOWN THE WIRE`, the fields freeze as printed text (the message joins the log), and a receipt line prints the transmission delay for the *current page depth* — a reader writing from the lake chapter waits longest, and the form says so.
- **Motion spec:** Submit → button state `TRANSMITTING` with a wire-static shimmer on its surface (the §6.3 speckle at button scale) for the simulated transmission: duration = the delay function at the reader's current scroll depth (2.0–6.0 s) capped at 3.0 s for a form (a form is a shallower thing than a dispatch; the cap is specified and the receipt prints the true uncapped figure with the note `FORM DELAY CAPPED — WIRE DELAY WOULD BE +{x}s`). Then the stamp press (420 ms `stamp`), fields freezing (opacity to 0.85, inputs replaced by their printed values over 300 ms `wire`), receipt line arriving by wire fade (1.2 s).
- **Choreography timeline:** 0 ms — submit, validation first (errors per §11/§12, instantly, in ink — errors never travel). 100 ms — TRANSMITTING state, shimmer begins. 100–3100 ms — transmission window (progress is shown as the button's cable underline filling, §6.10's device — never a percentage). 3100 ms — stamp press begins. 3100–3520 ms — stamp lands: SENT DOWN THE WIRE, rotated −3°. 3400–3700 ms — fields freeze to print. 3700–4900 ms — receipt line fades in: `RCVD AT SURFACE RELAY +3.0s · MSG № 88`. 4900 ms — rest; a pencil line offers `SEND ANOTHER` as a text link.
- **State matrix:** Rest: empty form, expectations line printed. Focus (field): border `--crevasse-deep` 2 px, label stays above (labels never float away in the cold — §11). Hover (button): warms 2% per §6.7's hand-warmth rule. Active: transmission timeline. Error: field border `--tent-deep`, message in ink beneath the field in the §12 voice (`THE WIRE NEEDS AN ADDRESS — AN EMAIL, SO THEY CAN WRITE BACK`), focus moves to the first error on submit. Success: the frozen, stamped form (above) — the success state is a document, not a toast. Disabled (during transmission): fields inert, button reads TRANSMITTING; a second submit is impossible. Touch: 48 px fields, full-width button.
- **Content-merge:** The form's fiction and its function are the same act — sending — and its receipt number derives from the count of messages the log prints (§6.17's record carries `messages received: 87`; the confirmation is № 88, and §8 example C shows the arithmetic). The expectations line is honest UX in costume: real expeditions really do answer slowly, and a site using this theme for a real team inherits a truthful pattern, not a gimmick.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — form in the column, stamp lands top-right of the form box. 768–1199 px — same, stamp inline above the receipt line. <768 px — single column fields at 1 rem, button full-width and sticky to the form's foot while the form is on screen (a thumb in a glove should not hunt for the send).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Network failure on the real submit → the stamp does not land; instead a pencil line prints `NOT SENT — THE WIRE IS DOWN. YOUR WORDS ARE KEPT HERE; TRY AGAIN WHEN IT MENDS.` and the message text remains in the (unfrozen) form — the theme never eats a message and never fake-stamps a failure. Validation errors are pre-transmission and instant (above). Reader at the surface (depth 0) → transmission 2.0 s, receipt +2.0 s. Duplicate submit (double-tap in gloves) → the transmission state swallows it; one message, one number.
- **Failure fallback:** The form is a real HTML form with a real action; without JS it submits plainly and the server returns a confirmation page built in this theme (§7's contact pattern) — the wire fiction survives as print. The stamp and shimmer are enhancements over a working form, never the mechanism.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** No shimmer, no press: on submit the button reads TRANSMITTING for the (still honest, still capped) delay with a static cable line, then the stamped state prints instantly. The delay remains because the delay is information; the theatre is what is waived.
- **Accessibility:** Labels are real `<label>`s, always visible. Errors are linked to fields with `aria-describedby` and summarised in an ink block at the form's head on submit (`TWO THINGS NEED MENDING BEFORE THIS CAN GO DOWN`). The transmission state is announced once via `aria-live="polite"` ("Transmitting"), success announced once ("Message sent down the wire, receipt number 88"). Focus moves to the receipt line's heading on success. Contrast: all form text `--generator` on `--ice` 14.1:1; error ink `--tent-deep` at 3.9:1 is used for the border only, with error *text* in `--generator` — colour never carries the error alone.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** The shimmer reuses §6.3's one noise tile; the stamp reuses §6.7's SVG. No new assets. Mobile: sticky button is the only addition.
- **Tokens:** `--generator`, `--ice`, `--crevasse-deep`, `--tent-deep` (error borders, stamp ink), `--tent` (hover warmth), `--pencil` (failure line), `--ease-stamp`, `--ease-wire`, `--ease-haul`.

### 6.16 The white-out transition & awaiting-signal skeleton

- **What & why:** Leaving a page in this theme is going back out into the weather: a brief white-out crosses the screen and the new page resolves out of it, exactly as §6.1's entrance, in reverse and shorter. While a page's content loads, the skeleton is not grey boxes but the log's own waiting state — ruled ice with a mono line, `AWAITING SIGNAL`, and the dateline skeleton of the dispatch to come. Loading, here, is a known condition with a name the team uses; the theme refuses the generic spinner with the same firmness it refuses the marquee.
- **Structure:** Transition: a full-viewport `--ice` layer with the §6.1 particle canvas reused at storm density, plus a frost wash. Skeleton: the dispatch layout (§4) printed in `--ice-deep` rules — dateline bar, headline bars at Archivo widths, prose lines — with the `AWAITING SIGNAL · +{expected}s` mono line where the dateline will be; expected delay is the target page's first-dispatch delay, printed honestly.
- **Motion spec:** Exit: on internal navigation click, density ramps 0→1 over 550 ms `gust`; the new page loads beneath; entrance resolves per §6.1 compressed to 1100 ms total (returning weather is briefer — the reader knows the way now). Skeleton: static rules; the mono line's ellipsis is *not* animated (no blinking); if the wait passes the printed expected delay, the line updates once to `SIGNAL DELAYED — STILL LISTENING` and the skeleton holds. Hard ceiling: 3000 ms — at 3000 ms the skeleton is replaced by whatever content exists, complete or not (content streams into the printed layout; it never waits for a full page to "reveal").
- **Choreography timeline:** Exit: 0 ms click → 0–550 ms white-out closes. Navigation resolves → entrance: 0–400 ms density holds at 1 (the between-pages white), 400–1100 ms thins to ambient as the new masthead resolves (§6.1's order, compressed). Skeleton path (slow load): 0 ms skeleton prints with expected delay → expected point passes → line updates once → content arrival replaces skeleton dispatch-by-dispatch via §6.3 arrivals, never all at once.
- **State matrix:** Rest: n/a (transient element). Active (transition running): page inert beneath the layer, focus held (the layer is `aria-hidden`, focus is moved to the new page's h1 on completion — the one licensed focus move, because the page changed). Interrupted (user hits back mid-transition): transition cancels, density drops instantly — the weather obeys the reader, always. Disabled (JS off): plain navigation, no layer, no skeleton beyond the server's own rendering. Touch: identical.
- **Content-merge:** The skeleton is the log's layout, not an abstract shimmer — its bars sit where the real dateline and prose will sit, at the real measure, so arrival is a filling-in of a known document. The transition's weather is §6.1's own system, reused, so entering and leaving are the same climate.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — full particle transition. 768–1199 px — 60 particles. <768 px — wash-only transition (no canvas): 700 ms total, because small devices and cold batteries are respected; skeleton identical.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** Instant loads (cache) → transition still completes its 1100 ms? No: if the new page is ready before the exit ramp completes (550 ms), the entrance compresses to 500 ms — the weather never detains a ready page for ceremony. Slow load → skeleton path (above) with its 3000 ms ceiling. Prefetch failure → the click behaves as a normal navigation; the layer never traps a failed route (on error, density drops and the browser's own navigation proceeds).
- **Failure fallback:** The transition is an enhancement layered over standard navigation (`<a href>` throughout); any failure — JS, canvas, timing — degrades to an ordinary page load. Watchdog: a 1400 ms ceiling on the layer's life in any state; a layer that outlives it is removed by the watchdog, unconditionally.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** No white-out, no skeleton animation (the skeleton is static anyway): navigation is instant, and slow loads show the printed `AWAITING SIGNAL` layout without weather.
- **Accessibility:** The transition layer is decorative (`aria-hidden`, pointer-events none except during its 550 ms exit where it prevents double-navigation clicks — and keyboard navigation is never blocked: focus remains operable). Route changes are announced by the new page's h1 via the focus move. The skeleton's mono line is real text, so AT users get the same honest expectation.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** Reuses §6.1's canvas and noise tile; adds one layer node. Mobile: wash-only (above) — near-zero cost.
- **Tokens:** `--ice`, `--ice-frost`, `--ice-deep`, `--generator` (skeleton line), `--pencil` (delayed line), `--ease-gust`, `--ease-drift`.

### 6.17 The drill record — the log closes (footer)

- **What & why:** The footer is not a footer. It is the drill record itself, printed in full at the foot of the descent: the one declared source every number on the page traces to, typeset as the log's closing document — final readings, the lake's measured facts, the roster count, the messages count, the scale of the rail — signed off with a stamp. A reader (or a builder, or an auditor) who wants to check any figure on the page finds its source here, on the page. This element is the theme's law (§1) made into furniture: *the log does not embellish* — and here is the log.
- **Structure:** On the finale's `--night` ground (the record is filed under the ice): a mono table, two columns (ITEM / READING), printing: final depth 2,147 m; lake roof 2,147 m; lake dimensions 11.8 × 3.6 km; water column soundings 214 / 268 / 301 m; temperature at surface −58.0 °C, at roof −62.1 °C, rate −0.19 °C per 100 m; signal delay 2.0 s at surface, 6.0 s at roof; days on ice 79; team 9; dispatches in this log 14; messages received 87 (the count §6.15's next message becomes № 88 against); rail scale ≈ 0.9 m per px at 1200 px viewport height. Below the table: colophon links (SURFACE — back to top as a haul-up text link, THE TEAM, SEND WORD), the credit line in Newsreader italic (`Set from the Aurora Basin field log, season 3. No figure on this page appears that is not in the record above.`), and the log's closing stamp: `LOG CLOSED · DAY 79` (§6.7's press, printed, not animated, in the footer).
- **Motion spec:** The record arrives by the longest wire on the page — a 6.0 s `wire` fade, row by row in table order at 120 ms row offsets (the one licensed stagger in the theme: a table being received line by line, as tables actually arrive over slow links). The closing stamp is already pressed when the last row arrives (it does not animate; the log was closed before it was sent). Nothing else moves.
- **Choreography timeline:** Record's heading crosses 78% viewport: 0 ms — first row begins fade. Rows at 120 ms offsets, each 900 ms `wire` — total ≈ 0.9 + 13 × 0.12 ≈ 2.5 s of visible succession inside the 6.0 s envelope (later rows' fades overlap the envelope's tail). Credit line last, at the envelope's end. Stamp: present throughout (printed).
- **State matrix:** Rest: full record printed. Hover: rows warm to `--generator` at 30% (a thumb on a line of the table). Focus (colophon links): standard outline in `--ice` / `--crevasse-pale`. Scrolled-past: unchanged. Disabled: n/a. Touch: identical.
- **Content-merge:** This *is* the content every other element merges with — the cable's length, the counters' functions, the lake's size, the form's receipt number are all readings of this table. In a real build, the record is the data file (a single JSON/object) from which the page renders; the printed table and the runtime values are two printings of one source, which is why the §10 audit can grep them against each other.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** ≥1200 px — two-column table at column width, stamp right. 768–1199 px — table full column width, stamp below. <768 px — table becomes item-over-reading stacked pairs (same content, same order); the credit line and stamp follow; colophon links stack at 48 px.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** A season rolls over (new record) → the table's values change and every dependent element changes with them, because nothing hard-codes a figure elsewhere (§10's grep audit enforces: the strings `2147`, `62.1`, `11.8` may appear in the record and in datelines generated from it, never typed by hand into a component). A reading the team cannot yet give (lake sampled: pending) prints as `— NOT YET —` in pencil: the record admits its gaps in the same ink as its facts.
- **Failure fallback:** Static table, fully printed — the record is the fallback for the whole page, so it itself has no enhancement to lose. Its fade is JS-armed over the printed table.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Record prints in full, no row succession.
- **Accessibility:** A real `<table>` with caption ("The drill record — every figure on this page, from the log"). The closing stamp is decorative beside the real text `LOG CLOSED · DAY 79`. Contrast on `--night`: `--ice` 14.8:1; pencil gaps `--pencil` on `--night` 4.6:1 at 0.875 rem mono — licensed for these short readings.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** One table, ~14 rows; the fade is opacity on rows, composited. Mobile: stacked pairs, same nodes.
- **Tokens:** `--night`, `--ice`, `--pencil`, `--crevasse-pale` (link focus), `--tent-deep` (closing stamp ink on dark: `--tent` at full, 5.2:1 as graphic), `--ease-wire`, `--final-depth`, `--lake-l`, `--lake-w`, `--temp-base`, `--temp-rate`.

### 6.18 Signal lost — the 404

- **What & why:** The error page for a dispatch that never arrived: a ruled, empty dispatch frame with a dateline that reads `DAY — · DEPTH — · NO SIGNAL`, a headline `THIS DISPATCH DID NOT ARRIVE`, and two honest sentences — the wire may be down, or the address was written wrong in the cold — with the way back offered as the flagged route (§6.4's flags, pointing home). The theme's errors are log entries too; a 404 that breaks the fiction to show a cartoon mascot would be the loudest slop on the site.
- **Structure:** The standard dispatch layout (§4) with an empty frame where prose would go: dateline (mono, dashes for the missing readings), headline (Archivo), two sentences (Newsreader), and a route home: three flag links (§6.4) to THE LOG, THE TEAM, SEND WORD, plus the record's coordinate line so the reader can re-orient. A pencil note in the margin: `If you were given this address by hand, check the figures — cold fingers transpose digits.` (the §12 voice, being useful inside the fiction).
- **Motion spec:** Almost none, by specification: the page prints in full on load (a lost signal does not also make you wait — the wire rule is suspended for errors, stated here so builders do not "improve" it). The three flags flutter at idle (§6.4 rates) — the route home is alive; that is the only motion, and it is the point.
- **Choreography timeline:** 0 ms — page printed complete. 600 ms — flags begin idle flutter (they were always there; the reader notices them as they settle). Nothing else, ever.
- **State matrix:** Rest: as printed. Hover/focus (flag links): per §6.4/§6.10. Scrolled: the page is short; no scroll behaviours armed. Disabled: n/a. Touch: flag targets 48 px.
- **Content-merge:** The 404 reuses the log's own furniture — dateline, flags, coordinates, pencil marginalia — so the error is filed *in* the log rather than outside the site. Its one joke (the transposed digits) is also its one piece of genuine help.
- **Breakpoint behaviour:** All breakpoints: the dispatch layout at that size (§4/§6.3); flags inline in the dateline row below 768 px (per §6.4 mobile).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** A mistyped depth URL (e.g. a dispatch slug with a wrong day) → if the log can identify the nearest real dispatch (day off by one), the pencil note says so and links it: `DAY 66 EXISTS. DAY 67 DOES NOT — YET.` Search engines: the page returns a true 404 status; the fiction never soft-404s. The empty frame's height is fixed (one dispatch's measure) so the page does not collapse into a stub.
- **Failure fallback:** This page *is* a fallback; it is static HTML with the flags' flutter as its only enhancement.
- **Reduced-motion variant:** Flags printed static. Nothing else changes.
- **Accessibility:** The h1 is the headline; the missing readings are dashes in text, intelligible to AT ("DAY —" reads as day dash — the dateline also carries a visually-hidden gloss: "no signal"). Links are named for their destinations. Contrast per §2 throughout.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant:** The lightest page on the site: no canvas, no loop beyond the flags' shared flutter. Mobile identical.
- **Tokens:** `--generator`, `--ice`, `--pencil`, `--tent` (flags), `--crevasse-deep` (link focus), `--ease-gust`.

## 8. Worked examples

Three builds, end to end, on the Aurora Basin record (§6.17). Every figure below is the record's; the arithmetic is shown so a builder can check it the way the §10 audit will.

### Example A — The hero: "The round begins" (home, top)

**Content:** Masthead (§6.10): `AURORA BASIN DEEP DRILLING` / `78°35′S 85°16′W · AURORA BASIN · ELEV 2,847 M` / instruments `DEPTH 0 M · TEMP −58.0 °C · DAY —`. Headline (Archivo): `A LETTER FROM UNDER THE ICE`. First dateline: `DAY 34 · 0 M · −58.0 °C`. First dispatch (surface, mundane-adjacent — the arrival): the traverse in, the tent up, the cook's ruling that soup is a food group. A photograph of the tent (§6.9) — the hero's one licensed pool of orange, inside the photograph's own content.

**Scroll timeline:** Load → white-out (§6.1): 0–1200 ms storm, headline resolves ~1400 ms, instruments last at ~2600 ms, storm settles to ambient at 3000 ms. 3000–5000 ms — the first dispatch arrives over its 2.0 s surface delay (§6.3: delay(0) = 2.0 s), dateline leading. The tent photograph begins frost-clearing at the fade's 50% mark and settles as the reader's first scroll carries it toward centre (§6.9: frost 0.85→0.25). The rail shows the 0 M stamp landed and 2,100 m of unstamped cable below it — the whole journey, visible as absence. First flag (§6.4) flutters at the right margin beside the dispatch. By the time the reader has read the arrival entry, every system on the page has introduced itself exactly once: storm, wire, cable, stamp, lamp (first hover), flag, frost. No element is introduced later without being a deepening of one of these.

### Example B — Mid-page: Day 66, the acknowledged dispatch (1,180 m)

**Content:** Dateline `DAY 66 · 1,180 M · −60.1 °C` — check the record's arithmetic: temp(1180) = −58.0 − 0.19 × 11.8 = −58.0 − 2.24 = −60.2… the record's rule rounds the *rate* per completed 100 m stamp (§6.5: temperature steps at stamps): at the 1,100 m stamp, temp = −58.0 − 0.19 × 11 = −60.09 → printed **−60.1 °C**. The dateline prints −60.1 °C because the last stamp passed was 1,100 m — and the masthead agrees, because both read the step function. Headline: `THE GENERATOR, AGAIN` — a mundane dispatch (the mundane clause, §1): the generator's fuel line froze; the entry is about fixing it with a hair dryer and patience, and it is placed between the 1,000 m milestone and the sonar-echo entry precisely so the profound has something ordinary to stand on.

**Scroll timeline:** The 1,100 m rail stamp lands as the reader approaches (§6.2); the pinned stamp re-presses to `1,100 M · −60.1 °C` (§6.11). The dispatch's dateline crosses 78% → arrival begins: delay(1180) = 2.0 + (1180 ÷ 2147) × 4.0 = 2.0 + 2.198 = **4.2 s** on `wire` — static for the first 1.26 s, dateline locking first, prose by 4.2 s. The reader finishes, clicks ACKNOWLEDGE RECEIPT (§6.7): the stamp presses in the margin — `RECEIVED · RCVD +4.2s` — the printed delay identical to the experienced delay, because both are the same function of the same record. In the index (§6.12), this row's tick draws; the header count moves 8 → 9 received. Nothing else on the page reacts. The log notes the receipt and continues downward.

### Example C — Conversion: Send word down the wire (from the lake chapter)

**Content:** The reader has finished the log — lake drawn (§6.8), record read (§6.17) — and reaches §6.15's form, placed after the record as the log's last page: `SEND WORD DOWN THE WIRE`. Expectations line: `MESSAGES GO DOWN ON THE NEXT WEATHER WINDOW. REPLIES COME UP THE SAME WAY.` The reader writes as a schoolteacher from Invercargill (the worked example's persona): name, email, a four-line message asking whether the team would speak to her class by satellite.

**Scroll/submit timeline:** The form is at the foot of the finale, so the reader's current depth is 2,147 m: the true wire delay would be delay(2147) = 6.0 s; the form's cap applies (§6.15): transmission runs 3.0 s, and the receipt will print `+3.0s · WIRE DELAY WOULD BE +6.0s — FORM DELAY CAPPED`, because the theme does not hide its own mercies. Submit → validation passes → TRANSMITTING with the cable underline filling over 3000 ms → stamp press (420 ms): `SENT DOWN THE WIRE` → fields freeze to print → receipt line arrives: `RCVD AT SURFACE RELAY +3.0s · MSG № 88` — 88 because the record (§6.17) prints `MESSAGES RECEIVED 87`, and 87 + 1 = 88, on this page and in the record's next printing. The teacher's message is now, in the fiction and in the site's actual inbox, a dispatch with a number. If her email were malformed, none of this theatre occurs: the error prints instantly in ink before any transmission — `THE WIRE NEEDS AN ADDRESS` — because in this theme, as at the pole, you check the address before you brave the weather.

## 9. Anti-slop — what makes THIS theme look cheap, and the cure

1. **Stock-photo Antarctica.** A heroic iceberg from a library, uncaptioned. The theme dies instantly: its photographs are *plates from the log*, each stamped with day and depth (§6.9). Cure: no image without a day/depth caption; a missing image is a logged plate fault, never a stock substitution.
2. **Orange everywhere.** Tent orange used for buttons, links, highlights and a gradient header — warmth as branding. Cure: the ≤0.5% orange census (§2, §10). Orange is where a human has been; a CTA in orange is a lie about a human.
3. **Instant dispatches with a "loading" shimmer.** The delay faked as a skeleton for 400 ms. Cure: delays are the record's function (2.0–6.0 s), printed in the receipt (§6.7) so the reader can audit them; a delay that is not printed is not run.
4. **Numbers that disagree.** Dateline says −60.1 °C, masthead says −59.8 °C, because two components computed differently. Cure: one record (§6.17), one step function (§6.5), and the §10 grep audit. In this theme, inconsistent arithmetic is not a bug, it is the whole failure.
5. **A log of pure triumph.** Every dispatch a milestone; no generator, no soup. Reads as a brochure within two entries. Cure: the mundane clause (§1) — one mundane entry per three profound, scheduled in the content plan, checked at the gate.
6. **Snow as a screen-saver.** Particles over every section, all the time, denser than the hero's. Cure: snow lives in the hero and the transitions (§6.1/§6.16); below the hero the weather is *in the margins* (flags, frost). A page where it snows on the prose is a Christmas card.
7. **The cable as a progress bar in disguise.** Cable drawn as a smooth percentage with no stamps, no depths, no links. Cure: stamps every 100 m with day-anchored links (§6.2); if the rail does not know the depths, it is a bar wearing a costume.
8. **Typewriter effects on the prose.** Letters arriving one by one — the wire delivers *signals*, resolved as fades with static (§6.3), because dispatches are transmitted as text blocks and typed by the receiving operator into the log. Letter-by-letter typing is a film prop, not a wire. Cure: fades, never keystrokes.
9. **A mascot.** A cartoon penguin anywhere. Cure: absolute. The team's warmth is in the prose voice (§12) and the soup; nothing in this theme is cute.
10. **Glassmorphism / dark-tech styling.** Frosted-glass cards and neon-blue gradients — the generic "polar tech" look. Cure: this theme's frost is *on lenses and plates* (§6.9), its ground is paper-white ice, its dark is generator black in one authored chapter. No glass, no glow.
11. **The lake as a hero image.** A beautiful rendering of the lake used decoratively up-page. Cure: the lake appears once, at the bottom, drawn by sonar from the record's dimensions (§6.8). Its scarcity is its meaning.
12. **Fake urgency.** Countdown timers, "expedition ends soon", popups. Cure: the only clock at the pole is the day count, and it counts *up*, slowly, in mono.

## 10. Quality gate

A build of this theme may not ship until every check passes. Checks marked ◆ are blocking and theme-specific; the rest are the standing rules applied here.

1. ◆ **Figure audit:** grep the build for every number printed in §6.17's record (2147, 62.1, 11.8, 3.6, 79, 87) — each must trace to the record as its source; any hand-typed duplicate in a component fails the build.
2. ◆ **Delay audit:** for three dispatches (surface, mid, lake), the experienced arrival delay and the RECEIVED stamp's printed delay match the function `2.0 + (d ÷ 2147) × 4.0` within 100 ms.
3. ◆ **Orange census:** screenshots at five scroll positions; tent orange ≤ 0.5% of pixels outside photographic content in each.
4. ◆ **Mundane ratio:** the dispatch list contains ≥ 1 mundane entry per 3 profound entries (§1).
5. ◆ **Temperature agreement:** at five scroll positions, masthead TEMP, pinned stamp TEMP and the nearest dateline TEMP are identical.
6. ◆ **Watchdog drill:** with the animation loop forcibly killed, every dispatch is fully readable within 8000 ms of its trigger, the cable shows fully drawn, and the lake panel shows its completed state path (§6.8 ratchet completes on exit).
7. ◆ **No-JS read:** JavaScript disabled — the entire log reads top to bottom, all photographs visible, the record complete, the form submittable.
8. **Reduced-motion read:** with `prefers-reduced-motion`, no storm, no fades, no flutter, no pin; the full text and the printed lake are present.
9. **Loop census:** exactly one rAF loop drives scroll-scrubbed values; exactly two ambient loops exist (snow, flags); no element runs a private scroll listener.
10. **Cursor:** the system cursor everywhere; no custom cursor, no cursor follower (the headlamp follows *content hover*, never replaces the pointer — verified: the pointer is visible and native over every dispatch).
11. **Contrast spot-check:** body 14.1:1; dateline mono 14.1:1; links 4.7:1 at ≥1 rem; no orange text on ice anywhere (grep for the pairing in CSS).
12. **Plate audit:** every photograph has a day/depth caption and log-style alt text; zero stock imagery.
13. **Performance:** §14's budget met on a mid-tier Android over throttled 4G; frame budget held during the white-out and the lake draw.
14. **The stranger test:** a reader who knows nothing of the fiction can state, after one scroll: whose log this is, how deep the drill is now, and what the orange means. If any answer is missing, the theme has decorated instead of merged.

| **Modal** | **Refused.** Cold rooms have doors, not overlays. Replacement: the dispatch grammar — anything important enough to interrupt is written as a short dispatch page or an ink alert in flow. (The one exception in a real build — a cookie/consent notice — is rendered as a surface-station ink alert at the foot, dismissible by stamp.) |
| **Tabs** | **Refused on the log page** (a log is one column, read down). On the lake page, seasons of results use the band-marker grammar (§6.14) as stacked sections instead. |
| **Accordion** | The bearings pattern in log form: a dateline row that opens its dispatch inline. Used only in the archive index (§7) for long seasons; the chevron is a small cable segment that rotates to vertical — opening a section pays out line. |
| **Pagination** | Season navigation as stamps: `← SEASON 2` / `SEASON 4 →` as stamp boxes (§6.7's furniture) at the log's foot. Numbered page lists are refused — the log is continuous; you haul between seasons, you do not jump to page 7. |
| **Progress bar** | **Refused** — replaced by the cable (§6.2) and pinned stamp (§6.11). Inside the form, transmission progress is the cable underline (§6.15). |
| **Spinner** | **Refused** — replaced by AWAITING SIGNAL (§6.16). |
| **Skeleton** | §6.16's ruled-ice layout with the honest expected-delay line. |
| **Tooltip** | Pencil marginalia only, on the stamps and flags (§6.2/§6.4) — a note in the margin, never a dark bubble. |
| **Breadcrumb** | The descent itself is the breadcrumb on the log page; on sub-pages, a mono line: `SURFACE / THE TEAM / AKI TANAKA` — a route, walked. |
| **Footer** | §6.17 — the record. There is no other footer. |
| **Back-to-top** | A text link in the record: `HAUL TO SURFACE ↑` — named for the work it is. |
| **Search** | In the archive index (§7): a single mono input, results printed instantly as index rows. |
| **Audio player** (if the log carries voice dispatches) | A stamp-box play button + a mono elapsed/depth readout + the transcript printed beneath in full (the transcript is the dispatch; the audio is its plate). No waveform decoration that is not the real waveform. |

## 12. Copywriting voice

Two registers, never mixed within a sentence.

**The Log register** (dispatches, headlines, body): terse, dated, factual, first-person plural, written at the end of a shift. Sentences are short because breath is short. Numbers are given exactly and without drama — the drama is in what they mean, and the writer trusts the reader to feel it. Understatement is the house emotion: the lake is reached in the same tone as the generator is fixed.

- ✓ `DAY 66. Fuel line froze again. Fixed with the hair dryer. Drill turning by 14:00.`
- ✓ `We can hear the lake on the sonar now. It sounds like a room.`
- ✗ `An incredible milestone on our amazing journey to unlock the secrets of the deep!`
- ✗ `The team is thrilled to announce…`

**The Instrument register** (datelines, stamps, tables, system lines): mono, caps, abbreviations the log has earned (RCVD, ELEV, PLATE), no punctuation beyond the separator `·`. It never jokes and never apologises; it reports.

- ✓ `RCVD +4.2s · MSG № 88`
- ✓ `AWAITING SIGNAL · +6.0s`
- ✗ `Oops! Something went wrong :(`

**Headline style:** Archivo caps, concrete nouns, the day's actual subject — `THE GENERATOR, AGAIN` / `SOUP, AND WHAT IT COST US` / `THE WATER, AT LAST`. A headline that could run over any expedition's news is rewritten until it could only run over this one.

**Error voice:** plain, in ink, in the Log register, stating the fault and the remedy as a field note: `THE WIRE NEEDS AN ADDRESS — AN EMAIL, SO THEY CAN WRITE BACK.` / `NOT SENT — THE WIRE IS DOWN. YOUR WORDS ARE KEPT HERE.` Errors never blame the reader and never perform cheerfulness.

**Empty-state voice:** an honest log line, never a mascot: `NO DISPATCHES FROM THIS BAND YET — THE DRILL HAS NOT REACHED IT.` / index with nothing received: `0 RECEIVED — THE WIRE IS OPEN.`

**Fixed strings (verbatim, do not paraphrase):** the expectations line (`MESSAGES GO DOWN ON THE NEXT WEATHER WINDOW. REPLIES COME UP THE SAME WAY.`), the credit line (§6.17), the 404 headline (`THIS DISPATCH DID NOT ARRIVE`), and the all-received line (`All received. The wire is quiet.`). These four are the theme's liturgy; a build that rewords them has drifted.

**Numbers in copy:** always exact, always mono, always traceable to the record. `About two kilometres down` is forbidden while `2,147 M` exists. Superlatives are rationed to one per dispatch, and the lake dispatch spends the log's only exclamation mark — this is specified, and §10's stranger test will notice if it leaks.

## 13. Scroll choreography map

The home page, top to bottom — every motion beat in order, with what the visitor sees and feels. (Sub-pages excerpt this map; §7 says which beats each keeps.)

1. **The storm (0 vh, load).** White-out at full density; the masthead resolves out of it over 3 s (§6.1). *Feel: arrival in bad weather; patience asked once, immediately, and rewarded.*
2. **First signal (hero base).** The Day 34 dispatch arrives over 2.0 s (§6.3); the tent photograph clears (§6.9); the first flag flutters (§6.4). The rail shows one stamp and a long empty cable. *Feel: the wire works; the way down is long.*
3. **The descent establishes (100–800 m).** Stamps land every 100 m (§6.2); the pinned stamp takes over depth-telling (§6.11); temperature steps at each stamp (§6.5). Dispatches arrive at 2.4–3.2 s delays — the wire is lengthening, perceptibly. *Feel: a rhythm learned — scroll, stamp, signal.*
4. **First band (500 m).** The sastrugi break draws, the band note prints (§6.14). *Feel: a threshold, quietly marked.*
5. **The mundane middle (800–1,400 m).** Generator, birthday, soup (§1's clause) at delays 3.5–4.6 s; headlamp hover becomes the reader's habit (§6.6); first RECEIVED stamps pressed (§6.7). *Feel: the log becomes company; the reader starts stamping without being asked.*
6. **Second band (1,500 m).** The sastrugi texture fades out beneath this depth (§4); the page's ground goes cleaner, older. Band note: the ice here predates language. *Feel: the world simplifies as it deepens.*
7. **The sonar entry (≈1,900 m).** A dispatch whose prose reports the first echo; no visual yet — the theme withholds the lake's image for its instrument (§6.8). *Feel: approach, by ear.*
8. **LAKE APPROACH band (2,100 m).** The last band marker; the page ground darkens across one viewport into `--night` — the authored dark chapter begins (§2). The pinned stamp inverts. *Feel: going under.*
9. **The lake (2,147 m).** The cable terminates; the pinned draw runs 140 vh: prediction fades as the true sonar contour draws, soundings printing (§6.8); the final dispatch arrives over the full 6.0 s above it. *Feel: the slowest thing on the page is the most important; the reader has been trained for this pace since the hero.*
10. **The record (foot of the page).** The drill record receives itself line by line (§6.17); the closing stamp is already pressed. The index (§6.12) follows on the same dark ground, its received-ticks showing the reader their own shift. *Feel: the log closes; the reader's receipts are part of it.*
11. **Send word (after the record).** §6.15's form, the log's last page — offered only to a reader who has come all the way down. *Feel: writing back is earned by reading; the conversion is the correspondence.*

**Reverse pass:** scrolling up, the cable hauls in, stamps un-land, temperatures retrace (§6.2/§6.5); arrived dispatches stay arrived, the lake stays drawn, stamps stay stamped — the way back up is faster and already-known, like a real haul. **Jump scroll** ( scrollbar drag, anchor link): all scrubbed values take their jumped-to state in one frame; the lake ratchet and arrival completions apply their catch-up rules (§6.3/§6.8); no animation queues ever form.

## 14. Whole-page performance budget

| Budget line | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Total page weight (home, first load) | ≤ 640 KB | Fonts subsetted (Archivo 600/700/800, Newsreader 400 + italic, Plex Mono 500/700) ≈ 150 KB; images ≤ 380 KB; CSS+JS ≤ 110 KB |
| Largest single image | ≤ 160 KB | The tent hero plate; `srcset` steps at 480/800/1200/1600 |
| Animation frame budget | ≤ 1.6 ms/frame main-thread | Shared loop total: scroll lerp + cable + counters + frost + flags + snow (hero only) |
| Long tasks | None > 50 ms after load | Arrival fades are compositor opacity; static speckle is a pre-rendered tile |
| Snow canvas | ≤ 120 particles (44 mobile) | §6.1; removed from render tree off-hero |
| Concurrent compositing layers | ≤ 6 | Two arriving dispatches, one lamp, frost overlays counted per figure ≤ 6 figures/page |
| Layout shift | CLS 0.00 | Frames, stamps and the record table all reserve their boxes in the DOM's base state |
| Time to readable | First dispatch legible ≤ 3.5 s on throttled 4G | The storm caps at 0.7 density after 800 ms (§6.1) — weather never gates reading |

**Cut order when the budget is exceeded** (cut in this order, never skip ahead): 1) snow particle count halves, then halves again; 2) frost noise tile drops from 128 px to 64 px (grain coarsens, contract holds); 3) the sonar echo line (§6.8) is dropped on all sizes, not just mobile; 4) flag flutter reduces to gusts only; 5) photograph `srcset` top step removed. **Never cut:** the watchdogs, the fallback timers, the reduced-motion variants, the record table, or any caption. A build that cuts a fallback to afford a particle has inverted the theme's values, and §10 will catch it at the drill.

## 15. Build-order checklist

Build in this order; each step is verifiable before the next begins. The gate (§10) is signed by someone who did not build the page.

1. **The record first.** Encode §6.17's drill record as the single data source (depths, temperatures, delays, days, lake, roster, counts). No markup exists yet; the numbers must be right before they are beautiful.
2. **The printed page.** Build the complete static log — masthead, all dispatches, cable fully drawn, all stamps printed, lake fully traced, record table, form — with no JavaScript. Verify: the no-JS read (§10 check 7) passes *now*, before any animation exists.
3. **Typography and palette tokens.** §2/§3 as CSS custom properties with the exact names §6 cites; contrast spot-checks (§10 check 11) against the token pairs.
4. **The shared loop.** One rAF loop, lerp 0.10, inputs: scroll, pointer, clock. Nothing animates before this exists; nothing animates outside it after.
5. **The cable and stamps** (§6.2), then the pinned stamp (§6.11), then the instrument cluster (§6.5) — in that order, because each reads the previous one's state.
6. **Arrivals.** §6.3's pre-state arming + delay function + the 8000 ms watchdog, tested with the loop killed (§10 check 6) before any other element is armed.
7. **Weather.** White-out (§6.1), gusts and flags (§6.4), headlamp (§6.6), frost frames (§6.9) — enhancements over the printed page, each with its fallback verified by disabling it individually.
8. **Receipts.** §6.7's acknowledgement + session persistence + the index (§6.12) sync.
9. **The lake.** §6.8's prediction base state, scrub, ratchet, jump-scroll completion. Verify the ratchet by scrolling past, back, and jumping.
10. **Bands and breaks** (§6.14), the roster (§6.13), the record's arrival (§6.17).
11. **The form** (§6.15) against a real endpoint: success, failure, and validation paths all walked; the receipt number derived from the record, never incremented in the client alone.
12. **Transitions and skeleton** (§6.16), with the 1400 ms watchdog and the 3000 ms skeleton ceiling drilled.
13. **The 404** (§6.18) and the routed-out checkout link (§7).
14. **Reduced-motion pass.** Every element's variant checked with the media query forced; the page read end to end in that state.
15. **The gate.** §10's fourteen checks, in order, signed by a non-builder. The orange census and figure audit are run last, on the final build, because they audit everything else.
