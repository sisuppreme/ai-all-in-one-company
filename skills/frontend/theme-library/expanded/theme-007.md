# Theme 7 — The Proofreader's Desk (Expanded, v1 + v2)

*Expansion of theme #7 in `themes-001-025.md`, written directly to the full standard: the 10-part structure and per-element build specs of EXPANSION-STANDARD.md, plus every layer of the v2 deepening pass (per-element choreography timelines, state matrices, breakpoint behaviour, edge cases, accessibility specs and tokens; per-theme component inventory, copywriting voice, three worked examples, scroll choreography map, whole-page performance budget, build order). ## 1. Concept & world

The site is a galley proof caught mid-correction, on the desk of a proofreader who is still working. The page the visitor reads is not the finished article — it is the proof of the article, and the proofreader's hand is still on it: red pencil marks in the margins, transposition loops over swapped letters, carets where words will be inserted, stet dots where the editor changed their mind. As the visitor reads down the sheet, the page *improves itself*: exactly one correction fires per section, a weaker word is struck and a stronger one takes its place, and a red pencil line in the margin connects each correction to the next like a thread of attention. By the footer, the visitor has watched the whole page be edited — which is the product, demonstrated on the page's own sentences rather than described by them.

The fiction is precise and must stay precise. A galley proof is a long single-column sheet pulled from the press before imposition; the proofreader (the *reader*) works it against the copyholder, who reads the manuscript aloud. Marks are made in two hands and historically two colours: the reader's red pencil for marks on the text, the copyholder's blue for queries and tallies. Every mark on this page must be a real proofreader's mark used correctly — a transposition loop really does swap two letters, a caret really does mark an insertion point, *stet* really does mean "let it stand" and cancels a deletion, a delete loop really does remove. The theme's authority comes from this correctness: an editor visiting the page will check the marks the way a sailor checks knots.

**Mood.** Quiet, lamplit, exact. The pleasure of watching a competent person work. Nothing on the page is excited; the corrections are small, certain, and final. The red is used the way a proofreader uses it — sparingly, because a page bleeding red is a page that failed.

**Best-fit sites and audiences.**
- Editing and proofreading services (the native habitat — the page *is* the portfolio).
- Copywriting and brand-voice studios whose pitch is "we make words better".
- Grammar, style and writing-assistant tools — the correction theatre demonstrates the product's core action.
- Book coaches, manuscript assessors, academic editing services, translation/localisation QA.
- A publisher's or journal's author-services page, where the audience (authors) already speaks this language.

**When NOT to use it.**
- Anything whose promise is speed or automation at scale ("10,000 pages a second") — the theme's whole fiction is one careful human hand; high-throughput products contradict it.
- Products with no text at their centre (image tools, logistics, hardware). The marks annotate *copy*; a page with little copy has nothing for the editor to work on.
- Audiences who cannot be assumed to read closely (young children, low-literacy contexts, glance-media landing pages). The theme rewards reading; it punishes skimming by design.
- Legal, medical or compliance documents presented as final — a page that visibly corrects itself must never be the surface on which a binding text is delivered, because the fiction implies the text was recently wrong.
- Sites that need a true dark UI (dashboards, media players). A proof is paper; see the desk-lamp ruling in §2.

**The one law above all others:** every correction on the page must be a *real improvement to the real copy* — a genuinely weaker word replaced by a genuinely stronger one, in a sentence the visitor is actually reading. The moment a correction is fake (a strong word "corrected" to a synonym for theatre), an editor-visitor knows, and the theme collapses into costume.

## 2. Palette

Four inks, all from the source entry, plus derived tones that are physically what happens to those inks on paper (dilution, graphite dust, a second sheet underneath). No fifth hue exists in this theme. If a design seems to need one, the design is wrong.

| Token | Hex | Role | Usage ratio | Pairing & contrast notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | `#FBFAF6` | Galley white — the sheet itself; page background inside the sheet, card surfaces | ~78% of pixels | The ground. Graphite on it ≈ 10.6:1 (AAA). Never used as text on anything. |
| `--graphite` | `#3A3A38` | The manuscript ink — all reading text, headlines, body, data in the text column | ~15% | On `--paper` ≈ 10.6:1, AAA at every size. The only colour body text may ever be. |
| `--red` | `#D0342C` | Proofreader red — every mark the reader makes: strikes, loops, carets, ticks, the margin line, margin notes in the reader's hand | ~4% | On `--paper` ≈ 4.6:1 — AA for normal text, but red is never the *sole* carrier of meaning: every red mark is also a shape (a line through, a loop, a caret), so colour-blind visitors lose nothing. Red text (margin notes) is Kalam at ≥15px, where 4.6:1 passes AA. |
| `--blue` | `#2C4A73` | Copyholder blue — the second hand: queries, tallies, word counts, header apparatus, links | ~3% | On `--paper` ≈ 8.3:1, AAA. Links are blue AND underlined (the copyholder underlines what they query); blue never appears in the text column's reading text except as a link. |
| `--pencil` (derived) | `#8B877B` | Un-inked graphite — crop marks, hairlines' resting state, skeleton bars, the stet word in its struck state at rest | marks only, never text | ≈ 2.9:1 on paper — apparatus and decoration only. Any element a visitor must *read* may not be set in `--pencil`. |

| `--hairline` (derived) | `#E4DFD0` | Rules between galley sections, card edges, the style-sheet card border | hairlines only | 1px only; never thickened into a "border design". |
| `--smudge` (derived) | `rgba(58,58,56,.08)` | Graphite-smudge wash at the sheet's outer edge; hover wash on proof slips | edge/hover only | Always a gradient to transparent within 120px; a flat smudge rectangle is slop (§9). |
| `--red-faded` (derived) | `#D0342C` at 45% | A correction already passed — the strike on a word the visitor has scrolled beyond settles to this | passed marks | Signals "done, no longer live" without removing the mark; the record stays on the proof. |
| `--blue-wash` (derived) | `rgba(44,74,115,.10)` | Copyholder's highlight behind queried phrases; focus wash | transient | Never a background for a whole section. |

**Dark-mode behaviour — the desk-lamp ruling.** There is no dark version of the sheet, because a proof is a physical sheet: at night it sits under a lamp on a dark desk, it does not invert. Under `prefers-color-scheme: dark`, the *desk* changes and the sheet does not: the page surround (outside the sheet) goes to `#232320`, the sheet keeps `--paper` exactly, and a soft lamp vignette (`radial-gradient`, `--smudge` doubled to .16 at the sheet's corners) is laid over the sheet's outer 24px. All four inks keep their values and their contrast. A host product that *requires* an inverted UI must not choose this theme (§1).

**Ratio discipline.** The 78/15/4/3 split is a gate, not a guideline (§10): if red exceeds ~6% of a viewport's pixels, the page reads as *failing* copy, not *improving* copy. Red is spent on marks and nothing else — never on buttons' fills (buttons are graphite outline, §11), never on headings, never on prices.

## 3. Typography

Two typefaces, two hands, never mixed within a role.

- **Newsreader** (variable, opsz axis) — *the manuscript.* Every word that is "the copy": headlines, body, pull-quotes, captions in the text column, form labels (the author wrote them), table text. Newsreader's optical sizes do real work here: display text runs at `opsz 72` (soft, bookish serifs), body at `opsz 18` (sturdier), so the sheet feels typeset rather than web-set.
- **Kalam** (400 and 700) — *the pencil hand.* Every mark that a human made on the proof: margin notes, correction tallies, the "stet — let it stand" note, word counts, crop-mark labels, the style-sheet card's annotations, error notes in forms. Kalam is never used for copy, never for a heading, never for a button label — the hand annotates, it does not publish. Kalam's natural irregularity is the point; do not letterspace it into neatness.

| Role | Font | Size / line-height | Tracking | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display headline (the corrected hero) | Newsreader, opsz 72, 500 | 54px / 1.04 (≥1200px); 40px / 1.08 (768–1199); 32px / 1.12 (<768) | −0.01em | The headline is copy, so it is manuscript, not hand. Its transposed letters are the same font — the error is in the letters, not the typesetting. |
| Section heading | Newsreader, opsz 40, 500 | 32px / 1.15 | −0.005em | Headings are section titles *of the galley* — they get folio numbers (§6.13) like proof sections. |
| Body (the reading text) | Newsreader, opsz 18, 400 | 19px / 1.68 | 0 | 62ch measure (§4). The generous leading is the proof's double-spacing — room for marks between lines is the theme's founding gesture, even where no mark appears. |
| Lead paragraph | Newsreader, opsz 24, 400 | 22px / 1.55 | 0 | First paragraph of the hero galley only. |
| Pull-quote | Newsreader italic, opsz 36 | 27px / 1.3 | 0 | Italic is the manuscript quoting itself; never Kalam. |
| Caption / apparatus in text column | Newsreader, 400 | 14px / 1.5 | 0.01em | Folio lines, "continued overleaf" notes. |
| Margin note (reader's hand) | Kalam 400 | 16px / 1.35 | 0 | Rotated −1.2° (the hand rests at an angle). Red. Max 34 chars per line — a margin note that wraps past 3 lines is an essay; rewrite it. |
| Margin note (copyholder's hand) | Kalam 400 | 15px / 1.35 | 0 | Rotated +0.8° (the second hand leans the other way). Blue. Queries and tallies only. |
| Tally / word count (data) | Kalam 700 | 15px / 1.2 | 0.02em | Counts are the copyholder's arithmetic — hand-written, tabular in behaviour: the tally element is fixed-width per digit count so a counting-up tally never jogs the margin. |
| Style-sheet card rules | Newsreader 500 small caps (`.94em`, letterspaced .04em) for the rule; Kalam for its annotation | 15px / 1.45 | — | The card is a printed house document annotated by hand — the one place the two faces share a surface, rule in print, note in pencil. |
| Button label | Newsreader 500 | 16px / 1 | 0.01em | Buttons are published objects, not marks. The pencil never labels a control. |
| Form input text | Newsreader 400 | 18px / 1.5 | 0 | The visitor's manuscript goes in the manuscript face. |

**Type laws.**
1. A word that will be corrected exists in the DOM twice — struck original and replacement (§6.2) — both in Newsreader at identical size; the correction must never change the line's length by more than the replacement's natural width, and the layout must be built so the swap causes zero reflow of anything below the line (the line box is height-locked; a longer replacement wraps *within* the locked box's reserved second line only in the subhead unfold case, §6.3).
2. Kalam is rationed like red: if a viewport shows more than ~40 Kalam words, the desk has become a scrapbook. Margin notes are sentences, not paragraphs.
3. No third typeface for "data". Counts are Kalam (above). Tabular lining figures inside body text (dates, prices in copy) are Newsreader's own `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`.

## 4. Layout language

**The sheet.** The page is one galley sheet, centred, `--paper`, max-width 1240px at ≥1200px, on a desk surround (`#E9E5D8` light scheme; `#232320` dark scheme per §2). The sheet has a real edge: a 1px `--hairline` border and a 24px soft shadow on the desk side only (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px rgba(58,58,56,.08), 24px 32px 64px -32px rgba(58,58,56,.25)`), so it reads as paper lying on a surface, not a card floating in a void. On <1200px the sheet goes full-bleed (the desk is smaller than the sheet) and the shadow is dropped.

**The three columns.** Inside the sheet, a grid of three tracks at ≥1200px:
- **Left rail, 76px** — the folio rail: section numbers, crop marks, and the top anchor of the margin pencil line (§6.4). Apparatus only; no prose ever.
- **Text column, 62ch (≈ 620px at 19px body)** — the manuscript. All reading happens here.
- **Proof margin, 300px** — where the editor works: margin notes, the pinned style-sheet card (§6.8), tallies, the pencil line's working length. `--margin-tint` background from the sheet's top to the footer, separated from the text column by the red margin rule (§6.4) — a single 1.5px vertical line that is *drawn* by the pencil-line system, not a static border.

At 768–1199px the margin narrows to 200px and the text column to 58ch; margin notes shorten (the build must supply short-form notes, §12). Below 768px the margin collapses *into* the sheet: notes become interlinear slips directly beneath the paragraph they annotate (still Kalam, still red/blue, full width, no rotation), the style-sheet card unpins and sits above its first demonstration, and the pencil line moves to a 3px-wide track at the sheet's left edge (§6.4 breakpoints). Nothing is *removed* on mobile — the desk gets narrower, the proofreader works closer to the text.

**Spacing rhythm.** An 8px base unit, but the *felt* rhythm is typographic: sections are separated by 96px (≥1200) / 64px (<768) of blank sheet — the gap between galleys pinned to the same board — and each section opens with its crop marks (§6.10) and folio number before its heading. Paragraph spacing is 0.9em, deliberately proof-like (double-spaced manuscript lines: body line-height 1.68 is doing the same work).

**Density rules.** One correction per section (§6.2) is also a *layout* law: it caps red ink per viewport and guarantees white space around every mark. A section may be at most ~90 lines of galley; longer content is split into two galleys with a "continued" folio line, never allowed to run into a wall.

**Whitespace as material.** The margin is the theme's whitespace — it is not empty, it is *staffed*. Blank margin is allowed (the editor hasn't reached that paragraph yet), but blank text-column space is not: a gap in the manuscript with no mark, note or crop mark within 200px vertically reads as a layout bug, and the quality gate (§10) checks it.

## 5. Full motion system

Every motion term, in the proofreader's own language. The theme's motion vocabulary has one rule beneath all of it: **things are drawn, struck, swapped or slid — nothing fades in from nowhere and nothing scales up like a balloon.** Ink appears the way ink appears: along a path, at hand speed.

**Named easings (the theme's hand).**
- `--ease-pencil` — `cubic-bezier(.45,.05,.55,.95)`: stroke-drawing motion (loops, carets, strikes, ticks). Near-linear with a soft start and a decisive end — a pencil does not ease out like a UI panel.
- `--ease-settle` — `cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)`: paper and slips moving (proof slip, galley transition, card nudge).
- `--ease-hop` — `cubic-bezier(.3,1.6,.5,1)`: the letter hop-swap only; a slight overshoot because letters *jump* the loop.
- `--ease-count` — `steps(1)` per digit change under a 90ms interval driver: tallies count like a hand writing numerals, in discrete steps, never a smooth odometer roll.

| Motion term | In this theme | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| Scroll-triggered reveals | One correction per section (§6.2) + self-drawing symbols (§6.10) | Trigger: section's correction line crosses 62% viewport height; strike 300ms `--ease-pencil`, replacement settles 260ms `--ease-settle` |
| Parallax | The proof margin drifts slower than the text column — a loose sheet on the desk | Margin translates at 0.94× scroll speed via the shared rAF loop (lerp 0.14); max drift 28px per section, reset per section so it can never accumulate into misalignment |
| Pinned scenes | The style-sheet card (§6.8) pins in the margin while its rules are demonstrated below | `position: sticky` within the margin track; pin range = the demonstration sections' full height |
| Masked line reveals | Pull-quotes uncovered by a sliding proof slip (§6.7) — a physical mask, not a CSS mask on text | Clip-path wipe on the slip, 600ms `--ease-settle` |
| Blur-to-sharp | **Refused.** A proof is never out of focus; blur belongs to photography. Text is either struck, replaced, or standing. (Stated plainly so no builder "fixes" the omission.) | — |
| Animated counters | Word counts and the corrections tally count up in the margin (§6.4, §6.13) | Per-digit steps, 90ms interval, `--ease-count`; total run ≤ 900ms regardless of the number's size |
| Progress indicators | The margin pencil line itself (§6.4) — progress is the editor's drawn line connecting corrections | Scroll-scrubbed, rAF lerp 0.14; plus the header tally "corrections: n" |
| Marquees | **Refused.** Nothing on a proofreader's desk scrolls sideways forever. The footer carries a static "passed for press" line instead (§6.17). | — |
| Ambient loops | Pencil-smudge gradient breathing at the sheet's edge; graphite dust on correction events (§6.9) | Smudge: opacity .06→.10, 7s ease-in-out infinite alternate — the only loop in the theme, and it is at the paper's edge, never under text. Dust: one-shot 500ms per correction, never looping |
| Micro-interactions | Stet-on-hover (§6.6), pencil-tick click (§6.11), proof slips tilting 0.6° like nudged paper on hover | 160–260ms, `--ease-settle` |
| Hover physics | Slips and cards lift 2px with a paper shadow and a 0.6° settle-rotation — paper nudged, not elements "popping" | 220ms `--ease-settle`; rotation origin at the slip's bottom-left (where a thumb would catch it) |
| Page transitions | Fresh-galley whisk (§6.12): old sheet slides up off the desk, fresh sheet slides under | 350ms `--ease-settle` each way, overlapped by 80ms |
| Image treatments | Images are "plates" taped to the proof: a 6px paper border, a tape strip at the top corners (Kalam-caption beneath), and they are *corrected* like text — a plate may carry one red crop mark showing a tighter crop the editor recommends, drawn on entry | Crop-mark draw 400ms `--ease-pencil` at section entry |

**Global motion machinery.**
- **One rAF loop** drives everything scroll-linked (pencil line, margin parallax, folio advance). Lerp factor 0.14. The loop sleeps when the tab is hidden and when no scroll-linked value is within 0.1px of its target.
- **One IntersectionObserver** (threshold 0, `rootMargin: 0px 0px -38% 0px`) fires section events: corrections, symbol draws, tally increments. An element's event fires **once**; scrolling back up never un-fires a correction (a proof does not un-correct itself — §6.2 state matrix).
- **The 1s fallback timer.** A single global timer, started at `DOMContentLoaded`: at 1,000ms, any correction whose trigger has not fired but whose section is at or above the current scroll position is applied instantly (no animation), and any element still in a pre-animation state is forced to its end state. Combined with the no-JS end state (below), this makes it structurally impossible for a visitor to meet half-corrected or missing text.
- **No-JS end state.** With JavaScript disabled, every correction is shown in its *applied* state and the original word appears in the margin in Kalam (the source's standing rule) — the proof reads as a finished, annotated proof. All pre-animation states are set by JS, never by CSS alone.
- **Reduced motion.** `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` → the page loads as the finished proof: corrections applied, originals in the margin, pencil line drawn in full, no loop, no dust, no hop. Nothing is *removed*; the information content is identical (§10 gate checks this).

## 6. Signature elements

Each element below carries the full build spec — what it is, structure, motion, states, content-merge, fallback, reduced-motion variant, performance budget, mobile variant — plus the v2 deepening layers: a millisecond choreography timeline, a full state matrix, breakpoint behaviour, edge cases and failure modes, an accessibility spec, and the exact tokens it consumes.

### 6.1 Self-correcting hero headline

- **What & why.** The hero headline arrives with a deliberate transposition typo in it — two adjacent letters swapped ("Edtiing that reads like a person wrote it"). A beat passes; a red transposition loop draws itself over the two letters; the letters hop-swap into their correct places. It belongs because the headline is the first copy on the page and the product is correction: the visitor's first experience of the site is watching its own first sentence get fixed. The typo must be a *transposition* (the classic proofreader's catch), never a misspelling that makes the brand look careless — the loop mark is what separates "deliberate demonstration" from "error".
- **Structure.** An `<h1 class="galley-headline">` whose transposed word is built as `<span class="tr-word">` containing two `<span class="tr-letter" data-pos>` inline-blocks for the swapped pair; the rest of the headline is plain text. The loop is one inline `<svg class="tr-loop" aria-hidden="true">` absolutely positioned over the pair, its path drawn with `stroke-dashoffset`. Both letter orders exist in the DOM from the start: the accessible name of the headline is the *corrected* text (set via the natural reading order — the spans are in corrected DOM order and only visually offset pre-swap, so screen readers never meet the typo).
- **Motion spec.** Load-triggered sequence (timeline below). Loop draw 400ms `--ease-pencil`; each letter hop 260ms `--ease-hop`, arc peak translateY −6px, horizontal travel = the other letter's measured width; letters swap in the same 260ms window, staggered 40ms. Headline is fully legible throughout — the typo is a reading-speed bump, not a hide.
- **Choreography timeline.** t=0 `DOMContentLoaded` + 50ms: headline at rest with typo visible, crop marks (§6.10) already drawn. t=700ms: the beat ends — loop path begins drawing at the left letter's top. t=1,100ms: loop closes under the right letter. t=1,120ms: left letter hops (peak −6px at t≈1,250ms). t=1,160ms: right letter hops. t=1,380ms: both settled; the margin tally (§6.13) counts its first correction. t=1,400ms: sequence closed; a `data-corrected="true"` flag is set and the sequence can never replay (including SPA back-navigation — session flag).
- **State matrix.** *Rest (pre-trigger):* typo visible, loop undrawn. *Correcting:* loop drawing, then letters mid-hop. *Corrected (terminal):* correct spelling, loop remains drawn at `--red-faded` after 2s (the mark stays on the proof). *Hover:* none — the headline is not interactive; hovering does nothing (a headline that reacts to hover is a toy). *Focus:* the headline is not focusable. *Scrolled-past / return:* corrected state, faded loop. *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* identical — the sequence is time-triggered, not gesture-triggered.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: as specced, 54px headline. 768–1199px: 40px; loop stroke-width scales 2.5px→2px. <768px: 32px; the transposed word is chosen by the build to sit on the headline's first line (a swap mid-wrap is unreadable), and hop distance is measured live so wrapped pairs still swap cleanly.
- **Edge cases.** *Slow font load:* the sequence waits for `document.fonts.ready` OR the 1s fallback, whichever first — measuring letter widths in a fallback font would misplace the loop. *Very long headline:* only one transposition per headline, in the most content-bearing word (never the brand name — the brand is never the typo). *User scrolls during the beat:* the sequence completes anyway (it is 1.4s and above the fold); it is never scroll-cancelled. *Revisit:* corrected state instantly, loop pre-drawn faded.
- **Accessibility.** The typo is visual-only: DOM order and accessible name are the corrected headline at all times, so assistive tech never encounters the error or the animation. The loop SVG is `aria-hidden`. The hop is decorative motion — under reduced motion it is skipped entirely (variant below). Contrast: headline graphite on paper ≈10.6:1 throughout, including mid-hop.
- **Tokens.** `--graphite`, `--red`, `--red-faded`, `--ease-pencil`, `--ease-hop`, `--font-ms` (opsz 72). Stroke: 2.5px, round caps.
- **Content-merge.** The correction happens to the headline's own letters. Remove the animation and the headline is simply correct — the element *is* the content, demonstrating the service on the page's first sentence.
- **Failure fallback.** If JS fails or fonts time out, the headline renders in its corrected DOM order with no loop — a clean, correct headline. The 1s global timer (§5) forces the corrected state if the sequence stalls mid-hop, snapping letters to final positions.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Headline loads already corrected; the loop is present but pre-drawn at `--red-faded` (the record of the catch, without the performance). Margin tally starts at 1.
- **Performance budget.** Zero assets; two transform animations on compositor-only properties; letter widths measured once, cached. **Mobile variant:** identical sequence; hop arc reduced to −4px (smaller type, shorter throw).

### 6.2 One correction per section

- **What & why.** The theme's engine. As each section of the page enters, exactly one weaker word in its copy is corrected: a red strike draws across it, a caret marks the insertion point, and the stronger replacement settles into the line — with the original preserved in the margin in Kalam ("~~reliable~~ → *dependable*"). One per section, never two: the discipline is the realism. A page corrected everywhere at once is a page that was never readable; a proofreader works steadily, and the visitor reads a page that is *becoming* better at reading speed.
- **Structure.** Each correctable word is a `<span class="corr" data-corr>` containing both states in the DOM: `<s class="corr-old">reliable</s>` (the original, styled without native strike styling) and `<span class="corr-new" hidden>dependable</span>`, plus an inline SVG strike line and caret positioned over the word. The margin twin is a `<span class="corr-note">` in the section's margin block, linked by `aria-describedby` from the correction span. The build supplies the pair from content — corrections are authored in the copy, never generated at runtime.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: the correction's line crosses 62% viewport height (observer, §5). Strike draws left-to-right 300ms `--ease-pencil` (an SVG line scaling its dashoffset, 1.8px, sitting at 58% of x-height — where a pencil actually strikes, not the font's default line-through). At strike completion the old word's colour drops to `--pencil`; caret draws 180ms; replacement fades/slides 4px from the caret point over 260ms `--ease-settle` as the old word collapses (its width animates to the replacement's width over the same 260ms, so the line reflows once, gently, inside its locked line box). Margin note fades in at strike completion. Graphite dust fires (§6.9). Tally increments (§6.13).
- **Choreography timeline.** t=0: line crosses trigger. t=0–300ms: strike draws. t=300ms: old word greys; dust puff starts (500ms, §6.9). t=320–500ms: caret draws beneath the insertion point. t=380–640ms: width swap — old collapses, new settles. t=640ms: margin note fully in; tally steps +1. t=2,640ms: strike settles to `--red-faded` (the correction is now history, not news).
- **State matrix.** *Rest (below fold):* original word standing, no strike — the copy reads as the "before" draft, fully grammatical (the original must always be a *plausible* word, never gibberish). *Correcting:* per timeline. *Corrected:* replacement standing, strike faded, original in margin. *Hover on corrected word:* stet-on-hover engages (§6.6) — strike lifts, original darkens. *Focus (keyboard, the correction span is focusable, see Accessibility):* same preview as hover, plus the margin note is outlined. *Scrolled-past:* corrected, strike at `--red-faded`. *Scroll back up:* stays corrected — corrections never reverse. *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* no hover — a tap on the corrected word toggles the §6.6 preview; tap elsewhere dismisses.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: as specced; margin note in the 300px margin. 768–1199px: margin note in the 200px margin, short form. <768px: the note becomes an interlinear slip directly under the paragraph (§4); the strike/caret/swap are unchanged — the correction itself never degrades, only its annotation moves closer.
- **Edge cases.** *Content longer than expected:* if a section has no authored correction, none fires — the builder must never invent a correction to keep the rhythm (the rhythm yields to the copy; a section may pass uncorrected, and the tally simply doesn't increment). *Short sections* (heading + one line): the correction may live in the heading. *User scrolls fast:* corrections in sections already passed are applied instantly by the 1s-fallback logic extended on scroll (any section above the viewport is corrected without animation — the visitor must never scroll up into an uncorrected "before" state that then animates belatedly). *Print:* the print stylesheet shows corrected text with originals in margin notes — a finished proof.
- **Accessibility.** Both states are in the DOM; the correction span is a single focusable unit (`tabindex="0"`, role `note`, `aria-label="Corrected: 'reliable' replaced with 'dependable'"`) so the change is announced as a fact, not performed as a visual trick. The strike is decorative duplication of that label (`aria-hidden` on the SVG). Colour is never the only signal: strike shape + margin note + label all carry it. The width-swap animation moves following text by ≤ the width difference (authoring rule: replacement within ±40% of the original's width) — no paragraph-scale reflow.
- **Tokens.** `--red`, `--red-faded`, `--pencil`, `--graphite`, `--ease-pencil`, `--ease-settle`, `--font-ms`, `--font-hand` (margin note).
- **Content-merge.** The correction is *in* the sentence the visitor is reading, improving the actual copy. The element cannot be lifted off the page — it is the page's text, edited.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → applied state: replacement standing, original in the margin (§5). Observer failure → the 1s timer applies all corrections on or above the fold and each section's correction as it is reached by scroll position check in the rAF loop (the loop carries a positional backup trigger for every observer-registered event in this theme).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Corrections are pre-applied at load with originals in the margin; strikes are drawn statically at `--red-faded`. Nothing animates; no information differs.
- **Performance budget.** Per correction: 3 small SVG path animations + 1 width transition, all compositor/paint-cheap; max 1 correction animating at any moment (observer queue). **Mobile variant:** identical, with the width-swap shortened to 200ms (smaller lines, less travel).

### 6.3 Caret-insertion unfold

- **What & why.** Sometimes the proofreader doesn't replace — they *add*. In the hero subhead, and once per long chapter, a caret appears in a line and new words unfold downward into the text, pushing the line open the way an interlinear insertion crowds a manuscript line. It belongs because insertion is the second fundamental edit (after substitution), and its unfold is the theme's one moment of *growth* — the page gaining words it needed.
- **Structure.** The insertion point is `<span class="ins-point">` in the line; the inserted words live in `<span class="ins-words" hidden>` styled in Newsreader italic (inserted copy is still manuscript — the author accepts it) with a caret SVG (`∧` drawn, not a font glyph) anchored at the insertion point and a fine red rule under the inserted span. The line box reserves nothing in advance: the unfold genuinely pushes following lines down inside a height-locked *section* (the section's min-height is set by JS to its final height at setup, so the page below never jumps — the growth is absorbed by the section's own reserved space).
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: load + 900ms (subhead, sequenced after the hero headline's correction begins) / section entry (chapter instances). Caret blinks twice (2 × 380ms opacity steps — a pencil tapping the spot), then draws itself 200ms `--ease-pencil`; inserted words unfold via max-height 0→measured + opacity, 450ms `--ease-settle`; the caret then moves to sit at the *end* of the inserted span (a proofreader's caret ends where the insertion ends).
- **Choreography timeline.** t=0: trigger. t=0–760ms: two caret blinks at the insertion point. t=760–960ms: caret draws solid. t=960–1,410ms: unfold (max-height + opacity, following lines easing down inside the reserved space). t=1,410–1,610ms: caret slides to the end of the insertion (120ms) and fades to `--red-faded`; red rule under inserted words settles. Total ≈ 1.6s — the slowest single edit on the page, rationed to ≤ 2 instances per page.
- **State matrix.** *Rest:* line reads complete without the insertion (the sentence must parse cleanly pre-insertion — the inserted words deepen, never repair, the sentence). *Unfolding:* per timeline. *Inserted (terminal):* words standing in italic, caret faded at their end, rule at `--red-faded`. *Hover:* hovering the inserted span shows the margin note ("inserted — the sentence needed its reason") — no visual change to the words themselves. *Focus:* the insertion span is focusable as one unit with an aria-label naming it an insertion and reading the inserted text. *Scrolled-past / return:* inserted state. *Touch:* no hover note; the margin/interlinear note is simply present.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: as specced. 768–1199px: same; unfold measured height capped at 3 lines (authoring rule tightens: insertions ≤ 14 words). <768px: the unfold becomes a two-step: caret draws, then the insertion appears with a 200ms fade only (no max-height animation — small screens + moving text = lost reading place; the section-reserve trick still prevents page jump).
- **Edge cases.** *Insertion near a line end:* the build places the caret so the inserted span starts a fresh visual line when unfolded (an interlinear insertion is written *above/between* lines — starting it mid-line at a wrap point is the authentic look, and the spec embraces it). *Very long insertion:* forbidden by authoring rule (> 18 words = rewrite the sentence instead; the gate checks word counts). *Font swap mid-unfold:* heights are re-measured on `document.fonts.ready`; if fonts arrive after the unfold, the section's reserved height is recalculated once and the inserted span re-settles without animation.
- **Accessibility.** Pre-insertion and post-insertion sentences are both complete; the DOM contains the final sentence in natural order (inserted words are in reading position from the start, visually withheld), so screen readers always read the finished sentence once, in order. The caret is `aria-hidden`. The unfold moves text — under reduced motion it does not happen (variant below). Focus is never inside the span while it animates.
- **Tokens.** `--red`, `--red-faded`, `--graphite`, `--ease-pencil`, `--ease-settle`, `--font-ms` italic.
- **Content-merge.** The inserted words are real copy in a real sentence, placed exactly where a caret puts them. The unfold is the sentence growing its missing clause.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → the final sentence stands complete, caret drawn statically faded, no italics distinction lost (italic is in the static HTML). Timer fallback → insertion appears instantly in final state.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Final sentence at load; caret pre-drawn at the insertion's end in `--red-faded`; the margin note explains the insertion as usual.
- **Performance budget.** One max-height/opacity animation per instance, ≤ 2 instances per page; measured heights cached. **Mobile variant:** fade-only (above), 200ms.

### 6.4 Margin pencil-line progress

- **What & why.** A red pencil line is drawn down the proof margin, scrubbed to scroll, and it *connects* the corrections: as the visitor passes each correction, the line reaches it, kinks slightly toward it, and continues. It is the page's progress indicator, but drawn by the editor's hand and routed through the work itself — progress here means "how much of this page has been read *and* proofed", and the line is the physical link between margin and text that a proofreader's eye-line makes. The copyholder's header tally (§6.13) is its numeric twin.
- **Structure.** One inline `<svg class="margin-line" aria-hidden="true">` spanning the margin track's full height (absolutely positioned, `height: 100%` of the sheet's content column), containing a single path: vertical at the margin rule's x-position, with a pre-authored kink (a 14px horizontal jog and return) at each correction's y-position. The path's total length is measured at setup; drawing is `stroke-dashoffset` scrubbed. The vertical segment doubles as the margin rule itself (§4) — before the line is "drawn" to a given depth, the rule below that depth is present at `--hairline` weight (the printed rule), and the red line overdrawing it is the pencil's progress. A small pencil-point dot (3px circle) sits at the line's live end.
- **Motion spec.** Scroll-scrubbed through the shared rAF loop, lerp 0.14: drawn length = (scroll progress through the sheet) × path length, so the pencil point sits level with the visitor's reading position (offset: the point tracks 62% viewport height, the same line the correction triggers use — the pencil arrives exactly when the correction fires). Kinks are part of the path geometry, so they "happen" as the draw passes them with no extra animation. The dot pulses once (scale 1→1.6→1, 240ms) at the moment the line reaches a correction kink.
- **Choreography timeline.** Continuous, not event-based: at scroll position 0 the line is drawn to the hero correction's kink (the hero correction is at t=0 of the page's story — the line starts there, not at zero length, because the editor has already read the headline). Each subsequent kink is reached at the same scroll moment its correction's strike begins (§6.2 t=0). At page bottom the line terminates in a hand-drawn underline flourish (the path's final 60px curve) exactly as the footer sign-off (§6.17) enters.
- **State matrix.** *Rest (top):* drawn to the hero kink. *Scrolling:* drawing/undrawing with scrub — this is the one element that *does* reverse with scroll (a progress line that can't retreat is a lie about position), unlike corrections, which never reverse. *At a correction:* dot pulse. *End of page:* full path drawn, flourish complete. *Hover/focus:* none — pure apparatus, not interactive. *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* identical (scroll is scroll).
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: in the 300px margin at the rule x-position, kinks jog toward the text. 768–1199px: same, in the 200px margin; kink jog shortens to 10px. <768px: the line moves to a 3px track at the sheet's left edge (no room for kinks — the path is straight), corrections' connection is carried by the interlinear notes instead; the dot remains. The progress *information* is identical at every size.
- **Edge cases.** *Path length mis-measure* (fonts late, images loading): length is re-measured on `fonts.ready`, on `load`, and on resize (debounced 200ms); between measurements the offset is clamped so the line can never overshoot the sheet. *Very short page* (one section): the line still draws hero-kink → flourish over the short scroll; the flourish is never skipped. *Horizontal scrollbar / zoom:* the SVG is positioned in document flow of the margin track, so zoom scales it with the sheet — no fixed-position drift.
- **Accessibility.** `aria-hidden` throughout: the line is a visual duplicate of two accessible facts — scroll position (native) and the corrections tally (real text in §6.13's header, `aria-live="off"`, updated in place). Nothing is communicated by the line alone. The dot pulse is motion-only decoration.
- **Tokens.** `--red`, `--red-faded` (passed kinks' segments settle to faded as the line advances past them by > 1 viewport — history again), `--hairline` (the undrawn rule), `--ease-pencil` (dot pulse).
- **Content-merge.** The line's route is *through the corrections in the text* — its kinks are at the corrections' own heights. It is a map of the page's edits, drawn on the page.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → the rule renders as a static `--hairline` rule with the full red line pre-drawn at `--red-faded` (a finished proof's margin line). rAF failure → the line jumps in section-sized steps driven by the observer (degraded but positionally truthful).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** The line is fully drawn from load at `--red-faded`; the dot does not pulse. Progress is still readable from the tally.
- **Performance budget.** One path, one dashoffset write per rAF frame, shared loop (§5); path length ≤ ~40,000px on the longest galley — dashoffset on a single path is paint-cheap. **Mobile variant:** the straight edge track (above); same loop, no kinks to compute.

### 6.5 Stet restoration

- **What & why.** The theme's human beat, carried from the source entry's anti-slop detail into a full element. Late in the page, one word that was struck through earlier in the visitor's reading — or struck within its own section — is *restored*: the editor changed their mind. A dotted line draws beneath the word (the stet dots), the strike fades away, the word returns to full graphite, and the margin note appears in Kalam: "stet — let it stand". It belongs because real proofreading includes reversal; a page where every correction is final feels algorithmic, and one visible change of mind makes every other mark on the page feel *judged* rather than generated.
- **Structure.** A `<span class="stet-word">` whose word initially renders in the corrected-away state (struck, `--pencil`) — its section's §6.2-style correction is presented as already-done when the section enters (this section's "correction" is the striking; the stet is a second beat in the same section, lower down the reading order). The stet dots are an SVG dotted path (5 dots, 2px, spaced 5px) under the word; the margin note is the section's `.corr-note` variant in the reader's hand. DOM reading order: the word itself (final state text), with a role-`note` wrapper whose aria-label tells the whole story ("'originally' was deleted, then restored — stet, let it stand").
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: the word's line crosses 55% viewport height (later than the standard 62% trigger — the visitor must have *lived with* the deletion briefly). Dotted underline draws 350ms `--ease-pencil` (dots appear sequentially, dashoffset on a dotted stroke); strike fades 300ms to 0 as the word's colour returns `--pencil`→`--graphite` over the same 300ms; margin note fades in at t=350ms. A single graphite-dust puff (§6.9) fires at the strike's midpoint — eraser dust this time, not pencil dust (same system, reversed implication, noted in §6.9).
- **Choreography timeline.** t=0: trigger line crossed. t=0–350ms: stet dots draw left to right beneath the word. t=150–450ms: strike fades out; word re-inks to full graphite. t=350ms: margin note "stet — let it stand" fades in (200ms). t=450ms: settled — the word stands, dots at full red, strike gone but *remembered* (a ghost of the strike remains at 8% opacity permanently — a real stet leaves the deleted state visible under the dots; the proof keeps its history). Tally does NOT increment (a restoration is not a new correction; §6.13's tally counts it in a separate "stet" sub-tally shown only in the footer audit, §6.17).
- **State matrix.** *Rest (section entered, pre-stet):* word struck and grey — reads as deleted copy. *Restoring:* per timeline. *Restored (terminal):* word standing in graphite, red dots beneath, 8% ghost strike, margin note present. *Hover:* stet-on-hover (§6.6) does NOT apply to this word (it is already restored; hovering shows its margin note highlighted, nothing else). *Focus:* the word's wrapper is focusable; focus shows the note and the aria-label carries the story. *Scrolled-past / return:* restored state, dots settle to `--red-faded` after the section is passed (like all marks). *Touch:* identical; no hover dependency.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200 / 768–1199: as specced, note in margin. <768px: note becomes the interlinear slip under the paragraph; dots and fade unchanged (dot size holds at 2px — smaller dots vanish on high-density screens).
- **Edge cases.** *Placement discipline:* exactly ONE stet per page, in the final third. A second stet would make the editor look indecisive and the device would stop meaning "judgement" and start meaning "gimmick" — the gate (§10) counts them. *The restored word must matter:* it is always a word with flavour the "correction" would have flattened (the classic case: a vivid vernacular word the editor first normalised, then kept) — restoring a bland word wastes the beat. *If the page is short* (< 4 sections): the stet moves into the last section regardless of the final-third rule; never omitted.
- **Accessibility.** The full delete→restore story is in the wrapper's aria-label; visually, dots + ghost strike + note carry it without colour alone (dots are a shape, the ghost strike is a line). The word's final text is the DOM text at all times — screen readers read the standing word once, correctly, with the note available.
- **Tokens.** `--red`, `--red-faded`, `--pencil`, `--graphite`, `--ease-pencil`, `--font-hand` (note).
- **Content-merge.** The stet happens to a real word in a real sentence, and its margin note explains a real editorial judgement about that word. It is a beat inside the copy, not a decoration near it.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → the word stands restored with dots drawn and note present (the finished proof shows the stet as history). Timer fallback → restored state applied instantly.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Restored state at load; dots static; ghost strike present. The story is fully legible without a single frame of animation.
- **Performance budget.** One dotted-path draw + two colour transitions; negligible. **Mobile variant:** identical except note placement (above).

### 6.6 Stet-on-hover

- **What & why.** Every corrected word keeps its "before" one gesture away: hovering (or focusing, or tapping) a corrected word briefly *lifts* the correction — the strike fades to 15%, the replacement softens, and the original word darkens back into legibility beneath — so the visitor can compare the edit. Releasing re-applies the correction. It belongs because it makes the page's edits *inspectable*, which is what separates an editor's proof from a magic trick: the work can be checked. It is also the theme's only reversible motion, deliberately — reversibility is rationed to inspection, never to the page's own state.
- **Structure.** Operates on §6.2's `.corr` spans (and §6.1's headline pair, once corrected). No extra DOM: the preview is a class (`.corr-preview`) toggled on the span, driving CSS transitions on the strike's opacity, the old word's colour/opacity, and the new word's opacity. On touch, the first tap toggles the class and suppresses the tap's other behaviours on that span; a tap anywhere else (or a second tap) clears it. Only one preview may be open at a time (opening one closes any other).
- **Motion spec.** Enter preview: 180ms `--ease-settle` — strike opacity → .15, old word colour `--pencil`→`--graphite` and its strike remains faintly visible *through* the comparison (the visitor sees the original *as a struck word*, restored in attention, not in fact). New word opacity → .35. Exit: 220ms back to the corrected state. The margin note for that correction gains a 1px red underline while its preview is open (the note and the word light each other).
- **Choreography timeline.** Pointer/focus enter t=0 → t=180ms preview fully open. Hold: static (no loop, no pulse — the visitor is *reading*). Exit t=0 → t=220ms correction re-applied. Keyboard: focus enters = pointer enters; blur = exit. Tap: t=0 toggle; auto-close after 4s if untouched (a forgotten preview must not leave the page looking un-corrected).
- **State matrix.** *Rest:* corrected state (§6.2 terminal). *Hover/focus/tap (preview):* as above. *Preview + scroll:* if the span leaves the viewport while previewed, the preview closes (no off-screen state). *Active (mid-click on the span):* preview holds; click itself does nothing else on a corrected word (clicking is §6.11's tick, which applies to paragraphs, not to correction spans — the two never share a target). *Disabled:* n/a. *The stet word (§6.5):* excluded — its preview is its note highlight only.
- **Breakpoints.** Identical at all sizes; on <768px the tap targets are the constraint: the correction span's touch area is padded to a 32px minimum height via line-height inheritance (the word sits in body text at 19px/1.68 = 32px line box — exactly enough; the build must not reduce body leading below 1.6 or this breaks, §10 gate).
- **Edge cases.** *Rapid hover sweeping across several corrections:* previews debounce 60ms on entry so a sweep doesn't strobe the page; exit is instant-cancel of any pending entry. *Preview during the correction's own animation:* impossible — §6.2's span only gains preview behaviour when its correction completes (`data-corrected`). *Two corrections in one line:* each previews independently; the line's other correction is untouched.
- **Accessibility.** This element exists *for* inspection, so it is fully keyboard-operable: every `.corr` span is focusable in reading order, focus opens the same preview, and the aria-label (§6.2) states both words so the comparison is available without vision at all. The preview changes no DOM content and moves no focus. Contrast in preview: old word at full graphite ≈10.6:1 (it must be *readable* in preview — that is its purpose); new word at .35 opacity is the de-emphasised state and is not required to be read in that moment.
- **Tokens.** `--red` (strike at .15), `--pencil`, `--graphite`, `--ease-settle`.
- **Content-merge.** The preview compares two versions of the same word in the same line — it is an interaction *with the copy*, not with a widget near the copy.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → the comparison is already complete on the page: replacement standing, original in the margin (§5 end state). The hover is an enhancement of a comparison that exists without it.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Preview toggles instantly (no transition); everything else identical.
- **Performance budget.** Opacity/colour transitions on one span; zero layout. **Mobile variant:** tap-toggle as specced; no other change.

### 6.7 Proof-slip pull-quote reveal

- **What & why.** Pull-quotes on a proof desk are covered by a proof slip — the small sheet a reader lays over a passage while checking another. As a quote enters, its slip slides away and comes to rest tucked at the quote's edge, still visible, still part of the furniture. The reveal is a physical uncovering, not a fade: the quote was *there* all along, under paper. It belongs because it is the theme's only masked reveal (§5) and it keeps the desk's fiction intact — things on this desk are hidden by other things on this desk.
- **Structure.** A `<figure class="pullquote">` containing the `<blockquote>` and an absolutely positioned `<div class="proof-slip" aria-hidden="true">`: a `--margin-tint` sheet slightly larger than the quote block, with a Kalam slip-caption at its foot ("checked against copy — R.") and a dog-ear fold (CSS triangle) at its bottom-right. The slip covers the quote exactly (inset −8px) at rest-before-entry. After the reveal it rests translated to the quote's right edge, peeking 26px into the margin (≥1200px) or stacked beneath the quote (<768px).
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: quote's top crosses 70% viewport height. Slip slides right and slightly down (translate(102%, 10px) of its own width) with clip-path inset animating so its leading edge stays crisp, 600ms `--ease-settle`; at rest it settles with a 0.4° rotation. The quote itself never moves or fades — it is uncovered, not animated.
- **Choreography timeline.** t=0: trigger. t=0–600ms: slip travels; the quote is progressively uncovered left-to-right (the reading direction — the visitor can start reading the quote's first words at ~t=200ms while the slip still covers its tail). t=600–680ms: settle rotation and shadow deepen (the slip lands on the desk). t=680ms: done; slip is now furniture.
- **State matrix.** *Rest (below fold):* quote covered by the slip, slip's caption visible — an approaching visitor sees a covered quote and a note, which is itself a small promise. *Revealing:* per timeline. *Revealed (terminal):* quote fully readable, slip tucked at its edge. *Hover on the slip:* it tilts 0.6° (§5 hover physics) — it is paper, it can be nudged; the quote is unaffected. *Focus:* the quote is ordinary text (focusable only if it contains a link, per content). *Scrolled-past / return:* revealed state; never re-covers. *Touch:* same trigger; slip tilt on tap of the slip only.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: slip rests 26px into the margin. 768–1199px: margin is 200px — the slip rests overlapping the quote's right edge by 40% (tucked *behind* the quote's edge visually via z-order, peeking 18px). <768px: no margin — the slip slides *down* instead of right (translateY 104%) and rests as a caption strip beneath the quote, its Kalam caption reading as the quote's source line. The uncover direction on mobile is top-to-bottom, matching the narrower sheet.
- **Edge cases.** *Quote taller than viewport* (long pull-quote on mobile): trigger moves to when the quote's top crosses 85% height, and the slip's travel is capped so its rest position never sits below the quote's own box (it would cover the following paragraph — forbidden: animated objects never cover content, §5 standing rules; the build clamps rest position to the quote's bounds). *Two quotes in one section:* allowed; slips reveal independently; their rest positions must not collide (the second slip rests on the opposite edge if the first took the right — build-time alternation). *No-JS:* slip is not rendered (JS-injected); quote stands bare with its caption as a normal figcaption.
- **Accessibility.** The quote is in the DOM uncovered at all times (the slip is an overlay sibling, `aria-hidden`, `pointer-events: none` except its own hover tilt zone which is non-interactive). Screen readers read the quote once, in place. The slip never traps focus and never covers text for a screen-magnifier user after its reveal completes (its rest position is outside the quote's text box at ≥768px, and below it at <768px).
- **Tokens.** `--margin-tint`, `--paper`, `--red` (slip caption is the reader's hand — Kalam red), `--hairline`, `--ease-settle`, `--font-hand`.
- **Content-merge.** The slip covers *the quote itself* and its caption refers to the quote's checking. It is furniture belonging to this quote, in this position, for this reason.
- **Failure fallback.** Observer/timer fallback: at the 1s global timer, any slip whose quote is at/above the viewport is removed instantly to its rest position (or not rendered). A quote can never be found permanently covered: the slip element is only ever inserted by the same JS that animates it, and its CSS animation end-state is enforced by the timer.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No slip is inserted; the quote stands with its caption as a figcaption in Kalam — the checking note survives, the theatre doesn't.
- **Performance budget.** One transform/clip-path animation per quote, ≤ 3 quotes per page by authoring rule. **Mobile variant:** downward slide (above), 450ms.

### 6.8 Pinned style-sheet card

- **What & why.** Every publishing house has a style sheet — the house rules: serial comma, numerals spelled out under ten, em-dash spacing. On this page the style sheet is a physical card pinned in the margin, and it *stays* pinned while the sections below it demonstrate its rules being applied: the section about the serial comma fires a correction that *is* a serial-comma fix, and the card's corresponding rule ticks itself as demonstrated. It belongs because it turns the margin furniture into the page's syllabus — the visitor learns the house rules by watching them enforced, and the pinned card is the reason the corrections below it feel governed rather than random.
- **Structure.** An `<aside class="style-card">` in the margin track, `position: sticky; top: 96px`, containing: a printed header ("House Style — Sheet № 1", Newsreader small caps), a list of 3–5 rules (`<li data-rule>`), each with a Kalam annotation, and a pin — a drawn brass pin (SVG circle + shadow) at the card's top centre, the one non-paper object on the desk. Below, the demonstration sections each carry `data-demonstrates="rule-id"`; when a demonstration section's §6.2 correction fires, the matching rule's Kalam tick draws (§6.10 machinery) and the rule's annotation updates ("— applied, §3"). The card's pin range is the full span of its demonstration sections.
- **Motion spec.** Pinning is sticky (no JS motion). Rule ticks: 240ms `--ease-pencil` draw when the linked correction completes. Card entrance: the card is present from load (it is furniture, pinned before the visitor arrives — no entrance animation; a card that "arrives" breaks the desk's stillness). When the pin range ends, the card un-pins and scrolls away with the sheet naturally, its final demonstrated rule ticking as it leaves if the last demonstration is the last section.
- **Choreography timeline.** Page load: card pinned at the first demonstration section's approach. Per rule: demonstration section enters → its correction runs (§6.2 timeline) → at correction t=640ms (completion), the card's rule tick draws (240ms) and the annotation crossfades to its "applied" form (150ms). Page end: card unpins at its track's end; no exit animation.
- **State matrix.** *Rest:* pinned, rules listed, ticks absent (rules not yet demonstrated). *Demonstrating:* the currently-demonstrating rule's text darkens to full graphite (others at 85%) — the eye is led between card and text. *Demonstrated:* tick drawn, annotation updated; the rule stays ticked for the rest of the page. *Hover on a rule:* the rule highlights (`--blue-wash`) and, if demonstrated, the page smooth-scrolls nothing — hover never moves the visitor; instead the linked section's margin note pulses once (opacity dip 200ms) to show where the demonstration lives. *Focus:* rules are focusable list items; focus = hover behaviour; Enter on a demonstrated rule scrolls its section into view (the one permitted navigation-by-card, keyboard users need the same link the pulse gives mouse users). *Scrolled-past the pin range:* card gone with the sheet; a compact "House Style ✓ (4/4 applied)" line appears in the footer audit (§6.17) so the record persists. *Touch:* tap a rule = focus behaviour; tap a demonstrated rule twice = scroll to section (first tap highlights, second confirms — no accidental jumps).
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: pinned in the 300px margin, card width 264px. 768–1199px: 200px margin — the card becomes a compact card (rules only, annotations move to tooltips-on-focus / the interlinear position); pin top offset 72px. <768px: **no pin** — the card sits as a normal block above its first demonstration section, and each subsequent demonstration section carries a one-line Kalam reference back ("house rule 2 — see sheet") in its interlinear margin; rule ticks still draw on the card when corrections fire, even off-screen (state stays truthful for when the visitor scrolls back).
- **Edge cases.** *Demonstration section without an authored correction:* forbidden — a rule with no demonstration is dead weight; the build must either author the correction or cut the rule (gate, §10). *More rules than sections:* rules cap at the number of demonstration sections; a house style sheet for this page lists only what this page demonstrates (3–5). *Sticky failure* (overflow ancestor breaks sticky): the card degrades to a static margin block at its first position; ticks still work; nothing overlaps (the margin track reserves the card's height in flow before sticky applies). *Card taller than viewport* (many rules, short screen): cap 5 rules keeps it ≤ ~430px; at viewport heights < 560px the card unpins (media query on height) rather than trapping content off-screen.
- **Accessibility.** The card is a labelled `<aside aria-label="House style sheet">`; its rules are a real list in reading order. Tick states are text, not just marks: each rule's annotation carries the state in words ("applied in section 3" / not yet). The scroll-to-section action is a real button behaviour on the rule item with a visible focus ring (2px `--blue` outline, offset 2px — the copyholder underlines what they query; focus in this theme is always blue, §11). The pinned card never overlaps text at any breakpoint (it lives in the margin track, which is layout space, not overlay).
- **Tokens.** `--paper`, `--margin-tint`, `--hairline`, `--graphite`, `--red` (ticks, reader's hand), `--blue` (focus, copyholder), `--blue-wash`, `--font-ms` small caps, `--font-hand`.
- **Content-merge.** The card's rules are the literal rules the corrections below enforce; each tick is caused by a correction in the text. Remove the text's corrections and the card has nothing to tick — the two are one system.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → static card, all rules shown with their annotations in "to be applied" neutral form, and the demonstration sections show their corrections applied with margin notes referencing the rules — the teaching survives intact.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Card identical; ticks appear instantly (no draw); the margin-note pulse is removed (state shown statically).
- **Performance budget.** Sticky positioning is compositor-native; tick draws reuse §6.10's SVG machinery; zero scroll listeners of its own. **Mobile variant:** unpinned block + interlinear references (above).

### 6.9 Graphite-dust puff

- **What & why.** When a pencil draws a mark, graphite sheds. At the moment a correction's strike completes, a tiny puff of 6–8 graphite particles rises from the mark and disperses — 500ms, then gone. It is the theme's smallest element and its most important piece of *causality*: the marks on this page feel drawn because drawing has a by-product. The stet (§6.5) fires the same puff in reverse implication — eraser dust — using the identical system with warmer-grey particles.
- **Structure.** A single shared `<canvas class="dust" aria-hidden="true">` overlaying the sheet (position absolute over the content column, `pointer-events: none`, z-index above text but below nothing interactive — it never receives events), with one particle pool reused by every correction. Particles: 2–3px irregular rects (canvas-drawn, not images), graphite at 25–45% opacity. Emission point = the strike's midpoint coordinates, converted to canvas space at fire time.
- **Motion spec.** On a correction event: 6–8 particles spawn with upward velocity 12–20px/s, lateral drift ±6px/s, slight rotation; each fades to 0 over 380–500ms (per-particle lifetime, randomised ±60ms so the puff doesn't die in sync). Gravity is *negative* and tiny — dust rises off a pencil line on warm paper, it does not fall like debris. The whole puff is over in ≤ 560ms.
- **Choreography timeline.** Fired at §6.2 t=300ms (strike completion) / §6.5 t=150ms (strike fade midpoint). t=0: spawn. t=0–500ms: rise + fade. t=560ms: pool empty, canvas cleared, rAF subscription for the canvas released until the next event (the canvas costs nothing between corrections).
- **State matrix.** *Idle:* canvas empty, invisible. *Puffing:* per spec. *Hover/focus/active:* none — the dust is not interactive and ignores the pointer entirely. *Scrolled-past:* a correction that was applied instantly (fast-scroll path, §6.2) fires NO dust — dust is a reward for *watching* a correction, and firing it off-screen would spend the budget on nobody. *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* identical (corrections fire the same way).
- **Breakpoints.** Identical at ≥1200 and 768–1199. <768px: particle count drops to 4–5 and rise velocity halves (smaller marks, less room — a full puff on a phone reads as a smudge on the lens).
- **Edge cases.** *No canvas support / canvas context fails:* the element is simply absent — it is the one element in the theme whose total failure is acceptable, because it carries zero information (the spec says so plainly so no builder gold-plates it). *Many corrections close together:* the pool caps at 24 live particles; a fourth simultaneous correction reuses the oldest slots. *Tab hidden mid-puff:* rAF stops; on return, particles are cleared rather than resumed (stale dust is worse than none).
- **Accessibility.** Pure decoration: `aria-hidden`, no information, no focus, no text alternative needed or given. Under reduced motion it never fires (below). It never overlays a control (pointer-events none, and its z-layer is below the header's interactive stratum).
- **Tokens.** `--graphite` at 25–45% (pencil dust) / `#9A8F7D`-adjacent warm grey at 30% (eraser dust — the single place a warm grey appears, justified as a *material*, not a colour), particle size 2–3px.
- **Content-merge.** The dust comes from the mark being drawn, at the mark's own coordinates. It is the correction's physical trace — remove the correction and there is nothing for the dust to come from.
- **Failure fallback.** Absence (above). No fallback content exists or is needed.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Never fires. The canvas is not created.
- **Performance budget.** One small canvas, ≤ 24 particles, rAF only during the 560ms puff window; zero cost at all other times. **Mobile variant:** reduced count/velocity (above).

### 6.10 Self-drawing proof symbols

- **What & why.** The theme's native line-work: transposition loops, carets, delete loops (the "dele" curl), stet dots, crop marks, paragraph marks (¶) — every proof symbol on the page is a drawn SVG path that draws itself when it enters or when its event fires. Never icon fonts, never unicode glyphs in a mismatched face: a ¶ typed in Newsreader looks like a typeset paragraph mark; a *drawn* one looks like the reader's hand. This element is the shared machinery the other elements call (§6.1's loop, §6.2's caret, §6.8's ticks), specced once here so two builders produce the same hand.
- **Structure.** A build-time symbol library: each symbol is an inline `<svg class="mark" viewBox="0 0 24 24">` with one or two `<path>`s, stroke `--red` 2px (1.8px below 768px), round caps/joins, fill none (stet dots: fill `--red`, drawn as 5 circles scaling in sequence — dots are placed, not stroked). Paths are authored with natural hand wobble (±0.4px control-point jitter baked into the path data — a mathematically perfect loop reads as a font glyph, §9). Each symbol's total path length is normalised at build time so draw durations are consistent across sizes (symbols render at 14–28px depending on context).
- **Motion spec.** Draw = `stroke-dashoffset` from full length to 0, `--ease-pencil`. Durations by symbol: caret 180ms, tick 200ms, transposition loop 400ms, delete loop 450ms, ¶ mark 380ms, crop-mark pair 300ms (both arms together), stet dots 350ms (5 × 70ms sequence). Trigger: scroll entry (decorative instances: crop marks at section corners) or the owning element's event (functional instances). Draws are one-shot; a symbol never redraws on re-entry.
- **Choreography timeline.** Per symbol, t=0 at its trigger: dashoffset runs its duration; a 1px "pencil lift" overshoot — the path's final 4% draws 20% faster (a hand accelerating off the paper) — is baked into the easing segment split (two-segment dash animation: 96% of length over 88% of duration, final 4% over 12%). Crop marks at a section's corners stagger: top-left t=0, top-right t=90ms, bottom-left t=180ms, bottom-right t=270ms (proof order — the order a reader checks a sheet's trim).
- **State matrix.** *Rest (below fold):* undrawn — but the symbol's *space* is present (fixed-size box), so drawing never shifts layout. *Drawing:* per timeline. *Drawn (terminal):* full mark; functional marks settle to `--red-faded` when their section is passed (decorative crop marks stay full red — they are the sheet's furniture, not events). *Hover:* symbols are not hoverable individually (their owning elements own hover). *Focus:* not focusable. *Scrolled-past:* drawn state. *Touch:* identical.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200 / 768–1199: identical; stroke 2px. <768px: stroke 1.8px, symbols render at the smaller end of their range, crop-mark stagger compresses to 60ms steps (shorter attention, same order).
- **Edge cases.** *Symbol needed at an unusual size* (a large ¶ as a section ornament): the library caps symbol display at 40px — beyond that the hand-drawn fiction breaks (nobody's pencil draws a 90px tick); larger ornaments must be composed of rules and type instead. *Missing symbol in the library:* the build fails loudly at build time (a referenced symbol with no path is a compile error in this system, never a silent blank — a blank where a mark belongs reads as a bug to every visitor, not just editors). *RTL locales:* transposition loops and carets mirror horizontally (the hand works in the reading direction); stet dots and ticks do not mirror (dots are dots; a tick is a tick).
- **Accessibility.** Every symbol is `aria-hidden`; each symbol's *meaning* is always carried by adjacent text or by its owning element's aria-label (§6.2, §6.5). No symbol is the sole carrier of information anywhere in the theme — this is a hard rule, because a screen reader cannot be handed a drawing.
- **Tokens.** `--red`, `--red-faded`, `--ease-pencil`; stroke widths 2px / 1.8px; the symbol path library itself is the token set (referenced by name: `mark-caret`, `mark-transpose`, `mark-dele`, `mark-stet`, `mark-tick`, `mark-para`, `mark-crop`).
- **Content-merge.** The symbols are drawn *on* the text they annotate — a caret sits in its line, a crop mark at its section's corner. They are the interface marks of the page's own content.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → symbols render fully drawn (the dashoffset start state is JS-set). A symbol whose draw is interrupted by the 1s timer snaps to drawn.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** All symbols static, fully drawn, at their settled colours.
- **Performance budget.** Path draws are single-property animations; ≤ 6 symbols drawing concurrently (observer queue); total SVG weight for the library ≤ 4KB inline. **Mobile variant:** stroke/stagger adjustments (above).

### 6.11 Pencil-tick click

- **What & why.** The copyholder's gesture of verification: clicking a paragraph (or list item, or table row) draws a small red pencil tick in its margin — "checked". The header tally of *checked* items is separate from the corrections tally; this is the visitor's own hand joining the desk. It belongs because it gives the visitor the one interactive verb the fiction offers — not liking, not saving, but *checking against copy* — and on service pages it doubles as a genuine micro-checklist (a services list the visitor ticks as they recognise needs; a requirements list on a briefing form, §6.16).
- **Structure.** Tickable units carry `data-tickable`; the tick is a §6.10 `mark-tick` SVG injected into the unit's margin slot (a reserved 28px gutter inside the unit's box at ≥768px; at <768px the tick lands at the unit's right end, inline). State persists per page in a JS Set (and in `sessionStorage` so a galley transition, §6.12, doesn't uncheck the visitor's work — the copyholder's ticks survive a page whisk because the desk is the same desk).
- **Motion spec.** Click → tick draws 200ms `--ease-pencil`; the unit's text does not change at all (a tick approves, it does not alter). Second click → the tick is *crossed* by a short diagonal stroke (150ms) and fades to `--pencil` at 40% — the copyholder un-checks by crossing, never by erasing (the record remains). Third click → restores the tick (cross fades, tick re-inks to full red, 150ms). Dust: none (a tick is too small to shed — §6.9's discipline).
- **Choreography timeline.** t=0 click: unit's background washes `--blue-wash` for 120ms (the copyholder's attention — blue, because checking is the copyholder's job, not the reader's). t=30–230ms: tick draws. t=230ms: checked-count in the section's margin tally steps +1 (§6.4 machinery, blue hand). Un-check: t=0 click → cross draws t=0–150ms → tally −1 at t=150ms.
- **State matrix.** *Rest:* unticked; unit shows a faint dotted margin slot on hover/focus only (the affordance is discoverable but the resting page is clean). *Hover:* dotted slot outline appears (120ms fade) + the unit's cursor is the normal system pointer (never a custom hand — standing rules). *Focus:* tickable units are buttons in behaviour: focusable, focus ring 2px `--blue`; Enter/Space toggles. *Active (ticked):* tick full red; unit gains a "checked" Kalam micro-note only where the content defines one (e.g., "✓ against copy" is forbidden — words, not glyphs; the note, where used, is the copyholder's phrase for that list, §12). *Crossed (un-ticked):* tick crossed, faded. *Disabled:* a unit may be `data-tickable="locked"` (e.g., a required item pre-ticked by the house) — tick shown, clicks explain via its note ("checked by the house — always included"), never silently dead. *Touch:* tap = click; the dotted slot shows for 1.2s after any tap in the same list (affordance echo).
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200 / 768–1199: margin-slot ticks. <768px: inline end-of-unit ticks; the dotted affordance slot is at the unit's right; minimum touch target: the whole unit is the target (min-height 44px enforced on tickable units — paragraphs are naturally taller; list items get padding to reach 44px).
- **Edge cases.** *Accidental double-click:* toggles twice = net zero with two draws — acceptable and honest (the hand did it twice). *Tick state and the corrections system:* ticks never interact with §6.2 corrections on the same paragraph (a corrected paragraph can also be ticked; the marks coexist — tick in the gutter, correction in the line). *Long lists (30+ items):* a "checked n of m" blue tally pins at the list's head (sticky within the list) — the only place a count of ticks is totalled. *Print:* ticked state prints (ticks at full red, crosses visible) — the visitor's checked proof is a printable artefact.
- **Accessibility.** Tickable units expose `aria-pressed` on a button-role element (or are real `<button>`s where the content allows); the tick SVG is `aria-hidden`; the state change is announced by the aria-pressed flip and, for lists with tallies, the tally text updates with `aria-live="polite"` throttled to once per second. Focus ring is blue 2px (never red — red is the reader's marks, blue is interaction's colour in this theme, §11). Touch targets ≥ 44px.
- **Tokens.** `--red`, `--pencil`, `--blue`, `--blue-wash`, `--ease-pencil`, `--font-hand` (tallies).
- **Content-merge.** The tick lands beside the actual paragraph the visitor checked, in the margin system every other mark uses. On a services list, the ticked set *is* the visitor's brief-in-progress — the interaction produces content (a checked list they can carry into the form, §6.16 pre-fills from it with consent stated in copy).
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → no ticks and no affordance slots; the content reads as a normal page (ticks are pure enhancement). Storage failure (private mode) → ticks work for the session, simply don't persist across the transition.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Tick appears instantly (cross likewise); the blue wash is removed (state shown by the tick alone).
- **Performance budget.** One 200ms path draw per click; state is a Set + one storage write per toggle (debounced). **Mobile variant:** inline ticks + whole-unit targets (above).

### 6.12 Fresh-galley transition + skeleton

- **What & why.** Navigation in a print shop is a paper cycle: the finished proof is whisked off the desk (slid up and away) and a fresh galley slides in under it. Loading states are skeleton galleys — grey-bar manuscript lines with pencil-outline corrections sketched where marks will land. It belongs because route changes are where most themes drop their fiction entirely; here the desk persists across pages, and even *waiting* happens on the desk.
- **Structure.** Transition: a wrapper `.galley-stage` holds the outgoing sheet; on route change the outgoing sheet gets `.whisk-off` (translateY −24px, opacity to 0 at its top edge only — a sheet lifted, not a fade) while the incoming sheet is inserted beneath with `.slide-under` from translateY +18px. In a static multi-page build this is a view-transition (`::view-transition-old/new` with the same keyframes); in an SPA it is class-driven. Skeleton: `.skel-galley` blocks — `--pencil` bars at 12% graphite in Newsreader line-heights (bars sized to the *expected* content's measure: 62ch column, margin block present), with 2–3 pencil-outline (dashed-stroke) correction marks sketched at plausible positions; bars resolve (fade 150ms, staggered 40ms in reading order) as real content lands, per block, not all at once.
- **Motion spec.** Whisk-off 350ms `--ease-settle`; slide-under 350ms `--ease-settle`, overlapped 80ms (the fresh sheet starts arriving before the old has fully left — one motion of the hand). Skeleton resolve: per block 150ms fade + the block's real corrections then run their normal §6.2 sequence when scrolled to (skeleton corrections are *outlines only* — they never pretend to be the real correction animation). Maximum skeleton display before the 1s rule applies: blocks that haven't resolved by 1s show a Kalam margin note "with the copyholder — a moment" (§12 voice) rather than spinning (there are no spinners on this desk).
- **Choreography timeline.** t=0 navigation committed: outgoing sheet begins whisk. t=270ms: incoming sheet begins slide-under (80ms overlap). t=620ms: incoming settled; its hero sequence (§6.1) may begin (it waits for the sheet to land — correcting a moving sheet is impossible, and the fiction knows it). Skeleton path: t=0 blocks shown; each block resolves on its data, staggered ≥ 40ms apart in reading order; t=1,000ms any unresolved block gains the copyholder's note.
- **State matrix.** *Rest:* current sheet settled. *Leaving:* whisk-off. *Arriving:* slide-under, then settled; during arrival the sheet is inert (pointer-events none until t=620ms — a half-landed sheet can't be clicked). *Loading:* skeleton blocks in the incoming sheet's layout (the sheet itself is fully formed — paper first, words second). *Error (route fails):* the outgoing sheet slides back down (350ms) with a margin note in the reader's hand at its head: "that galley didn't come back from the press — try again" (§12) and a real retry link. *Back-navigation:* the previous sheet returns with its state intact — corrections applied, ticks as left (§6.11 persistence), pencil line at its scroll depth (restored after settle, instantly drawn, no scrub animation). *Touch:* identical; swipe gestures are the platform's, never custom.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200 / 768–1199: as specced. <768px: the whisk becomes a simple 220ms crossfade + 8px rise (small sheets, small desk — the full slide reads as jank on a phone viewport), skeleton bars go full-width with the margin block stacked beneath the text bars.
- **Edge cases.** *Instant loads* (cached page): if the incoming content is ready before t=270ms, the transition still completes its shape but compresses to 200ms total — the desk's gesture is kept, the wait isn't manufactured. *Prefetch/preload:* prefetched sheets arrive fully corrected-state-ready; their §6.1 sequence runs on arrival as normal. *Very slow route (> 3s):* skeleton + copyholder note at 1s; at 5s the note gains a working "reload" link — never an infinite skeleton. *Reduced data (`Save-Data`):* skeleton blocks are skipped entirely; content streams into the formed sheet directly.
- **Accessibility.** Route changes move focus to the incoming sheet's headline (standard SPA practice) *after* the settle (t=620ms), announced via the page title; the transition is visual only and never delays focus or content availability for assistive tech (content is in the accessibility tree from insertion, not from settle). Skeleton blocks are `aria-hidden` with a single visually-hidden "loading" status on the stage. The error note is a real heading-level message with a real link, not a toast.
- **Tokens.** `--paper`, `--pencil`, `--margin-tint`, `--hairline`, `--ease-settle`, `--font-ms`, `--font-hand`.
- **Content-merge.** The transition moves *the sheets the content lives on*; the skeleton sketches the incoming page's own layout and correction positions. Neither exists apart from the pages they carry.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS / no view-transitions → plain navigation, instant, with all end states correct (§5). A failed transition mid-flight leaves the incoming sheet fully visible (the end state is the CSS default; animation classes only ever *start* from it via JS).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No whisk, no slide, no skeleton resolve animation: instant swap, skeleton (if shown) resolves instantly per block.
- **Performance budget.** Transforms/opacity only, two sheets max in the stage; skeleton is pure CSS bars (no images). **Mobile variant:** compressed crossfade (above).

### 6.13 The copyholder's header (navigation)

- **What & why.** The page's header is the copyholder's station at the top of the desk: the masthead (the service's name, set as a manuscript title), the navigation (the galley's section list — Contents, Services, Specimens, Rates, Send a manuscript), and the running tally — "corrections: n · words checked: m" in the copyholder's blue hand. It belongs because every proof carries its job ticket at the top: what this is, what's in it, how much work has been done on it. The tally makes the header *live* — it is the one header in any theme that changes as you read, because the work it reports is happening below it.
- **Structure.** A `<header class="copyholder-head">` inside the sheet (never fixed to the viewport — the desk's furniture sits on the sheet; a slim sticky variant exists for long galleys, below): left, the masthead (Newsreader 500, 22px) with a red rule under it (the reader has underlined the title once — a drawn 2px rule, static); centre/right, `<nav>` as a contents list with folio numbers (Newsreader small caps, numbers in Kalam blue); right, the tally block (Kalam blue, two lines: corrections count, words-checked count). The tally's numbers are real: corrections = count of fired §6.2/§6.1 events; words checked = cumulative word count of sections that have entered (computed from the DOM at build/setup, so the header can never claim work that didn't happen).
- **Motion spec.** The header does not animate on load (furniture is already on the desk). Tally increments: per-digit steps (§5 `--ease-count`, 90ms interval) when events fire. The red rule under the masthead draws once, 500ms `--ease-pencil`, at first paint — the reader underlining the job title before starting. Sticky variant: on galleys longer than ~4 viewports, a compact bar (masthead short form + tally only, 44px) condenses in at the sheet's top edge when the full header scrolls away (translateY −100%→0, 240ms `--ease-settle`), and retracts near the footer (the desk's edge — the sign-off, §6.17, is where the job ends, and the tally hands over to it).
- **Choreography timeline.** Load: rule draws t=200–700ms (after the hero headline lands, before its correction beat at §6.1 t=700ms — the desk is set before the work starts). Each correction (§6.2 t=640ms) → tally step begins within 50ms. Words-checked increments when a section's last line crosses 62% viewport height (the copyholder has finished reading it aloud) — batched: the count runs once per section, ≤ 900ms.
- **State matrix.** *Rest:* full header on the sheet. *Reading (scrolled):* full header gone; sticky compact bar present on long pages. *Tally updating:* digits stepping; the tally never counts *down*. *Hover on nav items:* the copyholder underlines — a blue 1.5px line draws under the item (180ms `--ease-pencil`); the current section's item carries a red ¶ mark (§6.10) at its left, drawn when the section becomes current. *Focus:* blue 2px ring (§11); underline draws as on hover. *Active/current:* ¶ mark + graphite full weight. *Near footer:* compact bar retracted; the footer audit (§6.17) shows final tallies. *Touch:* nav items are full 44px targets; the compact bar's tally is text only, not a target.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: three-part header as specced. 768–1199px: tally drops its words-checked line (corrections only) to protect the nav's room. <768px: header stacks — masthead, then nav as a horizontal scroll-free wrap (two rows max, folio numbers dropped), tally as a single line beneath; compact bar shows masthead initial + corrections count only.
- **Edge cases.** *Zero corrections yet* (a page whose first section is below the fold): tally reads "corrections: 0" — an honest zero, never hidden (the job has started; none done yet). *Single-page site* (no sections): tally counts the hero correction only; words-checked counts on load. *The nav's folio numbers must match the sections' real order* — derived from the DOM sections at setup, never hand-written, so the contents can't drift from the galley.
- **Accessibility.** A real `<header>` + `<nav aria-label="Galley contents">` with a list; the current section is `aria-current="location"` on its link (updated by the observer). The tally is plain text, `aria-live="off"` (it changes often; announcing every increment would be noise — a visually-hidden summary in the footer, §6.17, carries the final numbers for AT). Skip-link: "Skip to the manuscript" lands at the first section's heading — styled as a copyholder's slip when focused.
- **Tokens.** `--graphite`, `--red`, `--blue`, `--font-ms`, `--font-hand`, `--ease-pencil`, `--ease-count`, `--ease-settle`, `--hairline`.
- **Content-merge.** The header's contents list *is* the page's sections with their folio numbers, and its tally counts the page's own corrections and words. It is the job ticket for this exact galley.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → header complete and static; tally shows the *final* counts (the finished proof's ticket: total corrections the page contains, total words) — computed in the static build, so the no-JS header is truthful about the whole job.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Rule pre-drawn; tally values update instantly without stepping; compact bar appears/disappears without translation.
- **Performance budget.** Tally updates are text writes on observer events; the compact bar is one transform. **Mobile variant:** stacked header (above).

### 6.14 The front galley (hero composition)

- **What & why.** The hero is the first galley on the desk, composed as a unit: a folio line ("Galley 1 · [service] · for [client's] consideration"), the self-correcting headline (§6.1), the subhead with its caret insertion (§6.3), one lead paragraph, and a single call to action set as a *proof instruction* — the primary button is styled as a stamped instruction slip ("Send the manuscript →") because on a desk, the next step is a routing instruction, not a marketing shout. The composition belongs because a galley's first sheet carries the job's whole promise: title, summary, and where it goes next.
- **Structure.** `<section class="front-galley" data-galley="1">`: folio line (Newsreader caps 13px + Kalam blue job note), `<h1>` (§6.1), subhead `<p class="standfirst">` (§6.3 insertion inside), lead paragraph, CTA row: primary button (graphite outline, Newsreader label, on a `--margin-tint` slip with a drawn red corner tick) + a secondary text link ("See a specimen first", blue, underlined — the copyholder offering the evidence). Crop marks (§6.10) at the section's corners. The margin holds the job note: client, word count of the whole page, deadline — Kalam, blue, the copyholder's intake handwriting.
- **Motion spec.** The composition's sequence is §6.1's timeline with §6.3's subhead insertion chained after it: headline correction completes t≈1,380ms; subhead caret sequence begins t=1,600ms (its own timeline, §6.3, compressed blinks: 2 × 300ms); CTA slip settles in at t=1,200ms (translateY 6px→0, 300ms `--ease-settle` — it is placed on the desk, not revealed); job note in the margin is present from load (handwriting doesn't animate in — it was written before you arrived; only *marks made while you watch* draw).
- **Choreography timeline.** t=0: folio line, lead, job note at rest; headline typo visible. t=200–700ms: header rule draws (§6.13). t=700–1,380ms: headline correction (§6.1). t=1,200ms: CTA slip settles. t=1,600–3,200ms: subhead insertion (§6.3 full timeline). t=3,200ms: hero complete; the margin pencil line (§6.4) is drawn to the hero kink; tally reads corrections: 2 (headline + insertion counts as the second — an insertion is a correction for tally purposes, stated here so builders don't improvise).
- **State matrix.** *Rest/loading:* all copy readable from first paint (the typo is readable; the subhead parses without its insertion, §6.3). *Sequencing:* per timeline. *Complete:* corrected headline, inserted subhead, CTA live. *Hover on CTA:* the slip lifts 2px + its red corner tick redraws (200ms) — the instruction is being picked up. *Focus on CTA:* blue ring + tick redraw. *Active:* slip presses (translateY 1px, shadow shortens 120ms). *Scrolled-past:* hero state persists corrected. *Touch:* CTA target ≥ 48px tall.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: as specced, job note in margin. 768–1199px: job note shortens to two lines (client · words). <768px: folio line wraps; job note becomes an interlinear slip under the lead paragraph; CTA goes full-width (a routing slip spans the sheet) with the secondary link beneath it.
- **Edge cases.** *Long brand names / headlines in translation:* the transposition word is chosen post-layout by the build (first content word of 5–9 letters on line one, §6.1); if no word qualifies, the hero uses a *substitution* correction instead (a weaker adjective struck and replaced in the headline, §6.2 machinery) — the hero must correct *something*, but never at the cost of a broken layout. *No subhead* (minimal hero): the insertion moves to the lead paragraph's second sentence. *CTA destination missing* (a draft build): the slip renders without a link and a build warning — a dead button is worse than an honest slip.
- **Accessibility.** One `<h1>`; the folio line is a `<p>`, not a heading. The sequence never delays content availability: everything is in the accessibility tree from parse. The CTA is a real link/button with a descriptive label ("Send your manuscript for editing" — the visible short label is completed by `aria-label` only if the visible text is shorter; visible text should carry the meaning wherever possible). Job note is real text, readable by AT in margin order (DOM order places it after the lead, matching visual reading order on mobile where it becomes interlinear).
- **Tokens.** `--graphite`, `--red`, `--blue`, `--margin-tint`, `--font-ms`, `--font-hand`, `--ease-settle`, `--ease-pencil`.
- **Content-merge.** Every animated beat in the hero happens to the hero's own words — headline, subhead — and the margin note describes this page's own job. The hero *is* the demonstration.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → corrected headline, complete subhead, CTA present, job note present: a finished front galley. Nothing in the hero depends on JS to be read or used.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Final states at load; CTA slip static; tally starts at 2.
- **Performance budget.** The hero contains no images by default (a plate may be added as §5 image treatment, lazy and below the headline); the full hero sequence is CSS/SVG only and must not delay LCP — headline text is in the first paint. **Mobile variant:** stacked composition (above), sequence timings unchanged.

### 6.15 Proof-slip service cards

- **What & why.** Services (or products, plans, specimens) are presented as proof slips — small sheets laid on the galley, each carrying one offer: its name as a manuscript heading, a short description in copy, a price/term line, and — the theme's touch — one correction per card, fired when the card enters: the card's own description gets edited ("We edit books" → the weaker word struck, the precise one in). A card that has been corrected reads as *worked on*, which for an editing service is the entire pitch, and for any other content it is the house style made visible at the exact point of decision.
- **Structure.** A `<ul class="slip-grid">` of `<li class="proof-slip-card">`: `--paper` surface, 1px `--hairline` edge, a tape strip at the top (a `--margin-tint` band, 18px, slightly rotated −0.5°), heading (Newsreader 500 21px), description (Newsreader 17px/1.55) containing the card's one §6.2 correction span, a term line (Newsreader caps 13px: "per 1,000 words · 7 days"), and a text-link CTA ("Brief this edit", blue underlined). The margin tally does not count card corrections separately — they are §6.2 events and increment the header tally like any other (consistency is the fiction's spine).
- **Motion spec.** Entry: slips are uncovered in a stagger as the grid enters — each slip slides 10px down into place, 320ms `--ease-settle`, stagger 90ms, in reading order (the reader laying slips on the desk one by one). Each card's correction fires on the card's own entry trigger (§6.2 rules). Hover: slip lifts 2px, rotates to 0° (straightened by attention), shadow deepens — 220ms. The tape strip never animates (it was stuck down before you arrived).
- **Choreography timeline.** Grid top crosses 70% viewport: t=0 first slip settles (320ms); t=90ms second; t=180ms third (cap the stagger at 4 — beyond 4, remaining slips are already settled when reached; a long grid must not become a waterfall). Each card's correction: its own §6.2 timeline from its own trigger. Hover tick: on hover of a card, its term line gains a small red tick (§6.10, 200ms) at its left — the reader noting the terms; on leave, the tick fades to `--red-faded` but remains (the reader *did* note them).
- **State matrix.** *Rest (below fold):* slips in place, descriptions in "before" state. *Entering:* settle stagger. *Settled:* corrections fire per card. *Hover:* lift + straighten + term tick. *Focus (card link):* blue ring on the link; card lifts as hover. *Active (link pressed):* slip presses 1px. *Scrolled-past:* corrected, ticks faded. *Disabled (a sold-out/full service):* the card is *struck by the house* — its heading carries a graphite (not red) strike and a Kalam note "fully booked — join the waiting list"; its CTA becomes the waiting-list link. (Graphite, because unavailability is a fact of the manuscript, not an editor's mark.) *Touch:* no hover lift; tap on card body does nothing (only the link acts) — cards are not secretly buttons.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: 3-across grid within the text column + margin stays clear for notes (cards never enter the margin). 768–1199px: 2-across. <768px: 1-across, full text-column width; tape strip stays (it's the card's identity at every size); hover behaviours replaced by the entry state (term tick pre-drawn faded).
- **Edge cases.** *Odd card counts:* the grid never leaves a single orphan card stretched — last row cards keep their natural width, left-aligned (slips on a desk don't stretch). *Long descriptions:* cards in a row equalise height by the grid, but the correction span's line is height-locked per §6.2 so equalisation isn't disturbed when corrections fire at different times. *A card with no natural correction:* the build authors one in the description (every offer description contains one improvable word — if it truly doesn't, the card's correction is a caret insertion of a missing qualifier, §6.3 machinery at card scale, 300ms unfold).
- **Accessibility.** Real list semantics; each card's heading is a heading; the correction inside a description follows §6.2's aria pattern. The whole card is never a link — only its CTA is (link text names the service: "Brief this edit — manuscript assessment"). The disabled/full card's state is text ("fully booked"), not just a strike.
- **Tokens.** `--paper`, `--margin-tint`, `--hairline`, `--graphite`, `--red`, `--red-faded`, `--blue`, `--ease-settle`, `--ease-pencil`, `--font-ms`, `--font-hand`.
- **Content-merge.** Each card's correction edits that card's own description; the term tick marks that card's own terms. The slips are the offers, laid on the page that sells them.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → cards static, corrections applied with originals in each card's own footnote line (a smaller version of the margin note, inside the card — cards carry their own margins when the page margin isn't available to them).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No settle stagger, no lift; corrections pre-applied; term ticks pre-drawn faded.
- **Performance budget.** ≤ 6 cards per grid by authoring rule (more offers = a listing page, §7, which uses rows not slips); transforms only. **Mobile variant:** single column (above).

### 6.16 The manuscript submission form (conversion form)

- **What & why.** The conversion form is a manuscript submission: the visitor sends their document to the desk. Fields are manuscript lines (the visitor writes *on* the galley), labels are the author's own words (Newsreader), and every error is a margin note in the reader's red hand — specific, brief, and kind, because a proofreader's margin is where errors are *helped*, not punished. On submit, the form is stamped: a drawn "RECEIVED — with the reader" mark (§6.10 machinery at stamp scale) lands beside the button, and the confirmation is a routing slip. It belongs because the form is where the theme's fiction meets the visitor's real task — if the desk disappears at the form, the theme was wallpaper.
- **Structure.** A `<form class="ms-form">` on its own galley section: fields — name, email, manuscript type (select styled as a contents list), word count (number), the message/manuscript textarea, file attach (optional, styled as "paperclip the manuscript" — a text button, never a fake dropzone illustration). Each field: label above (Newsreader 500 15px), input as a ruled line (bottom border `--hairline`, focus thickens to blue — §11 inputs), help text as a copyholder's blue Kalam note where needed. Errors: a Kalam red note in the field's margin slot (desktop) or directly beneath (mobile), prefixed with a drawn caret pointing at the fault. The checked-services list from §6.11 may pre-fill the "what do you need" checkboxes — with a visible note saying it did ("from your ticks on the services galley — change anything"), never silently.
- **Motion spec.** Field focus: the ruled line draws a blue underline segment (180ms `--ease-pencil`, left-to-right — the copyholder ruling the line you're writing on). Error arrival: the margin note writes in (opacity + 2px settle, 200ms) with its caret drawing 180ms; the field's line turns red at 1.5px (a state, not an animation). Submit: button press (120ms), then the RECEIVED stamp: a rotated (−6°) outlined mark scales from 1.15→1 with opacity, 240ms `--ease-settle` (a stamp lands; it does not fade politely), accompanied by the form's fields locking (graphite at 70%, inputs disabled) and the routing slip (confirmation panel) sliding under the form (350ms, §6.12 machinery). No spinner: if the send takes > 800ms, a Kalam note appears by the button — "with the copyholder — sending" (§12).
- **Choreography timeline.** Submit t=0: press. t=0–800ms: sending note if needed. On success: t=0 stamp lands (240ms); t=150ms fields lock; t=250–600ms routing slip slides in with the reference line ("Your manuscript is on the desk — reference № 214") and the promised response time in copy. On validation failure (client-side, pre-submit): t=0 the first faulty field's note writes in and focus moves to that field (only on submit attempt — never on blur for a first visit; the desk corrects *submissions*, it doesn't heckle typing); subsequent errors appear as their fields are submitted again or corrected-and-rechecked on change.
- **State matrix.** *Rest:* ruled lines at `--hairline`, labels standing. *Focus:* blue rule draws; help note (if any) full opacity. *Filled:* the field's right end gains a small graphite tick (the copyholder noting receipt — §6.10 tick in graphite, because receipt is a fact, not an edit). *Error:* red line + red Kalam note + caret; the note states the fix ("the address needs its @ — as in you@example.press"), never just the fault. *Error corrected:* note is *struck through* by a hairline and fades (250ms) — the margin keeps the record that there was a fault and it's fixed (the desk's memory again); the line returns to graphite. *Submitting:* button disabled, sending note. *Submitted:* stamped, locked, slip shown. *Disabled (whole form, e.g., submissions closed):* a house note at the form's head in Kalam red — "the desk is closed to new manuscripts until Monday" — and fields disabled with the note as the reason, never a bare grey form.
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: form in the text column, error notes in the margin (the margin earns its keep most here). 768–1199px: notes in the narrowed margin, short form. <768px: notes interlinear beneath fields; the stamp scales to 0.8 and lands beside the button's row; touch targets ≥ 48px; the select becomes a native select (the desk does not fight the platform).
- **Edge cases.** *Long error text:* margin notes cap at 3 lines (§3); if a validation needs more, the note gives the one-line fix and the field's help note carries the detail. *Server errors* (not field-specific): a house note at the form head, red Kalam, with a reference and a retry — the form keeps the visitor's text (never clear a manuscript on failure). *Honeypot/anti-spam:* invisible and silent; the theme's voice never accuses a human of being a robot in the visible copy. *File too large:* error note on the attach field with the limit stated as a desk fact ("the tray holds 10 MB — split the manuscript or send a link").
- **Accessibility.** Native form semantics throughout; labels are `<label>`s; errors are tied by `aria-describedby` and the field gets `aria-invalid`; the error note is text (the caret is decorative). Focus order = reading order; after a failed submit, focus goes to the first error field with its note announced via the live region summary ("Two things to fix — the email address, and the word count"). The stamp and slip are status content: the slip has `role="status"` and contains the reference number as text. Contrast: red notes are Kalam ≥ 15px at 4.6:1 (AA) and always paired with the red field line + caret shape.
- **Tokens.** `--graphite`, `--red`, `--blue`, `--hairline`, `--margin-tint`, `--ease-pencil`, `--ease-settle`, `--font-ms`, `--font-hand`.
- **Content-merge.** The errors annotate the visitor's own entries; the stamp lands on the visitor's own submission; the pre-fill comes from the visitor's own ticks. The form's theatre is entirely about the visitor's manuscript.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → a plain, fully working form (standard submit, server-rendered errors as the same red notes in static HTML). Nothing about the form's *function* is JS-dependent; JS only adds the desk's manners.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Stamp appears instantly (no scale), slip appears without slide, notes without settle; all states and texts identical.
- **Performance budget.** The form adds no libraries; validation is hand-rolled per field (the rules are few and specific). **Mobile variant:** interlinear notes + native select (above).

### 6.17 The passed-for-press footer (sign-off)

- **What & why.** A proof's journey ends with a sign-off: the reader passes the page for press. The footer is that sign-off block: the final tallies (corrections made, one stet, words checked — the job's audit), the "passed for press" line with the reader's drawn signature flourish (the pencil line's final curve, §6.4, ends here), and the ordinary footer furniture (contact, small nav, legal) set as the imprint at a galley's foot. It belongs because it *closes the fiction with its own paperwork* — and it gives the page an ending with weight, instead of the usual footer that simply stops.
- **Structure.** A `<footer class="signoff">` inside the sheet: a hairline rule; the audit line (Kalam, blue — the copyholder's closing arithmetic: "corrections: 14 · stet: 1 · words checked: 3,214"); the pass line (Newsreader small caps: "Passed for press" + the date, set at build/deploy time — a real date, the page's own publication fact); the reader's mark — a drawn flourish (SVG, the §6.4 line's terminal curve echoed at signature scale) beside "— R., reader"; then the imprint row: service name, email (blue link), town, and the legal line in 13px Newsreader. A "back to the top of the galley" link styled as a folio reference ("↑ Galley 1") closes it.
- **Motion spec.** As the footer enters: the audit numbers count up one final time (they have been incrementing all page — here they *settle*: the stepping stops at the true totals, 600ms `--ease-count`), and the signature flourish draws (600ms `--ease-pencil`) exactly as the pencil line (§6.4) completes into it — the two are one continuous gesture visually (the line's end and the flourish share coordinates at ≥1200px). The "Passed for press" line does not animate (a stamp of record, set in type).
- **Choreography timeline.** Footer top crosses 80% viewport: t=0 audit count begins settling. t=0–600ms: flourish draws, synchronised with §6.4's terminal segment. t=600ms: audit at final totals; the compact header bar (§6.13) has already retracted (its job handed over). Nothing else in the footer ever moves again.
- **State matrix.** *Rest (unreached):* footer static, audit showing totals-so-far placeholders ("—") rather than wrong numbers (an audit that guesses is worse than one that waits; the static/no-JS build shows the true final totals, computed at build). *Entering:* per timeline. *Settled (terminal):* full audit, flourish drawn. *Hover:* only on the real links (blue underline draw, §6.13 machinery) and the back-to-top folio link (its ¶ mark draws on hover). *Focus:* blue rings on links. *Scrolled-past:* n/a — it is the end. *Touch:* links ≥ 44px targets (the imprint row wraps with generous line-height to achieve this).
- **Breakpoints.** ≥1200px: audit + pass line on one row, flourish at the right in the margin's foot. 768–1199px: stacked two rows. <768px: stacked; flourish above the pass line (the signature leads on a narrow sheet); audit wraps to two lines, never truncates a number.
- **Edge cases.** *Zero-correction page* (a page where nothing fired — should not happen, §6.2 authoring): the audit honestly reads "corrections: 0" and the pass line still signs — the gate (§10) flags the page, but the footer never fabricates a count. *Dynamic pages* (listings with variable sections): totals are computed from the DOM at settle time, so the audit matches the page actually rendered. *Date:* the pass date is the build/deploy date of the page, stated as such in the build notes — never a live "today" faked at view time (a proof was passed on a specific day; the fiction respects facts).
- **Accessibility.** The audit is real text in reading order — this is where AT users get the totals the visual tally danced around (§6.13). The flourish is `aria-hidden`. Footer nav is a labelled `<nav aria-label="Imprint">`. The back-to-top link's text names its destination ("Back to Galley 1 — the top").
- **Tokens.** `--graphite`, `--blue`, `--red` (flourish), `--hairline`, `--font-ms`, `--font-hand`, `--ease-count`, `--ease-pencil`.
- **Content-merge.** The audit counts this page's own corrections and words; the flourish is the terminal point of this page's own pencil line. The footer is the page's paperwork about itself.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS → static footer with true build-time totals and the flourish pre-drawn. Nothing is missing.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Totals shown statically (build-time values in the HTML, JS does not step them), flourish pre-drawn.
- **Performance budget.** One count-up + one path draw on entry; nothing ongoing. **Mobile variant:** stacked layout (above).

## 7. Page application map

**Home.** The full desk: front galley hero (§6.14), style-sheet card pinned through the services galleys (§6.8), proof-slip cards (§6.15), one pull-quote (§6.7), the stet in the final third (§6.5), sign-off footer (§6.17). Correction budget: 1 (hero transposition) + 1 (insertion) + 1 per section + 1 per card. A home page of 6 sections carries ~10 corrections — at the red-ratio ceiling (§2); do not add a seventh section without removing a correction elsewhere.

**Listing page** (services index, specimen archive, rate table). The galley becomes a *contents-and-entries* sheet: entries are ledger rows (Newsreader, hairline rules) rather than cards; each row may carry ONE small mark — a tick (§6.11) if the visitor is building a brief, or a single correction in the row's description for the first three rows only (a listing corrected everywhere is noise; the first three rows teach the device, the rest are allowed to simply be a list). Pagination is folio navigation: "Galley 2 of 4 →" as a text link with the §6.12 transition between. Filters are the copyholder's queries: a row of blue Kalam-labelled chips ("fiction · under 80k · rush OK") that filter the ledger — the one place blue leads, because filtering is a query, and queries are the copyholder's.

**Detail page** (one service, one specimen, one case study). The deepest galley: the specimen/case-study detail is where the theme shows real before/after work — a full sample paragraph with *three* corrections allowed in it (the one place the one-per-section rule relaxes, because here the corrections are the content being sold, presented inside a ruled "specimen" frame with its own margin). The style-sheet card returns if the detail demonstrates rules; the submission form (§6.16) closes the page.

**About page** (the desk itself). The fiction turns autobiographical: the copy is the reader's own introduction, and the corrections correct *the reader's claims about themselves* ("I am very fast" → struck → "I am careful" — the stet device (§6.5) belongs here at its most meaningful: a boast struck, then a humbler word restored). Team members are presented as hands: "R. — reader", "J. — copyholder", each with their hand's colour and one specimen mark.

**Contact / conversion page.** §6.16's form is the whole page's centre; the surrounding galleys are short: what happens after you send (three numbered steps as a contents list), the desk's hours as a job-ticket block, and the form. Corrections are rationed to one in the steps galley — near a form, the desk quiets down (the visitor is working now; the editor watches).

**Article / journal page.** Long-form: the article is the manuscript, sections are chapters with folio numbers, the style card pins through the middle chapters, pull-quotes use §6.7, and the corrections correct the article's own draft *as a framing device only where the article is about writing* — for general articles, corrections are limited to the standfirst and the closing line (an article about another subject must not pretend its facts were "improved" by the theme; the fiction annotates presentation, never substance).

**404.** The missing galley: a sheet with crop marks and a headline — "This galley isn't on the desk" — one correction (the visitor's likely typo, if the URL suggests one, struck and replaced with the nearest real page's name, linked). The most charming page in the theme costs one section; build it.

**Checkout / payment equivalents.** The theme hands over: payment itself happens on the processor's own sheet (a desk does not take card payments in fiction), but the *brief summary* before handover is a job ticket — items as ledger rows, totals as the copyholder's arithmetic, and the handover button labelled as a routing instruction ("Take this to the press →"). After payment, the return page is §6.16's routing slip at page scale: received, reference, what happens next.

### 8.1 The hero — an editing service's front galley, end to end

H1: "Editing that reads like a person wrote it" — with the transposition in *Editing*: rendered "Edtiing". Standfirst: "We read every line twice — once for sense, once for sound." with a caret insertion after "twice": "— aloud —" (the insertion is real: the studio does read aloud, and it's their differentiator). Lead: one paragraph, 3 lines. CTA slip: "Send the manuscript →" to §6.16's form; secondary link "See a specimen first". Margin job note (blue Kalam): "job: this page · 3,214 words · reader: R."

**Scroll/timing timeline.** t=0 first paint: everything readable, typo standing. t=200–700ms: header rule draws. t=700ms: transposition loop begins over "Ed/ti"; t=1,100ms loop closes; t=1,120–1,380ms letters hop-swap — "Editing". ## 9. Anti-slop — how this theme goes cheap, and the cure

1. **Corrections everywhere.** Every paragraph struck and re-struck until the page looks like a failing student's homework. *Cure:* the one-per-section law (§6.2) and the 6% red ceiling (§2) are gates, not vibes (§10).
2. **Fake corrections.** "Big" struck, "large" inserted — synonym theatre that fools nobody and insults editors. *Cure:* §1's law — every correction must be a genuine improvement (weaker→stronger, numeral→word per house rule, vague→precise), reviewed in the quality gate by a human reading the pairs aloud.
3. **Kalam for everything handwritten-looking.** Kalam body text, Kalam headings, Kalam buttons — the scrapbook collapse. *Cure:* §3's ration (≈40 Kalam words per viewport) and the rule that the hand annotates, never publishes.
4. **Hand-drawn wobble on everything.** Rotated cards, skewed sections, jitter borders — a desk is mostly *straight*; paper is cut square. *Cure:* rotations exist only where a hand placed something (margin notes −1.2°, slips 0.4–0.6°, the stamp −6°); all structure is square and ruled.
5. **The typo that reads as a real typo.** A hero misspelling with no loop drawn, or a loop drawn so late the visitor has already judged the brand careless. *Cure:* the 700ms beat is a ceiling, and the transposition is always in a *content* word, never the brand name (§6.1).
6. **Red as a brand colour.** Red buttons, red headings, red prices — the marks drown and the page screams. *Cure:* red is for marks only (§2 ratio discipline); buttons are graphite slips (§11).
7. **Marks that lie.** A tally that counts corrections that didn't fire; a style card ticking rules never demonstrated; a "words checked" number invented at build time. *Cure:* every number on the page is computed from the DOM or the event stream (§6.13, §6.17) — the gate audits one page by hand.
8. **Animation that strands text.** A correction half-fired leaving a word struck with no replacement (the classic failure). *Cure:* the architecture forbids it — both states in the DOM, end state is the CSS default, 1s timer forces completion (§5). The gate tests with JS killed mid-animation.
9. **The stet used twice.** Two changes of mind = an editor who can't decide. *Cure:* exactly one stet per page (§6.5), counted in the gate.
10. **Generic copy in a specific costume.** Stock marketing sentences ("We deliver excellence") wearing pencil marks. *Cure:* the copy must be written *for* correction — sentences with a genuinely improvable word in them, per §12's voice rules. If the copy can't be corrected, it can't carry this theme.

## 10. Quality gate — minimum bar before this theme may be used in a build

A build using The Proofreader's Desk must pass every item; partial credit does not exist for items marked ◆.

1. ◆ Every correction pair (original → replacement) is a genuine improvement, read aloud and approved by a human editor; zero synonym-theatre pairs.
2. ◆ Exactly one stet per page, in the final third (or final section), restoring a word with flavour.
3. ◆ One correction per section maximum (specimen frames excepted, §7); red ≤ ~6% of pixels in any viewport (checked by screenshot sampling at 3 scroll depths).
4. ◆ With JavaScript disabled, the page is the finished proof: all corrections applied, all originals in margins, all content and the form fully usable.
5. ◆ With JS killed *mid-correction* (throttled CPU, reload during animation), no word is ever left struck without its replacement reachable — the 1s timer and DOM end-states verified by test.
6. ◆ `prefers-reduced-motion` build: identical information, zero animation; corrections pre-applied; pencil line pre-drawn.
7. Both typefaces only in their roles (§3): no Kalam above 17px anywhere; no Kalam buttons, headings or body text.
8. Kalam word count ≤ ~40 in the initial viewport; margin notes ≤ 3 lines each.
9. All tallies and audit numbers computed from the DOM/events; hand-audit of one page matches (corrections, stet, words).
10. Style-sheet card: every rule has a real demonstration; every tick is caused by a real correction (§6.8).
11. The hero correction's typo is a transposition in a content word (never the brand), loop drawn by 1,100ms at the latest on a mid-tier connection.
12. Symbols all come from the §6.10 library (no icon fonts, no unicode mark glyphs); every symbol is `aria-hidden` with its meaning in adjacent text or label.
13. Focus is visible everywhere and always blue (§11); every interactive element reachable and operable by keyboard, including correction previews (§6.6) and tickable units (§6.11).
14. Contrast: body graphite ≈10.6:1; red text only as Kalam ≥15px (≈4.6:1, AA) and never as sole meaning-carrier.
15. Scroll performance: the shared rAF loop holds 60fps on a mid-tier Android over the longest galley; no layout thrash (path lengths and letter widths cached; re-measured only on resize/fonts).
16. Print stylesheet renders the finished proof (corrected text, margin originals, audit) — printed output is a designed artefact, not an accident.

## 11. Component inventory (v2) — every component, in this theme's treatment

The desk has a full component set; nothing falls back to a default UI look. The governing assignments: **print (Newsreader) for anything published, hand (Kalam) for anything annotated, graphite for structure, red for the reader's marks, blue for the copyholder's interactions.** Focus is always a 2px `--blue` outline at 2px offset — interaction belongs to the copyholder throughout the theme.

- **Buttons.** *Primary:* a routing slip — `--margin-tint` fill, 1px graphite border, Newsreader 500 label, a drawn red corner tick at its top-right (the reader has marked this as the way forward); hover lifts 2px with a paper shadow; active presses 1px. *Secondary:* text button, blue, underlined, underline draws on hover. *Destructive:* never red-filled (red is marks); a graphite outline button whose label is struck-and-replaced on hover is forbidden (too clever) — destructive actions are plain graphite with a confirming margin-note pattern in the modal (below). All buttons ≥ 44px tall.
- **Inputs & textareas.** Ruled lines, per §6.16: `--hairline` bottom rule, blue rule draws on focus, graphite tick at the right when validly filled, red line + Kalam note on error. Background transparent (you write on the sheet) except inside cards, where it's `--paper`.
- **Selects.** Native select, styled wrapper: Newsreader text, a drawn caret (§6.10 `mark-caret`, rotated) as the chevron. Custom listboxes are forbidden — the desk doesn't fight the platform (§6.16).
- **Checkboxes & radios.** Hand-drawn boxes: a 16px graphite-ruled square; checked = a red pencil tick drawn inside (§6.10, 200ms when toggled by the visitor; pre-checked states render drawn). Radios: a circle with a graphite dot (the copyholder's dot — selections are facts) — note the deliberate split: *choices the visitor verifies* get the reader's tick; *choices the visitor makes* get the copyholder's dot.
- **Tables.** Ledger tables: Newsreader, hairline row rules, a double rule under the header row (the galley rule), numerals tabular; row hover washes `--blue-wash` at 50%; a table may carry ONE correction in a cell per §6.2 if it's a content table on a themed page (never in a data-critical table — prices and dates are never "corrected" by the theme; they are facts).
- **Badges & tags.** Slips of paper: `--margin-tint`, Newsreader caps 12px, a hairline edge, no radius (paper is square). Status badges get a Kalam annotation instead of a colour dot ("in reading —" in blue hand). Colour-coded badge systems are forbidden; the desk has two inks, and words beat colours.
- **Alerts & notices.** Margin notes promoted to blocks: a `--margin-tint` strip at the content's edge with a drawn mark at its left — caret for information (blue), delete-loop for errors (red), tick for success (red — success on a proof *is* an editorial outcome). Alert text is Newsreader (it's published information) with a one-line Kalam gloss beneath. Alerts never float over content; they sit in the flow like everything on the desk.
- **Pagination.** Folio navigation: "← Galley 1 · Galley 2 of 4 · Galley 3 →" as text links with folio numbers; current galley carries a red ¶. Transitions use §6.12. Numbered page squares are forbidden — galleys are numbered like sheets, not like search results.
- **Modals.** A modal is a *slip laid over the galley*: the sheet beneath dims with `--smudge` at 35% (the desk in shadow, not a black overlay), the slip is `--paper` with crop marks at its corners and a Kalam title-note if the modal is a query ("are you sure? — R." in red hand for destructive confirms, with the confirm button labelled in plain Newsreader words: "Yes, delete the draft"). Focus traps inside the slip; Esc returns the slip (240ms slide-down fade). No modal may contain a correction animation (the visitor is deciding, not reading).
- **Toasts.** There are no toasts. Transient confirmations are *margin notes that appear in the nearest margin and stay until the next one replaces them* (a desk has no disappearing messages — if it mattered, it's written down). A save confirmation reads, in blue Kalam in the margin: "noted — 14:02" with the actual time.
- **Tabs.** Contents tabs: a row of folio-labelled tabs ("The service · Specimens · Rates"), the active tab underlined by a drawn red rule (300ms slide between tabs, `--ease-pencil`), panels swap with the §6.12 slide-under at tab scale (200ms). Tab panels are galleys — they keep their own corrections, which fire when the panel first opens (not before: an unopened galley hasn't been read).
- **Accordions.** Queries-and-answers: each question is a copyholder's query (blue Kalam prefix "Q —"), the answer the manuscript's reply (Newsreader). Opening draws a caret rotation (the §6.10 caret rotates 90°→0°, 200ms) and the answer unfolds with §6.3's unfold (350ms at this scale). One open at a time on service pages (a desk reads one query at a time); multi-open allowed on reference pages.
- **Search.** A query line at the contents' head: ruled input, and results are returned as a *marked-up list* — matched terms carry a blue copyholder underline (drawn on render), and the results count is a blue tally ("7 passages"). No results = an empty state in the copyholder's voice (§12).
- **Breadcrumbs.** Folio trail: "Galley 1 › Services › Manuscript editing" in Newsreader caps with Kalam folio numbers; the current sheet's name is struck by nothing and marked by a red ¶.
- **Progress (task) indicators.** Beyond §6.4: multi-step flows (the submission's steps) are a contents list with ticks — completed steps ticked in red (§6.10), current step marked ¶, future steps plain. Never a percentage bar; the desk counts sheets, not percents.
- **Tooltips.** Margin notes in miniature: a Kalam slip appearing beside the term (150ms settle), max 12 words, dismissed by Esc/blur. A tooltip that needs more words is a margin note in the layout, not a tooltip.
- **Avatars/people.** People are *hands*, not faces (§7 about page): initials in a ruled square, set in the hand's colour, with the role beneath ("R. — reader"). Photographs, where a product truly needs them, are §5 plates (taped, captioned).
- **Dividers.** Hairlines, and at major boundaries the double galley rule (1px + 3px gap + 1px). A section break may carry a centred drawn fleuron-like mark composed from the symbol library (¶ flanked by two carets) — the only ornament the theme permits itself.

## 12. Content & copywriting voice (v2)

The theme lives or dies on its words. Two voices share the page, and they never swap jobs.

**The manuscript voice (Newsreader — all published copy).** Plain, exact, quietly confident. It writes sentences that *contain* a correctable word on purpose in themed sections, and it never winks at the device. Rules:
- Headlines state the outcome in the reader's terms, with one strong word the correction can improve or transpose: "Editing that reads like a person wrote it" (§8.1). Headline length: 4–9 words — a headline is a title line, not a paragraph.
- Body copy prefers the concrete noun and the active verb *after* correction; the "before" text must still be respectable (a plausible first draft by a competent writer — if the before-text is embarrassing, the service looks like it fixes disasters, not polishes work).
- Labels (buttons, nav, form fields) are instructions a desk would give: "Send the manuscript", "See a specimen", "Brief this edit". Never "Submit", never "Learn more", never "Get started".
- Numbers in copy follow the house style the page itself teaches (§6.8): if the style sheet says numerals under ten are words, the copy obeys it everywhere except inside a deliberate demonstration.

**The hand voice (Kalam — all annotation).** Brief, specific, warm, a little dry. The reader is kind and exact; the copyholder is brisk and factual.
- Margin notes (reader): the correction's *reason* in ≤ 8 words — "house rule 2 — numerals under ten are words", "stronger verb, same meaning", "stet — let it stand". Never "oops", never exclamation marks (the reader has never used one), never emoji.
- Margin notes (copyholder): queries and facts — "word count?", "checked against copy — J.", tallies. Questions are real questions the visitor can answer (a query beside a form field is answered *by* the field).
- Error messages (reader's hand, in forms): state the fix, not the fault — "the address needs its @ — as in you@example.press"; "a number helps us quote — even a rough one". Blame is never assigned to the visitor; the manuscript is treated as a draft, and drafts are expected to have faults.
- Empty states (copyholder's hand): name what's not there and what would put it there — search: "nothing on the desk under that word — try the title, or a name"; an empty brief list: "no ticks yet — the services galley is one sheet up".
- Loading/delay notes: "with the copyholder — a moment" (1s), "still with the press — reload if this sheet doesn't arrive" (5s, §6.12).
- Success/confirmation: a fact + a reference + a named human — "On the desk — reference № 214 · Your reader is R." (§8.3).
- The 404: "This galley isn't on the desk" + the nearest real sheet offered as a correction (§7).

**Voice anti-rules.** The hand never sells (no Kalam in CTAs or prices). The manuscript never jokes about the editing (no struck-through puns in headings). Neither voice uses the words "magic", "supercharge", or "10x" — the desk's whole credibility is that it does one ordinary thing perfectly.

## 13. Full-page scroll choreography map (v2)

Every motion beat from the top of a standard home page to the footer, in order. Times are relative to each beat's trigger; the map assumes the §8 build (6 sections + cards grid + quote + form-adjacent CTA galley).

1. **First paint (t=0).** Sheet on the desk, fully readable. Header rule draws (200–700ms). Pencil line present, drawn to the hero kink.
2. **Hero correction (t=700–1,380ms).** Headline transposition loop + hop-swap (§6.1). Tally → 1.
3. **CTA settle (t=1,200ms).** The routing slip is placed.
4. **Subhead insertion (t=1,600–3,050ms).** Caret blinks, draws, "— aloud —" unfolds (§6.3). Tally → 2. *The page is now still until the visitor scrolls — stillness is a beat too.*
5. **Section 2 enters.** Crop marks stagger-draw at its corners (proof order, 270ms total). Its one correction fires at the 62% line (strike 300ms → swap by 640ms); dust puffs; the pencil line's dot pulses as it reaches the kink; margin parallax drifts the section's notes 0.94×. Tally → 3. Words-checked counts Section 1 as its last line passes.
6. **Style-sheet pin range begins.** The card (§6.8) sticks in the margin as Section 2's demonstration begins; Rule 1 ticks at its correction's completion.
7. **Sections 3–4.** Same rhythm: crop marks, one correction each, card ticks for demonstrated rules (Section 3 = §8.2's numeral correction). The rhythm is deliberately metronomic — the visitor has learned the desk's tempo by now, and the tempo *is* the brand.
8. **Pull-quote.** The proof slip slides away (600ms) as the quote's top crosses 70% — the one change of texture in the page's middle, placed where attention usually dips.
9. **Cards grid.** Slips settle in stagger (≤ 4 × 90ms); each card's own correction fires on its entry; term ticks draw on first hover.
10. **The stet (final third).** The struck word the visitor met earlier is restored — dots draw, strike fades, "stet — let it stand" (§6.5). The page's emotional peak is a *reversal*, deliberately placed after the visitor trusts the editor's hand.
11. **Final section + form galley.** Motion quiets: one correction in the steps galley at most; the form itself is still (§6.16) — the desk watches the visitor work.
12. **Footer sign-off.** Pencil line completes into the signature flourish (600ms); audit tallies settle at their true totals; the page ends *signed*. Total distinct motion systems the visitor has seen: 9 — every one of them an act of editing.

**Choreography laws.** Never two corrections animating simultaneously (the observer queue enforces a 150ms minimum gap between section events). Never any motion in the top 120px of the viewport while the visitor is mid-line in the text column (corrections trigger at 62% height precisely so they fire *below* the reading eye-line and are noticed in peripheral vision as the eye arrives). The page's loudest moment is the hero's 1.4s; everything after is quieter — a crescendo at the start, a long diminuendo to the signature.

## 14. Whole-page performance budget (v2)

- **Asset weight.** Total theme-specific payload ≤ 90KB gzipped beyond the host site's own content: Newsreader (variable, subset to Latin + punctuation + the №/¶/— glyphs) ≤ 38KB; Kalam (400+700, subset) ≤ 22KB; inline SVG symbol library ≤ 4KB; theme CSS ≤ 18KB; theme JS ≤ 8KB. No images are required by the theme; plates (§5) are the host's content and lazy-load below the fold.
- **Fonts.** `font-display: swap` with a metric-matched fallback stack (Georgia for Newsreader, cursive-generic for Kalam at 0.92 size-adjust) so FOUT is a softening, not a reflow; the hero sequence waits for `fonts.ready` (§6.1) but content never does.
- **Animation frame budget.** One shared rAF loop (§5); per frame ≤ 2 style writes on the scroll path (pencil-line dashoffset + margin transform) plus observer-driven class changes off the scroll path. No element animates `width`, `height`, `top` or `left` except §6.2's single-line width swap and §6.3's max-height unfold (≤ 2 instances/page), both on ≤ 1 element at a time. Long-task limit: no task > 50ms during scroll; path-length and letter-width measurements are cached and batched into setup/resize handlers only.
- **Interaction latency.** Click/tap responses (tick draw, preview toggle) begin within 100ms; the form's client validation runs < 16ms per field.
- **What gets cut first when the budget is exceeded** (in order): (1) graphite dust (§6.9) — pure ornament, first to go; (2) margin parallax (§5) — the line remains, the drift goes; (3) the smudge breathing loop; (4) card settle stagger (cards appear settled). Corrections, the pencil line, the style card and every fallback are *never* cut — they are the theme, not its garnish. The cut list is implemented as a `data-perf` tier set by a simple heuristic (deviceMemory ≤ 2GB or save-data → tier 2 cuts 1–2; tier 3 adds 3–4).

## 15. Build-order checklist (v2)

Build the theme in this sequence — each step depends on the ones above it, and the order is chosen so the page is *correct* at every stage, not just at the end.

1. **Static sheet first.** Layout (§4), palette (§2), typography (§3) with all content in its **final, corrected state** and originals in margins — the complete no-JS proof. If this page isn't beautiful and readable, stop; no animation will save it.
2. **Author the corrections.** Write every correction pair into the content (§6.2), the hero transposition (§6.1), insertions (§6.3), the stet (§6.5); human-edit the pairs (§10 item 1). Corrections are content work, not dev work.
3. **Symbol library.** Build the §6.10 SVG marks with baked hand-wobble; verify each at 14px and 40px.
4. **DOM state machinery.** Implement the two-state correction spans, the observer, the 1s fallback timer, and the JS-sets-start-state pattern (§5). Test with JS killed mid-animation now, not later.
5. **Correction engine.** §6.2 firing, §6.1 hero sequence, §6.3 unfold, §6.5 stet, §6.6 preview — in that order (each reuses the previous one's machinery).
6. **Scroll systems.** The shared rAF loop: pencil line (§6.4), margin parallax, folio/current-section tracking (§6.13).
7. **Furniture.** Header + tallies (§6.13), style-sheet card + demonstration wiring (§6.8), footer audit (§6.17) — all numbers computed from the DOM.
8. **Interactive layer.** Ticks (§6.11), proof slips (§6.7), cards (§6.15), form manners (§6.16), transitions + skeleton (§6.12).
9. **Atmosphere last.** Dust (§6.9), smudge breathing, hover physics — the garnish goes on after the meal exists, and is the first thing cut if §14's budget fails.
10. **Reduced-motion + print.** The reduced-motion build (all end states) and the print stylesheet; verify information parity between the three builds (animated / reduced / print).
11. **Gate.** Run §10's checklist on a real device at a real network speed; hand-audit the numbers; read every correction pair aloud one last time.
