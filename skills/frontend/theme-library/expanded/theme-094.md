# 94. The Long Exposure — Expanded (v1 + v2)

**Identity (preserved exactly from the original entry):** A photography darkroom — images don't load, they *develop*. Photographs emerge from chemical nothing: first a ghost, then contrast, then full tone, under red safelight. For photographers, analog labs, camera/film brands, photo books.

**Palette anchors (unchanged):** Darkroom black-red `#120607` · safelight deep red `#5E0F0F` · glow accent `#C1272D` (glow only) · wet-print silver paper `#D8CFC0` · tray grey `#3E3A34`.

**Fonts (unchanged):** Archivo (display — grotesque, lab-manual feel) + IBM Plex Mono (exposure notes and timings).

**Original signature elements (all deepened, none redesigned):** the developing hero print, squeegee wipe reveals, the developing title, the drying line, the enlarger focus pull, stamped exposure notations, the tong-clack flip, the exposure-timer progress, the wet-gloss sheen, tray-ripple ambience. Eight page-family elements (§6.11–§6.18) bring the total to 18.

**The theme's one law: *every print is accounted for by its exposure.*** The visible state of every image on the page must be reproducible from its stamped exposure record — aperture, seconds, paper grade, dodges and burns. Development progress `p` is a published function (§5.2), never an ad-hoc fade: no print may appear more developed than its exposure allows, no figure (a time, a count, a price) may appear anywhere that does not trace to a printed record on the same page, and the tray always yields the print — development is a filter over an already-loaded image, backed by a 1500ms auto-finish, so no visitor ever faces a blank tray.

## 1. Concept & world

The Long Exposure is a working darkroom rendered as a website. The visitor does not *view* photographs here; they stand at the bench while photographs are *made* — in the room's own chemistry, at the room's own pace. Every image arrives the way a silver gelatin print arrives: nothing, then a ghost swimming up through the developer, then contrast, then tone, then the finished print lifted out by tongs, squeegeed, stamped with its exposure notation, and clipped to the drying line. The interface furniture is the room's furniture. Sections are trays. The gallery is a drying line strung across the wall. Progress is not a bar but an exposure timer: the reader's scroll is time in the tray, and the page develops alongside its prints.

The mood is hushed, red, and patient. The room is lit by a single safelight, so the palette is a near-black red darkness against which wet paper glows like something dredged from a bath. Nothing in the room hurries: the motion language is chemical — asymptotic, easing into density the way silver halide darkens, fast at first (the ghost appears quickly) and slower as full tone approaches. Waiting is not a loading state in this theme; waiting is the medium.

**The fiction, stated plainly so builders hold it:** the site is the public room of a photographer or a small analog lab. The hero is the current print on the easel. The gallery is finished work on the line. The enlarger section is where a negative is examined. The exposure log is the lab notebook. The order form is the order desk by the door. Every element in §6 is one of these bench objects; if a proposed component cannot be named as a bench object, it does not belong to the theme (see §11 for the refusals and their in-room replacements).

**Best-fit sites and audiences:**
- Portfolio sites for film photographers whose process *is* the brand — the development is the portfolio's argument that the work is made, not rendered.
- Analog labs offering printing, scanning and darkroom hire, where the exposure log (§6.13) doubles as the services/bench-time table.
- Camera and film brands with a heritage line — a product page where the hero photograph develops under the visitor's scroll sells slowness as luxury.
- Photo books and print editions, where the order form (§6.14) sells a physical object whose making the page has just performed.
- Exhibition microsites for silver gelatin or alternative-process shows.

**When NOT to use it:**
- Never for colour-critical e-commerce where the product image must be colour-accurate at first paint — development deliberately passes through wrong-contrast, wrong-density states, and a buyer must never wonder which state is the product.
- Never for news, documentation, dashboards or anything scanned under time pressure: the theme's core gesture (an image that takes up to 4s, or a scroll's length, to reach full tone) is a tax on urgency.
- Never for sites whose imagery is mostly screenshots or diagrams — development filters on flat graphic content read as a cheap fade, not chemistry.
- Never where the audience is predominantly on very low-power devices *and* the page carries more than ~12 prints: the filter pipeline is GPU-cheap but not free, and §14's cut order degrades the theme to static prints quickly — if the static fallback is the expected common case, choose a theme that is designed static.
- Never with a light, airy brand. There is no light scheme (§2.4): the room is the scheme.

**The one law, restated for the quality gate:** *every print is accounted for by its exposure.* A build fails the gate (§10) if any image's visible development state cannot be reproduced from its printed notation via §5.2's function, if any number appears that no printed record sums to, or if any print can be left undeveloped by a missed trigger.

### 2.2 Pairing rulings (blocking)

- **Glow never sits on safelight.** `--glow` on `--safelight` measures 2.32:1 — the timer strip, stamps and record light must always be mounted on `--room`, `--tray` or `--paper`, never on a `--safelight` ground. A stamp that lands on a red-washed section is printed on a paper label first (§6.6).
- **Safelight is a ground, not an edge.** At 1.47:1 on `--room`, a `--safelight` block edge is nearly invisible; every safelight-ground block carries a `--tray-hi` hairline or a paper label so its boundary is perceivable.
- **Red text is rationed.** Small text (below 24px) in red on a dark ground is always `--glow-hi` (5.21:1), never `--glow` (3.41:1). `--glow` is reserved for fills, rings, glows and large display glyphs.
- **Paper-dim is rationed on tray.** 4.13:1 on `--tray` passes only for large or non-essential text; anything a visitor must read to act (prices, times, errors) on a tray ground is `--paper`.

### 2.3 The glow budget

`--glow` is the room's only saturated colour and it is spent like safelight bulbs are spent: the lit header strip, the timer dial's lit arc, stamp rings, the record light, focus rings, and the squeegee's wet edge are the only permitted glow surfaces on a page. §10's gate includes a **glow census**: more than seven distinct glow surfaces in the first viewport is a fail. Glow is rendered as colour plus a soft shadow (`0 0 18px color-mix(in srgb, var(--glow) 55%, transparent)`), never as a gradient wash across a section.

### 2.4 Dark-mode behaviour — there is no light scheme

The darkroom is the scheme in both `prefers-color-scheme` modes; inverting it would be a room with the white lights on, in which nothing can be developed. Two rulings instead of a scheme:
- Under `prefers-color-scheme: light`, the room warms by one step: section grounds alternate `--room` / `--redwash` more frequently (the lamp feels closer) — no token changes value.
- Under `forced-colors: active`, all development filters are disabled by the platform anyway; the build must therefore never *depend* on a filter to convey state — notations, captions and the log carry every fact in text (§6 passim), and prints present fully developed. The build adds an explicit `@media (forced-colors: active)` block that sets every print to its printed state and removes sheen, ripple and glow shadows.

## 3. Typography

Two families, as in the original entry — **Archivo** for display and prose (the lab manual's grotesque) and **IBM Plex Mono** for everything the room *records*: exposure notations, timings, frame numbers, prices in the log, form confirmations. No third family is admitted; handwriting, where the fiction wants it (a pencil note on a notation back), is a *treatment* of IBM Plex Mono — letterspaced, `--paper-dim`, rotated −1.2° — never a script font.

### 3.1 The five type laws

1. **Numerals are mono, always.** Any number that is a measurement — seconds, f-stops, frame numbers, counts, prices in records — is IBM Plex Mono with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. A numeral set in Archivo anywhere on the page is a defect (§9).
2. **Development never moves type.** The developing title (§6.3) changes colour and blur only; letter positions, tracking and line breaks are identical in the ghost state and the finished state. Zero layout shift is a law, not a nicety: the page is a bench, and benches do not rearrange themselves.
3. **Type develops on paper, not on the room.** Display headlines are printed matter — they sit on paper grounds or develop from `--paper-dim` to `--paper-hi` against the room (§6.3's two sanctioned treatments). A headline that fades in from transparent black is off-theme (§9).
4. **Notation type is never larger than the print it annotates.** Stamps and border notations are capped at 11px mono; the record must never shout over the photograph.
5. **The mono voice is terse and complete.** Labels are written the way a printer writes on a print back: quantity, unit, condition — "f/8 · 12s · Gr 3", never "exposed at f/8 for twelve seconds".

### 3.2 Type scale

Base 16px; the scale is a bench scale, not a marketing scale — one display size, used once per page.

| Role | Family | Size / line-height | Tracking | Case & notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display (page title) | Archivo 600 | clamp(40px, 6.2vw, 76px) / 1.02 | −0.015em | Sentence case; develops per §6.3 |
| Section head (tray label) | Archivo 600 | 28px / 1.15 | −0.01em | Printed on a paper tray label (§6.11 pattern) |
| Sub-head | Archivo 500 | 19px / 1.35 | 0 | `--paper-hi` on dark, `--ink` on paper |
| Body | Archivo 400 | 16.5px / 1.62 | 0 | `--paper` on room (12.90:1), `--ink` on paper (11.14:1) |
| Caption / secondary | Archivo 400 | 13.5px / 1.5 | 0.005em | `--paper-dim` on room; on tray only ≥14px per §2.2 |
| Notation / stamp | IBM Plex Mono 500 | 10.5–11px / 1.45 | 0.04em | Uppercase for stamps; the rationing law (§3.1.4) caps it at 11px |
| Data / timings / prices in records | IBM Plex Mono 500 | 13px / 1.5, tabular | 0.02em | Timer readout is 15px `--glow-hi` |
| Form labels | Archivo 500 | 13px / 1.4 | 0.06em, uppercase | On paper fields, `--ink` at 70% |
| Buttons | Archivo 600 | 14px / 1 | 0.05em, uppercase | Text is ink on paper, or paper on tray — never glow text |

### 3.3 Per-role rules

- **Display:** one per page; it is the title of the print or the room's name, and it *develops* (§6.3). It never wraps to a third line at ≥1200px — shorten the title, not the type.
- **Headings** name bench objects and processes ("The drying line", "From the log"), not benefits. A heading that could appear on a SaaS page fails §12's voice test.
- **Body** measure is 58–68ch on dark grounds; on paper grounds (cards, notation backs) 34–46ch, because paper is a physical width here.
- **Captions** sit inside the print's border or directly beneath it, never floating on the room — a caption belongs to a print.
- **Data** columns in the log (§6.13) right-align; the seconds column is the table's spine and is never truncated.

## 4. Layout language

The page is a darkroom bench photographed from above: a dark ground (`--room`), objects laid on it (trays, prints, the line), and one light source (the safelight, top-left — every shadow on the page falls to the bottom-right, consistently, or the room reads as fake).

### 4.1 The bench grid

- A 12-column grid at ≥1200px, max content width 1240px, gutters 28px. Prints never span more than 7 columns; the room must always show around its objects — a print that touches both edges is a billboard, not a print (§9).
- Vertical rhythm is the **tray rhythm**: sections are separated by 96px of room at ≥1200px (64px at 768–1199px, 48px below 768px), and each section is a *tray* — a `--tray` or `--redwash` block with a 2px `--tray-hi` rim on its top edge only, as if lit from above, internal padding 40px (24px mobile).
- The drying line (§6.4) is the one element allowed to break the grid horizontally: it strings across the full content width at a slight sag, because a line is strung wall to wall.

### 4.2 Density rules

- **One developing thing at a time.** At any scroll position, at most one print may be mid-development (0 < p < 1) in the viewport. The choreography map (§13) sequences developments so prints finish before the next begins; a viewport full of half-developed prints reads as a loading failure, not a darkroom (§9).
- **Paper ration:** paper surfaces (prints, cards, labels, form) may cover at most ~38% of any viewport at ≥1200px. The room must stay dark and dominant; if a layout draft reads "mostly beige", it has left the darkroom.
- **The reading line is at 55%.** Content the visitor must *read* (body text, the log, the form) is choreographed to arrive at full legibility by the time its top crosses 55% of the viewport height; development of decorative prints may continue below that line, reading matter may not be illegible above it.

### 4.3 Whitespace as chemistry

The dark space between objects is not empty — it is the room's air, and it is where the safelight lives. Glow (§2.3) is placed in the whitespace (the strip, the dial, a stamp's halo), so the eye crosses lit darkness between paper objects. Sections therefore never touch each other; the 96px tray gap is a held breath, and §13's choreography gives the visitor two beats of pure room between major developments.

### 4.4 Print geometry

Prints are physical: a white-ish paper border of 10px on the sides and 26px at the bottom (the notation margin, where §6.6 stamps land), the image inset within it, a shadow of `0 18px 40px rgba(0,0,0,.55), 0 3px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.5)` cast bottom-right. Aspect ratios come from the negative formats the fiction prints: 3:2, 5:4, 1:1, 16:9 for panoramic frames. A print's border, notation margin and shadow are part of the element, not decoration around it — the notation margin is where the theme's record-keeping visibly happens.

## 5. Full motion system

### 5.1 Named easings

Five easings, each named for the bench action it performs. No other easing may be introduced; an element that needs a different curve has misunderstood its action.

| Easing | Curve | Performs |
|---|---|---|
| `--ease-develop` | `cubic-bezier(0.16, 0.84, 0.3, 1)` | Chemistry: the ghost arrives fast, density approaches asymptotically — all development, counters, tone builds |
| `--ease-squeegee` | `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.25, 1)` | The squeegee's steady push: constant pressure, clean stop — wipes and sheen sweeps |
| `--ease-stamp` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 1.35, 0.3, 1)` | The stamp press: down fast, a breath of overshoot as ink meets paper — stamps, seals, confirmations |
| `--ease-sway` | `cubic-bezier(0.45, 0, 0.55, 1)` | The pendulum: symmetric in-out for the drying line's sway and the dial's settle |
| `--ease-tongs` | `cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.2, 1)` | The tongs: grip (slow), lift, set down (decisive) — flips, print lifts, tray-to-tray transitions |

Plus one drawing curve for the line itself: `--ease-draw: cubic-bezier(0.6, 0, 0.2, 1)`, used only by §6.4's SVG line and §6.5's focus reticle.

### 5.2 The development function (published, binding)

Development progress `p ∈ [0, 1]` maps to the print's rendered state by exactly this function, shared by every developing element (§6.1, §6.4, §6.12, §6.15):

- `opacity = 0.34 + 0.66p`
- `contrast = 0.52 + 0.50p` (0.52 → 1.02)
- `brightness = 1.38 − 0.38p` (1.38 → 1.00)
- `saturate = 0.08 + 0.92p` (0.08 → 1.00)
- `blur = 6px × max(0, 0.25 − p)` (1.5px at the ghost, 0 from p = 0.25 on)

Stages, named in the room's language and used in notations and the choreography map: **Ghost** (0 ≤ p < 0.25), **Low contrast** (0.25 ≤ p < 0.55), **Tone** (0.55 ≤ p < 0.85), **Full** (0.85 ≤ p ≤ 1). At p = 1 the filter is the print's printed resting state (contrast 1.02 is the paper's own slight snap — it is *not* removed, because the printed state is the artefact). The squeegee (§6.2) fires when p crosses 0.85; the stamp (§6.6) fires at p = 1.

**The floor:** no print that has entered the viewport may rest below p = 0.34 opacity-equivalent legibility — the ghost state is already a visible photograph, never a blank. Development is a filter over an already-loaded, already-laid-out `<img>`; with JS disabled or filters unsupported, the print simply appears at its printed state. That is the fallback, and it is total.

### 5.3 The shared loop and the scrub rule

One `requestAnimationFrame` loop drives every scroll-coupled value in the theme: hero development (when scroll-scrubbed), the timer dial and strip (§6.8), sheen positions (§6.9), focus-pull blur (§6.5), sway phase (§6.4) and the counters (§5.4). Scroll handlers never write styles; they write targets. Every driven value approaches its target by `current += (target − current) × 0.12` per frame (lerp 0.12) — the room's single smoothing constant. Time-based runs (the hero's 4s develop, stamps, flips) use CSS transitions/animations on the same easings, started by the loop's state changes, so a throttled tab cannot desynchronise them: each carries its own watchdog (§6 passim).

### 5.4 The motion terms, in this theme's language

- **Scroll-triggered reveals:** prints develop (§5.2) — there is no other reveal in the theme. Nothing fades up, nothing slides in from below; matter appears the way it appears in a tray.
- **Scroll-driven / scrubbed animation:** the hero's development when the visitor scrolls early (§6.1), and the enlarger focus pull (§6.5) — both 1:1 with scroll through their ranges, lerped at 0.12.
- **Parallax:** the drying line's prints sway and translate at three shallow depths (§6.4) — maximum differential 14px across a viewport of travel; prints never overlap their neighbours.
- **Pinned sections:** exactly one — the enlarger (§6.5), pinned for 160vh of scroll at ≥1200px. A second pin on a page is forbidden; the room has one enlarger.
- **Smooth scrolling:** the lerp loop *is* the smoothing; no scroll-jacking library. Anchor jumps (index → print, §6.12) animate scroll over 700ms `--ease-squeegee` unless reduced motion, in which case they jump.
- **Progress indicator:** the exposure timer (§6.8) — dial + safelight strip + mono readout, three printings of one progress value.
- **Staggered reveals:** stamps stagger at 90ms within a tray (§6.6); title letters stagger at 45ms (§6.3); line prints clip on staggered by 120ms (§6.4). Stagger is always *sequence in the fiction* (prints are handled one at a time), never decoration.
- **Masked reveals:** the squeegee wipe (§6.2) — a clip-path mask pushed left to right, once per print, at Full.
- **Kinetic typography:** the developing title (§6.3) only. No marquee exists in this theme (refused, §11): a darkroom has no ticker; the room's one moving text is the timer readout changing its numerals.
- **Counters:** exposure times count in whole seconds over 700ms `--ease-develop`, quantised — a time is never displayed as a fraction. F-stops do not count numerically; they *step* through the aperture series (f/1.4 → 2 → 2.8 → 4 → 5.6 → 8 → 11 → 16), one step per 90ms, because apertures are detents, not a continuum.
- **SVG line-draw:** the drying line draws itself (§6.4); the focus reticle draws at the enlarger's entry (§6.5). Nothing else draws.
- **Image scale-in-frame:** forbidden for prints (a photograph does not zoom inside its border; §9). The enlarger's image *sharpens* in frame instead — focus, not scale.
- **Hover / magnetic / tilt micro-interactions:** hover lifts a print 6px toward the light and deepens its contrast by +0.04 (§6.7's rest state borrows this); magnetic pull is refused — tongs grip or they don't; tilt is refused — a wet print is flat or it is ruined. The tong-clack (§6.7) is the theme's click signature.
- **Custom cursor:** none. The system cursor, everywhere, always (standing rule).
- **Skeleton loading:** unexposed paper (§6.16) — a blank sheet with the faintest latent grid; it does not shimmer (paper does not shimmer; §9).
- **Page transitions:** tray-to-tray (§6.16) — the current print is lifted by tongs and the next is already in the tray developing; hard ceiling 1500ms, the theme's auto-finish constant.
- **Reduced motion:** every element ships its finished state (§6 passim): prints developed, line still, focus sharp, stamps printed, dial stepped. The page reads as a room with the work done — which is a complete, honourable page.

## 6. Signature elements

Eighteen elements: the ten originals (§6.1–§6.10), deepened and never redesigned, plus eight page-family elements (§6.11–§6.18) that give the room its navigation, listing, table, form, footer, transition and error states. Every element carries the full v1 build spec and every v2 layer. Shared constants — the development function, the easings, the lerp, the 1500ms auto-finish — are defined once in §5 and referenced, never re-invented per element.

### 6.1 The developing hero print

- **What & why:** The page's hero is a photograph in a tray, developing. On load it ghosts in and builds tone over ~4s in the four stages of §5.2; if the visitor scrolls before it finishes, development hands over to scroll, scrubbed 1:1 — time in the tray becomes the visitor's own time. It is the theme's thesis performed once, at full size: in this room, images are made, and making takes the time it takes. The hero print sits in a visible tray (a `--tray` block with rim and a shallow chemical sheen) on the `--room` ground, with the developing title (§6.3) above it and its notation (§6.6) destined for its bottom border.
- **Structure:** A `<figure class="print print--hero">` inside a `<section class="tray tray--hero">`. The figure is paper: `--paper` ground, 10px side borders, 26px notation margin at the bottom (§4.4). Inside, a wrapper with the tray's liquid surface — a pseudo-element sheen (see §6.10) clipped to the image area — and the `<img>` itself, loaded eagerly, `fetchpriority="high"`, fully laid out from first paint. Development is a single CSS custom property `--p` on the figure; the filter chain of §5.2 is computed from it in CSS (`filter: opacity(calc(...)) contrast(...) ...` via registered custom properties, with a JS-driven style fallback for unregistered-property browsers — the JS writes the same computed filter string from the same function, so both paths render identically).
- **Motion spec:** Two sanctioned drives, one function. *Time drive (default):* on load, p runs 0 → 1 over **4000ms** with `--ease-develop`, beginning after a 250ms safelight settle (§13, beat 1). *Scroll drive:* if the visitor scrolls more than 40px before p reaches 1, the time drive is cancelled and p is scrubbed to scroll through the first **120vh** of page travel (p = clamp(scrollY / (1.2 × viewportHeight))), lerped at 0.12 by the shared loop — the visitor who hurries develops the print by travelling, at exactly the rate they travel. At p = 0.85 the squeegee (§6.2) fires; at p = 1 the notation stamps (§6.6) and the timer readout (§6.8) prints the hero's 12s as the page's reference exposure.
- **Choreography timeline:** t=0 — safelight strip glows up (§6.11, 600ms). t=250ms — p begins (time drive). t≈700ms — Ghost stage fully present (p≈0.25 on the develop curve); the title's first letters sharpen (§6.3). t≈1900ms — Tone (p≈0.55–0.85); tray sheen brightens one step. t≈3100ms — p crosses 0.85: squeegee crosses, 800ms. t=4000ms — p=1: stamp lands in the notation margin (180ms), timer readout settles on `T+12s / 12s`. If scroll drive engages at any point, the timeline is abandoned and p follows scroll; the 0.85 and 1.0 crossings fire their events whenever they occur.
- **State matrix:** *Rest (pre-trigger):* p=0 ghost — but only as a JS-armed state; the DOM ships at p=1 (§5.2 floor). *Developing:* as above; pointer events on the print are live throughout (the flip, §6.7, is disabled until p=1 — tongs do not touch a print in the developer). *Hover (p=1, desktop):* lift 6px toward the light over 240ms `--ease-tongs`, contrast +0.04, shadow deepens bottom-right. *Focus:* identical to hover, plus a `--glow` 2px focus ring offset 3px — the enlarger light finds it. *Active:* the tong-clack (§6.7) takes over. *Scrolled-past:* p stays 1 forever; development never reverses — a developed print does not undevelop when you scroll back up. *Disabled:* not applicable (the figure is not a control); its flip button is §6.7's element. *Touch:* no hover; the lift is skipped, tap = flip (§6.7) once p=1.
- **Content-merge:** The hero *is* the content — the current print, the photographer's lead image or the lab's featured work. Its caption, printed in the notation margin beside the stamp, carries the image's real title and date. Nothing about the element exists without the photograph.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** The DOM ships developed (p=1 as the no-JS state); JS *resets* p to 0 only after confirming the image has loaded and the filter path works, within the same frame as the reset, so no visitor sees a flash of finished print followed by a ghost. Watchdogs: (1) a **1500ms auto-finish** — if p < 1 1500ms after the print's tray section leaves the viewport's centre band, or if the tab was backgrounded mid-run, p animates to 1 over 300ms `--ease-develop`; (2) if `filter` is unsupported, p is never reset and the print stays printed. A missed trigger therefore leaves a visible print, never a blank tray — the original entry's safety rule, kept verbatim in effect.
- **Reduced motion:** No development. The print is shown fully developed from first paint; scroll drive is never armed; the stamp and readout are printed, not animated.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — tray spans 7 columns, print at its negative's aspect (3:2 default), title above-left. 768–1199px — tray spans full width, print max 640px, time drive shortened to 3200ms (smaller room, smaller tray). <768px — tray padding 16px, print full width, time drive 2600ms, scroll-drive range shortened to 90vh; the notation margin stays 26px (the record is not cut on mobile).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Slow network:* the `<img>` may arrive after p's run begins — p is held at the Ghost floor (p=0, but the paper and caption are present) until `load` fires, then the run starts; the paper is never an empty frame because the figure's paper ground and printed caption render without the image. *Huge image / decode jank:* `decoding="async"` plus a `content-visibility` exemption for the hero (it is above the fold by definition). *Visitor scrolls to the footer instantly:* the auto-finish completes the hero unseen; on return it is a finished print with its stamp — correct darkroom behaviour. *Print is portrait:* the tray narrows to 5 columns at ≥1200px; the title moves above the tray, never beside it (the bench is read top-down).
- **Accessibility:** The `<img>` carries real alt text; development is decorative and is never the only carrier of information — the caption and notation are text. The developing state is not announced (no aria-live on a filter); a visually-hidden status line in the figure reads "Photograph: [title], fully developed print" at all times, which is true in the shipped state and becomes true within 4s in the animated state. Focus order: title → hero flip button (§6.7) → tray contents. Contrast: caption `--ink` on `--paper` 11.14:1.
- **Tokens:** `--room`, `--tray`, `--tray-hi`, `--paper`, `--paper-hi`, `--glow`, `--ease-develop`, `--ease-tongs`; consumes `--p` (element-local).
- **Performance & mobile:** One eager image (the page's LCP; budget ≤ 320KB at 1600w, served responsive). Filter animation on a composited layer — the loop writes one custom property per frame, ~0.2ms script cost. Mobile variant: same element, shorter drives (above); no separate asset.

### 6.2 Squeegee wipe reveals

- **What & why:** When a print reaches Full (p crosses 0.85), a squeegee passes over it left to right and pushes the water off: the last chemical gloss clears in a moving band and the print settles to its dry, printed matte. It is the gesture that says *done* — in a real darkroom the squeegee is how a print leaves the tray for the line. Every developing print in the theme (§6.1, §6.4, §6.12) receives exactly one squeegee pass, at exactly the same point in its development, so the gesture becomes the room's full-stop.
- **Structure:** A pseudo-element band on the print's image wrapper: a 64px-wide vertical gradient (transparent → `--paper-hi` at 18% → transparent) with a 2px `--glow`-tinted leading edge (the wet meniscus catching safelight), clipped to the image area, translated from −80px to width+80px. Behind the band, a clip-path on a "wet" overlay (the gloss layer of §6.9 at double strength) is wiped away in sync, so the wipe visibly *removes* something rather than merely passing over.
- **Motion spec:** **800ms**, `--ease-squeegee`, triggered once per print at p = 0.85 crossing (either drive). The band's speed is constant-pressure: the easing's long midsection keeps velocity near-uniform, ends clean. No delay, no stagger — one print, one pass. If two prints in one tray cross 0.85 within the same frame (possible on fast scroll), their wipes are sequenced 120ms apart by the loop — prints are handled one at a time (§5.4).
- **Choreography timeline:** t=0 — p crosses 0.85; the gloss overlay is at full wet strength. t=0–800ms — band travels; overlay clip recedes with the band's leading edge; the print's contrast steps the final +0.02 as the band passes its centre (the driest point reads sharpest). t=800ms — band exits; overlay removed from the DOM flow (display:none), print is at its resting printed state; the stamp (§6.6) is now armed for p=1.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* absent (no band in the DOM-visible layer). *Running:* pointer-events none; the print beneath remains fully interactive only after p=1. *Hover/focus/active:* no effect — the squeegee does not respond to the visitor; it responds to the chemistry. *Scrolled-past mid-wipe:* the wipe finishes off-screen (800ms is short); on return, the print is dry. *Disabled:* if the print's development was never armed (no-JS, reduced motion, filter fallback), the wipe never runs and the print is simply dry — its resting state. *Touch:* identical; no touch variant needed for a non-interactive layer.
- **Content-merge:** The wipe removes the wet-gloss layer that §6.9 maintains — it is an operation *on the print's own surface state*, not a reveal effect pasted over content. A print that was never wet is never wiped.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** The wet overlay is an enhancement layer; if the wipe's animation is interrupted (tab hidden, element removed), a **900ms watchdog** from the p=0.85 crossing force-removes the overlay and the band. The print can never be left glossy-locked or half-wiped in a way that obscures it: the overlay's maximum opacity is 0.18 and it sits above the image but below the notation margin.
- **Reduced motion:** No wipe. Prints arrive dry; the gloss overlay is never applied.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — band 64px. 768–1199px — band 48px, duration 700ms (smaller prints). <768px — band 40px, duration 600ms; the leading-edge glow is dropped (the census, §2.3, is spent on stamps and the strip first).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Very narrow prints* (contact frames, §6.12): the wipe still runs the full width — duration scales to 420ms so the gesture reads as a flick, not a crawl. *Print re-enters viewport:* the wipe never repeats; dry is a permanent state (a `data-dried` flag). *Backdrop-filter unsupported:* the gloss overlay uses plain opacity, and the wipe still reads.
- **Accessibility:** Purely visual; nothing is announced; the print's information content is unchanged by wet/dry state. The band is `aria-hidden` and the overlay is excluded from the accessibility tree.
- **Tokens:** `--paper-hi`, `--glow`, `--ease-squeegee`.
- **Performance & mobile:** One composited transform + one clip-path animation per print, once — negligible. Mobile: shorter durations as above; no other change.

### 6.3 Developing title

- **What & why:** The page title develops like a print of itself: each letter begins as a grey blur — `--paper-dim`, blurred 3px, at ghost contrast — and sharpens to full `--paper-hi` density, staggered letter by letter like separate exposures on the same sheet. Typography in this room is photographic paper too; the title is the first thing the visitor watches develop, and it teaches the room's rule (things here *arrive by process*) before the hero print finishes.
- **Structure:** The `<h1>` split into per-letter `<span class="dev-letter">` by JS at arm time (the DOM ships the whole word, readable, as one text node — splitting is an enhancement, and screen readers keep the unsplit heading via `aria-label` on the h1 and `aria-hidden` on the spans). Each letter carries a custom property `--lp` (its own 0→1 progress) driven by the same §5.2 curve restricted to colour/blur: `color: color-mix(...)` between `--paper-dim` and `--paper-hi`, `filter: blur(calc(3px × (1 − lp)))`. On paper grounds (the tray-label variant, §6.11), letters develop from `--paper-dim` to `--ink` instead.
- **Motion spec:** Total **1100ms**: each letter's own run is 500ms `--ease-develop`; letters stagger by **45ms** in reading order; a two-word title of 11 letters completes at 500 + 10×45 = 950ms; longer titles compress the stagger (stagger = min(45ms, 600ms / (letters − 1))) so the title never outlasts 1100ms. The run starts at t=150ms, just before the hero print's t=250ms — type first, then image, the way a printer reads the notation before lifting the print.
- **Choreography timeline:** t=150ms — first letter begins. t≈400ms — the first word is legible at ghost contrast. t≈800ms — all letters past blur 0 (sharp), tone still building. t≤1100ms — full density; the letters' filters are removed (set to `none`) so text renders at native crispness thereafter. The hero print (§6.1) is at Tone stage as the title completes — the room's two developments are deliberately offset, never simultaneous at full intensity (§4.2's one-developing-thing rule applies to *prints*; the title's run is the sanctioned exception because it finishes before the hero's Full stage).
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* shipped sharp and full-density (no-JS state). *Developing:* as above; the heading remains selectable and readable throughout (ghost letters are `--paper-dim` at 7.28:1 — legible, merely soft). *Hover/focus:* the h1 is not interactive; no states. *Scrolled-past / return:* never re-runs — a developed title stays developed (session flag). *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* identical.
- **Content-merge:** The title is the page's actual title — the photographer's name, the exhibition, the lab. There is no decorative headline in the theme; if a page has nothing to title, it has no developing title, and the hero print's caption takes the h1.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** If the letter split fails or the run is interrupted, a **1200ms watchdog** from arm time removes all letter filters and restores full density. Because the shipped state is finished, every failure path lands on finished.
- **Reduced motion:** The title is simply printed, full density, no split performed.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — display size per §3.2. 768–1199px — same run, stagger floor 30ms. <768px — titles over two lines develop line by line (letters still stagger within the line, lines offset by 120ms) so a wrapped title doesn't blur as one mass.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Very long titles* (>24 letters): stagger compresses per the formula; below 12ms stagger the run becomes a single 700ms whole-title develop — granularity is sacrificed before duration is extended. *Right-to-left scripts:* stagger follows logical reading order (`dir` aware); blur/density are direction-neutral. *Font not yet loaded:* the run waits for `document.fonts.ready` (max 600ms) — developing in a fallback font and then swapping would move letters, violating §3.1 law 2; if fonts time out, the title prints without developing.
- **Accessibility:** The h1's accessible name is the plain title (aria-label = text content; letter spans aria-hidden). Ghost-state contrast 7.28:1 passes AA for large text at every instant of the run. Focus never lands on letters (they are not interactive).
- **Tokens:** `--paper-dim`, `--paper-hi`, `--ink`, `--ease-develop`.
- **Performance & mobile:** Per-letter filters on ≤24 short-lived spans — the run is 1100ms and then filters are removed entirely; mobile identical except the two-line rule.

### 6.4 The drying line

- **What & why:** The gallery is a line strung across the room: an SVG cord that draws itself wall to wall, from which finished prints hang by clothespins. Each print clips on as it finishes developing, and once hung, sways almost imperceptibly — out of phase with its neighbours, because no two prints on a real line move together. The gallery is therefore not a grid of images but a *place work goes when it is done*, and the visitor reads the room's production in order: tray below, line above.
- **Structure:** A `<section class="line-gallery">` containing an inline SVG (the cord: a single path with a natural sag — a quadratic curve dipping 14px at centre — plus two wall pins) and an ordered list of `<figure class="print print--line">` items, each hung from a CSS clothespin (a 10×22px `--tray-hi` body with a `--glow`-free, paper-toned jaw — pins are hardware, not glow surfaces). Each print is positioned along the line by its index; its top edge follows the cord's sag (precomputed offsets per position, so no JS geometry is needed at rest). Prints develop in their trays *within* the line section (a shallow tray strip beneath the line) and are then translated up to their pin — in practice the figure itself is the hung print, and development happens in place, the pin dropping in as development completes: the clip-on is the completion gesture.
- **Motion spec:** *The draw:* on first entry, the cord's path draws via stroke-dashoffset over **1200ms** `--ease-draw`. *Clip-on:* as each print reaches p=1 (its own development per §5.2, time-driven over 2400ms once its tray strip enters, staggered 120ms apart), its pin drops 8px and settles over **260ms** `--ease-stamp`, and the print takes a 1-frame 0.3° rotation jolt — the weight arriving on the line. *Sway:* once hung, each print rotates ±0.6° around its pin point with a **7000ms** period, `--ease-sway`, phase offset = index × 0.7 rad, driven by the shared loop (transform only). Adjacent prints are always ≥0.35 rad out of phase — the offsets guarantee it by construction. *Parallax:* the line section's prints translate vertically at three shallow rates by row position (0, −7px, −14px across the section's travel) — the far end of the line is nearer the wall.
- **Choreography timeline:** Section enters → t=0 cord begins drawing. t=1200ms — cord complete; first print's development begins (its tray strip has been in view). Each print: develop 2400ms (staggered starts +120ms) → squeegee at its 0.85 → stamp at its 1.0 → pin drop 260ms → sway begins, phase-locked to the loop's clock so late prints join the room's rhythm, not their own. A line of six prints is fully hung ≈ 1200 + 2400 + 5×120 + margin ≈ **4.4s** after entry — the slowest sanctioned sequence in the theme, and it is a gallery, where slowness is the exhibit.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* shipped state — cord drawn (as a static SVG path), all prints hung, developed, still. *Developing:* per-print, as §6.1's matrix, flip disabled until hung. *Hover (desktop):* the print lifts 4px and its sway pauses (a held print doesn't swing) — 240ms `--ease-tongs`; contrast +0.04 as §6.1. *Focus:* same lift via the print's flip button (§6.7), sway pauses, `--glow` ring. *Active:* §6.7 flip. *Scrolled-past:* sway continues only while the section is within 1.5 viewports (the loop gates ambient motion by proximity — §14); prints never undevelop, pins never release. *Disabled:* n/a per print; the section has no global control. *Touch:* no hover lift; sway amplitude halves to ±0.3° (a nudged line on a small screen reads as instability); tap = flip.
- **Content-merge:** The line holds the gallery's actual finished works, in the order the photographer hung them (curated order, stated in the section's tray label). Each print's notation (§6.6) is on its border; the line is the portfolio.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** The shipped DOM is the finished line (cord drawn, prints hung and developed, sway off). JS arms development only for prints whose images have loaded. Watchdogs: per-print 1500ms auto-finish (§5.2); a section-level **5000ms watchdog** — if any print on the line is still undeveloped 5s after the section's first entry (throttled tab, IO failure), all remaining prints complete over 300ms and pins drop without stagger. The cord's draw has a CSS fallback: if the animation never runs, dashoffset is 0 in the shipped state (JS sets the offset to arm the draw — the inverse of the usual pattern, chosen so failure = drawn).
- **Reduced motion:** Static line: cord drawn, prints hung and developed, no sway, no parallax, pins already dropped.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — up to 6 prints per line at 3:2/5:4 mixed aspects, sag 14px. 768–1199px — up to 4 per line, sag 10px, sway ±0.45°. <768px — the line becomes a vertical string: prints hang one per row from a left rail cord (draws top-to-bottom), sway ±0.3°, parallax off — a line on a narrow wall runs down, not across.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *More prints than the line holds:* a second line is strung below (the section repeats its cord) — never shrink prints to fit; the room adds a line. *A print fails to load:* its paper hangs with the printed caption and a notation reading "frame fogged — no image" (an honest in-world empty state); the pin still drops, the count of hung prints is the DOM count, so §6.13's log and any "prints on the line" figure elsewhere must count loaded prints only — the log's entry for the fogged frame says so. *Very long captions:* the notation margin grows; the pin position follows the print's top edge, not a fixed grid line.
- **Accessibility:** The list is a real `<ol>` — reading order is hanging order. Sway and parallax are decorative and `aria-hidden` in effect (transforms only). Each print's flip button (§6.7) is labelled "Turn over print: [title]". The cord SVG is `aria-hidden="true"`.
- **Tokens:** `--room`, `--tray-hi`, `--paper`, `--paper-dim`, `--ease-draw`, `--ease-stamp`, `--ease-sway`, `--ease-tongs`.
- **Performance & mobile:** Sway is transform-only on ≤6 composited layers per line; the loop's per-frame cost for a full line is <0.3ms. Mobile: vertical string variant (above) — cheaper (one axis), amplitude halved, parallax removed.

### 6.5 Enlarger focus pull

- **What & why:** One section of the page is the enlarger: a negative in the carrier, projected large. The section pins, and as the visitor scrolls through the pin, the projected image pulls from a soft blur to tack-sharp — the reader focuses the print by reading. It is the theme's one pinned scene and its one true scroll-scrubbed *image* transformation (the hero's scrub moves development; this moves focus), and it earns its pin because focusing an enlarger is a two-handed, whole-body pause in the darkroom's workflow.
- **Structure:** A `<section class="enlarger">` with a sticky stage (the easel): the projected print (a large `<figure class="print print--easel">` without notation margin — it is a projection, not yet a print), flanked by the enlarger's furniture rendered in CSS/SVG: the carrier frame, a focus reticle (SVG crosshair + circle, drawn per §5.4), and a mono readout of the focus distance that counts as focus pulls (see Motion). The pin is implemented with `position: sticky` inside a 160vh-tall section — no JS pinning library.
- **Motion spec:** Scroll range 160vh at ≥1200px maps to focus progress q ∈ [0,1] (lerped 0.12). The image: `blur = 14px × (1 − q)`, `scale = 1.045 − 0.045q` (the projected image is slightly oversized when unfocused, as a real projection blooms), `contrast = 0.9 + 0.12q`. The reticle draws over the first 15% of the range (`--ease-draw` equivalent via dashoffset scrub) and fades to 30% opacity once q > 0.6 (the focuser is put away as sharpness arrives). The readout prints the easel's focus distance as a real-feeling number derived from q: `focus = 412mm − 26mm × q`, one decimal, mono, updated only when the displayed value changes (throttled by the loop to value changes ≥0.1mm — no layout thrash, no flicker of digits). At q = 1 the print is tack-sharp and a caption line prints beneath it: "Focused. This is the sharpest this negative gets." — an honest line: the readout never claims more resolution than the source has.
- **Choreography timeline:** Section top reaches viewport top → pin engages; reticle begins drawing (q 0→0.15). q 0.15–0.6 — the pull proper: blur 11.9px → 5.6px, the image's subject separates from its ground; the readout counts down through the 390s. q 0.6–0.9 — reticle recedes; fine detail arrives; contrast reaches the print's own 1.02. q 0.9–1 — final 1.4px of blur resolves; at q=1 the caption prints and the pin releases at the section's end. Scrolling back up reverses the pull exactly (focus is reversible, unlike development — an unfocused projection has lost nothing).
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* shipped tack-sharp (q=1 state) — the no-JS page shows the focused print and its caption. *Scrubbing:* as above; the image is never interactive mid-pull. *Hover/focus:* the easel print is not a control; the section's only focusable items are its links/caption actions, which behave normally and do not disturb q. *Scrolled-past:* the print remains sharp above; re-entering from below re-arms the pull in reverse (q follows scroll, so this is automatic). *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* the pin is shorter (120vh) and the pull identical — touch scroll is still scroll; if the device reports coarse pointer *and* the section is reached via a jump (anchor), q is set to 1 directly (a jumped-to focuser is assumed focused).
- **Content-merge:** The enlarger examines one specific work — typically the hero print's negative, or the piece the page most wants studied (a detail-rich photograph whose sharpness rewards attention). Its caption carries the negative's real data (film stock, frame number) in mono. A page whose imagery has no detail to reward must not include an enlarger section (§9: a focus pull on a soft image is a promise broken).
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** Shipped sharp; JS arms the blur only when the section's sticky stage is confirmed measurable. Watchdog: if q has not reached 1 within **2000ms** of the section's bottom passing the viewport centre (pin mis-measured, sticky unsupported), q is forced to 1 over 300ms. If `position: sticky` is unsupported, the section renders unpinned and sharp — the pull is an enhancement, the print is the content.
- **Reduced motion:** No pin, no pull: the section is a normal section with a sharp print and its caption; the reticle is printed at 30% as a quiet graphic, the readout prints the final distance.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — pin 160vh, easel print up to 880px wide. 768–1199px — pin 120vh, blur ceiling 11px (smaller screens show blur less). <768px — **no pin**: the pull becomes a short time-based run (1400ms `--ease-develop`) fired on entry, because a pinned section on a phone eats the page; if that run's trigger is missed, the §5.2 auto-finish logic applies (sharp within 1500ms of entry).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Low-resolution source:* the caption's honesty line exists for this — if the source is under 1200px on its long edge, the build must reduce the blur ceiling to 8px (a heavy blur on a soft image produces mush, not focus) and the caption reads "Focused — the negative is a half-frame; this is all the sharpness it holds." *Scroll jumps (keyboard Page Down, anchors):* q follows the lerped loop, so a jump produces a fast pull (≤ ~400ms to settle) — acceptable, and the watchdog covers pathological cases. *Multiple enlargers:* forbidden (§5.4) — a build with two pinned focus sections fails §10.
- **Accessibility:** The blur is decorative; alt text and caption carry the content. The readout is `aria-hidden` (it is a focus *simulation*; announcing changing millimetres would be noise) — a visually-hidden static line states the final focus distance for assistive tech. Pinning must never trap focus: the section's focusable elements remain in normal document order inside the sticky stage, and the stage is exactly one viewport tall so nothing is ever off-screen while pinned.
- **Tokens:** `--room`, `--redwash`, `--paper`, `--paper-dim`, `--glow`, `--ease-draw`; consumes q (element-local).
- **Performance & mobile:** Blur is the most expensive filter in the theme — one image only, composited, and the loop writes at most three properties per frame. Mobile replaces the pin with a 1400ms run (above) and caps blur at 10px; devices reporting `prefers-reduced-data` or a hardwareConcurrency ≤ 2 get the sharp static variant via the same gate as reduced motion.

### 6.6 Stamped exposure notations

- **What & why:** As each print completes, its border stamps in — a small rubber-stamp impression in the notation margin carrying that image's real darkroom recipe: aperture, seconds, grade, and the hand-work ("f/8 · 12s · Gr 3 · +1 dodge on the left hand"). The stamp is the theme's anti-slop made visible (§9): the recipe is specific to the image, printed on the artefact itself, and it is the record that the law (§1) audits — the development the visitor just watched must be the development this stamp describes.
- **Structure:** A `<span class="stamp">` inside the print's notation margin (the 26px bottom border of §4.4), absolutely positioned at a slight rotation (seeded per print: rotation = −2.4° + (index × 37 mod 50)/10°, deterministic from DOM order — never runtime-random, so two builds stamp identically). The stamp is text (mono, 10.5px, uppercase, letterspaced) inside a 1.5px ring border, inked in `--glow` on paper — used at stamp size as a *graphic* (ring + large glyphs are the glow's sanctioned use, §2.1; the stamp's text at 10.5px is `--ink` at 88% with the glow ring doing the colour work — the pairing ruling of §2.2 is honoured by keeping small text ink-dark). A faint ink-bloom pseudo-element (the same text, blurred 0.8px, at 35% opacity) sits beneath for the wet-ink edge.
- **Motion spec:** At the print's p = 1: the stamp presses in over **180ms** `--ease-stamp` — from scale 1.14, opacity 0, blur 1.2px to scale 1.0, opacity 1, blur 0, with the easing's overshoot giving the press its settle; the ink bloom fades in over the following 240ms. Within a tray or line, stamps stagger by **90ms** in print order. The stamp is also the print's completion signal in the choreography (§13): nothing else in the room needs to announce "done".
- **Choreography timeline:** t=0 — print reaches p=1 (squeegee completed at its 0.85). t=0–180ms — press. t=180–420ms — bloom settles; the print is now a finished, documented object; if the print is on the line (§6.4), its pin drop follows at t=180ms in parallel (the stamp and the pin are the same handling moment). t>420ms — static forever; stamps do not animate again.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* shipped printed — the stamp is in the DOM, visible, unrotated by animation (its static rotation is part of its printed state). *Pressing:* 180ms, pointer-events none. *Hover (on the parent print):* the stamp does not move; on the flip (§6.7) the stamp is on the front and stays with the front face. *Focus:* n/a (not a control). *Scrolled-past:* printed; a print scrolled past unviewed still carries its stamp when the visitor arrives — the record precedes the audience. *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* identical.
- **Content-merge:** The notation *is* content — the actual exposure data of the actual photograph, written by the photographer. A build must source each stamp's text from the same record that drove the print's development (§6.13's log is that record's table form). Generic stamp text ("f/8 · 12s" on every print) is the theme's cardinal sin (§9, failure mode 1).
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** Shipped printed. If a print's development was armed but its stamp event is missed (interrupted run), the print's own 1500ms auto-finish (§6.1) also fires the stamp — the two are bound in one completion function, so a finished print without its stamp is impossible by construction; §10's gate greps for it anyway.
- **Reduced motion:** Printed, no press animation; the bloom is at its settled 35%.
- **Breakpoints:** Identical at all sizes — the stamp is 10.5px mono at ≥1200px and 768–1199px; <768px the notation margin grows to 30px and the stamp may wrap to two lines rather than shrink below 10px (the record is never made illegible to save space).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Long notations* (extensive dodging/burning): the stamp ring becomes a rounded rectangle and the text wraps to two lines inside it; beyond ~64 characters the full recipe moves to the notation back (§6.7) and the stamp carries the short form ("f/8 · 12s · Gr 3 · see back"). *Prints on safelight grounds:* the stamp lands on the paper margin as always — paper is its ground, so the §2.2 glow-on-safelight prohibition never arises. *Duplicate exposures* (the same image printed twice on a page — e.g. hero and index): each printing carries its own stamp; if the exposures differ (a reprint at a different grade), the stamps differ and the log (§6.13) lists both — the room does not pretend two printings are one.
- **Accessibility:** The stamp is real text, read by assistive tech as part of the figure's description (it sits inside the `<figcaption>` flow). Its rotation is visual only. Contrast: ink text on paper 11.14:1; the glow ring is decorative.
- **Tokens:** `--paper`, `--ink`, `--glow`, `--ease-stamp`.
- **Performance & mobile:** One 180ms transform/opacity animation per print; the bloom is a static blurred duplicate (no ongoing cost). Mobile: two-line wrap rule (above); no other change.

### 6.7 Tong-clack flip

- **What & why:** A print has two sides. The front is the photograph; the back is where the printer wrote everything — the full exposure record, the paper, the chemistry, the notes. Clicking a print clacks it over: a two-frame tong jolt, then the print flips to its notation back. It is the theme's principal interaction and its knowledge architecture: the beautiful surface is the front, but the *craft* lives on the back, and the visitor who flips prints is reading the lab's real notebook one card at a time.
- **Structure:** Each flippable print is a `<div class="print-flip">` with two faces: `.face--front` (the §6.1/§6.4 print, complete with border, image and stamp) and `.face--back` (a paper card: `--paper` ground, `--ink` mono text — the full notation in a ruled list, the print's title and date, and one pencil-treatment line, §3). The flip container is a real `<button>`-wrapped control: the button covers the print and carries the accessible name; the faces are presentational inside it. 3D: `perspective: 1400px` on the wrapper, `transform-style: preserve-3d`, `backface-visibility: hidden` on faces. The back's height equals the front's (the wrapper's aspect is the print's aspect) — a print and its back are the same sheet.
- **Motion spec:** On activation: a **60ms clack** — translateY(1px) + rotate(0.4°) and back, two frames of jolt, with an optional 28ms mechanical click *only* if the site ships audio and the visitor has enabled sound (off by default; §12's voice rules govern the toggle's label) — then the flip: rotateY 0→180° over **520ms** `--ease-tongs`. Flip back is the same path reversed (460ms — a practised hand is quicker the second time). Only one print per line/tray may be face-down... face-up-back at a time in a *line* section (turning one back flips the previously turned print home over 460ms, staggered 80ms later) — on a real line, you hold one print at a time; in tray grids, multiple backs may show.
- **Choreography timeline:** t=0 — activation. t=0–60ms — clack jolt. t=60ms — flip begins. t≈310ms — edge-on: the paper's edge catches a 1-frame `--paper-hi` highlight line (the sheet's thickness, lit). t=580ms — back face lands, flat; a 120ms settle of the back's shadow. The back's content is fully rendered from t=0 (it is in the DOM) — the flip reveals, it does not load.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* front showing. *Hover (desktop):* the §6.1/§6.4 lift (6px, contrast +0.04) — the print offers itself to the tongs. *Focus:* lift + `--glow` ring on the button; the ring is the enlarger light finding the print. *Active:* clack + flip as above. *Flipped:* back showing; hover on the back does not lift (you are reading it; it lies flat on the bench), focus ring persists on the button. *Scrolled-past:* flip state persists per print for the session (a `data-turned` flag) — the visitor's handling is remembered; prints never reset behind their back. *Disabled:* the flip is disabled (button `disabled`, print shown front, a printed line in the notation margin reads "back not available for this frame") only when a print genuinely has no back content — which §10's gate treats as a build defect unless the frame is §6.18's unexposed one. *Touch:* tap = same path; no hover lift; the clack jolt is halved (0.2°) so it reads as a tap-acknowledgement, not a shake.
- **Content-merge:** The back carries the print's *own* full record: exposure (matching the front's stamp, extended), paper stock and grade, developer and dilution, the dodging/burning map in words, edition number, and the photographer's note. It is the difference between decoration and documentation — a back that repeats the front's caption fails §9.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** If 3D transforms are unsupported, the flip degrades to a 180ms crossfade between faces (`--ease-develop`) — the information path is identical. If JS never runs, the back's content is not lost: it is printed as a `<details>`-style notation block beneath the print in the no-JS layout (the build ships backs as `<noscript>`-visible blocks under each print; JS folds them into the flip). A flip interrupted mid-rotation (element removed, tab hidden) resolves to the nearest face on return — a **700ms watchdog** from activation snaps `rotateY` to 0 or 180, never an edge.
- **Reduced motion:** No clack, no rotation: the faces crossfade over 120ms, or, at the strictest setting, swap instantly. The information path is unchanged.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — perspective 1400px, full choreography. 768–1199px — perspective 1100px, flip 480ms. <768px — prints are near full-width; a 3D flip of a tall print can clip against the tray rim, so the flip axis switches to rotateX (the print tips toward the reader, like lifting the near edge with tongs) at 500ms, perspective 900px.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Very tall prints (portrait 4:5 at mobile):* the rotateX variant (above) plus a temporary z-index raise so the tipping print clears its neighbours. *Print inside the pinned enlarger (§6.5):* the easel print is a projection — it has no back and is not flippable; its data lives in its caption. *Keyboard rapid-repeat:* activations during a flip are queued to one (the current flip completes, then the queued one runs) — tongs finish one motion before starting another. *Back content longer than the face:* the back scrolls internally (`overflow:auto`, styled thin `--tray-hi` scrollbar) — the sheet is the sheet; its writing may be long.
- **Accessibility:** A real `<button aria-pressed>` with the name "Turn over print: [title]" / "Turn back print: [title]"; the state is announced by the pressed state, not by live regions. Both faces are in the accessibility tree in order (front description, then back) with the hidden face marked `inert` + `aria-hidden` while turned away — content is never focusable on an invisible face. Touch target: the whole print (≥ 120×90px in practice) — far above minimums. Focus ring per §6.1.
- **Tokens:** `--paper`, `--ink`, `--paper-dim`, `--paper-hi`, `--glow`, `--ease-tongs`, `--ease-develop`.
- **Performance & mobile:** One 3D transform per interaction on one layer; backs are DOM content (text) — no assets. Mobile: rotateX variant (above).

### 6.8 Exposure-timer progress

- **What & why:** The page's progress indicator is the darkroom's exposure timer, mounted at the top of the room: a dial whose hand turns as the visitor travels the page, a safelight-red strip that fills beside it, and a mono readout that counts the exposure in seconds. The reader's position *is* a developing time — the page is in the tray with its prints, and the timer is the proof. It is also the third printing of the page's one progress value: dial angle, strip width and readout numerals are three renderings of a single number, and they must agree at every instant (§10 audits this).
- **Structure:** Inside the header (§6.11): an inline SVG dial (a 34px circle: tick ring for the 12 seconds of the reference exposure, a hand, a centre pin), a horizontal strip (the `--glow` fill on a `--tray` track, 3px tall, flex-growing between dial and nav), and a `<span class="timer-readout">` in 15px mono `--glow-hi`. The reference exposure is the hero print's stamped time (§6.6) — the dial's full sweep *means* "the hero's exposure, elapsed across the whole page". If a page has no hero print, the reference exposure is the first print's, and the dial's tick count is that print's seconds (the SVG ticks are generated from the record, not hard-coded to 12).
- **Motion spec:** All three renderings are driven by the shared loop from page progress `s ∈ [0,1]` (scrollable distance travelled, lerped 0.12): hand angle = `s × 330°` (a 30° reserve keeps the hand off its own stop pin at rest and at full); strip width = `s × 100%`; readout `T+{round(s × Tref)}s / {Tref}s` where Tref is the reference exposure in seconds, updated only when the rounded value changes. There is no independent animation: the timer is a gauge, and a gauge that moves on its own is lying (§9).
- **Choreography timeline:** Continuous with scroll. At s=0 the hand rests at −30° (before the first tick — the timer is set but not started). The first tick is crossed as the hero's development completes in a typical reading (the map in §13 aligns s≈0.08 with the hero's p=1 on a standard page — a deliberate rhyme, not a mechanism: the two values are independent and may drift on unusual pages, which is honest, because the timer measures the *page*, not the print). At s=1 the hand rests at 330°, the strip is full, the readout prints the full reference time, and the strip's glow settles (its shadow halves — the exposure is done, the lamp dims its effort).
- **State matrix:** *Rest (s=0):* hand at rest angle, strip empty, readout `T+0s / 12s`. *Scrolling:* all three track s. *Hover/focus:* the timer is not a control; no states — but the dial is a link home on pages where the header wordmark is absent (then it takes button states per §6.11). *Scrolled-past / at end:* the full state persists; scrolling up reverses all three exactly (a timer can be wound back; a print cannot undevelop — the asymmetry is intentional and is why the timer measures the page, not a print). *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* identical; the readout's tabular numerals prevent jitter.
- **Content-merge:** The timer's reference is a real print's real exposure, and its ticks are that exposure's seconds. It is not an abstract progress bar wearing a costume: change the hero print and the dial, ticks and readout all change, because they are generated from the record (§6.13's log lists the same Tref as its first row on the worked-example page).
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** The shipped state is the *full* timer (hand at 330°, strip full, readout complete) — a no-JS visitor has, by definition, the whole page available, and the timer says so. JS arms it to s=0 on load. If the loop dies, a **2000ms watchdog** comparing the readout's printed value against actual scroll position snaps all three renderings to the true s — a stuck timer is a lying timer (§9).
- **Reduced motion:** The three renderings step discretely: the hand jumps tick to tick (one tick per Tref-th of the page), the strip fills in Tref steps, the readout counts — no continuous sweep. All values still agree.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — dial 34px, strip full flex width, readout shown. 768–1199px — dial 30px, readout shown, strip min-width 120px. <768px — the dial is dropped (the header is narrow; a 26px dial reads as a logo, not a gauge): strip + readout only, readout 13px. The three-renderings rule degrades to two, stated here so the §10 audit expects two on mobile.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Very short pages* (s reaches 1 within one viewport): the timer completes almost immediately — correct: a one-print page is a short exposure. The build must not stretch the page to flatter the timer. *Tref > 60s* (long-exposure work — the theme's namesake case): the dial gains a minute ring (a second, inner hand at s × 330° / 60 per minute) and ticks mark 10s intervals with labels at 0/30/60; the readout format becomes `T+{m}:{ss} / {Tref}s`. The function is unchanged; only the dial's furniture scales. *Tref not an integer:* exposures are whole seconds in this room (§5.4 counters) — a fractional Tref in source data is rounded in the log and the stamp first, and the timer consumes the rounded record.
- **Accessibility:** The timer is decorative progress furniture: `role="progressbar"` on the strip with `aria-valuenow` as a percentage (updated at most once per second), the dial and readout `aria-hidden` (three renderings, one announcement path). It never steals focus and is not a control.
- **Tokens:** `--glow`, `--glow-hi`, `--tray`, `--tray-hi`, `--room`, `--ease-sway` (hand settle only).
- **Performance & mobile:** Three property writes per frame from the shared loop (transform, width via transform scaleX on the fill — width is never animated directly — and a text update on change). Negligible. Mobile: two-rendering variant (above).

### 6.9 Wet-gloss sheen

- **What & why:** Prints in this room stay wet for a while. As a finished print passes the viewport's centre, a gloss sheen slides across its surface — wet paper catching the safelight at the angle of travel. It is the print's *state* made visible (fresh off the line, still drying), and it is the theme's subtlest motion: miss it and nothing is lost; catch it and the room feels humid.
- **Structure:** A pseudo-element gloss layer over the print's image area: a linear-gradient band (transparent → `--paper-hi` at 14% → transparent, 130% wide), `mix-blend-mode: screen` where supported, clipped to the image (never the border — gloss is on the photograph, not the paper margin), `pointer-events: none`. The layer exists only on prints flagged `data-wet` — prints that completed development during this session (a print developed in a previous visit, or in the no-JS state, is dry stock and carries no sheen; §6.2's wipe halves its strength, and it fades entirely 90 seconds after the print's completion — the room dries its work).
- **Motion spec:** Driven by the shared loop: the band's translateX maps the print's centre offset from the viewport centre across a ±35%-of-viewport-height band to −120% → +120% of the print's width — the sheen moves *because the print moves through the light*, never on a timer. Opacity peaks at 0.5 (of the layer's own 14% gradient — an effective ~7% brightening) at exact centre and falls to 0 at the band's edges. No easing is applied beyond the loop's lerp 0.12 — the light does not ease.
- **Choreography timeline:** Continuous and positional. A print entering from below: sheen absent → band's leading edge enters at the band's lower threshold → crosses as the print crosses centre → gone at the upper threshold. Total visible travel ≈ 70% of a viewport height of scrolling. After §6.2's wipe the same pass runs at half strength; after 90s, no pass at all.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* no sheen (print outside the band, or dry). *In band:* as above. *Hover:* the sheen freezes at its current position while the print is lifted (§6.7) — a held print holds its light. *Focus:* same freeze. *Active:* during the flip (§6.7) the sheen is removed (the back has no gloss; the front's gloss returns when flipped home, if still within its wet window). *Scrolled-past:* prints above the viewport are dry or drying; the sheen never runs off-screen (the loop gates by proximity, §14). *Disabled:* the wet flag is never set when filters/blend modes are unsupported. *Touch:* identical (scroll is scroll); the band widens to ±45% of viewport height, because touch scrolling is faster and the sheen must remain catchable.
- **Content-merge:** The sheen exists only on prints that actually developed in this session — it is a property of the work's freshness, not a hover gimmick applied to stock imagery. A page assembled from pre-developed prints (a listing page using §6.12 frames at small sizes) does not use it at all.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** The layer is an enhancement with no content role; its failure modes are absence (blend unsupported → plain low-opacity gradient, still positional) or removal. A **per-print 90s timer** (the drying timer) removes `data-wet` — if that timer is throttled, the flag is also checked and cleared by the loop on the print's next viewport entry. A sheen can never obscure: max effective brightening ~7%, and it never covers the notation margin.
- **Reduced motion:** No sheen. Wet state is conveyed by the print's notation ("still drying" never needs saying — the stamp's bloom, §6.6, is slightly stronger on wet prints in the static state: a printed difference, not a moving one).
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — band ±35% vh, gradient 130% width. 768–1199px — band ±40% vh. <768px — band ±45% vh (above), gradient 110% width, peak opacity 0.4 (small prints, less gloss).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Dark images:* on a near-black photograph the screen-blend sheen can bloom — the layer's opacity is multiplied by the print's mean luminance class (the build tags prints `data-lum="dark|mid|bright"` from the image's actual average, computed at build time or first load; dark prints cap at 0.3). *The hero print:* its sheen is §6.2's business until wiped; after the wipe, §6.9 governs at half strength. *Prints inside the enlarger:* projections have no wet state — excluded.
- **Accessibility:** Purely decorative; `aria-hidden`, no information. Its motion is a transform of a gradient — no flashing (peak luminance change ~7%, far below any photosensitivity threshold, and it moves at scroll speed, not autonomously).
- **Tokens:** `--paper-hi` (the gloss layer), plus the shared loop (§5.3); the layer's removal is §6.2's wipe, which carries its own tokens.
- **Performance & mobile:** One transform per wet print in the band — at most two prints are ever in the band simultaneously (§4.2 sequencing). Blend mode falls back gracefully. Mobile: wider band, lower peak (above).

### 6.10 Tray-ripple ambience

- **What & why:** The room idles chemically. Tray blocks carry a slow ripple shimmer — the surface of standing developer, barely disturbed — so a section the visitor is reading still feels like a bath, not a box. It is the theme's one purely ambient motion, and it is deliberately the weakest: ambience with a source in the fiction, at an amplitude that survives being ignored.
- **Structure:** A pseudo-element on each `.tray`: two overlapping radial-gradient rings (transparent centres, `--paper-hi` at 3% on the rim), sized 140% of the tray, positioned at the tray's upper-left (under the lamp), animated by background-position drift. No canvas, no SVG filters — gradients and position only, so the cost is a repaint of a background layer, and the fallback is a static gradient that still reads as a sheen.
- **Motion spec:** Two cycles, both very slow: a **9000ms** linear drift of the gradient position by 24px along the tray's diagonal (out and back — a 18000ms full breath), and a **14000ms** opacity breath between 0.02 and 0.05 effective. The ripple never reflects content, never moves text, and never exceeds 5% brightening anywhere. It runs only while the tray is within 1 viewport of the screen (loop-gated, §14) and pauses entirely when the tab is hidden (the loop itself stops — no separate handling).
- **Choreography timeline:** Continuous. On a tray's first entry the ripple fades in from 0 over 1200ms `--ease-develop` (a still tray that suddenly shimmers is a jump scare; the chemicals were always moving, the visitor just arrived). Phase is per-tray (offset by tray index × 1300ms) so adjacent trays never ripple in sync — synchronised ambience is a screensaver (§9).
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* shipped as a static sheen (the gradient at its mid position, 3.5%) — present but still. *Ambient:* as above. *Hover/focus within the tray:* no change — the ripple does not acknowledge the visitor; it is not an interaction surface. *Active (a print developing in the tray):* the ripple's amplitude doubles locally around the developing print (to 10% in a 200px radius) — agitation: a print in the tray moves the chemistry. This is the one state change, and it is caused by the fiction, not the cursor. *Scrolled-past:* gated off. *Disabled:* unsupported gradients → flat tray ground; nothing is lost. *Touch:* identical.
- **Content-merge:** The ripple lives on tray surfaces — the containers that, in the fiction, hold liquid. It is never applied to paper surfaces (prints do not ripple; §6.9 is their surface behaviour), never to the room ground (the air does not ripple), never behind body text at reading sizes (tray *labels* and headings sit on the tray's rim zone where the gradient is at its weakest — the build keeps the gradient's bright rim out of the tray's top 64px where its label sits).
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** No watchdog is needed for a decorative gradient; its absence is its fallback. If the animation is removed by the platform (reduced motion, forced colors), the static sheen remains or vanishes with no content impact.
- **Reduced motion:** The drift and breath stop; the static sheen (3.5%) remains as a printed quality of the tray — a tray photographed, not filmed.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — full spec. 768–1199px — drift distance 16px. <768px — **static only**: the drift is cut (small trays, small batteries — §14's cut order spends here first); the static sheen remains.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Trays with heavy content (the log table, §6.13):* the ripple is disabled on the log's tray — figures must not swim (§9); the log tray gets the static sheen at 2%. *Long trays:* the gradient scales to the tray (140% of its box), so a tall tray's ripple stays a surface effect, not a repeating pattern — `background-repeat: no-repeat` is mandatory. *Agitation overlap:* two adjacent developing prints in one tray do not stack amplitude beyond 12% (the loop clamps).
- **Accessibility:** Decorative, `aria-hidden` in effect (a background). The luminance variation (≤5%, slow) is far below photosensitivity thresholds and never flashes.
- **Tokens:** `--tray`, `--tray-hi`, `--paper-hi`, `--ease-develop` (entry fade only).
- **Performance & mobile:** Background-position animation on large layers can be repaint-expensive on low-end GPUs: the layer is promoted (`will-change` is *not* used; the loop instead toggles the animation only for in-range trays), and §14's budget caps simultaneous rippling trays at 2. Mobile: static (above) — the cheapest variant is the default there.

### 6.11 Safelight header & tray navigation

- **What & why:** The room needs a door, a sign, and a way to move between benches. The header is the darkroom's doorway wall: the room's name in developed type, the safelight strip burning along its top edge, the exposure timer (§6.8) mounted beside the door, and navigation links named for the room's stations — The Line, The Enlarger, The Log, Order a Print. Navigation in this theme is not a menu bar pasted on; it is the labelled doors of one room, and moving between pages is moving between trays (§6.16).
- **Structure:** A `<header class="room-head">`, sticky at the top, `--room` ground with a 1px `--tray-hi` bottom rule. Topmost: the safelight strip — a 4px band whose lit portion is §6.8's strip (the header *contains* the timer; the strip is the header's own top edge, lit by progress). Below it, one row: wordmark (Archivo 600, 19px, `--paper-hi`, developed — it uses §6.3's treatment at small scale on first load only), the dial (§6.8), the readout, then `<nav>` as a list of station links. Each link is a *tray label*: `--paper` text on a `--tray` chip with a `--tray-hi` top rim, its station name in Archivo 500 13px uppercase, and a mono station number (01–04) — the room's stations are numbered like trays in a process line.
- **Motion spec:** On first load: the strip glows up over **600ms** `--ease-develop` (the safelight warming — the room's first event, §13 beat 1), the wordmark develops over 700ms (§6.3's curve, whole-word, no letter split at this size), and the station chips settle in staggered 60ms from 4px above (`--ease-stamp` without overshoot — chips are placed, not stamped). Sticky behaviour: the header is 64px tall expanded; after 240px of scroll it compacts to 48px (the strip and readout remain; the wordmark shrinks to its initials mark) over 240ms `--ease-tongs`. The current station's chip carries a lit state: its top rim becomes `--glow` and a 6px record dot (a filled circle, the room's "this tray is in use" light) appears beside its number.
- **Choreography timeline:** t=0 — strip begins warming. t=600ms — strip lit; wordmark mid-develop. t=700ms — wordmark full; chips settled by t≈940ms (4 chips × 60ms stagger + 240ms settle). The hero print (§6.1) starts at t=250ms, *under* the still-warming lamp — the room lights before the work begins, but the work does not wait for full light. On scroll past 240px: compact over 240ms. On return to top: expand over 240ms. Neither transition ever moves page content (the header is `position: sticky` within its own reserved height — the compact state overlays the header's own lower padding zone, so there is no layout shift; §3.1 law 2 applies to the whole page).
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* shipped expanded, strip shown *full* (the no-JS timer state, §6.8), wordmark developed, current station lit if the page declares one. *Hover (link):* chip lifts 2px, its rim lights `--paper-hi` over 160ms. *Focus:* `--glow` 2px ring, offset 2px. *Active:* chip presses 1px (`--ease-stamp`, 120ms) and §6.16's transition begins. *Current:* the lit-rim + record-dot state (persistent, not animated). *Scrolled (compact):* as above. *Disabled:* a station with no page (a room not built yet) is not rendered as a link at all — the room has no fake doors (§9); it appears, if at all, as a printed line in the footer (§6.17) reading "The scanning bench — not yet built." *Touch:* chips are ≥44px tall in the expanded header; in compact state the nav collapses to a single "Stations" chip that opens §6.12-pattern index sheet (a paper sheet sliding up over 300ms `--ease-tongs`, listing stations full-width) — the room's door list, on paper.
- **Content-merge:** The stations are the site's real sections/pages, named in the room's vocabulary but never at the cost of findability: "Order a Print" is the form, "The Log" is the services/record page. A nav label that a first-time visitor cannot map to its destination fails §12's voice test — the fiction serves the wayfinding, never the reverse.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** Shipped expanded and fully linked — every state (compact, sheet, lit rims) is JS/CSS enhancement. If the sticky header misbehaves in an embedded webview, a media-query fallback (`@supports not (position: sticky)`) renders it static at the top; navigation is never the price of an effect. The compact/expand has a **1000ms watchdog**: if scroll events stop firing mid-transition (some webviews), the header settles to whichever state the current scrollY dictates.
- **Reduced motion:** No warm-up (strip lit, wordmark printed), no compacting (the header stays expanded — height changes are motion too), the stations sheet swaps instantly.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — full row: wordmark, dial, strip, readout, 4–5 chips. 768–1199px — wordmark shortens to the room's short name, dial 30px, chips keep numbers + names. <768px — compact pattern by default (strip + readout + Stations chip + sheet), dial dropped per §6.8.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Many stations (>5):* the header caps at 5 chips; further stations live in the footer's tray (§6.17) and the contact index (§6.12) — a darkroom door wall with nine doors is a corridor, not a room. *Long station names:* chips truncate with an ellipsis at 16ch — station names are chosen short (§12); the full name appears in the sheet and footer. *The current page is a sub-page (a single print):* its parent station is lit, and the print's own name appears in the readout's place... no — the readout is the timer's; the sub-page's name appears as a printed crumb in the tray label of its first section (§7, detail pages).
- **Accessibility:** A real `<header>` + `<nav aria-label="Darkroom stations">`; the current station carries `aria-current="page"`. The strip's progressbar semantics live on §6.8's element. Focus order: wordmark/home → stations in order → sheet trigger (mobile). All chips ≥44px touch height in both header states (compact chips are 48px tall by design — compact refers to the wordmark zone, never to target size).
- **Tokens:** `--room`, `--tray`, `--tray-hi`, `--paper`, `--paper-hi`, `--paper-dim`, `--glow`, `--ease-develop`, `--ease-stamp`, `--ease-tongs`.
- **Performance & mobile:** The header's animations are one-time (load) plus one 240ms height-class transition; the strip is §6.8's cost. Mobile: the sheet pattern (above) replaces the row — one extra DOM node, no library.

### 6.12 Contact-sheet index

- **What & why:** A listing page in a darkroom is a contact sheet: the whole roll, printed small, frame by frame, each frame numbered in the margin the way a printer pencils frame numbers on a contact sheet. The index is how visitors browse the archive — works, products, sessions — and each frame is a miniature print that develops in miniature when it arrives, because on a contact sheet even the thumbnails were made in the tray.
- **Structure:** A `<section class="contact-sheet">`: a `--tray` ground (the sheet's board) holding a grid of `<article class="frame">` items. Each frame: a small print (paper border 6px, notation margin 18px) with the image, a frame number in mono at the margin's left ("№ 07" — numbered from the real DOM order, never hand-written per item), the item's title in the margin, and one line of its key data (its exposure for photographs; its price or date for listed items — the frame's *datum*, set in mono). Frames link to their detail pages. A filter row above the sheet is a row of *grade chips* (§11) — filtering a contact sheet is choosing which frames to print.
- **Motion spec:** Frames develop on entry with §5.2's function, time-driven over **1400ms** `--ease-develop`, staggered **90ms** in reading order, in batches: only the frames in the entering row develop together (the sheet is contact-printed a row at a time). Each frame's squeegee (§6.2) runs at its 0.85 at the small-print duration (420ms). Frame numbers are printed from the start (they are pencilled before development — a contact sheet's numbers exist before its images). Hover: the frame lifts 4px and its number lights `--glow-hi` — the printer's finger on the frame they mean.
- **Choreography timeline:** Row enters → t=0 first frame begins; frames complete at 1400 + (n−1)×90ms; their stamps (§6.6, short form only — aperture + seconds) land at each completion, staggered by the same 90ms. A 4-frame row is fully printed in ≈ **1.7s**. Rows further down wait for their own entry — the sheet develops as it is read, never all at once (§4.2).
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* shipped printed — all frames developed, numbered, still (the no-JS contact sheet is complete). *Developing:* per frame; the frame's link is live throughout (a visitor may open a frame mid-development; the detail page does not care). *Hover:* lift + number light (above). *Focus:* lift + `--glow` ring on the frame's link. *Active:* §6.16 transition to the detail page. *Scrolled-past:* developed forever. *Disabled:* a frame whose detail page does not exist is not a link — it renders as a frame with a printed "not printed" line in its margin (an honest unprinted frame), never a dead link. *Touch:* no hover; the number light is skipped; tap follows the link.
- **Content-merge:** The frames *are* the listed items — the photograph, the product, the session — with their real numbers (frame № from DOM order; the datum from the item's record). The contact sheet is the archive's own tool, so nothing about it is a costume: remove the fiction and the grid still works as a listing; keep it and the listing explains how the archive is made.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** Shipped printed. Per-frame 1500ms auto-finish (§5.2) applies; a sheet-level watchdog mirrors §6.4's: **4000ms** after a row's entry, any undeveloped frame in it completes over 300ms. Filter changes (grade chips) never re-develop frames — filtered-out frames are removed from display, filtered-in frames appear printed (they were printed already; the visitor is re-sorting the sheet, not re-printing it).
- **Reduced motion:** All frames printed; filtering swaps instantly; no lifts.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — 4 frames per row, frame image aspect from the item's negative format. 768–1199px — 3 per row. <768px — 2 per row, frame notation margin 16px, datum line may wrap to two lines (numbers never shrink below 10px mono).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Huge archives (100+ frames):* the sheet paginates by roll — "Roll 2" is a new sheet section with its own frame numbers continuing (№ 37…), because frame numbers are the archive's, not the page's. Virtualised/infinite loading is refused (§11): a contact sheet is finite by nature; §7's listing pattern uses explicit rolls. *Mixed aspect ratios:* frames align by their top edges on the sheet's row line; margins below vary — a contact sheet is not a masonry wall, and forcing uniform crops would falsify the negatives (§9). *An item with no image:* its frame is §6.18-pattern blank paper with the printed datum — the frame exists, the exposure doesn't.
- **Accessibility:** The grid is a list of links with real names ("Print: Low Water, Morecambe Bay — frame 7"); frame numbers are decorative duplicates of position and are `aria-hidden` (position is announced by list semantics). Filter chips are real buttons with `aria-pressed`. Contrast: margin text `--ink` on `--paper` 11.14:1; the number's lit state `--glow-hi` is on the tray ground beside the frame — checked at 5.21:1 against `--room`-adjacent tray tones for the number's 13px mono (the number sits on the sheet's board, which the build renders at `--redwash` behind the number zone to keep it ≥5:1 — a build detail the gate verifies).
- **Tokens:** `--tray`, `--redwash`, `--paper`, `--ink`, `--paper-dim`, `--glow`, `--glow-hi`, `--ease-develop`, `--ease-tongs`.
- **Performance & mobile:** Thumbnails are small responsive images (≤ 480px wide, ≤ 60KB each); a row's development is four filter runs of 1400ms, staggered — the loop's cost is bounded by the one-row-at-a-time rule. Mobile: 2-per-row (above), developments shorten to 1100ms.

### 6.13 The exposure log

- **What & why:** Every darkroom keeps the book: a ruled log where each exposure is entered — frame, aperture, seconds, grade, result. On the site, the log is the table that carries the room's records in public: the services and bench-times of a lab, the session list of a photographer, the edition data of a print run. It is also the law's ledger (§1): the stamps (§6.6), the timer's reference (§6.8) and the footer's totals (§6.17) are all printings of rows that live here, and the log's totals row must sum its own columns — a table whose total is typed rather than summed is a lie with rules drawn around it (§9).
- **Structure:** A `<table class="log">` on a paper ground inside a tray (the book lies in a tray to protect it — the fiction is thin and everyone is fine with it): ruled rows (`--tray-hi` hairlines on paper), a mono header row (FRAME · APERTURE · TIME · GRADE · RESULT/DESTINATION), body rows in Archivo with mono data columns right-aligned per §3.3, and a totals row separated by a double rule. On the worked-example page the log has six entries whose seconds sum to the printed total of 47s (§8.2) — in any build, the totals are computed from the rendered rows by the build (and re-verified by §10's DOM audit), never authored separately.
- **Motion spec:** The log does not develop (records are not photographs). Its one motion is the *entry write-in*: when the log scrolls into view, its rows are already printed; the totals row's seconds count from 0 to the true sum over **700ms** `--ease-develop`, quantised to whole seconds (§5.4) — the bookkeeper totalling the column in front of you. If a row is added by a live action on the page (an order placed, §6.14), the new row writes in: its text develops (colour/blur only, §6.3's curve) over 500ms and the total re-counts over 400ms to the new sum. Nothing else in the table ever moves.
- **Choreography timeline:** Entry → t=0 rows visible (printed). t=200ms — totals count begins. t=900ms — total settled on the true sum. Live-add path: action completes → row develops 500ms → total recounts 400ms → the footer's third printing (§6.17), if in view, updates in the same frame (all printings of a number change together or the page is inconsistent — the loop updates them from one value).
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* printed, complete. *Hover (on a linked row — rows that are records of prints link to them):* the row's paper brightens one step (`--paper-hi` wash) over 160ms; data cells do not move. *Focus:* `--glow` inset ring on the row link. *Active:* §6.16 transition. *Scrolled-past:* static; the count does not re-run (a total, once struck, stays struck — re-counting on every pass is a cash register, not a ledger). *Disabled:* rows without destinations are plain rows — no hover state, no pointer. *Touch:* no hover; linked rows tap through.
- **Content-merge:** The log's rows are the site's real records — the same data objects that stamp the prints and set the timer. A lab's services page *is* its log (service = an exposure type, bench time = its seconds); a photographer's sessions page is its log. The table is not a theme skin over unrelated data: if the data has no frame/aperture/time/grade-shaped truth, it does not belong in the log — it belongs in body text (§11 refuses the generic data-table skin for arbitrary content).
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** The table is static HTML; the shipped total is the true sum (computed at build time and printed). JS re-computes and *verifies* on load — if the DOM sum and the printed total disagree (a hand-edited build), JS corrects the printed total to the DOM sum and flags the build in console: the audit trail fixes itself toward the rows, never toward the typed number. The count-up is the only animated part; its watchdog is the §5.4 quantised counter's own (700ms, then snap).
- **Reduced motion:** Totals are printed; live-added rows appear printed; no counting.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — full five columns. 768–1199px — GRADE folds into the FRAME cell (as "· Gr 3" appended in mono) — four columns. <768px — the table becomes a stack of log entries (each row a ruled block: frame + result on the first line, aperture/time/grade mono on the second) — a logbook read page by page; the totals row remains a row, double-ruled, at the stack's foot. Horizontal scrolling of a table is forbidden in this theme (paper doesn't scroll sideways; §9).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Long logs (40+ rows):* the book paginates by sitting ("Tuesday's sitting" subheads as ruled section rows), each sitting with its own subtotal and a grand total at the book's foot — sums within sums, all computed. *A row with an unknown value* (a frame whose time wasn't recorded): the cell prints "—" and the row is excluded from the totals, with a footnote under the table: "One frame unrecorded; totals cover the 5 recorded exposures." — the book admits its gaps (§12's voice). *Duplicate printings of one frame* (§6.6's reprint case): separate rows, suffixed frame numbers (07, 07b), both summed.
- **Accessibility:** A real `<table>` with `<th scope>` headers and a `<caption>` naming the book ("Exposure log — Tuesday sitting, 6 exposures, 47 seconds"). Totals are in a `<tfoot>`-equivalent row with `scope="row"`. The count-up is `aria-hidden`; the printed total is the accessible value at all times (JS counts a duplicate visual layer, or sets the text once at the end for AT — implementation must ensure the accessible text is never a mid-count number).
- **Tokens:** `--paper`, `--paper-hi`, `--ink`, `--tray-hi`, `--glow`, `--ease-develop`.
- **Performance & mobile:** A static table plus one 700ms counter. The DOM audit is a single pass over rows on load (<1ms for any sane row count). Mobile: the stacked-entry variant (above).

### 6.14 The print-order form

- **What & why:** The conversion surface is the order desk by the door: a paper form on which the visitor orders a physical print of a photograph the page has developed in front of them. The form's fiction is exact — you are not "submitting a lead", you are placing an order for a sheet of paper that will be put in a tray — and its confirmation is an order slip stamped with a number that comes from the room's own book (§6.13): the order is entered in the log, and its number is the log's next entry. Commerce here is a continuation of the craft the page performed.
- **Structure:** A `<form class="order-desk">` on a `--paper` card (the order pad) inside a tray section: fields for the print (a select whose options are the page's actual prints, pre-set to the print the visitor is looking at when invoked from a print's "Order this print" action), size (a select of the lab's real paper sizes with real prices — prices live *here*, in the form's terms, and nowhere else on the page as numbers), quantity (a stepper built as a test strip — §6.15's pattern in miniature: five graded segments, the chosen quantity's segment fully exposed), name and address (text inputs), and a submit button styled as a paper tag: "Place in the tray". Errors print beneath their fields in the printer's voice (§12) in `--ink` with a `--glow` left rule — on paper, errors are ink, not red alerts.
- **Motion spec:** Field focus: the field's paper brightens (`--paper-hi`) and a `--glow` hairline draws under it over 200ms `--ease-squeegee` — the printer's pencil underlining the line being written. Quantity stepper: each step exposes one more segment over 160ms `--ease-develop`. Submit: the button presses (`--ease-stamp`, 140ms), the form's card lifts 2px (taken by the tongs) and the confirmation replaces it: an order slip (a smaller paper card) that *develops* its key line — "Order № 07 — Low Water, Morecambe Bay, 12×16" — over 900ms §5.2's curve in miniature (text developing on paper, §6.3's treatment), then the room's stamp presses onto the slip at **180ms** `--ease-stamp` with the order number inside the ring. The slip is the visitor's receipt; it remains on the page, printable (`@media print` renders the slip alone, developed, on white).
- **Choreography timeline:** Submit (valid) → t=0 button press; t=140ms card lift; t=200ms form fades to paper-dim (it is being filed) as the slip rises in its place over 300ms `--ease-tongs`; t=500ms slip's key line begins developing; t=1400ms line full; stamp presses (180ms); t≈1600ms — settled. Simultaneously, §6.13's log (if on the page) gains its row per its own write-in path, and §6.17's footer totals update in the same frame. Invalid submit: no choreography — the first error field is focused, its error line *prints* (no animation beyond a 120ms fade) and the form waits; the desk does not shake, bounce or blush (§9: a shaking form is a vending machine).
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* printed form, all fields at paper tone. *Focus:* brighten + underline (above). *Filled:* a completed field's label gains a small mono tick "✓ entered" in `--paper-dim`... in ink at 60% — quiet bookkeeping, not gamification. *Active (submit):* press + the confirmation path. *Error:* the field's underline becomes `--glow` at full width and stays; the error text is printed beneath; correcting the field clears both on the next blur if valid. *Submitting (async):* the button text becomes "In the tray…" and the form is inert for at most the §6.16 ceiling — if the request exceeds **3000ms**, the desk prints "The tray is busy — your order is held, not lost. Check the log in a moment." and re-enables (the order's idempotency key, below, makes a retry safe). *Disabled:* the whole desk is disabled only when ordering is genuinely closed, and then it is replaced by a printed line in its tray: "The order desk is closed this week — the log reopens Monday." — a state with words, not a greyed form. *Touch:* all targets ≥48px; the stepper segments are ≥44px wide each.
- **Content-merge:** The form sells the page's actual prints at the lab's actual sizes and prices; its confirmation writes the room's actual log. Pre-selection from a print (§6.7's back carries "Order this print") means the form is a continuation of handling the object — the visitor has held the print, read its back, and now orders its twin.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** A plain HTML form with a real `action` — with JS disabled it submits conventionally and the server returns a confirmation page styled as the slip (printed, not developed). Every order carries an idempotency key (the slip number is reserved from the log's count at render time: `orderNo = logEntries + 1`, and the key is that number plus the print ID) so a double-submit cannot enter two rows. Watchdog: the 3000ms busy ceiling above; and if the confirmation's develop animation is interrupted, the slip's line snaps to full (the §5.2 floor applies to text-develops too).
- **Reduced motion:** The slip appears printed with its stamp already on; focus underlines swap instantly; no lift, no fade.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — the pad sits in a 6-column tray beside the print it defaults to (5 columns, still developed, small). 768–1199px — pad full width, the print above it. <768px — single column; the size select becomes the test-strip stepper pattern as radio segments (sizes are few and tappable); the slip confirmation is full-width.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *A print sells out mid-session* (edition exhausted): its option in the select is struck through in mono with "(edition complete)" and cannot be chosen — the log's edition row is the source; the form reads it at render. *Quantity above the remaining edition:* the stepper caps at the remaining count and prints why: "Only 3 remain in this edition." — the number traces to the log. *Address validation:* in the printer's voice (§12) — specific, unembarrassed, never "Invalid input". *Prices in other currencies:* the form's terms line is the single source; no other element prints prices, so no second printing can disagree.
- **Accessibility:** Native form semantics: `<label>` per field, errors linked via `aria-describedby`, the error summary (if multiple) is a printed list at the form's head that receives focus on invalid submit. The confirmation slip has `role="status"` and its text is plain, complete and non-animated for AT (the develop is a visual layer over the same text). Focus returns to the slip's heading after confirmation. Contrast: all form text `--ink` on `--paper` 11.14:1; the glow underline is decorative reinforcement, never the only error signal (error text + underline + focus movement).
- **Tokens:** `--paper`, `--paper-hi`, `--ink`, `--glow`, `--tray`, `--ease-squeegee`, `--ease-stamp`, `--ease-tongs`, `--ease-develop`.
- **Performance & mobile:** A form — no ongoing cost; the confirmation path is ~1.6s of small animations. Mobile: radio-segment sizes (above), stepper segments full-width rows of ≥44px.

### 6.15 Test-strip grade scrubber

- **What & why:** Before committing a print, a printer makes a test strip: one image under a stepped mask, five bands of increasing exposure, to choose the grade and time. The scrubber is that strip as a control — the visitor drags across five graded bands of the *same* photograph (Grade 0 through Grade 4 contrast, or five exposure steps) and the main print above re-grades to the band under their finger. It is the theme's comparison tool: for a lab it demonstrates paper grades; for a photographer it shows the same negative printed soft and hard; for a product page it is a before/after that could only exist in a darkroom.
- **Structure:** A `<figure class="test-strip">`: the main print (full §4.4 geometry) and beneath it the strip — a single wide thumbnail of the same image divided into five vertical bands, each band rendered at its grade (contrast steps: Grade 0 = §5.2 contrast 0.78, Grade 1 = 0.9, Grade 2 = 1.02 (the print's normal), Grade 3 = 1.16, Grade 4 = 1.32, with matched brightness compensations −0.04 per step above 2, +0.04 below — the strip is one image with five filter slices, clipped per band). A handle (a tong-grip: a 44px-wide `--tray` tab with two `--tray-hi` grip lines) sits on the strip's dividing line for the selected grade; band labels in mono beneath ("Gr 0 … Gr 4"). The control is a real `input[type=range]` (0–4, step 1) visually bound to the strip — the strip *is* the slider's track.
- **Motion spec:** Dragging the handle (or arrow keys on the range) moves the dividing line with the pointer (no lerp — the tongs are in the visitor's hand; direct manipulation is the one place the theme drops smoothing), and the main print's grade cross-fades over **220ms** `--ease-develop` to the newly selected band's filter — a regrade is a small development. On release, the handle settles to the band centre over 140ms `--ease-stamp` and the print's notation (§6.6 pattern, a small strip-stamp under the main print) updates its grade token: "Gr 2" → "Gr 3" with a 180ms stamp press. Auto-play is forbidden: the strip never cycles on its own (§9).
- **Choreography timeline:** Interaction-driven. Pointer down on handle → bands' boundaries print darker (the strip "activates": hairlines 160ms). Drag → dividing line follows; main print cross-fades 220ms per band crossed (queued: rapid crossings blend directly to the final band — the print never lags more than one cross-fade behind the hand). Release → settle 140ms → stamp press 180ms → the strip rests. Keyboard: each arrow press = one band, same 220ms cross-fade and stamp.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* Grade 2 selected (the print's printed grade — the strip's default is the print's own record, not a neutral zero). *Hover (handle):* grip lines light `--paper-hi`. *Focus (range):* `--glow` ring on the handle. *Active/dragging:* as above; the main print's caption appends the live grade in mono ("— viewing Gr 3"). *Scrolled-past:* the selected grade persists (it is the visitor's print choice; if the strip controls an orderable grade, §6.14's form reads it). *Disabled:* a print with a single sanctioned grade (an edition printed only at Grade 2) renders the strip as a *printed* strip — five bands, no handle, a caption: "Printed at Grade 2 only — the strip is shown for the record." — the control becomes documentation rather than disappearing. *Touch:* the handle's target is 44×56px; drag is horizontal with vertical tolerance (the strip captures the gesture once horizontal movement exceeds 10px, leaving vertical scrolling intact otherwise).
- **Content-merge:** The strip is made from the print's own image and its grades are the real contrast grades of the paper stocks the lab sells (§6.13's log lists grade among its columns; the strip's Grade 2 must equal the log's grade for this frame). It demonstrates a property of the actual work — never a stock photo with a filter toy attached.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** Without JS, the strip prints as five static bands with labels and the main print at its recorded grade — a complete, honest artefact (printers read test strips on paper). Filter support failure: bands fall back to opacity-stepped duplicates (a lesser but still graded strip). Watchdog: if a cross-fade is interrupted, the main print snaps to the selected band's filter within **300ms** — the print and the handle may never disagree about the grade for longer than a breath (§10 audits handle/print agreement).
- **Reduced motion:** Band changes swap instantly (no cross-fade); the stamp prints without pressing; drag remains direct.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — strip height 92px, five bands across the print's width. 768–1199px — strip height 76px. <768px — the strip rotates its layout: bands become five horizontal rows (a strip held vertically) of 44px each, handle on the left — a phone's strip is read top-to-bottom, and drag becomes vertical; the range input's orientation follows.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Monochrome source at Grade 0:* the contrast floor (0.78) on an already soft image can wash to grey — the build clamps per-image: if the source's own contrast class is "soft" (tagged at build, §6.9's luminance/contrast tagging), Grade 0 renders at 0.84 and the band label prints "Gr 0 (soft negative)" — the strip admits the negative's nature. *The visitor drags beyond the ends:* the handle stops at the outer bands' centres (grades are detents; there is no Grade 4.4). *Strip used for exposure instead of grade:* the same element with time bands (4s/6s/8s/12s/16s) — the notation stamp then updates the seconds token; the two modes are never mixed on one strip.
- **Accessibility:** A genuine range input with `aria-valuetext="Grade 3"` (and the print's live region, polite, announcing "Now viewing Grade 3" on settle only — never mid-drag). Band labels are visible text. The main print's alt text is unchanged by grade (grade is presentation, not content). Keyboard: arrows step, Home/End jump to Grade 0/4.
- **Tokens:** `--paper`, `--ink`, `--tray`, `--tray-hi`, `--paper-hi`, `--glow`, `--ease-develop`, `--ease-stamp`.
- **Performance & mobile:** The strip is one small image rendered five ways via clip + filter (GPU-cheap); the main print's cross-fade is one filter transition. Mobile: vertical strip variant (above).

### 6.16 Tray-to-tray transition & unexposed paper

- **What & why:** Moving between pages in a darkroom is moving a print between trays: the current print is lifted by the tongs, carried, and set into the next tray — where the incoming page is already present, developing. The transition never shows an empty room. Its companion is the loading state: an *unexposed sheet* — blank paper with the faintest latent grid — which is what a print looks like before anything has happened to it, and the only honest skeleton this room can show.
- **Structure:** On navigation, a transition layer (a full-viewport `--room` sheet with a centred tray) holds: the outgoing print's thumbnail (the actual image, small) lifted at a tong angle, and beneath it the incoming page's hero area as unexposed paper — a `--paper` sheet at ghost tone with a latent grid (hairlines at 5% ink, the sheet's unexposed fibres) and the incoming page's title printed in the notation margin (the sheet is labelled before it is exposed). When the incoming page's real content is ready (its hero image decoded and its first tray laid out), the sheet develops per §5.2 over 600ms and the layer lifts away. In a single-page build (client routing), the same sequence runs without a document load; in a multi-page build it runs as an entry sequence on the new page, with the outgoing lift omitted (the visitor arrives mid-carry — the sheet and its develop are the transition).
- **Motion spec:** Outgoing lift: 260ms `--ease-tongs` (grip, rise 14px, tilt −1.2°). Sheet develop: 600ms §5.2 curve from p=0.15 (a sheet that has been in the tray a moment — never from absolute blank, per §5.2's floor). Layer exit: the tray sheet fades to the real page over 240ms `--ease-develop`. **Hard ceiling: 1500ms** total from navigation start to real content interactive — the theme's auto-finish constant applied to navigation: if the incoming page is not ready at 1500ms, the layer exits anyway onto whatever has rendered (a page with its paper and captions is always renderable — §5.2's floor is architectural, not per-element).
- **Choreography timeline:** t=0 — navigation fires; lift begins (multi-page: skipped). t=260ms — sheet present, labelled, at p=0.15. t=260–860ms — if content is ready, the sheet develops to p=1 across 600ms, overlapping the real page's own hero development (the real hero then starts at p=0.55 rather than 0 — it has, fictionally, been in the tray during the carry: the two developments are one). t≤1500ms — layer gone, page interactive. If content arrives at t=1400ms, the develop compresses to the remaining 100ms as a snap — better a quick print than a held door.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* no layer. *Running:* the layer is `inert` for the page beneath; focus is held (see Accessibility). *Interrupted (visitor navigates again mid-transition):* the newest navigation wins; the layer re-labels its sheet and restarts its ceiling from the new t=0 — a carried print can be re-routed, never duplicated. *Back/forward (bfcache):* the layer never persists into a restored page — pages restored from cache show finished content with no transition (the room does not re-develop what it already printed). *Disabled:* if the router/JS is absent, navigation is a plain document load and each page's own entry sequence (§13) is the whole experience. *Touch:* identical.
- **Content-merge:** The sheet is labelled with the *actual destination's* title and its hero develops into the actual page — the transition is a preview of the real artefact, not a branded interstitial. A transition that shows a logo instead of the destination's sheet is a loading screen in costume (§9).
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** The **1500ms ceiling is the watchdog**: a timer armed at navigation start force-exits the layer. A second guard: the layer is removed on `pageshow` regardless of state (bfcache), and on any unhandled route error the layer exits onto the error state (§6.18 pattern) rather than hanging.
- **Reduced motion:** No lift, no develop: the sheet appears labelled for exactly as long as loading takes (it is a status, not an animation) and is replaced instantly when content is ready; the ceiling still applies.
- **Breakpoints:** Identical across sizes; <768px the sheet is full-bleed (the tray is the screen) and the outgoing lift is skipped even in single-page builds (a small room, a short carry).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Instant loads (cached page):* if content is ready before t=260ms, the transition collapses to a 240ms crossfade — the room does not perform a carry for a print that was already in the tray. *Slow hero image on the destination:* the sheet's develop waits for the hero's decode, but the ceiling does not: at 1500ms the page shows with its hero at the §5.2 ghost floor, developing in place per §6.1. *Skeleton use beyond navigation* (a listing roll fetching, §6.12): unexposed sheets stand in for unloaded frames, each labelled with its frame number — and they never shimmer (§5.4): a sheet brightens only by developing, once, when its image arrives, over 600ms.
- **Accessibility:** The layer carries `role="status"` with the destination's name ("Going to: The Log") announced once; focus is not moved into the layer; on completion, focus moves to the destination page's heading per the router's normal behaviour. The ceiling guarantees the status never becomes a trap.
- **Tokens:** `--room`, `--tray`, `--paper`, `--paper-dim`, `--ink`, `--ease-tongs`, `--ease-develop`.
- **Performance & mobile:** The layer is two small elements + one thumbnail; its cost is bounded by the ceiling. Mobile: full-bleed sheet variant (above).

### 6.17 The wash-tray footer

- **What & why:** The last tray in the process line is the wash: prints rest in clean water, and the work is done. The footer is that tray — a wide, shallow `--tray` basin holding the room's closing record: the colophon (who made this room, on what paper, in what chemistry — the site's credits in the room's language), the station list repeated for wayfinding, and the day's totals, printed for the third time: the log's (§6.13) sums restated as the wash water's accounting — "6 exposures · 47 seconds of bench time · 19 prints still on the line" — each figure tracing to its record. A footer in this theme is not a sitemap with a dark background; it is the end of the process, and it signs the work.
- **Structure:** A `<footer class="wash">`: a full-width tray (rim on top edge only) containing three paper labels (colophon, stations, totals) laid side by side as prints resting in the wash — slight seeded rotations (±0.8°, same deterministic formula as §6.6), paper grounds, ink text. Beneath the labels, a single mono line, the room's signature: the lab's name, the year, and the fixed string (§12) "Washed and dried." The totals label's figures are `<output>`-style live text bound to the same values as §6.13/§6.8 (one value, three printings — §6.13's live-update rule applies: when the log changes, the wash changes in the same frame).
- **Motion spec:** The footer does not develop (it is the end of the process; its contents are finished work). Its labels settle in once, as the footer first enters: each label drops 6px and settles over **300ms** `--ease-stamp`, staggered 90ms — prints laid into water, not stamped (the easing's overshoot reads as buoyancy at this size, and the build keeps the overshoot: at 6px it is a bob, not a bounce). The wash water itself carries §6.10's ripple at its lowest amplitude (2–3%), static on mobile.
- **Choreography timeline:** Footer enters → labels settle (300ms × stagger) → ripple at rest amplitude. Nothing else, ever: no counters re-run here (the totals were counted in the log; the wash *states* them), no glow events (the census is spent), no parallax.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* printed labels, still water. *Hover (station links):* the label lifts 2px (§6.11 chip behaviour, halved — a print in water barely lifts). *Focus:* `--glow` ring on links. *Active:* §6.16 transition. *Scrolled-past:* it is the foot of the page; no past state. *Disabled:* a station that doesn't exist appears as the printed "not yet built" line (§6.11's rule) — in the wash, honesty is a printed line, not a greyed link. *Touch:* links ≥44px.
- **Content-merge:** The colophon's credits are real (photographer, lab, printer, the site's builder in the room's terms: "Set in Archivo and Plex Mono; printed on your screen"), the stations are the real nav, the totals are the real records. The wash is where the page's numbers come to rest — if a figure appears here that no record sums to, §10 fails the build on the law (§1).
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** Static HTML; the settle is JS-armed over a shipped settled state (labels at rest position). No watchdog needed beyond the arm pattern: failure = settled.
- **Reduced motion:** Labels at rest, ripple static.
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — three labels in a row. 768–1199px — three labels, narrower, rotations halved. <768px — labels stack vertically (prints laid one above another in a narrow tray), rotations 0 (a stacked print is squared to the tray), the signature line wraps to two lines rather than shrinking below 10px mono.
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Totals that change while the footer is on screen* (an order placed above, §6.14): the wash's figures update in the same frame as the log's — the visitor watching the footer sees the new total print (a 200ms colour pulse through `--glow-hi` on the changed numeral only — the one sanctioned attention gesture in the footer, because a number that changes silently in two places is how inconsistencies are born). *A page with no log* (a single-print page): the totals label prints that page's own record instead (its print's exposure and edition figures) — the wash always accounts for *this* page's work.
- **Accessibility:** A real `<footer>` with labelled nav (`aria-label="Darkroom stations, closing list"`). The totals are plain text (the pulse is `aria-hidden` decoration; a polite live region is *not* used — footer totals are not announcements). Contrast: ink on paper 11.14:1 throughout.
- **Tokens:** `--tray`, `--tray-hi`, `--paper`, `--ink`, `--paper-dim`, `--glow`, `--glow-hi`, `--ease-stamp`.
- **Performance & mobile:** Negligible: three small labels, one gated ripple. Mobile: stacked variant (above), ripple static.

### 6.18 The unexposed frame — 404 & empty states

- **What & why:** Some frames were never exposed: the shutter never fired, the film came back blank. The 404 page is that frame — a sheet of paper that went through the whole process and holds nothing, because nothing was ever put on it. Its choreography is specified as *none*, and that is the point: every other element in the theme performs a process; this one performs an absence, honestly. The same pattern serves empty states (an empty roll, a search with no frames): the blank sheet, its number, and one true sentence.
- **Structure:** A centred print (full §4.4 geometry) whose image area is unexposed paper: `--paper` at its palest, the faintest latent grid (§6.16's sheet), *no image, no ghost* — a ghost would imply an exposure happened. In the notation margin, the frame's number (the next number after the log's last entry — the blank frame is in the book) and one mono line: "Frame № 08 — never exposed." Beneath the print, in body text: what the visitor can do — return to the line, open the log — as two tray-label links (§6.11 pattern). For search-empty, the line reads "No frame matches — the roll was searched in full." with the search's terms printed (the record of the attempt).
- **Motion spec:** **None.** No development (nothing was exposed — developing a blank frame is the theme's one impossible event, and faking it would break the law: there is no exposure record for this frame, so §5.2 has nothing to compute). No sway, no sheen, no ripple on its tray (the tray beneath a blank frame is still). The links behave as links (§6.11 states). The page's header timer (§6.8) still runs — the room continues; only this frame is empty. This stillness is specified, not omitted: a builder who " livens up" the 404 has failed §10.
- **Choreography timeline:** t=0 — the page is complete. There is no t=anything.
- **State matrix:** *Rest:* the blank frame, printed. *Hover/focus/active:* only on the two links, per §6.11. *Scrolled:* the page is short; no states change. *Disabled:* n/a. *Touch:* identical.
- **Content-merge:** The frame number continues the site's real sequence (it is the log's next №), and the recovery links are the site's real stations. The emptiness is *located* in the archive — a visitor learns that the archive has a book, a sequence, and that this position in it is blank. A generic "page not found" with a dark background fails this element's spec.
- **Failure fallback & watchdog:** The element *is* a fallback; it has none of its own. Its one requirement: it must render with zero JS and zero images — the blank frame is pure HTML/CSS, so the error page can never itself error.
- **Reduced motion:** Identical (there is no motion to reduce).
- **Breakpoints:** ≥1200px — the print at 5 columns, centred. 768–1199px — 7 columns. <768px — full width minus tray padding; the notation margin stays 26px (the record's dignity is size-independent).
- **Edge cases & failure modes:** *Empty states that are the visitor's doing* (they filtered the contact sheet to nothing, §6.12): the blank frame appears *within* the sheet, numbered as the roll's next frame, with the line "No frame at this grade — loosen the filter." and a single action (clear the filter) — the emptiness instructs, in the room's voice (§12). *A genuinely missing image on an existing page* (a print whose file 404s): §6.4's fogged-frame pattern applies on that page ("frame fogged — no image"); §6.18 is reserved for missing *pages* and empty *sets*, so the two absences never blur.
- **Accessibility:** The page's h1 is the developing title's *printed* variant (no develop — nothing was exposed) reading "A frame never exposed". The blank image area has no img and no alt to lie about; the notation line is text. HTTP status is a real 404 (the pattern never ships on a 200).
- **Tokens:** `--paper`, `--paper-dim`, `--ink`, `--tray-hi`, `--room`.
- **Performance & mobile:** The lightest page on the site by construction. Mobile: full-width variant (above).

## 7. Page application map

The room is the same room on every page; what changes is which bench the visitor is standing at.

- **Home (the room's front):** Header (§6.11) → hero tray with the developing print (§6.1) and title (§6.3) → a body tray (the room's short statement, printed — body text never develops) → the drying line (§6.4) with the current six → the enlarger (§6.5) examining one negative → the log (§6.13) → the order desk (§6.14) → the wash (§6.17). This is the full process line, in process order: expose, develop, dry, examine, record, order, wash.
- **Listing / archive (rolls of contact sheets):** Header → filter grade chips → contact-sheet rolls (§6.12), one roll per section, frame numbers continuous across rolls → the wash. No hero print, no enlarger: an archive page is all bench, no easel. The timer's reference exposure is the first frame's (§6.8's rule).
- **Detail (a single print):** Header → the print large, in its tray, developing once (§6.1's element at detail scale, time-driven 3200ms) with its full notation back (§6.7) → its test strip (§6.15) if the print is orderable in grades → the order desk (§6.14) pre-set to this print → a short line of its line-mates (three §6.12 frames: the frames before and after it on the roll) → the wash. The print's frame number and roll are printed as a crumb in the first tray's label.
- **About (the printer):** Header → a portrait print developing (§6.1 pattern, smaller) → body trays (the printer's statement, printed text) → the log as biography: sittings by year, each with subtotals (§6.13's long-log pattern) → the wash. No order desk unless the printer takes orders.
- **Services (a lab):** Header → the log as the service book (§6.13: service rows in exposure form — process, format, bench time) → a drying line of recent client work (with permission, stamped as usual) → the order desk reframed as the booking desk (same element, its select lists services; the slip is a booking slip) → the wash.
- **Checkout / order confirmation:** Not a separate styled surface — the confirmation *is* §6.14's slip, and a standalone confirmation page is the slip printed large in a tray with the log row beneath it. The room does not grow a second commerce dialect.
- **404 / empty:** §6.18, always.

### 8.1 The hero — "Low Water" develops

**The record.** Frame 01 of Tuesday's sitting: *Low Water, Morecambe Bay*, shot on the ebb; printed f/8, 12 seconds, Grade 3, with a one-stop dodge on the left hand where the wet sand would otherwise block up. **The build.** The header (§6.11) ships expanded; its strip is dark. A visitor who scrolls at t=1000ms instead hands development to the scroll: p scrubs through the next 120vh of travel; the squeegee fires mid-scroll as 0.85 passes; the stamp lands while the print is still on screen or not at all visually — it is printed regardless (§6.6's rest state). Either way, by the time the visitor reaches the line (§6.4), the hero is a finished, documented object, and its flip (§6.7) is live: the back carries the full record — paper (Ilford Multigrade FB Classic, gloss), developer (Ilford Multigrade 1+9), the dodge described, edition of 25.

## 9. Anti-slop — what makes THIS theme look cheap

Each failure mode is specific to the darkroom; each cure is mechanical, not a matter of taste.

1. **The same stamp on every print.** "f/8 · 12s" stamped across a whole gallery is the theme's cardinal sin — it announces that the notations are clip-art. *Cure:* every stamp's text is generated from that image's own record (§6.6, §6.13); §10 greps the built page for duplicate stamp strings — duplicates are permitted only for genuine reprints, which must be suffixed in the log (07, 07b).
2. **Development as a generic fade.** An opacity fade (or worse, a fade-and-rise) wearing dark colours is not this theme. *Cure:* the only sanctioned appearance is §5.2's function — contrast, brightness, saturation and the ghost's slight blur moving together on the chemical curve. If a print's arrival could be mistaken for a lazy-load fade, it is wrong.
3. **A viewport full of ghosts.** Several prints mid-development at once reads as a broken page, not a busy darkroom. *Cure:* §4.2's one-developing-thing rule, enforced by §13's sequencing; §10 checks it by scrolling the built page and counting simultaneous 0 < p < 1 prints in the viewport (must be ≤ 1, title excepted per §6.3).
4. **Glow everywhere.** Safelight red used as a general accent colour — red buttons, red links, red headings — floods the room and kills the fiction (and the contrast: §2.2). *Cure:* the glow census (§2.3, §10): ≤ 7 glow surfaces in the first viewport; red text only as `--glow-hi`; links are paper-toned with a glow *underline light* on hover, never red text.
5. **Numbers that don't trace.** A "47 seconds" in the footer that the log's column doesn't sum to; a timer whose ticks aren't the hero's seconds; an edition count that differs between the index and the form. *Cure:* one record per fact, printed in several places (§§6.8, 6.13, 6.14, 6.17); §10's DOM audit recomputes every total from the rendered rows.
6. **Prints that zoom, tilt or bounce.** A photograph scaling inside its border on hover, a wet print tilting like a card in a SaaS grid, a springy overshoot on a 400g sheet of fibre paper. *Cure:* hover is a 4–6px lift and +0.04 contrast (§6.1); tilt and scale-in-frame are refused (§5.4); overshoot exists only in the stamp's 180ms press, where a physical press justifies it.
7. **Shimmering skeletons.** A shimmer sweep on the unexposed sheet — paper does not shimmer, and a moving skeleton contradicts the room's physics. *Cure:* §6.16's sheet is still; it changes only by developing, once.
8. **A focus pull on a soft photograph.** The enlarger (§6.5) promising sharpness the negative doesn't hold. *Cure:* the honesty caption is mandatory, the blur ceiling drops for low-resolution sources (§6.5), and images without detail to reward don't get an enlarger section.
9. **Archivo numerals.** Prices, times or frame numbers set in the display grotesque because it "looked cleaner". *Cure:* §3.1 law 1 — measurements are mono, always; §10 greps computed styles on data cells.
10. **The shaking form.** Error states that shake, bounce or flash red. *Cure:* §6.14 — errors print, in ink, in the printer's voice, and the desk waits. Stillness is the error state.
11. **Safelight as a light theme.** A "light mode" that inverts the room to beige with red accents — the room with the white lights on, in which nothing can develop. *Cure:* §2.4 — there is no light scheme; the warm-step under light `prefers-color-scheme` is the whole concession.
12. **Synchronised ambience.** Every tray rippling in phase, every print swaying together — a screensaver, not a room. *Cure:* per-element phase offsets are structural (§6.4's 0.7 rad steps, §6.10's 1300ms offsets), and §10 checks two adjacent prints' sway phases differ.
13. **The marquee of frame numbers.** Any ticker, carousel autoplay or cycling strip. *Cure:* refused outright (§11) — the room's only autonomous motions are sway, ripple and sheen, each with a physical source.

## 10. Quality gate

A build may not ship on this theme unless every blocking check passes. Checks are mechanical wherever the theme's law makes them mechanical.

**Blocking:**
1. **Law audit (figures):** every number printed on the page traces to a record: log totals recomputed from the rendered DOM rows equal the printed totals (in the log, the footer, and anywhere else); the timer's tick count and readout denominator equal the reference print's stamped seconds; edition figures agree between index, form and log.
2. **Law audit (development):** for each developing print, its visible stage sequence follows §5.2's function with its stamped exposure — no print reaches Full faster than its drive allows; no print's stamp text duplicates another's except logged reprints.
3. **No blank tray:** with JS disabled, with filters unsupported, and with the network throttled to 3G, every print is visible at its printed state and every caption readable. (The DOM ships finished — this check verifies the build didn't invert the arm pattern.)
4. **Auto-finish drill:** block IntersectionObserver callbacks (or background the tab mid-development): every armed print reaches p=1 within its watchdog (1500ms from leaving the centre band) and carries its stamp.
5. **One-developing-thing:** scripted scroll of the full page: at no sampled moment is more than one print at 0 < p < 1 in the viewport (title's run excepted).
6. **Glow census:** ≤ 7 glow surfaces in the first viewport; no `--glow` text below 24px; no `--glow` element on a `--safelight` ground anywhere on the page.
7. **Contrast spot-check:** paper-on-room body text ≥ 12:1 measured; stamp text (ink on paper) ≥ 11:1; timer readout (`--glow-hi` on room) ≥ 5:1; `--paper-dim` never used below 14px on `--tray`.
8. **Timer agreement:** at five sampled scroll positions, dial angle, strip width and readout agree with the single progress value within rounding (angle ±2°, readout exact).
9. **Flip integrity:** every flippable print's back contains its full record (exposure extended, paper, developer, edition) and differs from its front caption; the hidden face is inert while turned away; flip state persists across scroll-away and return.
10. **Reduced-motion pass:** with `prefers-reduced-motion`, the page is complete and static: prints developed, title printed, timer stepped and agreeing, no pin on the enlarger, no sway/ripple/sheen motion.
11. **Forced-colors pass:** with forced colors active, all content is present and legible; no information exists only in a filter, a glow or a sheen.
12. **404 stillness:** the §6.18 page contains no developing element, no ambient animation, and returns a real 404.

**Advisory (ship with a note, not a blocker):**
- Sway phase offsets verified distinct on adjacent prints (§9.12).
- The enlarger's honesty caption matches the source's actual resolution class.
- Print `prefers-reduced-data` / low-core gate (§6.5) exercised once on a throttled device profile.

## 12. Content & copywriting voice

Two registers, kept apart the way the room keeps chemistry and paper apart.

**The Bench register** (body copy, headings, about text): Archivo, sentence case, plain and unhurried — a printer describing work. Short sentences. Process verbs (expose, develop, fix, wash, dry, hang). No marketing superlatives: the work is "printed on fibre paper", never "stunning" or "world-class". Headlines name bench objects and sittings ("Tuesday's sitting", "From the line"), not benefits.

**The Record register** (notations, log, timer, confirmations, frame numbers): IBM Plex Mono, terse, complete, uppercase for stamps. A record states quantity, unit, condition — and admits its gaps ("One frame unrecorded"). Numbers are never rounded in the Record register; if a figure is approximate, the record says "about" and the approximation is itself recorded.

**Fixed strings (verbatim, do not paraphrase):**
- The footer's signature line ends: **"Washed and dried."** (§6.17)
- The enlarger's honesty caption: **"Focused. This is the sharpest this negative gets."** (or its half-frame variant, §6.5)
- The unexposed frame's line: **"never exposed."** as in "Frame № 08 — never exposed." (§6.18)
- An unfilled video/media slot's equivalent in this theme — an unprinted frame in a list: **"not printed"** (§6.12).

**Examples:**
- Headline — ✓ "Prints from Tuesday's sitting" · ✗ "Discover Our Latest Amazing Collection!"
- Button — ✓ "Place in the tray" · ✗ "Submit" / "Buy Now!!!"
- Error — ✓ "The street line is empty — the postman needs it." · ✗ "Error: invalid input." / "Oops! Something went wrong."
- Empty state — ✓ "No frame at this grade — loosen the filter." · ✗ "Nothing here… try again?"
- Confirmation — ✓ "Order № 07 — entered in the log." · ✗ "Success! Your order has been placed successfully."
- Sold out — ✓ "(edition complete)" struck through in the select, and in prose: "The edition is complete; the negative rests." · ✗ "Sold out!!" badges.
- Loading — ✓ the sheet's label: the destination's name, nothing else. · ✗ "Loading… please wait" with a spinner.

**Voice rules:** exclamation marks do not occur in either register. Prices are never shouted (no "only £95!"). The room never says "Oops". Uncertainty is stated in the Record register with its source ("Pattern of this sitting unverified — two frames unrecorded"), never smoothed over in the Bench register.

## 13. Full-page scroll choreography map

The home page (§7's process line), beat by beat, from first paint to the wash. Times are for a visitor who loads and then scrolls at a reading pace; scroll-driven beats follow the visitor, not the clock.

1. **The lamp warms.** Header strip glows up (600ms); wordmark develops (700ms). The room exists before anything in it moves.
2. **The title develops.** t=150–1100ms, §6.3. The visitor learns the room's rule from type before image.
3. **The hero develops.** From t=250ms (time drive, 4000ms) or by scroll (120vh scrub). Squeegee at its 0.85; stamp at its 1.0; tray ripple at agitation amplitude while it develops. The timer's three renderings begin tracking the page.
4. **Two beats of room.** The statement tray is printed text — nothing develops, nothing moves but the ripple at rest amplitude. The visitor reads in a quiet room (the §4.3 held breath).
5. **The line draws; the six hang.** Cord draws 1200ms; prints develop staggered (2400ms each, +120ms), squeegee, stamp, pin, sway. The slowest beat on the page, placed where a gallery belongs — after the statement, before the examination.
6. **The enlarger pins.** 160vh of focus pull (§6.5): reticle, blur resolving, readout counting down, honesty caption at q=1. The page's one held breath at full attention.
7. **The book totals.** The log tray enters; its total counts 0 → 47s over 700ms and rests. The room's records, struck in front of the visitor.
8. **The desk waits.** The order desk is printed and still. Its only choreography is the visitor's: focus underlines, the stepper's exposures, and — on submit — the slip's develop-and-stamp (§8.3) with the log's write-in and recount in the same frame, and the wash's totals updated for when the visitor arrives.
9. **The wash.** Footer labels settle (300ms staggers), ripple at its lowest, the signature line printed: "Washed and dried." The timer's hand rests at 330°, its strip full, its readout at the reference time. The page is a finished print.

Reverse pass: scrolling back up, developments stay developed, the line keeps swaying, the enlarger *re-focuses in reverse* (focus is reversible; development is not — §6.5), the timer winds back, and nothing re-runs: no stamps re-press, no totals re-count. Jump scroll (anchor, scrollbar drag): the loop's lerp settles driven values within ~400ms; any print jumped past is auto-finished by its watchdog; the choreography degrades to a finished room, never to a half-developed one.

## 14. Whole-page performance budget

**Asset budget (home page, §7):** total ≤ **860KB** compressed, of which: hero image ≤ 320KB (1600w responsive set), line prints ≤ 6 × 70KB, enlarger image ≤ 180KB (its sharpness is the point — it gets the second-largest budget), contact/strip thumbnails ≤ 40KB total on the home page, fonts ≤ 120KB (Archivo 500/600 + Plex Mono 500, subset to Latin + the № · — glyphs the room actually prints), CSS+JS ≤ 90KB. No other imagery exists.

**Frame budget:** the shared loop's per-frame script cost ≤ **1.6ms** on a mid-tier phone (measured with all ambient systems live: sway ×6, ripple ×2, sheen ×2, timer ×3 properties, one development). Filter animations are compositor work; the loop writes custom properties and transforms only — no layout reads inside the loop (all geometry is cached on resize/intersection).

**Long-task limits:** no task > 50ms after first paint; the letter-split (§6.3) and stamp generation (§6.6) are build-time or first-frame work, chunked if a page carries > 12 prints. Image decoding is async everywhere except the hero.

**Cut order when the budget is exceeded** (cut in this order, stop as soon as the budget is met):
1. Tray ripple drift → static sheen (§6.10's mobile variant) on all trays.
2. Sway amplitude halved, then sway off for prints beyond the first line.
3. Sheen (§6.9) restricted to the hero print only.
4. Line prints' development shortened (2400ms → 1400ms) — never removed; development is the theme, not an effect.
5. Enlarger blur ceiling reduced (14px → 9px) on low-power devices.
6. Timer dial dropped on mid screens (strip + readout remain, §6.8's mobile rule extended upward).

**Never cut:** the development function itself (§5.2), the auto-finish watchdogs, the stamps and notations (the record is the theme's law), reduced-motion variants, the log's DOM-audit, focus visibility. A build that meets budget by cutting the record has not met the budget.

## 15. Build-order checklist

Build in this order — the room is assembled bench by bench, and each step is verifiable before the next begins.

1. **The records first.** Define the data: prints (with their full exposure records), the log's rows, editions, prices. Compute every total from the rows. If the records don't reconcile on paper, stop — no design step can fix the book.
2. **The static room.** Build the complete page with no JS: room ground, header (strip full, wordmark printed), all prints developed and stamped, line hung and still, enlarger sharp with its caption, log printed with true totals, form functional, footer settled, 404 blank. Verify §10 checks 1, 3, 6, 7 against this static build — it must already pass the law.
3. **Tokens and type.** Wire §2's tokens and §3's scale; run the contrast spot-checks (gate 7) on the static build.
4. **The arm pattern.** Add the JS arming layer: shipped-finished → armed states, with every watchdog (§§6.1, 6.2, 6.4, 6.5, 6.16) in place *before* any animation is pretty. Drill gate 4 now.
5. **The shared loop.** One rAF loop, lerp 0.12, cached geometry, proximity gating (§14). No element gets its own loop.
6. **Development.** §5.2's function + §6.1 (hero, both drives), then §6.12's frames and §6.4's line prints on the same function.
7. **Completion gestures.** Squeegee (§6.2), stamps (§6.6), pin drops (§6.4) — bound into the single completion path per print.
8. **The title** (§6.3) and header behaviours (§6.11): warm-up, compacting, stations sheet.
9. **The enlarger** (§6.5): sticky pin, scrub, readout, watchdog; verify the one-pin rule.
10. **The timer** (§6.8) from the reference record; run gate 8 (agreement at five positions).
11. **Interactions:** flip (§6.7) with its no-JS `<details>` fallback folded in; test strip (§6.15); sheen (§6.9); ripple (§6.10) last among ambience — it is the most cuttable (§14).
12. **The desk and the book's write-in** (§§6.14, 6.13): idempotency, the busy ceiling, live totals propagating to the wash (§6.17) in one frame.
13. **Transitions & sheets** (§6.16) with the 1500ms ceiling drill.
14. **The unexposed frame** (§6.18) — built as pure HTML/CSS, verified to render with JS and images disabled, real 404 status.
15. **The gate.** Run §10 in full, including the scripted scroll (gate 5), the glow census (gate 6), reduced-motion and forced-colors passes (gates 10–11), signed off by someone who did not build the page. The room opens when the gate passes, not when the build feels done.
