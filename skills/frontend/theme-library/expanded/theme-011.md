# Theme 11 — The Telegraph Wire (Expanded, v1 + v2 standard)

## 1. Concept & world

The year is 1911 and the news does not arrive — it is *received*, letter by letter, over a wire, in a room that smells of ozone and hot brass. The Telegraph Wire builds a site as a working wire room: a pinned punched-tape header feeds the latest headline sideways forever; below it, a single dispatch column receives bulletins in order, each one announced by a stamped dateline, typed in with an operator's irregular cadence, and closed not with a period but with the word **STOP** struck like a key. The wire-room clock ticks in one-second steps. Progress through the page is not a scrollbar gimmick but a brass marker travelling a second, scroll-fed tape, counted in the wire's own units: MESSAGE n OF N.

The fiction is strict, and the strictness is the design. In 1911 there is no glassmorphism, no rounded card, no soft drop shadow, no gradient except the yellowing of paper that has sat too long near a window. Every surface is paper or tape; every mark is ink, stamp, punch or brass. Every animation is a mechanical event in the room: a key strikes, a stamp lands, tape feeds, a slip is pulled from the basket. If a motion cannot be named as something the wire room physically does, it does not belong in this theme.

The copy is under the same discipline as the chrome. Wire style is a real editorial system, not a costume: the lead carries the whole story in the first 25 words; facts descend in order of importance (the inverted pyramid) so a dispatch can be cut from the bottom at any point and still be complete; adjectives are banned — the wire charges by the word, and the reader is billed in attention. Datelines are places and dates, set in caps. Numbers are figures, never words. A correction is a new dispatch, never a quiet edit. When the theme is applied honestly, the *writing* feels urgent before a single pixel moves; the motion only confirms what the prose already claims.

**Best fit.** Breaking-news products and live blogs; sports scores and results services; financial data services (prices, tables, market bulletins); status and incident pages (a wire room is exactly what an incident feed is); shipping/port/airline movement boards; election-night and results products; any product whose truth changes by the minute and whose users scan rather than browse.

**When NOT to use it.** Long-form essays and features (the column and cadence fight sustained reading past ~600 words per dispatch); luxury, beauty and hospitality brands (urgency and thrift are the opposite of their promise); children's products (the fiction is an adult workplace and the palette is deliberately unkind); image-led portfolios (wirephotos are small, duotone and rationed — a hero gallery starves here); anything whose content updates slower than weekly — a wire room with no traffic is a museum, and the ambient tape loop will expose the emptiness within one 40-second pass.

**The one law.** *The transmission and the message are the same event.* Typing is not an intro animation before the news; the typing is the news arriving. The stamp is not decoration above the section; it is the section being filed. Any build that treats the wire machinery as a skin over ordinary content has failed the theme before the quality gate is even opened.

## 2. Palette

Four anchor inks, all from the source theme, plus derived tones that are mixes of the anchors — no fifth hue may be introduced. Brass is a metal, not a text colour: it is rationed to markers, rules and the key, and never carries body text.

| Token | Hex | Role | Usage ratio | Pairing / contrast notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | `#EFE3B8` | The ground. Wire-paper yellow — every page surface, the tape itself | ~62% of pixels | Ground for `--ink` (12.4:1) and `--red` (5.9:1) |
| `--paper-pale` | `#F7F0D4` | Slip surface — dispatch cards and form sheets sit one shade lighter than the ground, as fresh slips on old stock | ~18% | `--ink` on pale: 13.6:1. The only "card" surface in the theme |
| `--paper-deep` | `#E6D49C` | Tape strip and pinned header band — paper that has been handled, slightly deeper than the ground | ~9% | Header text is `--ink` (11.2:1); never white text on this band |
| `--ink` | `#26241E` | Telegraph ink — all body text, rules, perforation shadows, the clock hands | ~8% | On `--paper` 12.4:1 (AAA). The only colour body text may use in light mode |
| `--ink-70` | `#5A564A` | Derived (ink at ~70% over paper) — captions, bylines, filed-times, secondary apparatus | ~2% | On `--paper` 6.8:1 — captions and apparatus only, never dispatch body |
| `--red` | `#B02A20` | Signal red — dateline stamps, priority marks (FLASH), the STOP rule tick, focus rings, error stamps | <1% | On `--paper` 5.9:1 — passes AA for text at all sizes used (≥11px apparatus, stamps are bold). Never a background fill behind text |
| `--red-deep` | `#8C1F16` | Derived — a stamp's second strike: pressed/hover state of stamped elements, the correction slip's border | <1% | On `--paper` 7.8:1 |
| `--brass` | `#8C6D2F` | Brass key — the progress marker, the clock rim, key hardware, perforated-edge shading | <1% | On `--paper` 4.0:1 — non-text and large marks only (≥18px bold or pure geometry). **Banned for text below 18px** |
| `--brass-bright` | `#B08D3E` | Derived — the marker's lit face as it travels, hover state of the key button | <1% | Non-text only |

**Dark-mode behaviour — the night wire.** The wire room does not invert; it dims. At `prefers-color-scheme: dark` (or a host-forced dark), the token *values* swap to the night desk: ground becomes `--paper: #201D15` (a desk in a dark room, not black), slips become `--paper-pale: #2A2718`, the header band `--paper-deep: #262218`, and text becomes the paper colour itself, `--ink: #EFE3B8` (11.6:1 on the night ground) with `--ink-70: #B4AA8C` (6.4:1). Signal red brightens to `#D65344` (5.3:1 on the night ground — stamps must still read as stamps) and brass to `#C49A4A` (non-text, as ever). Every duration, easing and layout value is mode-invariant; only the five token values change. A build that re-specifies elements for dark mode has misunderstood the token system.

**Hard pairing rules.** (1) Body text is `--ink` on `--paper` or `--paper-pale`, full stop. (2) Red is a *mark*, never a field: no red backgrounds, no red body text below 14px, no red links (links are ink, underlined by the Morse rule treatment in §11). (3) Brass touches nothing readable. (4) Photographs are duotone in ink/paper (see §6.9) — a full-colour image anywhere on the page breaks the wire.

## 3. Typography

Two faces only. **IBM Plex Mono** (400/500/600) sets everything — dispatches, counters, labels, tables, form fields, buttons. **Libre Franklin** (800 only) sets datelines and nothing else: the dateline is the one piece of the dispatch that was stamped by a different machine, and the face change is how the page says so. Introducing a third face — above all a "typewriter" display font — is the theme's most common act of self-sabotage (§9).

| Role | Face / weight | Size / line-height | Tracking | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero dispatch | Plex Mono 500 | 30px / 1.45 (≥1200px); 24px (768–1199); 20px (<768) | −0.01em | Types in (§6.1). Max 3 lines; the lead must fit or the copy is rewritten, never shrunk below 20px |
| Dispatch body | Plex Mono 400 | 16px / 1.6 | 0 | The reading size. Never below 16px anywhere body copy appears |
| Dispatch headline (in-stack) | Plex Mono 600 | 19px / 1.35 | 0 | Present tense, caps optional; urgency from cadence, not size — headlines never exceed 19px in the stack |
| Dateline | Libre Franklin 800 | 12px / 1.2 | +0.18em, uppercase | Always `PLACE, MONTH DAY —` form; stamped (§6.2), rotated −3° |
| Apparatus / labels | Plex Mono 500 | 11px / 1.4 | +0.12em, uppercase | MESSAGE n OF N, FILED 14:02, bureau names, table heads |
| Counters / data | Plex Mono 500, tabular figures | 14–22px | 0 | `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` is mandatory — a counter that changes width is a broken counter |
| Caption / byline | Plex Mono 400, `--ink-70` | 13px / 1.5 | 0 | Wirephoto credits, operator initials |
| Form field text | Plex Mono 400 | 16px / 1.5 | 0 | 16px minimum — also prevents iOS focus zoom |
| STOP | Plex Mono 600 | inherits context size | +0.06em | Always the literal word STOP, struck (§6.6), followed by a 24px red rule tick |

**Type laws.** (1) *No italics.* The wire has no italics; emphasis is caps, weight 600, or a red mark. Plex Mono's italic is banned by token (`font-style` is never set in this theme). (2) *No size urgency.* Nothing in the stack may be set larger to make it feel more important — importance is order (inverted pyramid) and the FLASH stamp. (3) *Figures for numbers*, always, including in headlines ("3 KILLED" is wire style; "THREE KILLED" is a newspaper). (4) Line length for dispatch body is 58–68ch; the mono face at 16px makes 68ch ≈ 655px, which sets the column in §4.

## 4. Layout language

The page is a wire room seen from the operator's chair: a machine pinned at the top, a basket of slips below it, and a clock on the wall that never leaves your sight while the bulletins stack up.

**The pinned header (the machine).** A fixed band, 96px tall at ≥1200px, in `--paper-deep`, holding three things in a row: the sideways headline tape (§6.3) running its full width at the top (28px), and below it the masthead line — bureau name left, the wire-room clock (§6.13) centre-right, and the scroll-fed tape with its brass marker (§6.4) as the band's bottom edge (10px). Perforation rails — two rows of punched holes, 6px circles on a 12px pitch (`--tape-pitch`), rendered as a radial-gradient strip — border the tape top and bottom. The header never collapses, shrinks or hides on scroll: the machine is the room's fixed point, and hiding it would be a newsroom with the wire cut.

**The dispatch column.** One column, `--col: 68ch` (≈680px at 16px mono), left-aligned at a 24px gutter from the perforation rail on desktop, centred in the measure on tablet, full-bleed with 16px padding on mobile. Sections are not cards on a background — they are dispatches *in* the column, separated by Morse rules (§6.7), each opened by its stamped dateline (§6.2) breaking 12px into the left margin (the stamp was aimed at the slip, not at the grid). The column is the page; everything else is apparatus around it.

**The basket (listings).** Where dispatches repeat — scores, results, earlier wires — they sit in a basket grid: slips of `--paper-pale` with a perforated top edge, 2 columns at ≥1200px, 1 column below. Slips are never rounded, never shadowed with blur: a lifted slip casts a hard offset shadow, 4px/4px/0 in `--ink` at 18% — ink, not weather.

**Spacing rhythm.** The base unit is 6px, and the tape pitch (12px) is the page's metre: section gaps are 8 pitches (96px), dateline-to-body is 2 pitches (24px), slip padding is 2 pitches, the perforation rail is 1 pitch tall. Vertical rhythm is counted in pitches the way the room counts tape. Whitespace is the desk between slips — wide, plain, unpatterned `--paper`. The paper-yellowing grain (§5) is the only texture allowed in the whitespace, at 3% opacity, and it is a *surface*, never a vignette.

**Density rules.** A viewport at 900px tall should hold: the header, one dateline, and at least 9 lines of dispatch body — if a build's hero apparatus pushes the first dispatch below that, the apparatus is cut, not the news (§14 cut order). Maximum one wirephoto per dispatch. Maximum one FLASH stamp per page. The tape, the clock and the marker are the only elements allowed to move continuously; everything else moves on arrival and then is still — a wire room is busy at the key and quiet on the desk.

## 5. Full motion system

Every motion in the theme is a mechanical event in the wire room. The named easings are the room's physics, defined once here and consumed by every element in §6:

| Token | Value | The physics it names |
|---|---|---|
| `--ease-strike` | `cubic-bezier(.2,1.6,.35,1)` | A key or stamp striking: fast in, one overshoot, hard stop. Used by stamps, STOP, the key-clack |
| `--ease-feed` | `linear` | Tape feeding. Tape never eases; a tape that eases is a ribbon |
| `--ease-settle` | `cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)` | A slip settling onto the desk: slip lifts, hovers, card entries |
| `--ease-snap` | `cubic-bezier(.85,0,.15,1)` | The register snap: hover alignment, marker arrival at a message notch |
| `--ease-flutter` | `cubic-bezier(.37,0,.63,1)` (drives a 2-beat keyframe) | Paper flutter: rotation ±0.6°, two decaying oscillations, 380ms total |

Duration tokens: `--dur-stamp: 180ms` · `--dur-line: 250ms` (per tape-slot line) · `--dur-lift: 200ms` · `--dur-press: 90ms` · `--dur-draw: 600ms` (Morse rule) · `--dur-clear: 120ms` (wire-clear type-off) · `--dur-fade: 300ms`. Typing is governed not by a duration but by the cadence engine (§6.1): base 30ms/char, randomised 18–46ms, bursts of 3–7 chars, pauses of 150–500ms at phrase boundaries.

**The full vocabulary, translated:**

- **Scroll-triggered reveals** — a dispatch arrives when its dateline crosses 82% of the viewport height: stamp first (180ms), then the body types or slot-reveals (§6.2, §6.5). Arrival is one-shot; a dispatch never un-arrives.
- **Parallax** — tape strips and perforation rails translate at 0.92× scroll speed against the column's 1.0×; the clock and header are pinned at 0×. Nothing else parallaxes — parallax in the reading column would make the news swim.
- **Pinned scenes** — the header pins for the whole page (§6.13). One further pin is licensed: in the hero, the wirephoto pins for 40vh while its dispatch types beside it (§6.14). No other pins.
- **Masked line reveals** — the tape-slot clip-path (§6.5): lines slide out of a 1-line-high slot, 250ms per line, 60ms stagger.
- **Blur-to-sharp, translated** — the wire has no blur. Its equivalent is *strike-to-settle*: a typed line lands with a 1px ink-spread (`text-shadow: 1px 0 0 currentColor` at 40%) that clears over 120ms as the line "dries". Used on the hero dispatch and STOP strikes only — body lines arrive clean.
- **Animated counters** — wire stats (§6.8) tick in whole increments, one per dispatch read, tabular figures, 120ms per increment, never tweened decimals.
- **Progress indicators** — the brass marker on the scroll-fed tape (§6.4) plus MESSAGE n OF N in print. There is no other progress UI in the theme.
- **Marquee** — the sideways headline tape (§6.3): 40s per pass, linear, pausable, and it carries the real latest headline.
- **Ambient loops** — three only: the headline tape, the paper grain breathing (opacity 2.6%→3.4%, 9s), and the clock's second hand stepping each 1s. All three pause when offscreen or when the tab is hidden.
- **Micro-interactions** — slip-lift hover with flutter (§6.10), key-clack click (§6.11), hover-to-register on cards (§6.15).
- **Page transitions** — wire-clear (§6.12): the current dispatch types off in 120ms, a beat of blank tape (80ms), the next begins.
- **Image treatments** — wirephotos scale-in-frame 1.07→1 inside perforated borders, duotone ink/paper, while scrolling through (§6.9).

**Global motion laws.** (1) *One scheduler.* All typing, ticking and scrubbing runs on a single rAF loop with lerp factor 0.16 for scroll-scrubbed values; no element runs its own interval. (2) *Typing never blocks.* Any typed element completes instantly on click, on focus, or when scrolled past — and a global 1.5s fallback completes any half-typed line (§6.1). (3) *Stillness is the resting state.* When nothing is arriving, nothing moves except the three ambient loops. (4) *Scroll-up is silent.* Scrolling back up triggers no arrivals, no re-typing, no stamp replays — the wire does not un-send.

## 6. Signature elements

The twelve signature elements of the source theme (§§6.1–6.12), deepened — never redesigned — plus six page-family elements (§§6.13–6.18): the wire-room header, the front-bulletin hero composition, bulletin slip cards, the file-a-dispatch form, the end-of-wire footer, and the correction slip. Every element carries its v1 build spec and its v2 layers: choreography timeline, state matrix, breakpoint behaviour, edge cases, accessibility spec, and the exact tokens it consumes.

### 6.1 Irregular-cadence hero dispatch

- **What & why.** The hero is not a headline with a typing effect; it is the moment the wire comes alive. The lead dispatch types character by character with a human operator's rhythm — bursts, hesitations, a clatter-synced block cursor — because in this theme the *arrival* is the product being sold: news, faster than anyone else, while you watch.
- **Structure.** A `<p class="dispatch dispatch--hero">` whose full text is present in the DOM at load. A visually-typed overlay (`aria-hidden="true"`) renders the revealed prefix plus the block cursor (`▌` in `--ink`, blinking at the clatter rate); the real text is available to assistive technology in full from t=0. The dateline (§6.2) sits above it, already stamped. The closing STOP is a `<span class="stop">` inside the same paragraph.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: page load, after the dateline stamp lands (+180ms). Cadence engine: base 30ms/char randomised 18–46ms; bursts of 3–7 characters; pauses of 150–500ms after commas, 300–650ms after sentence-internal clause breaks; the cursor blinks 2× per burst-gap (steps, 90ms on/off). Strike-to-settle: each completed line carries the 1px ink-spread for 120ms, then dries. STOP does not type — it strikes (§6.6). Total hero typing is capped at 3.2s for a 140-character lead; longer leads type at a compressed cadence (base 22ms) rather than running long.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 dateline stamp begins → t=180ms stamp lands, first character strikes → bursts proceed (avg 30ms/char) → each line-break: ink-spread clears at +120ms → final character at ≈t=2,600ms → pause 220ms (the operator lifts the key) → STOP strikes at ≈t=2,820ms (180ms) → cursor holds solid 600ms → cursor fades to the ambient blink (1.06s period) at t≈3,600ms. Total ceremony ≤3.8s.
- **States (v1).** *Rest (pre-load):* full text visible (no-JS state). *Typing:* prefix + cursor. *Complete:* full text, cursor ambient. *Interrupted:* any click, keypress or scroll >40px completes the text instantly (the "strike-to-complete" rule).
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest: full text; hover: none (the hero is not interactive); focus: the paragraph is not focusable — focus lives on the hero's action key (§6.14); active: n/a; scrolled-past: complete text, cursor gone; disabled: n/a. Touch — identical, except interruption also fires on the first touch-scroll. Reduced-motion and no-JS collapse all rows to "complete text".
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: 30px type, full cadence. 768–1199px: 24px, pauses ×0.8. <768px: 20px, bursts lengthen to 5–9 chars and pauses halve (total ≤2.2s) — a phone reader is standing up; the wire knows it.
- **Content merge.** The typing *is* the content event: the text being typed is the site's actual lead story, marked up as the page's `<h1>`-equivalent lead paragraph. Remove the animation and the page loses nothing but the arrival.
- **Failure fallback.** The DOM text is complete at load; the overlay only ever *reveals*. If the cadence engine fails to start, a 1.5s watchdog completes the reveal instantly. If JS is absent, there is no overlay and the dispatch simply stands there, fully typed — which is also the print stylesheet's state.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Slow font load: typing waits for `document.fonts.ready` or 600ms, whichever first, because mono metrics changing mid-type would reflow the line being struck. Very long lead (>200 chars): the copy is at fault, not the engine — the gate (§10) fails leads over 160 chars; the engine still caps at 3.2s. Tab hidden mid-type: the scheduler pauses and resumes without a burst of catch-up characters. Scroll-up: nothing replays.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** The dispatch stands fully typed at load; STOP is present, unstruck (no scale); the cursor does not blink (static `▌` removed entirely — a still cursor is a typo, so it is deleted, not frozen).
- **Accessibility (v2).** The typed overlay is `aria-hidden`; the full text is the accessible content from t=0 — screen readers never wait for theatre. The cursor is decorative and hidden from AT. Contrast: ink on paper 12.4:1 at every frame (the ink-spread shadow is currentColor at 40%, decorative only). No focus is captured or moved by the typing. Touch target: n/a (non-interactive), but the strike-to-complete affordance is documented in the hero's apparatus line ("TAP TO RECEIVE IN FULL") at ≥768px.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--ink`, `--paper`, `--ease-strike`, `--dur-stamp`; engine constants `--type-base: 30ms`, `--type-burst: 3–7`, `--type-pause-min: 150ms`, `--type-pause-max: 500ms`, `--type-cap: 3200ms`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** The cadence engine is ≤1.2KB of the shared scheduler; per-frame cost is one text-node update, no layout (the overlay is `position:absolute` over a `visibility:hidden` full-text spacer, so typing causes zero reflow). Mobile: compressed cadence as above; the ink-spread is dropped (sub-pixel shadows on small text read as dirt).

### 6.2 Dateline-stamp section arrivals

- **What & why.** Every dispatch in the wire room begins the same way: place, date, then news — and the dateline arrives by stamp, not by typeset, because it was applied to the slip by a different hand at a different moment. The stamp is the section's filing act; without it, a section is an anonymous paragraph, and this theme has no anonymous paragraphs.
- **Structure.** A `<p class="dateline">` in Libre Franklin 800, 12px, uppercase, tracking +0.18em, rotated −3°, rendered as an inline-block with a 1.5px `--red` border on its bottom edge only (a stamp is a mark, not a badge — no box). Text form is fixed: `PLACE, MONTH DAY —` (e.g. `CHICAGO, JUNE 3 —`). An optional priority stamp (FLASH / BULLETIN) is a second, smaller stamp to its right, in `--red`, rotated −5°.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: the dateline's top crosses 82% viewport height on scroll-down. The stamp strikes: scale 1.3→1 with `--ease-strike` over `--dur-stamp` (180ms), rotation settling −4.5°→−3°, and a 1-frame ink-bleed (opacity 0→1 in the first 40ms, slight 0.5px spread at impact clearing by 180ms). Only after the stamp lands does the section's body begin its own arrival (§6.5 or typing). One stamp per section, struck once, never replayed.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 trigger line crossed → t=0–40ms stamp fades/strikes in at scale 1.3 → t=180ms stamp at rest (−3°, scale 1) → t=180ms body arrival begins → if a priority stamp exists, it strikes at t=240ms (offset, second hand) → section fully arrived by ≈t=700ms.
- **States (v1).** *Rest (pre-arrival):* dateline present in DOM, visually at rest — the no-JS state *is* the stamped state. *Striking:* the 180ms strike. *Stamped:* permanent rest state. *Scrolled-past:* unchanged.
- **State matrix (v2).** All rows collapse to "stamped" except the 180ms striking row. Desktop hover: none. Focus: datelines are not focusable. Touch: identical (trigger is scroll position, not gesture). Disabled: n/a.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: dateline breaks 12px left of the column edge. 768–1199px: flush with the column. <768px: rotation reduces to −2° and the priority stamp stacks below the dateline (a phone slip is narrow; two stamps side by side collide at 360px width).
- **Content merge.** The dateline is real metadata — the actual place and date of the dispatch it introduces, marked up with `<time datetime>`. It is the section's heading semantics (`role="heading"` via the section's `aria-labelledby` where the section has no visible headline).
- **Failure fallback.** Without JS the stamp simply exists — the strike is an enhancement layered on a complete element. If the trigger never fires (element loaded already in view), it strikes on load instead. A 1.5s global fallback (§5 law 2) forces any unstruck stamp to its rest state.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Jump-scroll (anchor link, find-in-page): stamps in the jumped-over region are set to rest instantly — the fallback sweep runs on any scroll delta >1200px. Content added later (live updates): new dispatches stamp on insertion if in view, else on entry. A section with no genuine place/date must not invent one — it takes the bureau dateline (`WIRE ROOM, TODAY —`) or the section is cut (§9).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No strike: the dateline is simply present, rotation kept (rotation is a state, not a motion).
- **Accessibility (v2).** The dateline text is real text, read in order before its dispatch. The rotation and border are decorative. `<time>` carries the machine date. Contrast: red on paper 5.9:1 at 12px bold — passes AA for the apparatus size; the same text in `--ink` is available wherever a host requires AAA (the stamp colour is a token, not a structure).
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--red`, `--red-deep` (second-strike state), `--font-dateline`, `--dur-stamp`, `--ease-strike`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** One transform + opacity per stamp; ≤14 stamps per page (one per section) — cost negligible. Mobile: as breakpoints; the ink-bleed frame is dropped.

### 6.3 Sideways headline tape

- **What & why.** Above everything, the wire's live output runs forever: a punched-tape strip feeding the latest headline sideways, holes and all. It is the theme's marquee, and it earns the exemption from the usual marquee ban because the tape is not a banner ad for the page's own content — it *is* the wire, still transmitting while you read. The moment it carries anything but the genuine latest headline, it becomes decoration and fails §9.
- **Structure.** A 28px strip inside the pinned header (§6.13): `--paper-deep` ground, perforation rails top and bottom (radial-gradient holes, 6px on 12px pitch), and a track (`display:flex; width:max-content`) holding the headline text twice for a seamless loop, each copy followed by a punched separator (three holes + `STOP`). The text is the latest dispatch's headline, set in Plex Mono 500 13px uppercase, followed by its filed time.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: continuous from load. The track translates X at constant speed (`--ease-feed`, linear) — one full pass (one headline copy) in ~40s, i.e. ≈11px/s at a 440px headline; speed is computed from copy width so every headline passes in 38–42s. The punched holes scroll *with* the text (they are punched in this tape, not a frame around it). Pause on hover, on focus-within, and when the tab is hidden or the strip is offscreen (it is in the pinned header, so offscreen means tab-hidden only).
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 page load: tape already moving (it was moving before you arrived — starting it from rest at load is a tell, §9) — implemented by beginning the loop at a randomised offset of 0–40s. Continuous thereafter; on headline update (live product), the new headline is spliced at the loop seam, never mid-pass.
- **States (v1).** *Feeding:* default. *Paused:* hover/focus/hidden. *Updated:* seam splice on new headline. *Scrolled-past:* n/a — the header is pinned, the tape is always present.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest: feeding; hover: paused within 200ms (ease-out of the *pause*, not the tape); focus-within: paused; active: n/a; disabled: if the product has no live headline, the tape shows the most recent filed headline with its date — a silent wire shows its last message, never a placeholder slogan. Touch — no hover; a tap on the tape pauses it and a second tap resumes (the strip is a button-role region, 44px touch height via the header's padding, though its visual height is 28px).
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: full strip, headline + filed time. 768–1199px: filed time dropped. <768px: the tape remains (it is the theme's signature) but the pass slows to ≈9px/s and the perforation rails reduce to the bottom rail only, returning 6px of header height to the news.
- **Content merge.** The tape's text is generated from the same data as the lead dispatch — one source, two renderings. Screen-reader users get the headline once, in the lead; the tape itself is `aria-hidden` (a loop that repeats in the accessibility tree is a defect).
- **Failure fallback.** No JS: the tape renders as a static strip showing the latest headline once, left-aligned — still informative, still in fiction. If the loop engine fails, the same static state is the fallback; a frozen mid-scroll tape is the only forbidden state.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Very long headline (>120 chars): truncated at a word boundary with `MORE ON THE WIRE` appended — the tape never wraps to two lines. Live updates arriving faster than one per pass: queue; the tape shows the newest at the next seam and the filed-time keeps it honest. Low-power device: the loop drops to a 12s-stepped feed (jump 44px every 480ms — tape on a cheap machine *does* judder; stepped feed is in-fiction, smooth feed is not required).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Static strip, latest headline, no loop — the tape at rest on the desk.
- **Accessibility (v2).** `aria-hidden="true"` on the whole strip; the headline's accessible home is the lead dispatch. The pause control (touch) carries `aria-label="Pause the headline tape"`. Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion` globally (§5). No information exists only on the tape.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--paper-deep`, `--ink`, `--tape-pitch: 12px`, `--ease-feed`; engine constants `--tape-pass: 40s`, `--tape-speed: ≈11px/s`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** One transform on one track, compositor-only; the track's width is capped at 2× headline copy. Mobile: as breakpoints; the stepped-feed variant is the default below 768px *and* on `Save-Data`.

### 6.4 Scroll-fed tape + brass marker progress

- **What & why.** Reading progress, in the wire's own units. A second tape — this one in the header's bottom edge — advances as the visitor scrolls, and a brass marker travels its length notch by notch, one notch per dispatch. Beside it, print: MESSAGE n OF N. The visitor always knows how much of the wire they have received, and the measure is messages, not percentages — percentages are for dashboards, and this is a wire room.
- **Structure.** A 10px tape track spanning the header's width, perforation-shaded, with N notch ticks (one per dispatch/section, positioned at each section's scroll fraction). The marker is a 14×18px brass key shape (CSS clip-path: a rectangle with a rounded head) riding the track. The counter is a `<p class="wire-count">` in apparatus type at the header's right: `MESSAGE 3 OF 9`. Both are driven by the shared rAF scheduler.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: scroll, scrubbed. Scroll fraction is lerped (factor 0.16) into tape advance (linear — tape does not ease) and marker position; when the marker crosses a notch, it snaps the last 3px with `--ease-snap` (90ms) and the counter increments with a key-strike tick (scale 1.12→1, 90ms, `--ease-strike`) — the count is *struck*, like everything the key touches. Scrolling up: the marker retreats and the counter decrements — received is received, but the marker shows where you are, not where you have been.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** Continuous: scroll input → lerp step each frame (≤16ms) → tape/marker transform update → notch crossing: snap 90ms + counter strike at the same frame → counter text swaps at the strike's midpoint (45ms) so the new figure lands with the key.
- **States (v1).** *At rest (top):* marker at notch 0, counter reads MESSAGE 1 OF N once the hero is received (the hero is message 1). *Travelling:* between notches. *At a notch:* snapped, counter struck. *End of page:* marker at the final notch; counter reads MESSAGE N OF N and the marker's bright face (`--brass-bright`) lights.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest/travelling/notched as above; hover over the header: the marker swells 1px (it is brass under a finger, nothing more); focus: the counter is reachable by keyboard as a `role="status"` region but is not a tab stop; active/disabled: n/a. Touch — identical; the marker never intercepts touches (pointer-events: none on the whole track).
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: full track + counter text. 768–1199px: counter shortens to `3 / 9`. <768px: the track remains (4px tall) but the counter text moves into the masthead line and shortens to `3/9` — progress is apparatus, and apparatus compresses before news does.
- **Content merge.** The notches are generated from the page's actual sections; N is the real dispatch count, computed from the DOM, never hand-written. A page with one dispatch renders one notch and the counter is omitted (MESSAGE 1 OF 1 is a lie the wire does not tell — §9).
- **Failure fallback.** No JS: no marker, no counter — the header stands with tape and clock, and nothing on the page references the missing apparatus. If the scheduler fails mid-page, the counter freezes rather than lying; a frozen count is a stopped clock, a wrong count is a false dispatch.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Content injected late (live feed appending dispatches): N recomputes, notches redistribute, the marker's fraction is preserved (position is a fraction, notches are annotations). Very long pages (N>40): notches merge into a continuous ruled track and the counter still counts sections — the marker's snap targets remain per-section. Resize: fractions recompute on the next frame; no animation is retriggered.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** The marker still tracks scroll (it is information, not ornament) but moves without lerp — direct 1:1 with scroll position, no snap, and the counter changes without the strike scale.
- **Accessibility (v2).** The counter is `role="status"` with `aria-live="polite"` throttled: it announces only when a notch is crossed *and* 2s have passed since the last announcement, so a fast scroll does not narrate numbers. The marker and track are `aria-hidden` (pure geometry). Brass on paper-deep measures 3.7:1 — the marker is a ≥14px non-text mark, which is its legal use; it never carries meaning alone (the printed counter duplicates it in text at 11.2:1).
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--brass`, `--brass-bright`, `--paper-deep`, `--ink`, `--ease-snap`, `--ease-strike`; scheduler `--lerp: 0.16`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** One transform + one text swap per notch; per-frame cost is a single style write inside the shared loop. Mobile: as breakpoints; the snap is dropped (marker is 1:1) to keep the loop at one write.

### 6.5 Tape-slot line reveals

- **What & why.** Dispatch bodies do not fade in — they come out of the machine. Each line of a bulletin slides out of a horizontal slot, the way punched tape emerges from the reader: the line is *issued*, not displayed. This is the theme's workhorse reveal, used by every non-hero dispatch whose copy does not type (typing is rationed to the hero and to confirmations — a page where everything types is a page nobody finishes reading).
- **Structure.** The dispatch body is a block of `<p>`/line spans, each line wrapped in a slot container (`overflow:hidden`, height = one line-height). Lines are pre-split by the build (server-rendered spans, not client-measured — splitting by JS measurement at runtime is fragile and banned). The full text is in the DOM; the slot clip is a visual state.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: the parent section's dateline has landed (§6.2, +180ms). Each line translates Y from 110% (inside the slot) to 0 over `--dur-line` (250ms) with `--ease-settle`, stagger 60ms per line. A dispatch of 6 lines completes in 250 + 5×60 = 550ms. Lines beyond the 8th do not slot — they are simply present (long dispatches arrive as a typed head and a printed tail; the machine's patience and the reader's are both finite).
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 dateline landed → line 1: t=0–250ms → line 2: t=60–310ms → … → line 8: t=420–670ms → STOP strikes at last line +120ms (§6.6) → dispatch at rest.
- **States (v1).** *Unissued:* lines in slots (visual only). *Issuing:* staggered slide. *Issued:* rest. *Scrolled-past without issuing* (jump-scroll): issued instantly by the fallback sweep.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest: issued; hover/focus/active: n/a (non-interactive); scrolled-past: issued. Touch — identical. Disabled: n/a. Every state except "issuing" is the full text, fully visible.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: full slot treatment, 8-line cap. 768–1199px: cap 6 lines. <768px: cap 4 lines and stagger tightens to 40ms — mobile dispatches are shorter in practice, and the ceremony shortens with them.
- **Content merge.** The lines are the dispatch's own lines; the slot is positioned at the dispatch's own left edge, so text emerges from where the machine would put it — at the column, not from a decorative portal elsewhere on the page.
- **Failure fallback.** The clip is applied by JS-added class only; without JS (or if the scheduler dies) lines are never clipped. A 1.5s watchdog after trigger forces the issued state. Print: fully issued.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Lines that rewrap on resize: the split spans are per-paragraph-line at build time for the *desktop* measure; at narrower measures a span may wrap to two visual lines — the slot height is set to the span's own height (not a fixed line-height), so a wrapped span still reveals cleanly, just taller. Very short dispatch (1–2 lines): slots normally — brevity is not a reason to skip the filing. Scroll-up: no reverse animation, ever (§5 law 4).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No slot, no slide: the dispatch is present when its section arrives, in full, instantly.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Slot containers are purely visual; the text is ordinary DOM text in reading order, available to AT in full before, during and after the reveal (no `aria-hidden` needed because nothing is ever removed — the transform moves, the text remains). Contrast is the body standard, 12.4:1. No focus interaction.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--ink`, `--paper`, `--dur-line`, `--ease-settle`; constants `--slot-stagger: 60ms`, `--slot-cap: 8`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** Transforms on ≤8 spans per dispatch, one dispatch issuing at a time (the scheduler queues arrivals 120ms apart if sections enter together). Mobile: cap and stagger per breakpoints; spans use `will-change` only during their 250ms.

### 6.6 STOP key-strike

- **What & why.** Wire copy has no full stops; it has STOP — the word the operator struck when the message ended, paid for like any other word, and therefore never wasted. On the page, STOP is the theme's punctuation made physical: every dispatch closes with the word striking in like a key-hit, with a hair of ink spread, followed by a short red rule tick. It is the smallest element in the theme and the one visitors will remember, because it turns the end of every message into an event.
- **Structure.** `<span class="stop">STOP</span>` as the final token of every dispatch paragraph, followed by an inline 24×2px `--red` rule (an SVG-free CSS bar, `aria-hidden`). STOP is set in Plex Mono 600, tracking +0.06em, in `--ink` — never red: the word is text; the tick is the mark.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: the completion of its dispatch's arrival (hero typing, slot reveal, or stamp sequence). The strike: scale 1.15→1 over 120ms with `--ease-strike`, plus a 1px ink spread (`text-shadow: 1px 0 0 currentColor`) present at impact and clearing by +200ms. The red tick draws left-to-right in the same 120ms (scaleX 0→1, origin left). Identical parameters for every STOP on the page — a STOP that strikes differently is a different word.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 last body character/line lands → t=0–120ms STOP scales 1.15→1, tick draws → t=120–200ms ink spread dries → rest. In the hero, add the operator's 220ms lift-pause before t=0 (§6.1).
- **States (v1).** *Unstruck:* present in DOM at rest scale (the no-JS state). *Striking:* 120ms. *Struck:* permanent rest. There is no hover, no click — STOP is punctuation, not a control.
- **State matrix (v2).** All platforms and states: struck/rest, except the single 120ms striking row. Touch: identical. Disabled/focus/active: n/a by design, and stated so a builder does not invent a STOP button.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** Identical at all sizes — punctuation does not responsive-design. The tick shortens to 16px below 768px only to preserve line measure.
- **Content merge.** STOP is part of the copy itself — authored in the text, styled in place. A dispatch whose text does not genuinely end (a developing story) ends `MORE FOLLOWS` instead, set the same way; STOP is never faked (§9).
- **Failure fallback.** Without JS, STOP stands at rest — the strike is pure enhancement. If a dispatch's arrival is interrupted (strike-to-complete), its STOP strikes in the same instant the text completes.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Screen-reader pronunciation: the word is real text and reads as "STOP" — correct, and intended; wire copy is meant to be heard that way. Copy supplied by a CMS without a closing STOP: the build appends nothing — instead the dispatch is flagged at the quality gate; silently rewriting wire copy is an editorial act the theme does not automate. Multiple dispatches arriving simultaneously: STOPs strike in dispatch order, 90ms apart (the scheduler's arrival queue).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** STOP present at rest; no scale, no spread; the tick pre-drawn.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Real text in reading order; the tick is `aria-hidden`. Contrast: ink on paper 12.4:1. The strike's overshoot (1.15) never shifts surrounding layout — the span is `display:inline-block` inside a line whose height is already reserved, so the strike causes zero CLS.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--ink`, `--red`, `--ease-strike`; constants `--stop-strike: 120ms`, `--stop-tick: 24px`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** One transform on one span per dispatch; negligible. Mobile: identical (tick 16px).

### 6.7 Morse-rule SVG draw

- **What & why.** Between dispatches, the wire idles — and an idling wire is not silent, it carries the operator's dots and dashes. The section divider in this theme is a Morse rule: an SVG line of dots and dashes that draws itself as the boundary enters. And it is honest Morse: the pattern encodes the *initials of the section it introduces* (S for Scores is `···`, M for Markets is `−−`), so the divider is a transmission about what follows, not random beeping rendered as décor.
- **Structure.** An inline `<svg class="morse-rule" aria-hidden="true">` spanning the column width, 10px tall: a sequence of circles (dots, r=2.5) and rounded rects (dashes, 18×5) in `--ink` at 80%, generated at build time from the next section's title initials using the real Morse table. Total pattern length is normalised to the column width by adjusting inter-element gaps, never by stretching dots into dashes.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: the rule's top crosses 88% viewport height. The pattern draws via stroke-dashoffset / clip wipe left-to-right over `--dur-draw` (600ms) with `--ease-feed`-adjacent linearity (a transmission does not ease; use `cubic-bezier(.4,0,.6,1)` to keep the ends clean). Elements appear in transmission order — a dash takes proportionally longer to "send" than a dot within the 600ms.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 trigger → elements wipe in sequence, each occupying its Morse-proportional share of 600ms (dot = 1 unit, dash = 3 units, intra-letter gap = 1, letter gap = 3) → t=600ms rule complete → the following section's dateline stamp is licensed to strike (its trigger is armed, §6.2).
- **States (v1).** *Undrawn:* present in DOM as a fully-drawn rule (no-JS state); the draw is a clip overlay. *Drawing:* 600ms wipe. *Drawn:* rest, permanent.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop/touch: identical; hover/focus/active: n/a; scrolled-past: drawn. Disabled: n/a.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: full pattern across 68ch. 768–1199px: pattern repeats the initials twice if the measure allows ≥2 full encodings. <768px: a single encoding, gaps compressed to a 320px minimum pattern; if the initials' encoding exceeds the measure even compressed, the rule falls back to the bureau's call sign `−·−·` (the wire's own "wait" — honest, and documented here so it is a decision, not an accident).
- **Content merge.** The rule *is* the boundary between two dispatches and it names the next one — apparatus and content in the same 10px. Its encoding is documented in the section's visually-hidden heading text for AT users ("Section: Markets"), since the SVG itself is decorative.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS: the rule stands drawn. SVG unsupported (effectively never): a 2px `--ink` bottom border on the preceding section is the CSS fallback — a plain rule, still a boundary.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Section titles beginning with characters outside A–Z/0–9 (e.g. "& Markets"): the first encodable letter is used; titles that are pure numerals encode as digits (Morse has digits). Duplicate initials on one page (Scores + Sport): both encode `···` — correct Morse, and the datelines disambiguate; the rule is a rhythm, not a unique ID. Long scroll jumps: rules in jumped regions are set drawn by the fallback sweep.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Rule present, drawn, static.
- **Accessibility (v2).** `aria-hidden="true"`; section identity is carried by real headings/datelines. The pattern's ink at 80% on paper measures ≈7.4:1 — comfortably visible as a boundary without competing with text.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--ink`, `--dur-draw`; constants `--morse-unit` (computed per rule), `--rule-h: 10px`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** One clip-path/offset animation per rule, ≤14 rules per page, never concurrent (arrival queue). Mobile: compressed pattern as above; draw shortens to 400ms.

### 6.8 Wire-stat tickers

- **What & why.** A wire room counts its traffic: words handled, dispatches filed. The page does the same — two mono counters (WORDS TODAY, DISPATCHES FILED) that tick up by exactly one increment per dispatch the visitor actually receives. The stats are the page's own log of this session's reading, which makes them true by construction — the instant a build hard-codes impressive numbers, they become advertising and fail §9.
- **Structure.** A stats line in the hero apparatus (§6.14) and repeated in the footer (§6.17): two `<output>` elements with tabular figures, labelled in apparatus type. WORDS TODAY counts the words of dispatches arrived so far (computed from the DOM at build, accumulated at runtime); DISPATCHES FILED counts arrived dispatches. Both start at the count of whatever is already in view at load (usually 1 and the hero's word count).
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: each dispatch arrival event (§6.2's stamp landing). The filed counter increments by 1 with a 90ms key-strike tick (scale 1.1→1, `--ease-strike`); the words counter counts up to its new total in whole words at 120ms per increment-step, chunking large jumps (a 300-word dispatch counts in steps of 7 words per 120ms — the ticker is a counter, not a slot machine, and it must finish within 900ms). Figures are tabular; the counters' widths are fixed by `ch` units reserved for the final totals, so ticking never reflows the line.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 a dateline lands elsewhere on the page → t=0 filed counter strikes to n+1 (90ms) → t=60ms words counter begins stepping → t≤960ms words counter rests at the true total → both still until the next arrival.
- **States (v1).** *Rest:* true session totals. *Ticking:* ≤960ms after an arrival. *Complete (all dispatches read):* totals equal the page totals; the apparatus label gains the word COMPLETE, struck as a mini-stamp (§6.2 parameters at 0.8 scale).
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest/ticking/complete as above; hover: none; focus: the outputs are not focusable; they are `aria-live` regions (below). Touch — identical. Disabled: n/a. Scrolled-past: counters live in the pinned hero apparatus only while the hero is in view; the footer instance is static text showing final totals (it does not tick — the footer is the filed copy, §6.17).
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: both counters in the hero apparatus line. 768–1199px: both, stacked right. <768px: DISPATCHES FILED only in the hero (words moves to the footer instance) — on a phone, the count of messages outranks the count of words.
- **Content merge.** The numbers are the page's own traffic, derived from the same DOM the visitor is reading. They are the rare stat block that cannot lie without the code visibly lying.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS: the counters render the *page totals* as static figures with the label `ON THIS WIRE` instead of `TODAY` — a true statement about the page, requiring no runtime. If arrival events fail mid-page, the counters freeze at the last true count (a stopped counter, never a guessed one).
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Live-appended dispatches: totals recompute; counters tick for arrivals the visitor witnesses and silently include offscreen arrivals in the words total only when those dispatches later arrive — no, simpler and truer: a dispatch counts when it *arrives* (is received), whether the append happened onscreen or the visitor scrolls to it later; its stamp event is the counting event, once. Re-reads (scroll up/down) never double-count: arrival is one-shot (§5). A page with a single dispatch: DISPATCHES FILED is omitted (see §6.4's law) and WORDS TODAY stands alone.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Counters update instantly to true totals on arrival; no strike scale, no stepping.
- **Accessibility (v2).** `aria-live="polite"` with a 2s throttle shared with §6.4's status region (the two never announce simultaneously; the filed counter has priority). Final totals are ordinary text. Contrast: ink on paper 12.4:1; the COMPLETE stamp is red at 5.9:1, bold, ≥11px apparatus — its legal use.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--ink`, `--red`, `--ease-strike`; constants `--tick-dur: 120ms`, `--tick-max: 900ms`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** Text updates only, inside the shared scheduler; width reservation prevents layout cost. Mobile: single counter in hero (above); stepping chunk doubles (14 words/120ms) to halve the ceremony.

### 6.9 Perforated wirephotos

- **What & why.** Pictures arrived by wire too — transmitted as wirephotos and printed on the same stock, in the same ink. A wirephoto in this theme is a duotone image (ink/paper, never full colour, never sepia — sepia is a photograph album, this is a newsroom) framed by the perforated border of the tape it arrived on, scaling in-frame as it passes through the reader's view. The perforations are not a frame style; they are the holes the feed rollers gripped.
- **Structure.** A `<figure class="wirephoto">`: an inner frame (`overflow:hidden`, aspect-ratio set from the image's real dimensions) holding the `<img>` (duotone applied at the asset level — a pre-processed ink/paper version, not a CSS filter, so print and no-JS get the same photograph), wrapped by perforation rails on all four edges (6px holes, 12px pitch, `--ink` at 12% punched through `--paper-deep`). A `<figcaption>` in caption type: what the photograph shows, then the credit line `WIREPHOTO — BUREAU` in apparatus type.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: scroll, scrubbed. While the figure crosses the middle 60% of the viewport, the image scales 1.07→1.0 (lerped, factor 0.16, `--ease-feed` discipline — the photo settles as the tape settles) and a 6% ink overlay burns off (opacity .06→0) — the photograph "develops" out of the wire's noise exactly once, on its first pass. Subsequent passes: static.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** Figure top enters at 90% viewport height → scrub window opens at 80% → scale 1.07→1.03 and overlay .06→.03 across the first half of the window → scale →1.0, overlay →0 at the window's centre → figure pinned at rest for the remainder; total scrub distance ≈60vh.
- **States (v1).** *In transit:* scaling/developing. *Filed:* rest, permanent. *Hover:* none on the image itself (a photograph is not a button); if the figure is a link (to the full dispatch), the perforation rail darkens 12%→20% on hover — the grip tightens, the picture does not move.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — transit/filed as above; focus (when linked): 2px `--red` outline offset 3px on the frame; active: n/a. Touch — transit/filed identical; no hover row (the rail-darken is skipped; focus row applies on tap-focus). Scrolled-past: filed. Disabled: a broken image shows the caption and a perforated empty frame with the apparatus line `PHOTO LOST ON THE WIRE` — the fiction absorbs the failure honestly rather than showing a browser broken-image icon (alt text still present for AT).
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: wirephotos may sit at 120% of column width, breaking 10% into each margin (the one licensed column break). 768–1199px: 100% of column. <768px: 100%, perforation rails on top/bottom only (side rails cost 28px a phone does not have), scrub window shortens to 40vh.
- **Content merge.** The photograph illustrates the dispatch it sits inside, credited like a real wirephoto, rationed to one per dispatch (§4). It is evidence in the story, not a hero banner — wirephotos never open a page (the hero's photo is part of the hero composition, §6.14, and is the exception that proves the ration).
- **Failure fallback.** No JS: the image stands at scale 1.0, no overlay — the filed state. No image at all: the `PHOTO LOST` frame above. Print: filed state, duotone asset prints as-is.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Colour asset supplied by a CMS: the build pipeline must duotone it; if it cannot, the photo does not ship — a colour photograph in this theme is a wrong note no layout can fix (§10 gate). Slow network: the frame holds its aspect-ratio with the perforated border and a blank-tape shimmer (§6.12 skeleton at 20% scale) until the image lands, then develops. Tall images (>4:3): capped at 3:2 frame with the overflow cropped by the frame, captioned `CROPPED AT THE BUREAU` when the crop is editorially significant.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No scale, no develop: the photograph stands filed from first paint.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Real `alt` text describing the photograph's news content (not "wirephoto of…"); the duotone treatment never reduces the alt obligation. Caption contrast 6.8:1 (`--ink-70`). The developing overlay is decorative and never drops effective contrast below 7:1 at any frame (overlay ≤6% over an already-duotone image).
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--paper-deep`, `--ink`, `--red` (focus), `--tape-pitch`; scheduler `--lerp: 0.16`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** Images are the page's weight risk: duotone assets compress hard (2-tone PNG/WebP, ≤90KB at 1200w, ≤45KB at 700w) and are lazy-loaded below the first dispatch. The scrub writes one transform on one image at a time. Mobile: side rails dropped, scrub window 40vh, asset ≤45KB.

### 6.10 Slip-lift hover + flutter tilt

- **What & why.** A dispatch on the desk is a slip of paper, and the reader's hand is always about to pick it up. Hovering a dispatch slip lifts it — 4px toward the reader, hard shadow growing beneath — and gives it a paper flutter: a ±0.6° tilt that oscillates twice and dies, the soundless memory of thin stock in a draught. It is the theme's one piece of pure tactility, and it is rationed to slips (cards, basket items, filed dispatches) — body dispatches in the reading column do not lift; you do not pick up the page you are reading.
- **Structure.** Applies to `.slip` elements (§6.15 cards, basket items). Transform origin at 50% 100% (the slip lifts from its bottom edge, as a pin would hold the top — no: a slip pulled from a basket lifts from where the fingers grip, the top edge: origin 50% 0%). The shadow is a hard offset pseudo-element (4px/4px/0 `--ink` 18%) that translates to 7px/9px at full lift.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: hover (pointer fine) or focus-visible. Lift: translateY −4px over `--dur-lift` (200ms, `--ease-settle`); shadow grows in the same 200ms. Flutter: rotate keyframes 0° → +0.6° → −0.4° → +0.15° → 0° over 380ms (`--ease-flutter`), starting at lift +60ms. Release: lift reverses over 160ms; flutter does not replay on exit (paper does not flutter on being put down — it is *placed*).
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 pointer enters → t=0–200ms lift + shadow → t=60ms flutter begins → t=440ms flutter dies, slip at full lift → hold while hovered → pointer leaves: t=0–160ms settle back → rest.
- **States (v1).** *Rest:* flat on the desk. *Lifted:* hover/focus state. *Pressed:* if the slip is clickable, §6.11's key-clack supersedes the last 90ms. *Scrolled-past:* rest.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest/lifted/pressed as above; focus-visible produces the lift without the flutter (keyboard users get position, not weather). Touch — no hover: the lift fires on touchstart and settles on touchend (120ms), giving the tap a physical read; a scroll gesture starting on a slip cancels the lift within 16ms (scroll wins — always). Disabled slip (a filed, non-actionable item): no lift; instead its dateline shows the FILED stamp (§6.15) — stillness as information.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px and 768–1199px: full lift + flutter. <768px: lift reduces to −2px, flutter is cut entirely (small slips, big fingers — tilt reads as instability on a 360px screen), shadow offset halves.
- **Content merge.** The slip being lifted is the dispatch itself — its dateline, headline and first line are the content under the reader's hand. The lift never reveals anything hidden beneath (no "back of the slip" gimmicks): what lifts is what you were already reading.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS or no transform support: slips are static; hover still gets the CSS-only shadow change (a `:hover` box-shadow, no motion). Nothing about the content depends on the lift.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Rapid pointer sweeps across a basket grid: lifts queue per-element but flutter is suppressed if the pointer has crossed >3 slips in 300ms (a draught across the whole basket is a wind, not a reading hand — the theme stays calm). Nested interactive children (a link inside a slip): the lift belongs to the slip, the focus ring to the link; both may show simultaneously and must not double-translate. Long-press on touch: no context-menu interference — the lift is visual only, `user-select` untouched.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No lift, no flutter: hover/focus changes the shadow only (instant), preserving the "this is handleable" signal without the motion.
- **Accessibility (v2).** The lift is never the only affordance: actionable slips carry real links/buttons with visible focus (2px `--red` outline, offset 2px, 5.9:1). The flutter is decorative and disabled for `prefers-reduced-motion` and for keyboard focus. Touch targets on slips ≥44px in the interactive axis. Contrast is unchanged by the lift (shadow is ink at 18% behind the slip, never under text).
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--ink`, `--paper-pale`, `--dur-lift`, `--ease-settle`, `--ease-flutter`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** Transform + shadow on one element; the shadow is a pseudo-element, not a filter. No `will-change` at rest (applied on first hover, removed on leave). Mobile: reduced lift, no flutter (above).

### 6.11 Key-clack click

- **What & why.** To acknowledge a dispatch — file it, save it, mark it read — the reader does what the operator does: strikes the key. A click on an actionable dispatch presses it 2px down like a key under the finger, and a small printed ✓ appears at its dateline: the acknowledgement, sent back down the same wire, in the wire's own mark. Every primary action in the theme (acknowledge, file, send, subscribe) is a key-clack; there are no pill buttons in 1911.
- **Structure.** Actionable dispatches and key buttons carry `data-key` behaviour. The press is a transform on the element (`translateY(2px)`) with its hard shadow collapsing 4px→2px (the key bottoms out). The ✓ is a `<span class="filed-tick">` injected at build/runtime into the dateline line, in `--red`, 12px, set in Plex Mono — a printed tick, not an icon font.
- **Motion spec.** Trigger: click/tap/Enter/Space on the actionable element. Press down: 90ms (`--dur-press`, `--ease-snap` — a key does not ease out, it *stops*). Hold while the action resolves (min 120ms so the press always reads). Release: 140ms `--ease-settle`. The ✓ strikes at release: scale 1.3→1, 120ms, `--ease-strike`, with the §6.6 ink spread. If the action fails (network, validation), the ✓ never appears and the dateline takes the correction treatment instead (§6.18) — the wire does not confirm what it did not send.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 pointer down → t=0–90ms press to 2px, shadow collapses → action fires at t=90ms → resolution (≥120ms floor) → release 140ms → ✓ strikes at release +0ms, dries by +200ms → element rests in its filed state (dateline gains FILED in apparatus type beside the tick).
- **States (v1).** *Rest:* unfiled. *Pressed:* the 90ms key travel. *Resolving:* held press. *Filed:* tick + FILED apparatus, permanent for the session; clicking again un-files (tick strikes *out* — a red rule through it, 120ms — because the wire corrects by striking through, never by erasing). *Disabled:* the key does not travel; the element is set in `--ink-70` with the apparatus reason printed (`LINE DOWN — TRY AGAIN AT 15:00` in the form's voice, §12).
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — hover: §6.10 lift (if a slip) or key brightens (`--brass-bright` on hardware keys); focus-visible: 2px red outline; active: pressed; disabled: as above. Touch — no hover; press fires on touchstart for tactility but the *action* fires on touchend (a scroll that began as a press must not file a dispatch); long-press (≥500ms) does not repeat the action. Scrolled-past: state persists (filed stays filed).
- **Breakpoints (v2).** Identical mechanics at all sizes; press depth stays 2px (a key's travel does not scale with the screen). Touch targets carrying the clack are ≥44×44px at every breakpoint.
- **Content merge.** The tick lands on the dispatch's own dateline — the acknowledgement is filed *on the message*, the way a clerk initials the slip. The action it triggers is the product's real action (save, acknowledge, subscribe); the clack is its confirmation, not a game layered over it.
- **Failure fallback.** The underlying control is a real `<button>`/link: without JS it still performs its action (form submit / navigation), minus the press and tick; the filed state is then rendered server-side on the resulting page. A JS failure mid-press releases the element (watchdog 400ms) — a key stuck down is a broken key.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Double-click: the second strike within 300ms is ignored (the key is already down). Action latency >2s: the press releases at 2s and the apparatus line shows `SENDING…` in the dateline until resolution — the wire is slow, not dead, and says so. Offline: the action queues and the tick is struck in outline only (an unconfirmed acknowledgement, visually distinct) until sync confirms it solid.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No press travel, no strike scale: the tick appears instantly on success; state changes are instant. The *information* (filed/unfiled) is identical.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Real buttons with real labels (`aria-pressed` for file/unfile toggles; the tick is `aria-hidden`, the state is in the label and the FILED text). Focus ring 2px `--red` at 5.9:1, offset 2px. The press animation never moves the element's hit area (transform only; layout box unchanged), so pointer and keyboard targets stay honest. Success/failure is announced via the shared `role="status"` region ("Filed: Markets dispatch") — never by the tick alone.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--ink`, `--red`, `--brass-bright`, `--dur-press`, `--ease-snap`, `--ease-strike`, `--ease-settle`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** One transform + one span per action; no layout. Mobile: identical, with the touchstart/touchend discipline above.

### 6.12 Blank-tape skeleton + wire-clear transition

- **What & why.** Two states of the tape itself. Loading: before a dispatch arrives, its place on the desk is a strip of *blank tape* — perforated edges, no message yet — which types in as the content lands. Navigation: leaving a page clears the wire — the current dispatch types *off* at machine speed, a beat of blank tape, and the next page's first dispatch begins. Loading and leaving are the same material in two directions, and the theme never shows a spinner: a wire room has no spinners, it has tape that has not been written on yet.
- **Structure.** Skeleton: a `.slip--blank` block per pending dispatch — perforation rails top and bottom, and 3–5 blank tape lines (full-width `--paper-deep` bars at line height, widths following the expected dispatch's line profile where known, else 100/92/97/60%). Transition: a full-column overlay in `--paper` carrying the outgoing dispatch's text clone for the type-off, removed from the accessibility tree (`aria-hidden`), while the real navigation proceeds underneath.
- **Motion spec.** Skeleton: static perforations; the blank lines carry a slow feed shimmer (a 24px `--paper-pale` band translating across each line, linear, 1.8s, staggered 120ms per line — tape creeping through the reader, waiting). When content arrives, the skeleton's lines type in via §6.5's slot reveal at double speed, and the perforations fade to the filed slip's single top rail. Transition: type-off at 8 chars per 16ms frame (≈120ms for a headline-length string, capped 200ms for longer), blank beat 80ms, route change, next page's hero begins (§6.1). Total transition budget ≤450ms; if the next page's data is not ready, its skeleton receives the visitor instead — the wire never shows a void.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** *Skeleton:* t=0 render blank tape → shimmer loop from t=300ms → content ready: shimmer stops mid-line (no completion flourish) → slot reveal 2× speed (≈280ms) → filed. *Transition:* t=0 nav intent → t=0–120ms type-off → t=120–200ms blank tape → t=200ms new page paints (hero dateline already stamped) → t=380ms hero typing begins.
- **States (v1).** *Skeleton:* waiting / receiving / filed. *Transition:* clearing / blank / receiving. *Aborted transition* (user hits back mid-clear): the type-off reverses by simply restoring the page — the clone is discarded, no state was ever changed.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop/touch: identical (both are system states, not interactions). Focus during transition: focus is moved to the new page's dateline heading on arrival (the one licensed focus move in the theme — a route change, announced via the page title). Disabled: n/a. Scrolled-past: n/a.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: skeleton mirrors the expected slip layout including side perforation rails. 768–1199px: top/bottom rails only. <768px: skeleton lines reduce to 3 and the shimmer is cut (a phone on a slow line gets stillness, not theatre, while it waits); transition type-off shortens to a 90ms fade of the column — small screens forgive a simpler wire.
- **Content merge.** The skeleton occupies the exact box the dispatch will occupy (same lines, same rails), so arrival is a filling, not a reflow — CLS from loading is 0 by construction. The transition's type-off uses the page's *own* headline text: the wire clears what it actually sent.
- **Failure fallback.** If content fails to arrive, the skeleton is replaced after the product's timeout by the correction slip (§6.18) — `NOT RECEIVED — THE WIRE APOLOGISES` with a retry key — never by an endlessly shimmering blank. No-JS navigation: ordinary full-page loads; the skeleton pattern still serves as the server-rendered loading shell for slow first paints.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Instant cache hits (bfcache): no skeleton, no transition — the page simply *is there*, and faking a ceremony over a restored page is slop (§9). Prefetched routes: transition shortens to the blank beat only (80ms). Very slow next page (>1.2s): hero skeleton receives the visitor (above). Reduced-data (`Save-Data`): shimmer off, type-off off — plain navigation.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Skeleton: static blank tape, no shimmer; content swaps in whole. Transition: no type-off — a plain route change with the blank-beat removed.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Skeleton containers carry `aria-busy="true"` on the region and a visually-hidden `Loading dispatches` label; the shimmer is `aria-hidden`. The transition clone is `aria-hidden`; the route change is announced by the new page's `<title>` and the focus move above. Nothing time-critical is communicated by the shimmer or the type-off.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--paper`, `--paper-deep`, `--paper-pale`, `--ink`, `--dur-clear`, `--ease-feed`; constants `--shimmer-dur: 1800ms`, `--clear-cap: 200ms`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** The shimmer is one transform per line on ≤5 lines per skeleton, ≤2 skeletons animating concurrently (further skeletons render static). The transition clone is a single text node. Mobile: reduced ceremony per breakpoints.

### 6.13 Wire-room header & navigation (page family: header/nav)

- **What & why.** The header is the machine itself, pinned for the entire visit: masthead, headline tape (§6.3), the wire-room clock, bureau navigation, and the scroll-fed progress tape (§6.4) as its lower edge. In a wire room you never leave the room — you receive the next message in it — so the header never hides, shrinks or transforms on scroll. Its permanence is the theme's wayfinding: the bureaus are always in the same place, like the keys on the desk.
- **Structure.** Fixed band, `--paper-deep`, 96px (≥1200px) / 84px (768–1199) / 76px (<768). Rows: (1) the headline tape, 28px (§6.3); (2) the masthead line, 58px: left, the bureau mark — the service name in Plex Mono 600 15px uppercase with a brass key glyph (a 10px brass square, rotated 0 — the key is a square of brass, not an emoji) — and the bureau nav (HOME WIRE / FOREIGN / MARKETS / SPORT / WEATHER, apparatus type, the current bureau underlined by a 2px `--red` rule — the red pencil of the editor); right, the **wire-room clock**: an SVG analogue clock, 34px, ink hands on a paper face with a brass rim, whose second hand *steps* once per second (telegraph clocks tick; they do not sweep), beside a mono readout `14:02:07` and the bureau's city. (3) The progress tape, 10px (§6.4).
- **Motion spec.** The clock's second hand: rotate 6° per second, 60ms `--ease-snap` step (a tick has a tiny mechanical overshoot — 6.4°→6°). Minute/hour hands move on their minute boundaries with the same step. Nav hover: the red rule draws under the hovered bureau (scaleX, 160ms, `--ease-settle`). Bureau change: §6.12's wire-clear. Nothing else in the header moves; the tape (§6.3) and marker (§6.4) run their own specs inside it.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** Continuous: each second boundary → second hand steps (60ms) → readout updates in the same frame. On scroll: §6.4's loop only. On load: the header is present from first paint (it is server-rendered; a header that animates in is a newsroom arriving late to its own story).
- **States (v1).** *Rest:* ticking, feeding. *Current bureau:* red-ruled. *Line down* (product offline state): the tape stops, the clock keeps ticking (clocks do), and the masthead gains the apparatus line `LINE DOWN` in red — the header is where the wire's health is posted.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — nav items: rest (ink), hover (rule draws), focus-visible (2px red outline offset 2px + rule), active/current (rule solid + weight 600), disabled (a bureau with no service: struck through in ink-70, not a link). Touch — no hover; tap targets are the full 58px row height per item, min 44px wide; current/disabled as desktop. Scrolled-past: n/a — pinned.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: all five bureaus inline. 768–1199px: bureaus beyond the third move behind a `MORE WIRES` key (a §6.11 key button opening a filed-slip menu — a plain absolutely-positioned slip, no animation beyond its dateline stamp). <768px: nav becomes a horizontally scrollable apparatus row under the masthead (the tape row and clock remain; the clock's readout drops its seconds — `14:02` — because a phone header is a crowded desk).
- **Content merge.** The header's contents are the product's real navigation, real clock (the visitor's local time, labelled with the bureau city it is *filed from* — if the product has no bureau city, the readout is omitted and the analogue clock stands alone; an unlabelled clock claiming a place it is not in is a small lie, §9) and the real latest headline.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS: header fully rendered, clock shows the server time at render with the apparatus note `AT PRESS TIME` — honest about being a printed clock. Nav is plain links.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Clock drift/timezone: the clock shows local time via `Date`, stepping aligned to the visitor's second boundary (±30ms tolerance — a clock that ticks at :07.4 of every second feels broken). Midnight rollover: the datelines on the page do not change (they are filed times, historical), the clock simply continues — the wire room does not re-file yesterday's news. Header height changes (responsive) never re-trigger section arrivals (§6.2 triggers are computed against the live header offset).
- **Reduced-motion variant.** The second hand still steps (it is a clock; a frozen clock is a wrong clock) but without the overshoot; the tape is static (§6.3); nav rules appear instantly.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Landmark `banner` + `nav` with `aria-label="Wire bureaus"`; current bureau `aria-current="page"`. The clock is `aria-hidden` (decorative) with the time available as text in the readout, itself `aria-hidden` too — the current time is apparatus, not content, and announcing it every second would be vandalism; a visually-hidden static line gives the bureau and date once. Focus order: masthead link → bureaus → (tape pause, touch only). All header text ≥5.9:1.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--paper-deep`, `--ink`, `--ink-70`, `--red`, `--brass`, `--ease-snap`, `--ease-settle`, `--tape-pitch`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** The clock is one transform per second — the cheapest animation on the page. Header total ≤3 concurrent animations (tape, marker, clock), all in the shared scheduler. Mobile: compressed rows per breakpoints; the analogue clock shrinks to 28px.

### 6.14 Front-bulletin hero composition (page family: hero)

- **What & why.** The hero is the front bulletin: the moment a visitor walks into the wire room and the operator is mid-transmission of the biggest story. It composes §6.1 (the typing lead), §6.2 (its dateline), §6.8 (the traffic counters), §6.9 (one wirephoto, pinned briefly) and one key action into a single filed unit. Everything in it is the lead story's own apparatus — there is no separate "hero design" laid over the news.
- **Structure.** Within the dispatch column, full width: (1) the hero dateline, stamped, with an optional FLASH stamp (rationed: one per page, only for a genuinely developing lead); (2) the hero dispatch (§6.1) as the page's lead paragraph, 30px; (3) a two-part row: left, the wirephoto (§6.9) at 46% column width; right, the apparatus stack — the wire-stat counters (§6.8), the filed-time and operator initials (`FILED 14:02 — OP. R.M.`), and the primary key action (e.g. `FOLLOW THE WIRE` subscribe key, §6.11 mechanics, brass-bordered); (4) the Morse rule (§6.7) closing the hero, encoding the next section's initials.
- **Motion spec.** Sequence is §6.1's timeline with the apparatus arriving around it: dateline strikes at load; typing begins +180ms; the wirephoto develops (§6.9) as the typing passes its line; counters start at the hero's own values (1 filed, hero's word count) — they do not count up from zero (the wire was running before you arrived); the action key is live from first paint (actions never wait for theatre). The wirephoto pins for 40vh of scroll after the hero while the first in-stack dispatch arrives beside/below it — the one licensed pin (§5).
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 first paint (all apparatus present, lead un-typed overlay armed) → t=180ms dateline landed, typing starts → ≈t=2,820ms STOP strikes → t≈3,600ms cursor to ambient → scroll: photo pin window (40vh) while dispatch 2 stamps in → pin releases exactly as the Morse rule draws.
- **States (v1).** *Receiving:* the typing window. *Filed:* complete hero, all apparatus live. *Developing story variant:* if the lead is live, the dateline gains a red `DEVELOPING` apparatus tag and the dispatch's ending is `MORE FOLLOWS` (§6.6) — the hero's state is editorial, declared in its own apparatus, never implied by extra motion.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest: filed; hover: only the key and photo link respond (§§6.10–6.11); focus: key focus ring; the lead text is not interactive. Touch — same, minus hovers; the pin window shortens to 24vh (long pins on small screens feel like the page is stuck). Scrolled-past: filed and still; the pin never re-engages on scroll-up. Disabled: if the lead story is withdrawn, the hero is replaced by a correction slip (§6.18) — a hero never silently swaps its text.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: as structured. 768–1199px: photo row stacks — photo 100% width above the apparatus row; pin cut. <768px: photo at 100%, counters reduce to DISPATCHES FILED (§6.8), key becomes full-column-width (the one full-width key on the page), pin cut, typing compressed (§6.1).
- **Content merge.** Every hero element is the lead story's own: its dateline, its text, its photograph, its traffic, and the product's primary action phrased as a wire act (`FOLLOW THE WIRE`, `FILE A TIP`). A hero whose copy could be swapped for another day's news without touching the apparatus is correct; a hero whose apparatus *is* the content is a poster, and fails.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS: the hero stands complete — typed lead, developed photo, static counters at page totals (§6.8 fallback labelling). Slow image: the text ceremony proceeds on schedule; the photo develops whenever it lands (the wire does not hold a bulletin for its photograph).
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Lead longer than 160 chars: gate failure (§10) — the hero does not shrink type to fit. No photograph available: the apparatus stack expands to full width and the pin is cancelled — an empty perforated frame in the hero is a hole in the front page. Live lead updating mid-visit: the hero does *not* retype in place (retyping under a reading visitor is vandalism); a correction slip (§6.18) arrives at the top of the stack announcing the update, and the hero updates only on the visitor's key-strike on that slip.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** Fully filed hero at load: no typing, no pin, photo developed, counters at true values.
- **Accessibility (v2).** The lead is the page's first heading-equivalent (the dateline is its `aria-labelledby` partner where no separate H1 exists; where an H1 exists it is the dispatch's own headline line, visually the first typed line — heading semantics never wait for the typing overlay, §6.1). Landmarks and order: dateline → lead → photo (alt per §6.9) → counters (status rules per §6.8) → key. All contrast per component specs.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** All tokens of §§6.1, 6.2, 6.8, 6.9, 6.11; plus `--col: 68ch`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** The hero is the LCP: lead text paints from DOM at first paint (the overlay never delays it), the photo is the only hero image (≤90KB), total hero JS is the shared scheduler. Mobile: pin and second counter cut, ceremony compressed — the hero's mobile budget is *less* theatre, same facts.

### 6.15 Bulletin slip cards (page family: cards)

- **What & why.** Wherever dispatches repeat — scores, results, earlier wires, related bulletins — they are filed as slips in the basket: pale paper, perforated top edge, a dateline, a headline, the first line of the story, and its filed time. A slip is a dispatch you can hold; the card system's whole job is to make twenty headlines feel like twenty physical messages, each complete enough to judge, each honest about being an excerpt.
- **Structure.** `<article class="slip">` on `--paper-pale`, perforated top rail (holes punched through to `--paper`), padding 24px. Contents in fixed order: dateline (mini, §6.2 at 0.85 scale, pre-stamped — basket slips do not stamp on scroll; they were filed earlier, and their stillness says so), headline (Plex Mono 600 17px), first line (16px, max 2 lines, cut with `— MORE` not an ellipsis: the wire cuts, it does not trail off), apparatus footer: filed time + word count + the slip's action (read / file, §6.11). Priority slips (FLASH) carry the red stamp at the dateline.
- **Motion spec.** Entry: basket slips arrive with the basket section — a settle (translateY 8px→0, opacity, 300ms, `--ease-settle`, stagger 70ms per slip, cap 6 animated; beyond 6, slips are simply present). Hover/focus: §6.10 lift + flutter. Click on the slip's action: §6.11 clack. Nothing else moves; a basket is a still place.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** Section trigger (dateline of the basket section lands) → slips settle t=0,70,140… (≤6) → rest → hover choreography per §6.10 → action per §6.11.
- **States (v1).** *Rest:* filed slip. *Lifted:* §6.10. *Filed/read:* once opened, the slip's dateline gains the FILED apparatus and its headline weight drops 600→500 — read news sits quieter in the basket. *Developing:* a red DEVELOPING tag (as §6.14). *Disabled/expired:* results slips past their day are set in `--ink-70` headline with the apparatus `CLOSED` — yesterday's scores are history, and the basket says so.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest/lifted/read/developing/closed as above; focus-visible: lift without flutter + red outline on the slip's action. Touch — lift on touchstart per §6.10; read/developing/closed identical. Scrolled-past: entry never replays. Disabled: closed row above.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: 2-column basket, slips at ~328px. 768–1199px: 2 columns narrow or 1 column at the column measure — choose 1 column when slips carry >2 lines (measure beats symmetry). <768px: 1 column, perforation rail on top only, entry stagger cap 3.
- **Content merge.** The slip's text is the dispatch's real dateline, headline and first line, generated from the same record as the full dispatch — never a separately-written teaser. The `— MORE` cut is at a word boundary in the real first sentence.
- **Failure fallback.** No JS: all slips present, unstaggered, fully readable; read-state is server-rendered where the product knows it. Broken data (missing filed time): the apparatus prints `TIME NOT FILED` — the wire admits the gap in apparatus rather than inventing a timestamp.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Very long headlines (>90 chars): capped at 2 lines with the `— MORE` cut applied to the headline itself; the full headline lives on the dispatch page and in the `title`/accessible name. Empty basket: §6.18's empty state (`WIRE SILENT`), never a blank grid. Mixed ages: slips sort by filed time descending, always — a basket in any other order is a pile, not a file.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No entry settle, no flutter; lift becomes shadow-only (§6.10 variant); read-state weight change is instant.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Each slip is an `<article>` with its headline as a heading; the action is a real link/button with a unique accessible name (`Read: Markets dispatch, filed 14:02`). Whole-slip click targets are built by stretching the action link (`::after` inset 0) so the target is ≥44px everywhere without nested-interactive violations. Read-state is conveyed by the FILED text, not weight alone. Contrast: headline ink on pale 13.6:1; apparatus ink-70 on pale 7.4:1.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--paper-pale`, `--paper`, `--ink`, `--ink-70`, `--red`, `--dur-lift`, `--ease-settle`, `--ease-flutter`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** Entry animation caps at 6 slips; slips are `content-visibility:auto` below the fold in long baskets. Mobile: single column, stagger cap 3, flutter cut (§6.10).

### 6.16 File-a-dispatch form (page family: forms / conversion)

- **What & why.** The conversion form — a news tip, a wire subscription, a results alert signup — is a blank telegram form: the ruled paper slip the sender fills in by hand and hands to the operator. Fields are not boxes; they are ruled lines on the form, labelled in apparatus type, and submitting is *sending*: the SEND key strikes, and the wire answers with a confirmation dispatch typed back to you. The form is the theme's one place where the visitor transmits — so it gets the full ceremony the reading pages only receive.
- **Structure.** A `<form class="wire-form">` on `--paper-pale`, perforated left rail, max-width 56ch. Header: the form's own dateline (`FORM 7 — FILE A DISPATCH`) pre-stamped. Fields in wire order (most important first — the inverted pyramid applies to forms: the message field precedes the name field, because the wire wants the news before the sender): each field is a label (apparatus, 11px) above a borderless input on a 1.5px `--ink` ruled bottom border, 16px Plex Mono text. The SEND key is a brass-bordered key button (§6.11), full form width at <768px. Errors appear as correction slips (§6.18) above the field, and the field's rule turns `--red`.
- **Motion spec.** Focus: the field's rule inks from 1.5px `--ink` at 40% to full `--ink` + a brass caret block blinking at the clatter rate (the operator's cursor, waiting). Submit: the SEND key clacks (§6.11: 90ms press); the form's fields strike-to-settle line by line (each line's ink-spread clears, 60ms stagger — the message being *sent*); on success, the form is replaced by the confirmation dispatch, which *types in* with §6.1's cadence engine at compressed cadence (the one other licensed typing site on the page): `RECEIVED STOP YOUR DISPATCH IS FILED AS NUMBER 214 STOP` — the number is the product's real reference. On failure, the wire-clear does *not* run; the correction slip arrives (§6.18) and focus moves to the first error.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 SEND struck → t=90ms press bottoms, submit fires → lines settle t=90–90+60×n ms → response: success — form fades to blank tape (150ms) → confirmation types from t≈400ms, ≈30ms/char compressed, STOP strikes at end → total ≤2.4s. Failure — correction slip stamps in at response +0ms (180ms, §6.2), focus moves at +200ms.
- **States (v1).** *Rest:* ruled, empty. *Focus:* inked rule + brass caret. *Filled:* the rule stays at 70% ink (a completed line is darker than an untouched one — the form remembers being written on). *Error:* red rule + correction slip; persistent until corrected (errors never time out on a form). *Sending:* key held, fields disabled, apparatus line reads `SENDING — HOLD THE LINE`. *Sent:* replaced by the confirmation dispatch; the form does not reappear (a `FILE ANOTHER` key below the confirmation starts a fresh blank form).
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — as above; hover on SEND: brass brightens. Touch — focus behaviour identical (the OS keyboard is the operator now); the SEND key's target is ≥48px tall; error slips are dismissible only by correcting the field. Disabled field (e.g. bureau preselected): set in ink-70 with the value printed, not greyed-out-invisible — a printed form shows its pre-prints. Scrolled-past: n/a.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: form at 56ch in the column, perforated left rail. 768–1199px: 100% of column. <768px: rail drops (padding returns), SEND full-width, field spacing grows to 20px (thumbs), confirmation typing compresses further (22ms/char).
- **Content merge.** The form's fields are the product's real conversion data; its labels are wire labels because the *act* is filing a dispatch — `YOUR MESSAGE`, `YOUR NAME FOR THE FILE`, `WHERE THE WIRE ANSWERS (EMAIL)`. The confirmation's reference number, the error texts, the success copy are all the product's real strings in wire voice (§12), not lorem ceremony.
- **Failure fallback.** A plain HTML form, server-validated, fully usable with no JS: labels, inputs, submit; errors re-render server-side as the same correction slips (static). The typing confirmation degrades to a static confirmation dispatch on the success page. Nothing about the transaction depends on the ceremony.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Double-submit: the key locks after the first strike until resolution (§6.11). Long messages (>the product's limit): the field's apparatus counts words — in wire voice, the counter is a *word count* (`WORDS: 212`) that turns red past the limit, because the wire charges by the word and so, here, does the product. Autofill: filled-state styling applies (70% rule) so autofilled forms look written, not broken. Session timeout mid-form: the draft persists (localStorage under the form's dateline) and the returning visitor finds the form *as filed so far*, with the apparatus line `DRAFT HELD AT THE OFFICE`.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** No line-settle, no typed confirmation: the confirmation dispatch appears whole; focus and error states apply instantly. The word counter and all states are unchanged.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Real `<label>`s (the apparatus labels *are* the labels, programmatically associated); errors linked by `aria-describedby`, announced via `role="alert"` on the correction slip, focus moved to the first invalid field on submit-failure only. Required fields marked in apparatus (`REQUIRED` printed, not an asterisk in a colour). Contrast: labels ink-70 on pale 7.4:1; error red on pale 6.4:1 plus the slip's text (never colour alone). The brass caret is decorative; the real caret is untouched. Targets ≥44px.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--paper-pale`, `--ink`, `--ink-70`, `--red`, `--brass`, `--brass-bright`, `--dur-press`, `--ease-strike`, `--ease-settle`; §6.1 engine constants.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** The form's JS is the shared scheduler + validation (≤2KB); the confirmation reuses the cadence engine. Mobile: per breakpoints; the line-settle stagger halves.

### 6.17 End-of-wire footer (page family: footer)

- **What & why.** A wire transmission ends, and so does the page: the footer is the end-of-file — the operator's sign-off, the office's imprint, and the final traffic totals, printed once and never animated again. It is the quietest element in the theme, deliberately: after a page of arrivals, the footer is the sound of the key at rest.
- **Structure.** A `--paper-deep` band (the desk's edge) above a `--paper` colophon block: (1) the sign-off line, set as a dispatch: `END OF FILE STOP` struck once when the footer first arrives (§6.6 parameters) — the page's last STOP; (2) the final wire stats (§6.8, static totals for the whole page, labelled `ON THIS WIRE`); (3) bureau links repeated as a filed index (plain apparatus list); (4) the imprint: service name, office address in dateline form, copyright with the year as figures; (5) the operator line: `RECEIVED AND FILED BY THE WIRE ROOM` with the page's own filed time.
- **Motion spec.** One motion only: the END OF FILE STOP strike on first entry (180ms discipline per §6.6). Everything else is print. The clock does not appear here (it lives in the header; a second clock is a second room).
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** Footer top crosses 88% viewport → t=0 STOP strikes (120ms) → rest, permanently.
- **States (v1).** *Unfiled (not yet reached):* complete in DOM. *Filed:* STOP struck. No other states exist; hover applies only to the index links (Morse-rule underline draw, 160ms, §11).
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop/touch: identical; links per §6.13 nav states. Scrolled-past: filed. Disabled: n/a.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px: colophon in two columns (index left, imprint right). 768–1199px: stacked, index first. <768px: stacked, stats reduce to DISPATCHES FILED (§6.8 rule), imprint last.
- **Content merge.** The footer's stats are the page's true totals; its index is the real navigation; its imprint is the real legal line. It is the filed copy of the page the visitor just received — the same relationship a carbon has to a dispatch.
- **Failure fallback.** Entirely static by default; no JS required for any part of it.
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Pages with one dispatch: stats omit DISPATCHES FILED (§6.8 law) and the sign-off stands alone. Legal requirements for extra links: they join the index in apparatus type — the footer absorbs bureaucracy without changing costume.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** The STOP is present unstruck. Nothing else changes.
- **Accessibility (v2).** `contentinfo` landmark; index as a `nav` with `aria-label="Filed index"`. Contrast: ink on paper-deep 11.2:1. The struck STOP is real text.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--paper-deep`, `--paper`, `--ink`, `--ink-70`, `--red`, `--ease-strike`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** Zero ongoing cost. Mobile: stacked layout per breakpoints.

### 6.18 Correction slip (page family: alerts / empty states)

- **What & why.** The wire's conscience. When the wire errs — a story corrected, a dispatch not received, a basket empty, a form refused — it does not apologise in a rounded toast; it files a correction slip: a red-ruled slip that stamps in *above* the thing it corrects, in the open, permanently part of the record. Corrections in this theme are never deleted, never auto-dismissed, and never phrased as anything but what they are. This element carries all of the theme's alert, error and empty-state duties.
- **Structure.** A `--paper-pale` slip with a 2px `--red-deep` top rule and perforated bottom edge, containing: the stamp `CORRECTION` (or `NOT RECEIVED` / `WIRE SILENT` for the empty state) in Franklin 800 red, rotated −3°; the correction text in dispatch body (what was wrong, what is true now, in wire voice, §12); and where action is possible, one key (§6.11: `READ THE CORRECTED DISPATCH`, `TRY THE WIRE AGAIN`). In forms it sits directly above its field (§6.16); in the stack, directly above the dispatch it corrects; as an empty state, alone in the basket.
- **Motion spec.** Arrival: the slip settles (translateY −6px→0, 220ms, `--ease-settle`) and its stamp strikes at +120ms (§6.2 parameters). It never leaves by animation: dismissal (where the product allows it) is a filing act — the slip gains the FILED apparatus and collapses to a one-line record (`CORRECTION FILED — SEE ABOVE`) over 200ms. Auto-dismiss does not exist in this theme.
- **Choreography timeline (v2).** t=0 the error/emptiness is known → t=0–220ms slip settles → t=120–300ms stamp strikes → rest, permanent → (optional filing: settle-out 200ms to the one-line record).
- **States (v1).** *Posted:* full slip. *Filed:* one-line record (where dismissal is allowed). *Superseded:* if a second correction corrects the first, the first gains `SUPERSEDED` in apparatus and the second posts below it — the record shows its own history.
- **State matrix (v2).** Desktop — rest: posted; hover: none (a correction is not a toy); focus: the slip is focusable as an alert region when it appears dynamically (`tabindex="-1"`, focus moved only for form errors per §6.16, never for passive corrections — focus theft for a footnote is vandalism). Touch — identical. Disabled: n/a. Scrolled-past: posted.
- **Breakpoints (v2).** ≥1200px / 768–1199px: slip at column width, stamp inline left. <768px: stamp stacks above the text (narrow slips), text stays 16px — error copy never shrinks.
- **Content merge.** The correction's text is the product's real correction, referencing the real dispatch it amends (`THE DISPATCH ABOVE, FILED 09:14, STATED…`). An empty state's text is the true state of the wire (`NO DISPATCHES SINCE 06:00 — THE WIRE IS LISTENING`) with the real next expected update where the product knows it.
- **Failure fallback.** Server-rendered corrections are static slips with pre-struck stamps — the pattern's default state. If JS fails while posting a dynamic correction, the fallback is a plain red-ruled block with the same text (the stamp is enhancement; the correction is content).
- **Edge cases & failure modes (v2).** Multiple simultaneous errors (form): one slip per field (§6.16) plus no global slip — a stack of global alarms is a panic, not a wire. Correction of a correction: the SUPERSEDED chain above, capped at 2 visible slips (older ones file to one-line records). Empty state on a *filtered* basket: the text names the filter (`NO DISPATCHES UNDER "SPORT" SINCE 06:00`) — an empty state that blames the wire for the visitor's filter is a false dispatch.
- **Reduced-motion variant.** The slip appears whole, stamp pre-struck; filing collapses instantly.
- **Accessibility (v2).** Dynamic corrections use `role="alert"` (assertive only for form-blocking errors; `role="status"` for passive corrections and empty states — the wire does not shout about an empty basket). The stamp word is real text, read first. Contrast: red-deep on pale 8.4:1 for the stamp; body ink on pale 13.6:1. The one-line filed record remains in the accessibility tree — a filed correction is still a correction.
- **Tokens consumed (v2).** `--paper-pale`, `--red`, `--red-deep`, `--ink`, `--dur-stamp`, `--ease-strike`, `--ease-settle`.
- **Performance budget & mobile variant.** One settle + one strike per posting; corrections are rare by design (a page posting them constantly has a product problem, not an animation budget). Mobile: stacked stamp per breakpoints.

## 7. Page application map

**Home (the front wire).** Header (§6.13) → front-bulletin hero (§6.14) with the day's lead → the bulletin stack: 4–8 dispatches, each dateline-stamped (§6.2) with slot reveals (§6.5), separated by Morse rules (§6.7), wire stats ticking (§6.8) → one basket of earlier wires (§6.15) → the file-a-dispatch form (§6.16) if the product takes tips/subscriptions → end-of-wire footer (§6.17). Maximum one wirephoto per dispatch; the hero's is the only pinned one.

**Listing / bureau page (scores, markets, results).** The bureau's dateline is the page's own (`MARKETS, JUNE 3 —`) stamped at load. Tables (§11) lead — on a bureau page the table *is* the dispatch, set in tabular mono with red-ruled leaders — followed by basket slips of the day's related dispatches. The tape carries the bureau's latest figure, not the site's lead story (the tape always carries the wire you are standing in).

**Detail / dispatch page.** One dispatch, full length: dateline, headline line, body in slot-revealed paragraphs (typing is *not* used past the hero — a 900-word dispatch types for nobody), wirephoto ration applies, corrections (§6.18) post above the paragraph they correct, inline in the record. The progress marker counts the page's sections; a single-dispatch page with no sections omits the counter (§6.4 law) and the marker rides alone. Footer carries `END OF DISPATCH` instead of `END OF FILE`.

**About / the wire room page.** The fiction explains itself, once, in its own voice: the bureau's history as a sequence of filed dispatches (founded, first wire, record day), the operators as byline apparatus, the clock given its one moment of glory — a full-width clock face section where the stepping second hand is the exhibit. Restraint returns immediately after; an about page that performs the theme harder than the news pages have inverted the product.

**Contact / subscribe (conversion).** §6.16 is the page: the form as the hero, its confirmation dispatch as the success state. Supporting copy is rationed to one dispatch above the form (why file, what the wire sends back) — a conversion page in this theme is a form with a dateline, not a landing page with a form buried in it.

**404 / line down.** The wire room's failure page: a correction slip (§6.18) at full column width — `NOT RECEIVED STOP THE PAGE YOU ASKED FOR DID NOT COME OVER THE WIRE` — the requested address printed as the failed dateline, one key back to the home wire. The clock keeps ticking in the header; the room is fine, the message is not.

**Article/feature exception.** If the product must run one long-form piece, it runs as a *special dispatch*: single column widened to 72ch, slot reveals only, no stamps after the first, Morse rules at its internal section breaks encoding its own initials. One per site, marked SPECIAL — a wire that sends features daily is a magazine in costume.

## 9. Anti-slop — how THIS theme goes cheap, and the cure

1. **Typing that blocks reading.** The hero types while the visitor waits, or worse, body dispatches type too. *Cure:* typing is rationed to the hero and confirmations (§§6.1, 6.16); strike-to-complete and the 1.5s watchdog are law (§5); body copy slot-reveals or stands.
2. **Fake Morse.** Random dots and dashes as "texture" between sections. *Cure:* every rule encodes its section's real initials (§6.7); if a builder cannot say what a rule transmits, it does not ship.
3. **A ticker of slogans.** The tape looping "WELCOME TO OUR NEWS SITE ★★★". *Cure:* the tape carries the genuine latest headline and its filed time, generated from the lead's data (§6.3); a silent wire shows its last real message.
4. **Invented counters.** WORDS TODAY: 1,204,553 on a page with nine dispatches. *Cure:* counters count this page's actual arrivals from the DOM (§6.8); the no-JS fallback relabels honestly (`ON THIS WIRE`).
5. **STOP as confetti.** STOP mid-sentence, STOP as a button label, STOP in the footer nav. *Cure:* STOP ends dispatches and the file, nowhere else (§6.6); the one licensed exception is wire-voice copy *inside* a correction or confirmation dispatch, where it is genuinely punctuation.
6. **The typewriter costume.** A "vintage typewriter" display font, grunge textures, torn edges, coffee stains. *Cure:* two faces only (§3); the paper grain at 3% is the only texture; this is a *working room in 1911*, not a scrapbook of one.
7. **Sepia photographs.** Warm brown photo filters "for the old-time feel". *Cure:* wirephotos are ink/paper duotone, pre-processed (§6.9); sepia is banned by name.
8. **Rounded retro.** Border-radius on slips "to soften it", blurred shadows, glassy header. *Cure:* radius is 0 everywhere except the clock face and the punched holes; shadows are hard ink offsets (§4).
9. **Sound effects.** Key clacks and stamp thuds as audio. *Cure:* the theme is silent. Every "clack" in §6 is a visual event; autoplay audio in a newsroom theme is a fire alarm in a library.
10. **Ceremony over cache.** Skeletons and wire-clears performed over instantly-available pages. *Cure:* bfcache/prefetch states skip the ceremony (§6.12); the wire does not act out receiving what it already holds.
11. **Adjective creep.** Copy that says "BREAKING: Amazing scenes as plucky underdogs stun mighty rivals". *Cure:* the voice rules (§12) — if the copy desk would strike a word, the build does not set it. The theme's premium feel is editorial before it is visual.
12. **The frozen tape.** A tape loop that starts at position 0 on every load, or jams mid-scroll. *Cure:* randomised loop offset (§6.3); the only legal tape states are feeding, paused-by-visitor, or static-by-fallback.

## 10. Quality gate

No partial credit. Every item must pass before a Telegraph Wire build ships; items marked ◆ are blocking and cannot be waived by deadline.

1. ◆ Full text of every dispatch is in the DOM at load; with JS disabled the page reads complete, in order, with all datelines stamped and all STOPs present.
2. ◆ Typing exists only in the hero and in form confirmations; strike-to-complete works by click, keypress and scroll on both; the 1.5s watchdog completes any partial line (tested by throttling the scheduler).
3. ◆ The tape's headline is byte-identical to the lead dispatch's headline, generated from the same source; the loop offset is randomised; hover/focus/hidden all pause it.
4. ◆ MESSAGE n OF N: N equals the DOM's dispatch count (script-checked); single-dispatch pages omit the counter entirely.
5. ◆ Every Morse rule's pattern decodes, via the standard Morse table, to the initials of the section it introduces (or the documented `−·−·` fallback) — checked by decoding, not by eye.
6. ◆ Counters equal the sum of arrived dispatches and their DOM word counts at every moment (instrumented test: scroll the page, compare).
7. ◆ Every dispatch ends with STOP or `MORE FOLLOWS`; no STOP appears anywhere else except inside correction/confirmation copy.
8. ◆ Photographs are duotone ink/paper assets (sampled: no pixel with saturation above the duotone ramp); no full-colour or sepia image ships.
9. `border-radius` is 0 on all elements except the clock face and perforation holes (CSS audit).
10. ◆ With `prefers-reduced-motion`, the page is fully filed at load: no typing, no loop, no slot reveals — and no information is missing.
11. Focus is visible on every interactive element (2px red outline), and the key-clack's filed state is exposed via `aria-pressed`/label, never by the tick alone.
12. ◆ The hero lead is ≤160 characters and fits 3 lines at 20px on a 360px screen without shrinking.
13. Scroll at 60fps on a mid-tier phone: the shared scheduler's per-frame cost ≤2ms average with tape, marker and clock live; no element runs a private interval (code audit).
14. The clock ticks on the visitor's second boundary (±30ms) and its readout matches the analogue hands.
15. ◆ A correction, once posted, cannot be dismissed into nothing: it files to a one-line record that remains in the accessibility tree.
16. Print stylesheet: dispatches print fully typed, header unpins, tape renders static with the latest headline.

- **Skeleton.** §6.12 blank tape. No grey-pulse blocks exist in this theme.
- **Divider.** §6.7 Morse rule (transmitted) or the table's printed dash (static) — the two are never interchanged.
- **Byline / avatar.** Operators are initials in apparatus (`OP. R.M.`), never photographs or avatar circles — the wire room is anonymous by profession.

## 12. Content & copywriting voice (v2)

The theme's premium is editorial. These rules govern every string the build sets, including the ones engineers usually write last and worst.

**The wire style laws.**
1. **Lead first.** Every dispatch opens with its whole story in ≤25 words. If the first sentence can be cut without losing the news, it was not the lead.
2. **Inverted pyramid.** Facts descend in importance; any dispatch must survive being cut from the bottom at any paragraph.
3. **No adjectives.** Evaluative adjectives are struck. Numbers, names and verbs carry the weight. (Wire exceptions: *first, last, only* — facts wearing adjective clothes.)
4. **Figures for numbers.** `3`, never `three`, in headlines, body and apparatus alike.
5. **Present tense headlines.** `LINER DOCKS`, not `LINER HAS DOCKED`.
6. **STOP ends dispatches.** No full stop appears at the end of a dispatch body; internal sentences use full stops normally — the ban is on wasting the last word.
7. **Datelines are places.** `PLACE, MONTH DAY —`. A dateline never contains a slogan, a section name, or the word "today" (the date is the date).

**Register by surface, with examples.**
- **Headlines.** ✓ `FOG DELAYS 6 LINERS — PILOTS HOLD AT THE HOOK` ✗ `Thick fog causes chaos for travellers in a nightmare morning at the port`.
- **Labels / apparatus.** Short, factual, caps: `FILED 06:12`, `WORDS TODAY`, `MESSAGE 3 OF 9`. ✗ Never `✨ Latest updates!`.
- **Buttons / keys.** Verbs of the wire: `FOLLOW THE WIRE`, `FILE A TIP`, `READ THE DISPATCH`, `TRY THE WIRE AGAIN`. ✗ `Submit`, `Click here`, `Learn more` (learn more is a brochure; the wire *files*).
- **Error messages.** Filed as corrections, factual, with the remedy in the same breath: ✓ `CORRECTION — THE ANSWER ADDRESS IS NOT COMPLETE STOP THE WIRE CANNOT REPLY STOP SUPPLY THE FULL ADDRESS` ✗ `Oops! Something went wrong 😢`.
- **Empty states.** The wire reports its own silence: ✓ `WIRE SILENT — NO DISPATCHES UNDER SPORT SINCE 06:00 STOP THE DESK IS LISTENING` ✗ `Nothing here yet! Check back soon for awesome content`.
- **Success / confirmation.** A received dispatch, with its number: ✓ `RECEIVED STOP YOUR DISPATCH IS FILED AS NUMBER 214 STOP` ✗ `Yay! You're all set! 🎉`.
- **Loading.** No words at all — blank tape (§6.12) is the loading voice. If a wait exceeds the product's timeout, the correction slip speaks (§6.18).

**Banned list (build-checked where feasible).** Exclamation marks in UI copy (the wire does not exclaim; a FLASH stamp is its shout); emoji; the words *awesome, amazing, seamless, leverage, oops*; "content" as a noun for news; exclamation in headlines even for sport — `UNITED WIN 3–1 AT THE DEATH` needs no help.

## 13. Full-page scroll choreography map (v2)

The canonical home page, top to footer, in order. Times are relative to each trigger; §6 holds the per-element detail.

1. **t=load.** Header stands complete; tape mid-pass (random offset); clock ticking on the second boundary. Hero dateline strikes (180ms); hero typing runs (≤3.8s ceremony); counters open at the hero's true values.
2. **First scroll.** Hero wirephoto pin window (40vh): dispatch 2 stamps and slot-reveals beside it; brass marker leaves notch 1; counter strikes to MESSAGE 2.
3. **The stack.** Each dispatch: Morse rule draws (600ms) → dateline strikes (180ms) → slot reveal (≤670ms) → STOP strikes (120ms) → counters tick (≤~1s tail). Beats never overlap by more than the counter tail — the visitor should feel a rhythm of *arrival*, roughly one dispatch per viewport, never a cascade.
4. **Mid-stack wirephoto.** Develops across its 60vh scrub window, files, and is still for the rest of the page.
5. **The basket.** Section dateline strikes; ≤6 slips settle at 70ms staggers; the basket then goes quiet — hovering is the visitor's business, not the page's.
6. **The form (if present).** No arrival ceremony (blank paper). Its choreography is interaction-time (§6.16): focus inks, SEND strikes, confirmation types.
7. **The footer.** END OF FILE STOP strikes once. Everything stops. The only things still moving on the page are in the header: tape, clock, and — if the visitor scrolls back up — nothing else wakes. Scroll-up across the whole map is silent (§5 law 4): no beat replays in either direction.
8. **Jump-scroll catch-up.** Any scroll delta >1200px runs the fallback sweep: all beats in the jumped region resolve instantly to their filed states, so the visitor lands on a *received* page, never a half-typed one.

**The feeling, stated once:** the page begins mid-transmission, receives its news in order at a readable rhythm, counts what it has received, and signs off. If a build's scroll feels like a showreel, the beats are too close together; if it feels like a document, the stamps have been cut — both are failures of this map, in opposite directions.

## 14. Whole-page performance budget (v2)

**Asset budget (home page, first load).** Total ≤ 480KB compressed: HTML/CSS ≤ 60KB; JS ≤ 38KB gz (the shared scheduler + cadence engine + counters + form logic — there is no framework line item; a framework is bought out of the image budget or not at all); fonts ≤ 120KB (Plex Mono 400/500/600 + Franklin 800, subset to Latin + figures, `font-display: swap` with the no-JS/full-text rule covering the swap window); images ≤ 240KB (hero wirephoto ≤90KB + ≤2 in-stack wirephotos ≤75KB each, duotone); grain texture ≤ 4KB (a single 128px tile).

**Runtime budget.** One rAF scheduler for everything (§5 law 1): per-frame JS ≤2ms average, ≤6ms worst frame, on a mid-tier Android (Moto G class) at 60Hz scroll; style writes per frame ≤4 (tape transform, marker transform, one scrub value, one counter at most); no long task >50ms after load; typing never runs on the main thread's critical path — it is a text-node write per burst. LCP ≤2.0s on 4G (the lead's first line, from DOM), CLS ≤0.02 (skeletons and counters are size-reserved by construction), INP ≤200ms (the key-clack's press is a transform; the action behind it is the product's own budget).

**Per-breakpoint ceremony budget.** ≥1200px: full ceremony as specced. 768–1199px: pin cut in the hero (§6.14), slot cap 6. <768px: hero ceremony ≤2.2s, slot cap 4, stepped tape feed, flutter cut, skeleton shimmer cut — the small-screen page is *faster and quieter*, and that is a feature of the wire, not a compromise of it.

**Cut order when the budget is exceeded** (cut in this order, never skip ahead): 1) paper grain breathing (grain goes static, then goes entirely); 2) wirephoto develop overlay (photo still scales); 3) wirephoto scale scrub (photos stand filed); 4) slip flutter (lift remains); 5) tape loop on <768px becomes the static strip; 6) slot reveals shorten (cap −2 lines per breakpoint). **Never cut:** the full-text DOM, the 1.5s fallback, strike-to-complete, STOP strikes, the progress counter's accuracy, focus visibility, the reduced-motion variants. If the budget still fails after step 6, the page carries too many dispatches — cut content, not law.

## 15. Build-order checklist (v2)

Build in this order; each step assumes only verified earlier steps.

1. **Tokens first.** Palette (incl. night-wire values), type scale, easings, duration tokens, tape pitch — as CSS variables with the exact names §6 consumes.
2. **The static page.** The complete no-JS build: header rendered, every dispatch fully typed, counters at page totals with `ON THIS WIRE` labels, form functional server-side. Pass gate items 1, 7, 9, 16 against this build before any motion exists.
3. **Header apparatus.** Clock (stepping, boundary-aligned), masthead nav states, tape as a *static* strip.
4. **The shared scheduler.** One rAF loop, lerp, arrival queue, fallback sweep, 1.5s watchdog — unit-tested against throttling before anything plugs into it.
5. **Arrivals.** Dateline stamps (§6.2), slot reveals (§6.5), STOP strikes (§6.6), Morse rules (§6.7, with the decoder check from gate 5 wired into the build).
6. **The cadence engine.** Hero typing (§6.1) with strike-to-complete; then the tape loop (§6.3) on the scheduler.
7. **Progress & counters.** Scroll-fed tape + marker (§6.4), wire stats (§6.8) driven by arrival events; instrument the gate-6 comparison test.
8. **Photographs.** Duotone pipeline (§6.9) with the saturation check from gate 8 in CI; scrub + develop.
9. **Handling.** Slip lift/flutter (§6.10), key-clack (§6.11) with filed states, basket (§6.15), accordion/tabs/search treatments (§11).
10. **The form.** §6.16 complete: ruled fields, word counter, correction-slip errors, typed confirmation, draft holding.
11. **States of the wire.** Skeletons + wire-clear (§6.12), correction slips (§6.18), 404, offline `LINE DOWN`.
12. **Footer.** §6.17 with the final STOP; print stylesheet.
13. **Reduced-motion & no-JS passes.** Full audit of both modes against §§5–6 variants; gate items 1, 10.
14. **Performance pass.** Budgets in §14 measured on the Moto G target; apply the cut order if needed; gate item 13.
15. **The gate.** §10 in full, in order, no partial credit. Then — and only then — the theme may be used.
