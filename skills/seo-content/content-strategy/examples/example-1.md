# Example 1 — The 200-city page matrix

## Before

The growth plan proposes one landing page per city the company might ever serve: "Plumbing services in {city}" × 200. Every page uses the same skeleton — same H2s, same paragraph order, same three benefit claims — with only the city name, a stock photo, and the phone number swapped. None of the pages carries anything true of one city and not another: no local staff, no local pricing, no local regulations, no local jobs done.

## After

The set is rejected at planning. One canonical "Plumbing services" page covers the offer, the process, and the service area. Individual city pages are admitted only for the cities where the company holds real local data — a named local team, city-specific pricing, the local permit rules it works under — and each such page leads with that data. Twelve cities qualify; the other 188 stay as one line in the canonical page's service-area list until real local operations exist.

Note: corrects CO-084 — a variant page ships only with page-specific verified data that survives the swap test.
