# Example 2 — The article nobody briefed

## Before

The calendar shows Thursday's slot filled: "Blog post about invoicing tips." No brief exists. Nobody can say who the post is for, what it should make them do, which query it answers, who owns it, or who checks its claims. The draft that arrives is 900 generic words that could sit on any accounting blog, cites no data, and links nowhere — written because the slot existed, not because a reader needed it.

## After

The slot is emptied until a brief exists. The brief that clears the bar names: audience — freelance designers who invoice monthly; goal — trial signups for the invoicing feature; intent — the queries Search Console shows the pricing page already drawing ("how to invoice as a freelancer"); owner and reviewer by name; and the evidence — the query data plus three support tickets asking for exactly this guidance. The article is written against that brief, links from the pricing page and the help center as the brief specifies, and ships with a review-by date.

Note: corrects CO-081 — no brief, no draft; a brief names audience, goal, intent, owner, and reviewer.
