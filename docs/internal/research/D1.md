# D1 — Product & Requirements: Phase 2 Research

Purpose: the evidence base Phase 3 drafters use to write the 13 D1 skills. Audience: skill drafters (Phase 3) and D1 reviewers (Phase 4). Method and failure modes are stated once here; per-skill notes carry only what differs.

## 1. Department standard

Professional product work today runs as a loop, not a handoff chain:

1. **Continuous discovery** (Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*): the product trio (PM, designer, engineer) holds at least weekly customer touchpoints, maps findings onto an Opportunity Solution Tree — desired outcome → opportunities (customer needs, pains, desires) → candidate solutions → assumption tests — and tests the riskiest assumptions before committing delivery capacity. Discovery and delivery run concurrently; discovery is not a phase gate.
2. **Requirements engineering** (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018, the current joint international standard, successor to IEEE 830): stakeholder needs are elicited, transformed into system/software requirements, and managed for the life of the product. A well-formed requirement is necessary, unambiguous, singular, feasible, and verifiable, and carries attributes (source, priority, rationale). Traceability is bidirectional: every requirement traces back to a stakeholder need and forward to design, code, and verification.
3. **Stories and acceptance**: stories follow Jeffries' card–conversation–confirmation; confirmation is written as Gherkin scenarios (Given/When/Then) in the customer's language, and those scenarios double as executable acceptance tests.
4. **Scope under fixed time**: DSDM/MoSCoW — Must/Should/Could/Won't-have (this time), with Must haves forming the Minimum Usable Subset and capped at roughly 60% of effort so lower classes act as contingency.
5. **Measurement and economics**: one North Star metric plus a few input metrics (Amplitude framework); unit economics per Skok (fully loaded CAC, margin-based LTV); estimates stated as ranges that narrow with decisions (McConnell's Cone of Uncertainty).
6. **Sequencing**: outcome roadmaps organized by confidence horizon (Now/Next/Later), not feature-date timelines.

D1's distinctive duty inside this company: it owns the **fact sheet**, the single source of truth every later department copies from. Most content fabrication detected downstream is a D1 failure that happened weeks earlier.

Sources: ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018, IEEE Standards Association — https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/29148/6937/ ; Torres, Opportunity Solution Tree — https://www.chameleon.io/blog/opportunity-solution-tree ; Amplitude North Star worksheet bundle — https://info.amplitude.com/rs/138-CDN-550/images/ai-product-metrics-worksheet-bundle.pdf

## 3. Per-skill research notes

### discovery

- **Method:** Run Torres' continuous discovery loop: weekly story-based customer interviews by the product trio; extract specific-moment stories; map opportunities onto the Opportunity Solution Tree under one desired outcome; select a target opportunity by comparing size, frequency, and strategic fit; generate multiple solutions per opportunity; enumerate each solution's assumptions (desirability, usability, feasibility, viability); test the assumptions that are both important and evidence-poor first.
- **Key rules:**
  - No solution enters the backlog without a named target opportunity on the tree.
  - Opportunities are framed as customer needs/pains/desires, never as solutions ("notify me before my ride arrives" is an opportunity; "add push notifications" is a solution).
  - Every candidate solution carries an explicit assumption list before any build commitment.
  - Interview evidence is recorded as stories about specific past moments, not as summarized opinions or feature requests.
  - A discovery cycle that produces no assumption test is incomplete, however many interviews it ran.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Backlog lint: every item carries a non-empty opportunity ID linking to the tree — items without one fail intake. (Manual) Assumption map exists for the target opportunity, with the riskiest assumption named and a test attached, before build starts.
- **Sources:** Torres, Opportunity Solution Tree — https://www.chameleon.io/blog/opportunity-solution-tree ; Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* (Product Talk Press, 2021), method summary — https://glasp.co/posts/teresa-torres-how-to-master-continuous-discovery-for-product-success-glasp-talk-31

### user-research

- **Method:** Triangulate: interviews and observation as the primary source; surveys, support tickets, and analytics as corroboration. The research file records method, participant count and source, and dates for every study. Findings (what was observed) are written separately from interpretations (what the team concludes). Personas and requirements may cite only findings.
- **Key rules:**
  - No persona, segment claim, or "users want X" statement without a cited study behind it.
  - Participants come from the actual target segment; team members, friends, and other builders are not user evidence.
  - Sample limits are stated in the file (who was *not* reached is part of the finding).
  - Quotes and observations keep their context (task, moment, segment); a decontextualized quote is not evidence.
  - Research older than its stated refresh date is marked stale and cannot justify new commitments.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Research-file template check: method, participant count/source, and date fields present and non-empty for every study entry. (Automatable) Persona traceability: every persona cites at least one finding ID.
- **Sources:** Nielsen Norman Group, "Personas Make Users Memorable for Product Team Members" — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/persona/ ; Torres interview practice — https://www.chameleon.io/blog/opportunity-solution-tree

### personas-jtbd

- **Method:** Build a small set of personas (typically 3–5) from clustered research findings, each with goals, context, constraints, and representative tasks. Express each persona's core need as a JTBD statement — "When [situation], I want [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]" — and analyze the forces of progress (push of the current situation, pull of the new solution, anxiety about change, habit of the present) before proposing features.
- **Key rules:**
  - The job statement names a circumstance and a progress sought, never a product feature or a demographic.
  - Demographics appear only where they change the job or its constraints; stock-photo biography (name, age, hobbies) without research behind it is forbidden.
  - One persona per distinct job cluster; two personas with the same job are merged.
  - Each persona is versioned with its evidence date and finding links.
  - Personas are cited by name in trade-off discussions; a decision that names no persona and no job is returned for rework.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Format lint: every JTBD statement matches the When / I want / so I can pattern; every persona carries at least one evidence link. (Manual) Persona review: each persona's job differs from every other persona's job.
- **Sources:** Christensen Institute, "What is Jobs to Be Done Theory?" — https://www.christenseninstitute.org/video/what-is-jobs-to-be-done-theory/ ; Harvard Business School Online, "The Jobs to Be Done Framework" — https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/jobs-to-be-done-examples

### requirements-writing

- **Method:** Follow ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018: elicit stakeholder needs, analyze for conflicts and gaps, specify each requirement as one singular, verifiable statement with attributes (unique ID, source, priority, rationale, verification method), then validate with stakeholders. Maintain bidirectional traceability: need → requirement → design element → acceptance test. Judge the set, not just items: complete, consistent, feasible as a whole.
- **Key rules:**
  - One obligation per requirement (one "shall"); compound requirements are split.
  - Vague terms (fast, user-friendly, robust, seamless, flexible) are banned unless the same sentence carries the measurable criterion.
  - Requirements state the need, not the design; a design constraint is allowed only when it is a real external constraint, and it is labeled as one.
  - Conflicts between requirements are resolved and the resolution recorded; they are never averaged into ambiguous wording.
  - Changes go through review and version history; a requirement edited silently is treated as a new, unvalidated requirement.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Lint pass: banned-vague-term scan, plus field-presence check (ID, source, priority, verification method) on 100% of requirements. (Automatable) Traceability coverage report: every Must-priority requirement has at least one linked acceptance criterion/test — coverage below 100% blocks handoff to build.
- **Sources:** ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018, IEEE Standards Association — https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/29148/6937/ ; 29148 tutorial and characteristic list — https://www.xopsschool.com/mod/page/view.php?id=3365

### user-stories

- **Method:** Apply Jeffries' 3Cs: the card is a token for a conversation, and confirmation is the acceptance criteria attached before build. Draft stories in a workshop with the persona present (by name), keep each story small enough to finish in one iteration, and split along workflow steps or business rules — never along technical layers. Use Bill Wake's INVEST as a diagnostic during refinement, not as a score.
- **Key rules:**
  - The role is a named persona or segment; bare "user" is allowed only when the capability genuinely serves every segment identically.
  - The "so that" benefit clause is mandatory; a story whose benefit cannot be stated is not ready.
  - A disguised task ("As a user, I want an API endpoint…") is relabeled a task and planned as one; it does not borrow story legitimacy.
  - A story too big for one iteration is split before estimation, not estimated hopefully.
  - No story enters build without at least one acceptance criterion (see acceptance-criteria).
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Template lint: story text matches "As a [role], I want [capability], so that [benefit]" (regex). (Automatable) Tracker rule: story status cannot move to "in build" with zero linked acceptance criteria.
- **Sources:** Mike Cohn / Mountain Goat Software, "What Makes a Good User Story?" (INVEST) — https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/agile/user-stories/good-user-stories

### acceptance-criteria

- **Method:** Product, engineering, and QA write criteria together before build, as Gherkin scenarios: Given a context, When one action/event, Then an observable outcome — in the customer's domain language, declarative (what rule holds), not imperative (which buttons get clicked). Enumerate scenarios from the business rule itself: happy path, boundary values, empty/none cases, duplicates, permission failures. The scenarios become the executable acceptance suite (Cucumber or the stack's BDD runner).
- **Key rules:**
  - One behavior per scenario; exactly one When — two Whens means two scenarios.
  - Then clauses state observable outcomes a test can pass or fail; adjectives ("works correctly", "is fast") fail review on sight.
  - Every business rule named in the story gets at least one scenario, including its failure case.
  - Scenarios describe behavior, not UI mechanics, so a redesign does not invalidate the specification.
  - Criteria are frozen for the iteration; a change re-opens the story for re-estimation instead of being absorbed silently.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) BDD suite run: every story in the release maps to at least one passing scenario, and the suite fails on undefined steps. (Automatable) Gherkin lint: one When per scenario; no scenario whose Then lacks an observable assertion. This skill is the direct prevention for DET-CON-028's undefined business rules.
- **Sources:** Gherkin reference (Cucumber project; mirror of the official reference) — https://mirror.niser.ac.in/cran/web/packages/cucumber/vignettes/reference-gherkin.html ; Abstracta, "A Guide to Good Cucumber Practices" — https://abstracta.us/blog/testing-tools/a-guide-to-good-cucumber-practices

### fact-sheet

- **Method:** Adapt the Model Cards pattern (Mitchell et al., 2019) to the whole product: one fixed template — identity and provenance, intended use, inputs/dependencies, measured performance broken down by relevant conditions, risks, limitations — filled by the producer, versioned, owned, and given a review date. The sheet is written *before* any public copy. Downstream departments may publish only facts that exist in the sheet, cited from it.
- **Key rules:**
  - Sheet-first: a fact enters copy, pricing pages, structured data, or sales material only after it exists in the sheet with its evidence. (Primary prevention for DET-CON-011, 012, 014, 015, 016, 017, 024, 028.)
  - Every quantitative claim traces to a measurement, dataset, or document the sheet links to; untraceable claims are removed or marked UNVERIFIED in the sheet itself.
  - No superlatives and no marketing register anywhere in the sheet.
  - Aggregate-only numbers are flagged; performance is disaggregated by the conditions that change it.
  - Unknown fields state "not measured" — a plausible fill is a fabrication. A sheet past its review date blocks new claims until re-verified.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Template completeness check: all sections present, Limitations section non-empty, review date in the future. (Semi-automatable) Claim-trace audit: sample quantitative claims from shipped copy; each must resolve to a sheet entry with an evidence link — one untraceable claim fails the release's content pass (mirrors DET-CON-011/014 checks).
- **Sources:** Mitchell et al., "Model Cards for Model Reporting," FAT* 2019 — https://arxiv.org/html/1810.03993 ; GOV.UK publishing guidance, "Use clear language" — https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/

### scope-control

- **Method:** Prioritize the requirements list with MoSCoW against a fixed timeframe (DSDM practice, maintained by the Agile Business Consortium): Must haves = the Minimum Usable Subset without which the release fails; Should/Could provide contingency; Won't have (this time) is written down in the same document. Per Consortium guidance, Must haves should take no more than about 60% of the effort, measured by effort, not by item count. Changes enter only by displacing equal effort or by a recorded re-baseline.
- **Key rules:**
  - A scope document with an empty Won't-have list is invalid — a boundary that excludes nothing bounds nothing.
  - Every Must traces to a stated project objective; a Must with no objective is demoted for review.
  - If Must effort exceeds ~60% of capacity, re-triage is mandatory before build starts; priority inflation is treated as a defect, not enthusiasm.
  - De-scope order is agreed in advance: Could first, then Should; Musts and the date hold.
  - Every scope change is logged with decider, date, and what it displaced.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Document check: Won't-have section exists and is non-empty; every Must carries an objective link. (Automatable) Effort-share computation from the requirements table: Must share ≤ ~60% of estimated effort. This skill prevents DET-CON-004 and DET-CON-018 (unbuilt or expired scope leaking into copy).
- **Sources:** MoSCoW prioritization guide (Agile Business Consortium reference definitions and effort split) — https://projeric.com/blogs/scope-management/moscow-prioritization ; Rework library, "MoSCoW Method: Prioritize Requirements the Right Way" — https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/moscow-prioritization

### competitive-analysis

- **Method:** Select 2–4 direct competitors plus the relevant indirect ones (including "do nothing" / the current workaround). Run the *same* core tasks in each product using trial accounts; record dated screenshots or notes per claim. Score against shared criteria — capability coverage, usability on the task, price as actually charged, proof offered — in one matrix. Conclusions (table stakes, gaps, differentiation) cite matrix cells, and the matrix is refreshed on a stated schedule.
- **Key rules:**
  - A competitor's marketing page is a claim, not evidence; features are verified hands-on or marked "claimed, unverified."
  - Every matrix cell carries a date and an evidence link; undated cells expire at the refresh date.
  - Pricing is captured from the actual pricing page or trial checkout, and handed to the fact sheet — never re-typed from memory (feeds DET-CON-016 prevention).
  - Analysis ends in decisions (build / skip / position against), recorded next to the matrix; a matrix with no decisions is unfinished.
  - Differentiation claims must name the comparison set; unsupported "#1/best" language is cut here before it reaches copy (DET-CON-026).
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Evidence coverage: 100% of matrix claims carry a dated evidence link. (Automatable) Freshness check: matrix age vs. its stated refresh interval.
- **Sources:** Nielsen Norman Group, "Competitive Usability Evaluations" — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/competitive-usability-evaluations/

### product-metrics

- **Method:** Use the North Star framework: write the North Star statement (the value customers get, in plain words), pick one metric that captures that value and leads revenue rather than being revenue, then define 3–5 input metrics across breadth, depth, frequency, and efficiency that teams can move weekly. Every metric gets an exact written definition (event, time window, segment, exclusions) in a metric dictionary, an owner, and a data source. Name guardrail metrics that must not degrade while the North Star grows.
- **Key rules:**
  - Vanity metrics (registrations, raw pageviews, total accounts) cannot serve as success measures; the North Star must rise only when customers succeed.
  - A metric's definition is versioned; changing a definition without a changelog entry is a defect (silent redefinition is how dashboards start lying).
  - No number is published in copy, decks, or the fact sheet unless it resolves to a dictionary definition and a queryable source (DET-CON-011 prevention).
  - Each input metric has a named owning team; an input nobody can influence is decoration and is removed.
  - Metric reviews check inputs against outcomes; an input that stops predicting the North Star is replaced, with the change recorded.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Dictionary completeness: every metric shown on any dashboard exists in the dictionary with definition, owner, and source. (Automatable) Instrumentation check: the event named in each definition exists in the analytics implementation under the same name.
- **Sources:** Amplitude, North Star / product metrics worksheet bundle — https://info.amplitude.com/rs/138-CDN-550/images/ai-product-metrics-worksheet-bundle.pdf

### pricing-monetization

- **Method:** Follow Stripe's sequencing: (1) choose the value metric — what the customer pays more of as they get more value; (2) choose the pricing model (per seat, tiered flat rate, usage-based, hybrid); (3) design tiers so customers graduate naturally as they grow; (4) instrument and measure. Implement prices as billing-provider objects (products/prices/subscriptions); the pricing page and checkout render from that single configuration, and the fact sheet mirrors it.
- **Key rules:**
  - The value metric must scale with value received, not with an internal cost the customer cannot see.
  - Prices are never hard-coded in page copy, emails, or ads; every price surface renders from the billing configuration (the direct prevention for DET-CON-016).
  - A free tier has explicit limits and a designed conversion path; an unlimited free tier is a pricing defect.
  - Promotions and discounts carry visible dates and terms (prevents DET-CON-018 stale-price copy).
  - Tier feature splits are decided from the value metric and upgrade path, never copied from a competitor's three price points.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Price-consistency scan: extract every price/plan mention across the site and diff against the billing provider's price list and the fact sheet — any mismatch fails (this is DET-CON-016 run as a build gate). (Automatable) Test-mode checkout and upgrade run: totals, proration, and entitlement changes verified against the pricing page.
- **Sources:** Stripe, "A Guide to SaaS Pricing and Packaging" — https://stripe.com/resources/more/saas-pricing-and-packaging-strategy

### unit-economics

- **Method:** Compute per-customer economics with Skok's definitions: CAC = fully loaded sales + marketing cost ÷ new customers in the same period; LTV = average revenue per account × gross margin ÷ churn rate; read LTV:CAC together with CAC payback (CAC ÷ monthly gross profit per customer). Skok's heuristics: LTV:CAC above 3 and payback under 12 months indicate a viable SaaS motion — heuristics, not laws. For project estimates, apply McConnell's discipline: decompose, state assumptions, quote a range positioned on the Cone of Uncertainty (an initial-concept estimate can run 0.25x–4x actual), and keep estimate, target, and commitment as three separate numbers.
- **Key rules:**
  - CAC is fully loaded (salaries, commissions, tools, agencies, content), never media spend alone; LTV is margin-based, never revenue-based.
  - Do not compute LTV:CAC before a repeatable acquisition motion exists; early numbers are labeled directional (Skok's own correction to his earlier advice).
  - Estimates are ranges with named assumptions and a method (analogy, decomposition, parametric); a single-point estimate fails review.
  - Every completed item logs estimate vs. actual; a team with no such history states that it is guessing.
  - Cohort before aggregate: blended LTV/CAC that hides a losing segment is a reporting defect.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Formula audit: recompute CAC, LTV, ratio, and payback from raw inputs with a script/spreadsheet and diff against reported figures. (Automatable) Estimate-record check: breakdown, method, assumptions, and range all present; estimate-vs-actual log updated for the last completed items.
- **Sources:** David Skok, For Entrepreneurs, "Why early-stage startups should wait to calculate LTV:CAC…" — https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/ltv-cac/ ; Steve McConnell, "Software Development's Cone of Uncertainty," Construx — https://www.construx.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/CxWhitePaper_ConeOfUncertainty.pdf

### roadmapping

- **Method:** Build an outcome roadmap in Now/Next/Later horizons (Janna Bastow, ProdPad): Now = validated initiatives in active build; Next = problems under active discovery; Later = strategic bets not yet shaped. Items are framed as outcomes/problems linked to objectives, placed by confidence, and moved between horizons as discovery reduces uncertainty. Keep two views: an internal roadmap with the real bets and detail, and an external one that is deliberately less specific.
- **Key rules:**
  - No dates on Next/Later items; a date on a Later item is a promise the team has not validated.
  - An item enters Now only after discovery (target opportunity + assumption tests) and with acceptance criteria attached or scheduled.
  - Items enter the roadmap through the opportunity tree and a MoSCoW class, not through stakeholder volume or seniority.
  - The external roadmap never promises a capability the internal one has not validated — public roadmap entries are product promises (cf. DET-CON-028).
  - Every roadmap change records what moved, why, and what it displaced; silent re-sequencing is a defect.
- **Gates:** (Automatable) Field check: every item carries an outcome statement, a linked objective, a horizon, and (for Now items) a discovery link. (Automatable) Staleness check: Later items not reviewed within the stated interval are flagged for re-triage.
- **Sources:** ProdPad glossary, "What Is a Now-Next-Later Roadmap?" — https://www.prodpad.com/glossary/now-next-later-roadmap/

## 4. Open risks

- **Discovery without customers.** Continuous discovery assumes weekly access to real customers; a single AI agent executing these skills often has none. Unless drafters encode an explicit degradation path (user-supplied interview notes, support-ticket mining, assumptions labeled UNVERIFIED with no downstream citation rights), `discovery` and `user-research` will quietly manufacture the invented personas and findings they exist to prevent.
- **The standard is paywalled.** ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018's full text is not freely readable; drafters will work from tutorials and summaries. Risk: the characteristic list gets cargo-culted while the standard's process and set-level requirements are lost. Mitigation: encode the verifiable surface (attributes, traceability, set checks), not clause citations.
- **BDD gates assume a runner.** The acceptance-criteria gates presume Cucumber or an equivalent exists in the project stack. For static or content projects, drafters must define a checklist degradation that keeps the failure-case and one-behavior-per-scenario rules, or the gate will be dropped entirely on exactly the projects that need it.
- **Sheet-first is the first casualty of deadline pressure.** The fact-sheet rule set only works if a release gate outside D1 (D8) enforces the claim-trace audit. Drafters should state this dependency in the skill itself; a fact-sheet skill that relies on goodwill will be skipped.
- **Heuristics travel badly.** LTV:CAC > 3 comes from B2B SaaS; the ~60% Must cap comes from DSDM timeboxed delivery; the Cone's ranges come from software project data. Drafters must encode each as a default with a recorded override, not a universal pass/fail — otherwise skills will fail healthy non-SaaS projects and teach users to ignore gates.
- **Pricing gates need provider access.** The price-consistency scan assumes billing-provider data is reachable. Where a project has no billing provider (brochure sites with quoted pricing), the gate must fall back to fact-sheet-as-truth — drafters need to specify that fallback explicitly or the gate silently does nothing.
