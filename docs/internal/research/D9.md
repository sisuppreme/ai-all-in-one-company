## 1. Department standard

Professional SEO/content practice today, as D9 will teach it:

- **Google Search Central is the baseline specification.** Where Google documents a requirement — crawlability, canonicalization, title links, structured data, sitemaps — that documentation is the rule, not agency folklore. The developer SEO guide is the entry point; the spam policies are the boundary. Sources: Google Search Central, SEO guide for developers, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/get-started-developers ; Google Search spam policies, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies
- **Content is judged people-first, however it is produced.** Google's stated position: ranking systems reward original, high-quality content demonstrating E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness), and "our focus on the quality of content, rather than how content is produced" is the guide. Automation — including AI — used with the primary purpose of manipulating rankings violates the spam policies; the March 2024 spam-policy update named this **scaled content abuse**: many pages generated mainly to manipulate rankings rather than help users, however they are made. The people-first page's self-assessment runs on **Who** (is the author evident and real?), **How** (is the production process, including automation, transparent where a reader would wonder?), and **Why** (does the page exist to help people, or to attract search visits?). Sources: Google Search Central Blog, "Google Search's guidance about AI-generated content" (2023-02-08), https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content ; Google Search Central, "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content", https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- **The Real Content Rule outranks every traffic argument.** DET-CON's severity rule is absolute: any fabricated testimonial, statistic, review, credential, or person is a blocker and fails the whole content pass. D9 enforces this through the real-content skill (see §3): every factual element in copy traces to fact-sheet.md or a producible source record, or it is cut. This is also law, not just house style — the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465) bans fake testimonials and reviews, including AI-generated ones. Sources: skills/detection/content/PARAMETERS.md (DET-CON-010…013); DET-CON-013's cited source on 16 CFR Part 465, https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/a-guide-to-understanding-ftc-s-4503495/
- **Structured data is generated, never decorated.** JSON-LD (Google's recommended format) is emitted from the same data that renders the visible page, carries every required property for its rich-result type, is validated with the Rich Results Test during development, and is monitored through Search Console after deployment — because a template refactor can silently invalidate markup site-wide. Markup that describes content the page does not show is both a spam-policy violation and DET-CON-024. Source: PROJECT_ANATOMY.md, "Structured data and metadata"; Google Search Central, introduction to structured data, https://developers.google.com/structured-data/
- **Sitemaps and internal links are one system.** The sitemap is a generated artifact of the route/content inventory — only canonical, indexable, live URLs, within the protocol limits (50,000 URLs / 50 MB uncompressed per file, sitemap index beyond that), referenced in robots.txt and submitted in Search Console. Internal links do the actual discovery work: important pages are reachable from navigation, hubs, and body content, never from the sitemap alone. Sources: PROJECT_ANATOMY.md, "Internal linking and sitemaps"; Sitemaps protocol, https://www.sitemaps.org/protocol.html
- **Email deliverability is an authentication and consent regime.** Since February 2024, Gmail requires senders of 5,000+ messages/day to personal Gmail accounts to authenticate with SPF and DKIM, publish a DMARC record (minimum p=none, aligned), send over TLS, keep the spam rate under 0.3%, and offer one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) on marketing mail, honored within 48 hours; enforcement escalated to temporary and permanent rejections from November 2025. Yahoo imposes a parallel regime. Underneath sit the legal floors: CAN-SPAM (US — accurate headers, honest subject lines, a working opt-out honored within 10 business days, a postal address) and GDPR/PECR consent rules in the EU/UK. Sources: Google, Email sender guidelines FAQ, https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414 ; FTC, CAN-SPAM Act compliance guide for business, https://www.ftc.gov/business-center/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- **AEO/GEO practice is taught on an evidence ladder, because the field mixes documentation with sales copy.** D9 skills must tag every answer-engine claim with its level:
  - **Documented (platform-stated).** Google's "AI features and your website" page is the whole of the official position: to be eligible as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet — "there are no additional technical requirements," "no special optimizations necessary," "you don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup," and "there's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add." The listed fundamentals are ordinary SEO: crawling allowed (including at CDN/hosting level), content findable through internal links, important content in textual form, structured data matching visible text, Merchant Center/Business Profile data current. AI-feature appearances are reported inside Search Console's overall Performance data. Source: Google Search Central, "AI features and your website", https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features
  - **Peer-reviewed, limited.** The term GEO comes from one paper: Aggarwal et al., "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (KDD '24). On GEO-bench (10,000 queries), adding citations, quotations, or statistics raised a source's visibility in generated answers by up to ~40% on the paper's position-adjusted word count metric; keyword stuffing did not help. Honest limits: the harness ran 2023-era models (GPT-3.5) over the top five Google results, the live-engine (Perplexity) gains were smaller, and "up to 40%" is an upper bound for specific methods and domains, not an average effect. Source: Aggarwal et al., arXiv:2311.09735, https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735
  - **Correlational (vendor studies).** Vendor analyses (e.g. Ahrefs' brand study) report that branded web mentions correlate more strongly with AI Overview presence than backlinks do. Correlation across brands that also do more of everything is not a mechanism and not a ranking factor; D9 may cite such studies only labeled as observational.
  - **Hype (contradicted or unsupported).** llms.txt is the worked example. It is a *proposal* (Jeremy Howard, Answer.AI, 2024-09-03; llmstxt.org) for a markdown site map for LLMs — not an adopted standard. Google has said on the record, repeatedly, that it does not use the file (Gary Illyes, Search Central Live Deep Dive, July 2025; John Mueller compared it to the keywords meta tag, April 2025). A study of nearly 300,000 domains (SE Ranking) found no correlation between having llms.txt and LLM citation frequency, and independent server-log studies record AI crawlers essentially never requesting the file. Shipping llms.txt is harmless hygiene; reporting it as an AI-visibility win is fabrication-adjacent and D9 forbids it. The same bucket holds "AI citation guarantees", LLM keyword-density targets, and any paid "GEO score" presented as a platform metric. Sources: Kevin Leary, "Do AI Systems Read llms.txt?", https://www.kevinleary.net/blog/llms-txt-myth/ ; "llms.txt for Brands: What It Does and Does Not Do", https://www.getdigitize.com/blog/llms-txt-for-brands/
- **Measurement closes the loop.** Search Console coverage and performance are reviewed like a production dashboard, the experiment register records losers as faithfully as winners, and every content decision (brief, refresh, retire) cites the data it rests on. Sources: PROJECT_ANATOMY.md, "Analytics" and "Growth experimentation".

## 3. Per-skill research notes

### technical-seo
- **Core method:** Own the crawl-and-index contract between the site and search engines. Rendering strategy first: indexable content ships as server-rendered or statically generated HTML, with client rendering treated as a verified exception, not a default. Then the directive layer: robots.txt (RFC 9309 syntax), robots meta and X-Robots-Tag, canonical tags, status codes and redirect chains, and XML sitemaps (with sitemaps). The defining ritual is the environment-promotion check: staging protections (noindex, Disallow: /, basic auth, staging canonicals) are diffed before every production release, because the classic technical-SEO disaster is a staging block shipped live. Search Console coverage is reviewed on a cadence like a production dashboard.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Every indexable page returns HTTP 200, carries a self-referencing canonical, and is reachable by links — a page failing any leg is a defect, not a ranking mystery (anatomy "SEO/discoverability").
  2. robots.txt never blocks the CSS/JS assets rendering requires; a blocked asset makes Google render a broken page.
  3. The promotion checklist includes an indexability diff (robots.txt, noindex, X-Robots-Tag, canonical host) between staging and production; any staging protection found in production is a release blocker.
  4. Redirects are single-hop where possible; permanent moves use 301/308; chains longer than two and soft 404s are fixed at the source.
  5. JavaScript-dependent content is verified in the *rendered* HTML (URL Inspection), never assumed from source or local builds.
- **Verification gates:** Automated staging-vs-production indexability diff run on every release (automatable); crawl of the production route inventory asserting 200 + indexable + canonical for every URL (automatable); robots.txt and sitemap fetch test in CI (automatable).
- **Sources:** Google Search Central, SEO guide for developers, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/get-started-developers ; RFC 9309 (Robots Exclusion Protocol), https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html

### on-page-seo
- **Core method:** One page, one job. Each indexable page is mapped to a single primary search intent drawn from real query data (Search Console, keyword research), and its title, H1, heading outline, and body are written to satisfy that intent better than the pages currently ranking for it. Metadata is written *last*, from the finished page, so the title and description describe what the visitor actually gets (DET-CON-022). Cannibalization is managed as inventory discipline: two pages competing for one intent are merged, redirected, or deliberately differentiated.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Unique title and meta description per indexable page; duplicates across the route inventory are a lint failure (DET-CON-022).
  2. Titles state the page's topic and offer plainly — keyword stuffing in titles is a spam-policy violation, not a technique.
  3. One H1; headings form a real outline of the page's argument, so both readers and extractive answer systems can navigate it (supports aeo).
  4. No page targets an intent another page already owns without a written differentiation (page-specific facts, audience, or format) — the on-page half of DET-CON-025.
  5. Meta descriptions promise only what the page delivers; a description advertising an offer the page lacks is a DET-CON-022 hit.
- **Verification gates:** Metadata lint in CI — duplicate/missing title and description detection across the route inventory (automatable); quarterly intent-map review sampling top pages against Search Console query data (manual, checklist-driven).
- **Sources:** Google Search Central, "Influencing your title links in search results" and snippet control documentation, via https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance ; Google Search spam policies, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies

### aeo
- **Core method:** Answer Engine Optimization is the discipline of being the passage an answer engine lifts: featured snippets, voice answers, and Google's AI features. The method is extractability — state the direct answer early in a self-contained passage, use question-shaped headings where the query is a question, and put procedural or comparative facts in lists and tables that survive extraction. For Google's AI features specifically there is no separate discipline: eligibility is ordinary Search eligibility (indexed, snippet-eligible), and Google states no additional requirements or special markup exist. AEO therefore rides on technical-seo, on-page-seo, and copywriting, plus a monitoring habit.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Answer first, expand second: the passage that answers the query must be true and complete when lifted alone — no "as mentioned above" dependencies.
  2. Facts are stated once, canonically, and match fact-sheet.md — answer engines amplify contradictions, so DET-CON-015 discipline is an AEO control, not just hygiene.
  3. Never maintain a separate "AI version" of content; one page serves people and engines (Google: same people-first content is what AI features surface).
  4. Do not block AI crawlers at the CDN or firewall layer by accident while allowing them in robots.txt — Google's AI-features page names CDN/hosting blocking as a real eligibility failure.
  5. Structured data supporting an answer must match the visible text exactly (DET-CON-024; restated in Google's AI-features guidance).
- **Verification gates:** Monthly answer-presence log — a fixed set of target queries checked in AI Overviews/featured snippets, citation recorded (manual, logged; semi-automatable with SERP tooling); passage self-containment review in the editorial checklist (manual).
- **Sources:** Google Search Central, "AI features and your website", https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features ; Aggarwal et al., GEO (KDD '24) — extraction/visibility mechanics, https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735

### geo
- **Core method:** Generative Engine Optimization targets inclusion and citation inside synthesized answers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, AI Overviews) rather than a ranked position. The defensible method has three layers: (1) the documented layer — ordinary search eligibility and accurate, consistent brand facts everywhere engines might read them; (2) the evidence layer — content built to be citable: original data, named sources, quotations, and statistics with provenance, per the one peer-reviewed study; (3) the measurement layer — a fixed panel of prompts run on a cadence per engine, citations logged, variance expected. Everything beyond these layers is labeled with its evidence level (§1) before it enters a plan.
- **Key rules:**
  1. No GEO claim without an evidence tag: documented, peer-reviewed, correlational, or hype. Plans that present vendor metrics as platform facts fail review.
  2. llms.txt may be shipped as hygiene but is never reported as a visibility result — Google has stated on the record that it does not use the file, and ~300,000-domain correlational evidence agrees.
  3. Statistics and quotations added for citability must themselves pass real-content: an invented statistic is a DET-CON-011 blocker even if it earns citations.
  4. Brand facts (name, offer, prices, locations) are kept identical across the site and major third-party profiles; engines synthesize across sources, so off-site contradiction is DET-CON-015 at one remove.
  5. Citation measurement uses a fixed prompt set and repeated runs; a single screenshot of one answer is an anecdote, never a report.
- **Verification gates:** Prompt-panel citation tracker with run dates and engine versions logged (semi-automatable); GEO plan audit — every tactic in the plan carries an evidence-level tag (manual checklist).
- **Sources:** Aggarwal et al., "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", KDD '24, https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735 ; Google Search Central, "AI features and your website", https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features ; llms.txt evidence summary, https://www.kevinleary.net/blog/llms-txt-myth/

### content-strategy
- **Core method:** A brief-first production system. Strategy sets audience, goals, and topic ownership; every piece of content starts from a brief naming its audience, goal, target query or intent, owner, and reviewer, then moves through drafting, accuracy/voice review, publication with distribution, and a scheduled refresh-or-retire review. The topic map is the anti-duplication instrument: one canonical page per topic, with variants admitted only when they carry substantively different, page-specific verified material. Volume is an output of how many briefs clear the bar — never a quota set in advance.
- **Key rules:**
  1. No brief, no draft. A brief without a named audience, goal, and intent does not enter production (anatomy verify step: every published item traces to a brief).
  2. "Pages per week" targets are forbidden as a planning unit; Google's scaled content abuse policy makes volume-for-rankings the violation, regardless of whether humans or AI wrote the pages (DET-CON-025).
  3. A location/keyword variant page ships only with real local data (staff, stock, pricing, regulations) that survives the swap test — swapping the keyword back must not make pages interchangeable (DET-CON-008, DET-CON-025).
  4. Every published piece carries an owner and a review-by date; dated claims are refreshed or retired on schedule (DET-CON-018).
  5. Authorship is real: pieces are credited to evidenced people or an accountable house byline, never an invented persona (DET-CON-020, DET-CON-021; Google's Who guidance).
- **Verification gates:** Calendar-to-brief traceability audit — published URLs diffed against the brief register (automatable); corpus similarity clustering to catch near-duplicate page sets (automatable, runs the DET-CON-025 check); stale-date scan over published copy (automatable, DET-CON-018 patterns).
- **Sources:** Mailchimp, content planning guide, https://mailchimp.com/resources/content-planning/ ; Google Search spam policies (scaled content abuse), https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies

### copywriting
- **Core method:** Write from the product and the fact sheet, not from the category. Every claim names a mechanism, a user, and a verifiable outcome; where a claim cannot be made specific, it is cut rather than padded. The skill includes a named editing pass against model-default register: generic benefit filler, AI vocabulary clusters, and hedge stacks are searched for and rewritten before anything ships. A house voice guide and naming list make the standard repeatable across writers and sessions.
- **Key rules:**
  1. The competitor-verbatim test applies to every benefit sentence: if a competitor could print it unchanged, it is rewritten with product-specific fact or deleted (DET-CON-006, applied at page level per the parameter's check).
  2. Vocabulary clusters (delve, tapestry, realm, "in today's fast-paced world") and hedge stacks (three-plus qualifiers on one claim) are editing defects with a search step, not matters of taste (DET-CON-007, DET-CON-009).
  3. Measurable superlatives ("#1", "best-rated", "largest") require substantiation on file or are rewritten as plain description; unmeasurable puffery is a style note, not a finding (DET-CON-026).
  4. Product, company, feature, and people names follow the naming list exactly, everywhere (DET-CON-017).
  5. Alt text and captions are written from the rendered image, never from the filename or the prompt that generated it (DET-CON-027).
- **Verification gates:** Pre-publish lint over copy for cluster terms, hedge patterns, and developer markers (automatable assist; human editor holds the verdict); substantiation spot-check — every measurable claim in a piece traced to its source during review (manual).
- **Sources:** DET-CON-006/007/009/017/026/027, skills/detection/content/PARAMETERS.md ; Google Search Central, "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content", https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

### real-content
- **Core method:** This skill is the Real Content Rule run as a publishing gate — D9's enforcement arm. Before publication, every factual element of a page is classified (testimonial, review, statistic, credential, person, price, promise, authorship) and matched to its evidence: a source record the business can produce, a fact-sheet.md entry, or the live data the page renders from. An element with no evidence is cut, not softened, and one fabricated element fails the entire pass. AI drafting is permitted at every step; a named human verifies the facts before publish, including metadata.
- **Key rules:**
  1. The blocker list is absolute: fabricated testimonials, reviews, statistics, credentials/press, and people (DET-CON-010, 011, 012, 013, 019) fail the whole content pass — there is no "mostly real" grade.
  2. Self-attested first-party counters ("84 million accounts") are not exempt: each needs a fact-sheet entry and must agree everywhere it appears (DET-CON-011 check (b), DET-CON-015).
  3. Prices render from one data source and are verified against checkout; a marketing page may never carry a price the checkout contradicts (DET-CON-016).
  4. Every operational promise (support hours, guarantees, cancellation terms) links to the policy or documented process that makes it true; no backing artifact, no promise (DET-CON-028).
  5. Bylines name real people with an author page and one independent trace, or an honestly collective house byline — never an invented individual (DET-CON-020).
- **Verification gates:** Pre-publish DET-CON pass over rendered pages and JSON-LD — the parameter scans (001–028) run as the checklist, most automatable, with source-record checks manual; fact-diff gate — checkable facts extracted from copy and diffed against fact-sheet.md (automatable assist, DET-CON-014).
- **Sources:** DET-CON-010…016, 019, 020, 028, skills/detection/content/PARAMETERS.md ; FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, 16 CFR Part 465 (via DET-CON-013's source), https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/a-guide-to-understanding-ftc-s-4503495/

### lifecycle-email
- **Core method:** Behavior-triggered message programs organized by lifecycle stage — acquisition, onboarding/activation, engagement, retention, win-back (the Braze model in the anatomy) — across email, push, and in-app. Each stage has entry and exit events stored on the user profile, a conversion goal, an owner, and a primary metric; each journey is a timed, branching sequence sent only to users whose opt-in status permits it, under frequency caps that stop stages colliding. Deliverability engineering (authentication, list hygiene, sunset rules for the unengaged) runs underneath the creative work, and every journey carries a control group so its effect is measured, not assumed.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Authentication before volume: SPF, DKIM, and an aligned DMARC record exist on every sending domain before any program sends; at bulk volume the Gmail/Yahoo regime applies in full — one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058), unsubscribe honored within 48 hours, spam rate under 0.3%.
  2. Consent is a send-time check, not a signup-time memory: each send verifies the profile's current opt-in status for that message type; transactional and marketing streams are never mixed to borrow consent (transactional sending itself is D11's skill — the boundary is crossed deliberately, not by drift).
  3. Merge fields ship with tested fallbacks, and a seed-account send on every channel is mandatory after any journey edit — an unrendered `{{first_name}}` in production is a release defect (DET-CON-003; anatomy slop).
  4. Suppression logic is part of the journey definition: win-back excludes recent purchasers and active users by event, not by hope (anatomy slop: "We miss you!" to yesterday's buyer).
  5. Subject lines and headers are honest — no deceptive subject lines, no fake "Re:" — per CAN-SPAM and because trust is the asset the program compounds.
- **Verification gates:** DNS authentication check (SPF/DKIM/DMARC lookup and alignment) for all sending domains in CI (automatable); seed-send rendering test asserting no unrendered template tokens in any message (automatable); journey audit — trigger, opt-in targeting, conversion goal, and control group present per journey (manual checklist, anatomy verify step).
- **Sources:** Google, Email sender guidelines FAQ, https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414 ; Braze documentation, creating a campaign, http://braze.com/docs/user_guide/messaging/campaigns/creating_campaign ; FTC, CAN-SPAM Act compliance guide, https://www.ftc.gov/business-center/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business

### structured-data
- **Core method:** One JSON-LD template per page type, generated from the same data store that renders the visible page, carrying every required property for the rich result the page type is eligible for. Validation happens during development (Rich Results Test / schema validator), and Search Console's enhancement reports are monitored after deployment because templating changes break markup silently. The template set is deliberately small: a type is added only when the page genuinely contains the content the type describes.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Markup claims nothing the page does not show — ratings, prices, dates, and authors in JSON-LD must have visible counterparts with identical values (DET-CON-024).
  2. No generator placeholders ever reach production: "Product Name", price 0.00, and sample organizations are build failures (anatomy slop).
  3. Review and aggregate-rating markup exists only where real, visible reviews exist — which routes the question to real-content before any markup is written.
  4. JSON-LD is the format; blocks are syntactically validated in the build, because one trailing comma invalidates the whole block (anatomy slop).
  5. Any template or data-model change re-runs validation on every affected page type before release.
- **Verification gates:** Schema validation in CI on sample URLs per page type (automatable with a JSON-LD validator); JSON-LD-vs-rendered-content field diff (automatable, runs the DET-CON-024 check); Search Console enhancement-report review on the web-analytics cadence.
- **Sources:** Google Search Central, introduction to structured data, https://developers.google.com/structured-data/ ; Schema.org vocabulary and validator, https://schema.org

### sitemaps
- **Core method:** The sitemap is a build artifact, generated from the actual route and content inventory at publish time — never a hand-maintained file. It lists only canonical, indexable URLs that return 200, respects the protocol limits (50,000 URLs / 50 MB uncompressed per file, sitemap index beyond that), is referenced from robots.txt, and is submitted in Search Console. Its count must reconcile with the inventory count, with every difference explained; that reconciliation is the audit that keeps it honest.
- **Key rules:**
  1. An entry is a canonical claim: no redirects, no 404s, no noindex or non-canonical URLs — ever (anatomy verify step).
  2. `lastmod` is truthful — the date the content last changed, not the last deploy; engines discount sitemaps that cry wolf.
  3. Regeneration is event-driven (publish, unpublish, route change), not scheduled by hand; a sitemap older than the newest content is stale by definition.
  4. Large sites shard by content type or section under a sitemap index, so a broken section is visible in Search Console instead of diluted.
  5. Staging sitemaps never reach production, and production sitemaps never list staging hosts (pairs with technical-seo's promotion diff).
- **Verification gates:** Sitemap audit script — fetch the sitemap, request every URL (or a statistically sound sample on very large sites), assert 200 + canonical + indexable (automatable); CI assertion that a content change regenerates the sitemap (automatable).
- **Sources:** Google Search Central, "Build and submit a sitemap", https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap ; Sitemaps protocol, https://www.sitemaps.org/protocol.html

### internal-linking
- **Core method:** Link architecture designed for users first and measured as a graph. Navigation, hub pages, breadcrumbs, and in-content links carry the structure; the sitemap is a fallback, not a linking strategy. The working artifacts are a crawl-derived link graph (depth, in-degree, orphans) reviewed per release, and a rule in the content brief: every new page names its inbound links before it is written. Anchor text is treated as a micro-description of the destination.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Every indexable page is reachable within a few clicks of the home page and linked from at least one non-sitemap page; an orphan page is a defect with an owner (anatomy slop: pricing reachable only from the sitemap).
  2. Anchor text describes the destination's topic; "click here" as a pattern is a defect, and identical anchors pointing to different pages are flagged.
  3. Internal links point at canonical URLs directly — linking through redirects wastes crawl and leaks the chain problem into every page.
  4. Commercially critical pages (pricing, signup, flagship content) receive deliberate in-content links from relevant high-traffic pages, not just nav placement.
  5. Link changes ship with content changes: when a page is retired, its inbound links and its redirect are handled in the same change (with content-strategy's refresh/retire flow).
- **Verification gates:** Crawl-based orphan and depth report per release (automatable); anchor-text audit on a sample of in-content links (semi-automatable); new-page checklist asserting planned inbound links exist at publish (manual).
- **Sources:** PROJECT_ANATOMY.md, "Internal linking and sitemaps" ; Google Search Central, "AI features and your website" (internal links named an AI-visibility fundamental), https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features

### social-open-graph
- **Core method:** Every public page emits Open Graph and Twitter Card tags generated from that page's own content: the four required OG properties (og:title, og:type, og:image, og:url) plus og:description and og:site_name, and a purpose-built share image (commonly 1200×630). Tags are part of the page template, not a plugin afterthought, and launch verification runs production URLs through platform debuggers because social platforms cache previews aggressively — a wrong card persists until forcibly re-scraped.
- **Key rules:**
  1. og:url is the page's canonical URL; a site-wide homepage og:url is a defect (anatomy slop; DET-CON-023).
  2. og:title and og:description are per-page and match the page's actual title and topic; only the *image* may fall back to a site-wide default, and that fallback is a known compromise, not the design (DET-CON-023).
  3. og:image is an absolute URL to a page-appropriate image at full share size — never the homepage logo stretched across every page, never a relative path crawlers cannot resolve.
  4. Type-specific properties are emitted where they apply (article:published_time and author on posts), and og:site_name is the site's own name — scaffold copies from other sites are a named slop pattern.
  5. After any template or branding change, key templates are re-run through the debuggers to force fresh scrapes.
- **Verification gates:** Site-wide tag dump diffed against page titles and current branding (automatable, runs the DET-CON-023 check); Meta Sharing Debugger / platform validator pass on home, article, and product templates at launch and after template changes (manual, anatomy verify step).
- **Sources:** The Open Graph protocol, https://ogp.me ; DET-CON-023, skills/detection/content/PARAMETERS.md

### web-analytics
- **Core method:** D9's measurement slice is search and acquisition: Search Console (performance by query and page, coverage/indexing, rich-result reports) and GA4 organic landing-page analysis, run on a fixed review cadence. (Product analytics and event taxonomy are D12's ownership of the shared anatomy element; D9 consumes, and does not redefine, the tracking plan.) The skill's product is decisions: each review produces briefs, refreshes, retirements, or technical fixes with the data cited, so content-strategy runs on observed demand instead of opinion.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Search Console is verified for every production property (including domain variants) and coverage is reviewed on cadence; an indexing regression is treated as an incident with an owner, not a statistic.
  2. No content decision without a citation: every brief, refresh, and retirement names the query/page data or landing-page evidence behind it.
  3. Consent state is respected end to end — analytics does not fire before consent where consent mode applies, and the configuration is verified, not assumed from the snippet being present (anatomy slop: pasted, never configured).
  4. AI-feature traffic is read where Google reports it — inside Search Console's overall Performance data — and never reported as a separate invented metric.
  5. Event and tag changes go through the shared tracking plan (with D12): consistent names, debug validation before release, no duplicate or misspelled variants fragmenting reports.
- **Verification gates:** Tracking-plan diff — live tags/events compared against the written plan, with a debug-view session completing the primary conversion flow (automatable assist + manual session, anatomy verify step); monthly Search Console coverage + performance review checklist with recorded actions (manual).
- **Sources:** Google Analytics developer documentation, https://developers.google.com/analytics ; Google Search Central, "AI features and your website" (measurement section), https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features

### growth-experiments
- **Core method:** A disciplined A/B testing program run from a written register. Each experiment states a hypothesis tied to a user problem, one primary metric, and a pre-computed sample size and runtime (at least one full business cycle) before launch. Traffic splits randomly; results are read on the primary metric with confidence intervals and health checks — sample ratio mismatch above all — and the decision (ship, kill, inconclusive-extend) is recorded in the register whatever the outcome, so learnings compound into the next hypothesis backlog. Losing results are documented as usable learning, per the Optimizely practice the anatomy cites.
- **Key rules:**
  1. No peeking decisions: the read happens at the pre-set horizon, or a sequential-testing method is chosen and named in advance — a test stopped because a variant was "winning" at day two is void (anatomy slop).
  2. The primary metric decides. Secondary metrics may generate the next hypothesis; they never overturn the primary result.
  3. One experiment per surface at a time unless an interaction-aware design exists; overlapping tests on one page invalidate each other.
  4. Variants are copy and claims, so they pass the same gates as any page: a variant may not test a fabricated claim, an unsubstantiated superlative, or a promise with no backing process (DET-CON-026, DET-CON-028 via real-content).
  5. Every concluded experiment leaves a register entry — hypothesis, primary metric, SRM/health result, decision — including inconclusive ones; an experiment with no record is treated as never having run.
- **Verification gates:** Experiment register audit — the last concluded experiment shows hypothesis, primary metric, SRM pass, and a recorded decision matching the data (manual, anatomy verify step); SRM chi-square check computed automatically in the results pipeline (automatable).
- **Sources:** Optimizely, "Take action based on the results of an experiment", https://docs.optimizely.com/experimentation-strategy/docs/take-action-based-on-the-results-of-an-experiment ; Kohavi, Tang & Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* (Cambridge University Press, 2020)

## 4. Open risks for Phase 3

- **The real-content rule vs SEO volume tension — the department's central design problem.** Search rewards topical coverage, and clients will ask for the arithmetic of content farms: more pages, more queries, more traffic. The Real Content Rule caps output at what can be briefed, evidenced, and edited — which is slower and smaller. Phase 3 must encode the resolution, not leave it to drafter judgment: content-strategy optimizes depth-per-topic over page count; programmatic pages are admitted only with page-specific verified data (the DET-CON-025 bar); real-content's blocker list is non-negotiable even when a competitor's fabricated social proof is visibly ranking. The skills also need client-facing language for this refusal, because the pressure arrives as a business request, not a technical one.
- **AEO/GEO measurement is immature and drafters will be tempted to hard-code vendor claims.** AI answers vary run to run and model to model; prompt panels are samples, not censuses; no platform publishes citation factors. The evidence-ladder discipline (§1) has to survive drafting: if a Phase 3 skill states a GEO tactic without its evidence tag, Phase 4 review should treat it as a defect. Expect this section of the skills to need the most frequent re-verification as platform documentation changes.
- **Platform drift is fast in exactly D9's territory.** Spam policies (scaled content abuse, 2024), AI features documentation (2025), and bulk-sender enforcement (2024–2025) all changed within two years; llms.txt went from launch to on-record rejection in one. D9 skills must carry dated sources and feed the repo's re-verification duty (§11) — an undated SEO claim in a skill should be presumed stale.
- **Boundary overlaps to resolve by cross-reference, not duplication.** web-analytics vs D12's product-analytics/event-tracking (one tracking plan, two consumers — the split must be written down); lifecycle-email vs D11's transactional email and webhooks (consent and stream separation) and vs D18's status-communications; copywriting vs D13's inclusive-copy and D10's documentation-writing (voice guide ownership); structured-data vs D3's frontend implementation of the templates. Each D9 skill should name its boundary explicitly in its Phase 3 draft.
- **Legal exposure lives inside ordinary copy.** Superlatives, guarantees, review displays, and testimonial use are advertising-law questions (FTC endorsement and testimonials rules; equivalent regimes elsewhere) as much as SEO questions. D9 flags and blocks; D14 owns the legal call. Phase 3 drafts must not let real-content read as legal advice, and must route jurisdiction-specific questions to D14's skills.
- **Several gates depend on tooling a single agent must build or borrow.** Corpus similarity clustering (DET-CON-025), fact extraction and diffing (DET-CON-014), sitemap auditing, and prompt-panel citation tracking are real engineering, overlapping D16's crawl tooling and D8's test infrastructure. If the tooling does not exist when a skill is drafted, the skill must say the gate is manual-with-checklist rather than pretend automation — a gate that claims to be automatic and is not is itself a real-content violation.
