# D18 — Customer Experience & Support: Phase 2 Research

Input to Phase 3 skill drafting for the 9 D18 skills. Method and failure-mode citations draw on PROJECT_ANATOMY.md Part 9; external claims are sourced inline and were verified against the cited pages on 2026-11-21. No response-time, deflection, or conversion benchmarks are quoted anywhere in this file: published vendor benchmarks describe other companies' support operations, not the reader's, so every target in these skills is set from the operator's own measured baseline.

## 1. Department standard

Professional CX/support practice today treats every customer-facing promise as a system property: if the product says it, a queue, an owner, and a measurement must exist behind it.

- **One queue, one record.** Every request — email, form, chat, API — becomes a ticket with requester, assignee, priority, type, and tags, moving through defined statuses (Zendesk's model: New → Open → Pending → Solved → Closed). Customers see their own request view; agents work the same record. A channel that does not create a ticket does not exist as a support channel.
- **Expectations are set from configuration, not copy.** Office hours and expected reply times are set in the support tool (Intercom defaults to 24/7 availability until real hours are configured) and the tool computes and displays them. The website never promises a response time the tool is not configured to measure against. Published SLAs are targets the operator actually staffs.
- **Self-service is an information-architecture discipline.** Help centers use a shallow category → section → article hierarchy, named in customer language, with articles written from real ticket patterns, one problem per article, and a review/archival cycle so content tracks the current UI (Zendesk's self-service planning guidance: keep the structure as simple as possible, organize by who the customers are).
- **Onboarding is aimed at a measured activation event.** The team defines activation as a specific instrumented event, validates with cohort analysis that early completion predicts retention, and builds checklists that complete only when the real event fires (Appcues' documented pattern: define the goal first, six or fewer checklist items, completion on real events). Tours that fire on every login or teach features outside the user's plan are defects.
- **Trials and cancellation are honesty surfaces.** Trial end dates, reminder timing, and no-payment-method behavior are configured explicitly in the billing platform (Stripe's `trial_will_end` webhook exists precisely so users are warned before a first charge). Cancellation is as easy as signup: one optional reason question, at most one matched save offer, then a clean confirmation. This is both the ethical floor and the legal one — ROSCA requires clear disclosure, express informed consent, and a simple cancellation method for online negative-option offers, and the FTC's dark-patterns staff report names obstruction, confirmshaming, and hidden renewal terms as enforcement targets. Note for drafters: the FTC's broader Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated on procedural grounds by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025 and rulemaking has restarted; skills must cite ROSCA, Section 5, and state auto-renewal laws as the operative requirements, not the vacated rule.
- **Status communication is a cadence commitment.** Components mirror what customers use, the first post goes out early with known impact before the cause is known, updates follow a stated cadence through consistent states (Investigating → Identified → Monitoring → Resolved), every update names when the next one will arrive, and major incidents end with a postmortem (Atlassian Statuspage's documented practice). A status page nobody updates during an outage is worse than none.
- **Feedback is a loop, not a form.** Surveys fire at lifecycle moments with frequency caps, scores and comments attach to the user/account record, detractors get follow-up, and shipped changes are announced back to the people who asked. A survey whose responses nobody reviews is removed, not left running.

## 2. Failure modes this department prevents

Drawn from the slop lines of PROJECT_ANATOMY.md Part 9 (element names cited):

- **Support ticketing:** "A Contact page whose form posts to an endpoint that stores nothing and sends no email, while the page claims 'we reply within 24 hours.'" The promise exists; the system behind it does not.
- **In-app chat:** "A chat bubble copied from a tutorial that loops a generic bot script, never reaches a human, and collects messages nobody monitors."
- **Help center:** "A 'Help' page with three generic FAQs written at launch, screenshots from an old UI version, and no working search — decoration, not self-service."
- **User onboarding:** "A fourteen-step modal tour that fires on every login, cannot be dismissed permanently, and teaches features the user's plan does not include."
- **Activation:** "Declaring 'activation = signed up,' or copying another product's famous milestone, with no validation against your own retention data."
- **Trial conversion:** "A '14-day free trial' banner on the marketing site while the product has no trial state at all — full price charged on day one, or trial access that never expires."
- **Feedback loops (in-product):** "An NPS popup that appears on every page load, stores responses in a spreadsheet nobody opens, and shows a 'Thanks!' message wired to nothing."
- **Churn prevention:** "A cancel button that errors out or demands an email to support, paired with a generic 'special offer' popup shown to every user regardless of their reason."
- **Status communications:** "A status page showing 'All systems operational' during a real outage because nobody owns updating it — worse than having no page at all."

## 3. Per-skill research notes

### support-ticketing

- **Core method:** Configure one ticketing system as the single intake for every channel (email, web form, chat escalation, API). Each ticket carries requester, assignee, priority, type, and tags; statuses follow a defined lifecycle (New → Open → Pending → Solved → Closed); a creation trigger immediately acknowledges receipt with a trackable ticket reference. Views and priority rules drive triage; SLA targets are set per priority from hours the operator actually works. No support address or form ships unless it creates a ticket in this system.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Every intake channel creates a ticket — a form or inbox that stores nothing and notifies nobody is removed or wired in, never left live.
  2. The acknowledgment is automatic and immediate, and contains the ticket reference the customer can quote.
  3. Published response-time promises must equal the SLA configured in the tool; if no SLA is staffed, the copy states actual support hours instead of a number.
  4. Public replies and internal notes are separate fields; nothing internal is ever customer-visible by default.
  5. Solved is not Closed: the customer gets a resolution notice and a window to reopen before the ticket closes.
- **Verification gates:** End-to-end intake test per channel — submit from an external address, assert a ticket ID is created, the acknowledgment email arrives, the ticket appears in the agent queue, and a reply reaches the requester (automatable as a scripted smoke test run on every support-config change). Config audit: published support copy diffed against configured SLA/office-hours values.
- **Sources:** Zendesk, Tickets API — https://developer.zendesk.com/api-reference/ticketing/tickets/tickets ; PROJECT_ANATOMY.md Part 9, "Support ticketing".

### in-app-chat

- **Core method:** Install the messenger on every page where users get stuck, with identity verified by a server-signed token (Intercom's model: server-generated JWT) so conversations attach to the real account and cannot be impersonated. Configure real office hours and expected reply times in the tool before launch; outside hours the messenger says when the team returns and offers the help center and a ticket path. Automation handles acknowledgment and article suggestions only — every conversation can reach a human or convert to a ticket, and an owner reviews the inbox on a stated schedule.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Identity is server-verified; a client-supplied user ID alone is never trusted (forged-ID test must fail).
  2. Office hours and reply-time expectations are configured from the operator's real availability before the widget goes live — the tool's 24/7 default is never shipped by accident.
  3. No bot script may loop without an exit: every automated flow ends in a human handoff, a ticket, or a help-center article.
  4. Copy says "message us," not "live chat," unless a human is actually staffed live in those hours.
  5. An unmonitored inbox is a launch blocker: a named owner and a review cadence exist, or the widget does not ship.
- **Verification gates:** Two-state conversation test — start conversations logged in and logged out, assert both land in the team inbox with correct identity, and assert a forged user ID is rejected (automatable with the messenger's test mode plus an API check on created conversations). Config check: office-hours and reply-expectation settings present and non-default.
- **Sources:** Intercom, Messenger installation — https://developers.intercom.com/installing-intercom/web/installation ; Intercom, "Set your default office hours" — https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/732390-set-your-default-office-hours ; Intercom, "Share your expected response time" — https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/732436-share-your-expected-response-time .

### help-center

- **Core method:** Build the help center in setup mode and activate only when the core content is real. Structure is category → section → article, shallow and named in the words customers use in tickets, not internal team names. The first articles are written from the top recurring ticket patterns; each article solves one problem, shows the current UI, and ends with a path to contact support. The center is surfaced in the product, in the chat widget, and in agent replies. A standing review cycle re-verifies or archives articles as the UI changes.
- **Key rules:**
  1. No placeholder or launch-day filler articles: an article exists because a real question recurs, and its steps match the current product.
  2. Search must work and is tested with the customers' own phrasing from real tickets, not the article titles.
  3. Screenshots and steps carry a review date; a UI release that changes a covered flow triggers re-verification or archival of the affected articles.
  4. The help center never states support promises (response times, availability) that the support operation does not keep — those live in one configured place and are linked, not restated.
  5. Every article is reachable unauthenticated unless it genuinely requires an account, and every link resolves.
- **Verification gates:** Top-questions test — take the five most common real support questions, answer each as an unauthenticated visitor using only help-center search, and assert every article link resolves and matches the current UI (automatable as a link-check crawl plus a scripted search-term list; run on each release). Zero-results search report reviewed on the review cycle — repeated failed searches become new articles.
- **Sources:** Zendesk, "Getting started with your help center" — https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408846795674-Getting-started-with-your-help-center ; Zendesk, "Planning your self-service content structure" — https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408883389338 .

### user-onboarding

- **Core method:** Start from the activation event (owned with activation-metrics): onboarding exists to get a new user to that event. A welcome step captures intent, then a short checklist (six or fewer items, per Appcues' documented guidance) sequences the essential tasks; each item completes only when its real product event fires, progress persists across sessions, and the checklist can be dismissed permanently. Tours and tooltips teach inside the doing, one step at a time, and only for features the user's plan includes. Follow-up messages branch on actual checklist state, not elapsed time alone.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Every checklist item is bound to a real event; an item that completes on a page view or a click of "Next" is a defect.
  2. Onboarding never restarts: a returning user resumes where they stopped, and a dismissed tour stays dismissed.
  3. Steps teach only what the user's plan and role can actually do — no locked-feature tours.
  4. The checklist is short enough to finish in the first session's realistic effort; anything non-essential moves to contextual help later.
  5. Different activation paths per persona are used only where the activation steps genuinely differ, not as cosmetic variants.
- **Verification gates:** Fresh-account walkthrough — create a test account, onboard using only in-product guidance, and assert each checklist item ticks exactly when its real event fires and that a second login does not restart the tour (automatable as an end-to-end test with event assertions in analytics). Event-binding audit: every checklist item maps to a named instrumented event in the tracking plan.
- **Sources:** Appcues, "Build effective Checklists" — https://appcues.helpjuice.com/checklists/checklist-best-practices ; PROJECT_ANATOMY.md Part 9, "User onboarding".

### activation-metrics

- **Core method:** Define activation as one specific, instrumented event (or a small set with one shared definition) that represents first receipt of core value. Validate the definition with cohort analysis: users who complete the event within the chosen window must retain measurably better than those who do not, on the product's own data. Publish the definition, instrument the event, and track the activation rate as a funnel metric from signup. Onboarding, checklists, and nudges are then aimed at the validated event, and the definition is re-validated as the product changes.
- **Key rules:**
  1. "Signed up" is never the activation event; activation is the first value-receiving action.
  2. Another product's famous milestone is a hypothesis to test, never a definition to copy.
  3. One shared definition across product, marketing, and data — the written definition and the instrumented event must match exactly.
  4. The validation is a retention comparison of activated vs. non-activated cohorts on this product's data, repeated when the definition or product changes.
  5. Report the activation rate with its window (e.g., within first session / first week) stated; a rate without a window is not a metric.
- **Verification gates:** Analytics recomputation — independently compute last month's signup-cohort activation rate and the 30-day retention split for activated vs. non-activated users from raw events, and diff against the dashboard figures (automatable as a scheduled query test). Definition-consistency check: the event name in the written definition exists in the tracking plan and fires in a test session.
- **Sources:** Amplitude, "Driving product engagement by obsessing over activation" — https://amplitude.com/blog/getting-started-activation ; PROJECT_ANATOMY.md Part 9, "Activation".

### trial-conversion

- **Core method:** Implement the trial in the billing platform, not in marketing copy: the subscription is created with an explicit trial end, the platform's trial-will-end webhook (Stripe fires `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` several days before expiry) triggers reminder emails and in-app prompts, and the no-payment-method outcome — invoice, pause, or cancel — is configured deliberately rather than inherited. The team chooses card-upfront vs. no-card trials as a stated decision with the trade-off recorded. Trial terms, including the exact first charge date and amount, are shown before signup and repeated in the reminder.
- **Key rules:**
  1. The marketed trial length and the configured trial state must match exactly; a trial banner with no trial state behind it is a release blocker.
  2. Every trial user gets a warning before the first charge — webhook-driven reminder plus in-app notice — naming the date and amount.
  3. Cancelling during a trial is as easy as starting it and takes effect without a retention maze (see churn-prevention; ROSCA's simple-cancellation requirement applies).
  4. The no-card-at-expiry behavior is chosen and documented; silently charging a card added later for other purposes is forbidden.
  5. Trial access that never expires and trials that charge on day one are both defects; test mode must prove the configured behavior for card and no-card cases.
- **Verification gates:** Test-clock trial run — in the billing platform's test mode, run a shortened trial and assert the trial-will-end webhook fires, the reminder email sends, and at expiry the subscription transitions exactly as configured for both card and no-card cases (automatable with Stripe test clocks / equivalent). Copy-vs-config check: marketed trial terms diffed against the billing configuration.
- **Sources:** Stripe, "Use free trial periods on subscriptions" — https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/trials/free-trials ; Gibson Dunn, "FTC Restarts Negative Option Rulemaking After Eighth Circuit Vacatur" (ROSCA duties remain the operative standard) — https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/ .

### feedback-loops

- **Core method:** Instrument a small set of feedback channels — post-resolution CSAT, lifecycle NPS, and a feature-request path — each triggered at a defined moment (after a support resolution, after onboarding completes, at set account-age intervals) and targeted by segment. Responses attach to the user or account record, never to a detached spreadsheet. A named owner reviews responses on a fixed cadence, detractors receive human follow-up, recurring themes route to the product backlog with the originating responses linked, and shipped changes are announced back to the users who asked.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Frequency caps are enforced per user across all surveys; no survey fires on every page load or every session.
  2. Every survey has a named owner and a review cadence before it launches; an unowned survey is removed, not left running.
  3. Low scores trigger a follow-up path to a human; collecting detractor pain with no response path is worse than not asking.
  4. Scores are reported with response counts and segment, never as a bare average, and never used as marketing claims unless they are real, current, and sourced from this system.
  5. The loop closes visibly: users who requested a shipped change are told it shipped.
- **Verification gates:** Test-submission trace — trigger each survey as a test user, submit a low score with a comment, and assert the response lands on the user record, reaches the owning team's queue or report, and that frequency caps block an immediate re-prompt (automatable via the survey tool's test mode plus record inspection). Ownership audit: every live survey lists an owner and a last-reviewed date.
- **Sources:** Intercom, "How your NPS survey responses make Intercom better" — https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/5256662-how-your-nps-survey-responses-make-intercom-better ; PROJECT_ANATOMY.md Part 9, "Feedback loops (in-product)".

### churn-prevention

- **Core method:** Split churn into voluntary and involuntary, and work both honestly. Voluntary: clicking cancel opens at most one short exit survey whose stated reason routes to at most one matched save offer — pause for low usage, downgrade or discount for price, support escalation for a bug — followed by a clear final confirmation that always completes the cancellation when the offer is declined. Involuntary: smart payment retries, automatic card-account updates, and dunning emails with a payment-update link run in the billing platform. Reasons, offers, and save outcomes are logged and reviewed; the save rate never justifies adding friction.
- **Key rules:**
  1. Cancellation takes no more steps than signup and is completable in-product, in the same medium — no "email support to cancel," no erroring cancel buttons (ROSCA simple cancellation; FTC dark-patterns report names obstruction and maze-like cancellation as enforcement targets).
  2. One reason question, one matched offer, one confirmation — no stacked save screens, no confirmshaming copy ("No, I don't want to save money") on the decline path.
  3. Offers are matched to the stated reason and actually apply in billing when accepted; a generic discount popup shown to everyone is a dark pattern, not a save flow.
  4. Declining the offer completes the cancellation immediately and the confirmation states the access end date and any final charge — no surprise renewals afterward.
  5. Dunning is part of churn prevention: failed payments get retries, card updates, and clear emails before any cancellation for non-payment.
- **Verification gates:** Cancel-flow walkthrough per reason — as a test subscriber, cancel for each exit reason, assert the matched offer applies correctly in billing when accepted, that cancellation completes cleanly when declined, and that the reason is recorded (automatable as an end-to-end billing test). Friction audit: count steps/clicks to cancel vs. to sign up; cancel must not exceed signup.
- **Sources:** Recurly Engage, "Cancel Save" — https://docs.recurly.com/recurly-engage/docs/cancel-save ; Stripe, "Automate payment retries" — https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/smart-retries ; FTC, "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (staff report, September 2022) — https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf .

### status-communications

- **Core method:** Run a public status page whose components mirror what customers actually use (sign-in, dashboard, API), not internal services. Incident practice follows Atlassian Statuspage's documented discipline: communicate early — the first post states known impact before the cause is known — update on a stated cadence (Statuspage's guidance is roughly every 30 minutes during an active incident) through the consistent states Investigating → Identified → Monitoring → Resolved, notify subscribers by email/SMS, embed status in the app and help center, and publish a postmortem after major incidents. Templates for the common incident shapes are written before they are needed, and one named role owns posting during every incident.
- **Key rules:**
  1. The first post goes out when customer impact is confirmed, not when the cause is found; "investigating" with honest impact beats silence.
  2. Every update states when the next update will arrive, and that time is kept — or an update is posted explaining the delay.
  3. The page never shows "All systems operational" during a known outage: a named owner updates it during the incident, and the incident drill proves the handoff works.
  4. Components are customer-facing capabilities; internal hostnames and vendor names stay off the public page.
  5. Major incidents end with a public postmortem summary linked from the incident; resolution posts state what was affected and for how long, in plain language.
- **Verification gates:** Incident drill — create a test incident and assert subscriber notifications send, the in-app embed reflects it, and timestamps show the first post and update cadence meeting the documented standard (automatable via the status tool's API: incident creation, notification dispatch, and embed state checks). Template inventory check: pre-written templates exist for the top incident shapes before launch.
- **Sources:** Atlassian Statuspage, "Incident communication tips" — https://support.atlassian.com/statuspage/docs/incident-communication-tips/ ; Atlassian, "Incident Communication Best Practices & Channels" — https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/incident-communication .

## 4. Open risks for Phase 3

- **Single-operator support honesty is the department's central tension.** These skills are used by solo builders and tiny teams. A one-person operator cannot truthfully promise 24/7 chat or one-hour responses, yet templates and vendor defaults push exactly those claims (Intercom's default is literally 24/7 availability until configured). Drafters must make every promise a configuration value the operator sets from their real hours — and make "state your actual hours" the default pattern, not a warning footnote. The Real Content Rule applies to support copy with full force: an unstaffed SLA on a pricing page is fabricated content.
- **Metric definitions drift across departments.** Activation (D18/D1/D12), feedback scores (D18/D12), and churn reasons (D18/D11 billing) are co-owned. Phase 3 must fix one canonical definition per metric in one skill and cross-reference it elsewhere, or the skills will quietly define the same metric three different ways.
- **Legal ground under cancellation is moving.** The Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated in July 2025 on procedural grounds and the FTC restarted rulemaking in 2026; ROSCA, Section 5, and state auto-renewal laws are the operative requirements today, but this will change. Cancellation and trial rules in the skills should cite the duty (clear disclosure, express consent, simple cancellation) rather than any single rule, and carry a review trigger for the renewed rulemaking (handoff to D14).
- **Vendor defaults vs. vendor neutrality.** The concrete procedures above are documented on Zendesk, Intercom, Stripe, and Statuspage because those vendors publish their models; the skills must teach the model (one queue, verified identity, configured expectations, cadence) in a tool-agnostic way, with vendor specifics confined to examples — otherwise the skills rot with any vendor's UI redesign.
- **Save-offer pressure.** Churn-prevention is the skill most likely to be misused: every technique in it (exit surveys, matched offers, dunning) sits one step from a dark pattern. Phase 3 drafting should include the negative examples and the friction audit as mandatory parts of the skill, not optional ethics commentary — the gate is "cancel completes cleanly when the offer is declined," tested, not asserted.
- **Status-page ownership for solo operators.** The cadence discipline assumes someone can post while someone else fixes. A single operator doing both will miss updates during real incidents; skills should include a degraded-mode pattern (pre-written holding updates, longer stated cadence honestly set) rather than pretending the two-person model always applies.
